Timeline B · Campaign B15

The Crownless War

Brevair Severance and Durrel Ridge Succession Era

Age of Founding, Reforged

Late c. 162 ARV, shortly after Campaign B14 Primary locations: Durrel Ridge, the Brevair border forests, a ruined extraction shrine, an abandoned Ridge hunting lodge, the Lantern river house, Rhalen's inner palace

Core Premise

Silver Bastion has been reclaimed, leaving Brevair as the greatest remaining concentration of organised Veil power in the region. Rhalen Valecour continues to rule without publicly wearing a crown — his authority embedded through Delryn, his husband and living Keystone, quietly routing military commands, civic appointments, household obedience, grain distribution, judicial decisions, gate permissions, and administrative authority through a single concealed magical architecture. Brevair functions because countless people are being denied the ability to refuse. When an operative penetrates Durrel Ridge carrying a request for help from inside Brevair, Taren is forced to decide whether Delryn can be extracted without destroying the structures built through him — a mission that becomes a confrontation with whether an entire nation should be preserved if its stability depends on magical enslavement. No ruler has the right to turn another person's past choice into permanent ownership — but liberation can also become coercive when imposed without full truth or consent. See the Lore page for how this campaign's central relationship fits the site's wider pattern of devotion redirected toward control.

Party & Key Figures

  • Taren Durrel PC Human Paladin, Oath of the Ridge, Level 8 rising to 9, ~46 years old, Warden of Durrel Ridge. His personal arc began in B9; B15 closes both that longer B9–B15 arc and the Rhalen–Brevair conflict introduced in B10.
  • Captain Mara
    Taren's longtime archer, confidante, and field captain; accompanies the Brevair strike and maintains its tactical discipline.
  • Cassien
    Arcane Warden of Durrel Ridge; removes Garran's execution collar, helps sever Delryn from the Keystone, and secretly enables Taren's final journey into Rhalen's palace.
  • Mike Rookvale
    Durrel Ridge scout and swashbuckler; infiltrator, battlefield skirmisher, and bridge operative during the extraction.
  • Caelen Durrel
    Oath of Vengeance paladin; helps destroy the retrieval perimeter and sever Rhalen's death-command from Delryn.
  • Garran Vey
    Brevair operative connected to the Ashen Hand; enters the Ridge as an intruder, reveals Delryn's request for extraction, becomes guide, witness, and eventual detainee.
  • Delryn Valecour
    Rhalen's husband, former player character of Campaign B10, public face of Brevair, and living Keystone through which Rhalen maintains national control.
  • Rhalen Valecour
    Hidden ruler of Brevair, architect of the Keystone, central antagonist of the B10–B15 Brevair arc.
  • Jack Durrel
    Warden of Meaning and Taren's cousin; holds Durrel Ridge during the mission and later accepts the position of Taren's named successor.
  • Jorah
    Jack's partner; part of the household receiving Taren home, and explicitly named in Jack's own terms for accepting the succession.

Act-by-Act

Act I — The Cedar Box and the Intruder in the Ridge

A cedar box and silver key appear inside Taren's locked solar, opening a Veil corridor within the supposedly secure house. A voice tests Taren through the breach and produces a false image of his deceased grandmother Seren Durrel; Taren recognises the manipulation for what it is and answers it with Luminara's light. Mara wounds the fleeing intruder. The Ridge locks down, and the intruder — Garran Vey, wearing imitation Ridge cloth and a black execution collar meant to kill him if his mission fails — is captured. The breach traces to a stolen fragment of old Heart Ward work, unknowingly carried home by Rellan Vos after Silver Bastion's reclamation; Rellan is confirmed not a collaborator, the fragment planted on him and triggered remotely.

Act II — The Reversal, the Black Ring, and the Ashen Hand

Rather than let the hostile tether remain a one-way weapon, Taren and Cassien perform a Radiant Reversal through the stolen fragment, sending Luminara's power back along the breach into a hidden ritual chamber inside Brevair — breaking part of the ritual, freeing several bound participants, and leaving Rhalen's system visibly marked by Light. Someone inside the chamber throws a black Valecour ring through the breach, "HELP" scratched into its inside. Cassien and Jack repair the Heart Ward openly rather than concealing the wound. Taren orders Garran's execution collar removed rather than letting it kill him — his heart briefly stops during the removal, held stable by Taren's aura — and gives him basic decency without automatic trust. Garran reveals his connection to the Ashen Hand, an internal Brevair resistance of magistrates, soldiers, servants, priests, and officials who helped build Rhalen's system before understanding they'd built a cage — and that their Grey Witness, holding current intelligence on Rhalen's network, is Delryn Valecour.

Act III — Delryn's Request and Taren's Succession Plan

Garran met Delryn six months earlier; Delryn requested extraction rather than immediate revolution or assassination, asking only to be taken somewhere Rhalen couldn't reach him for three days, fearing what happens when his removal causes the Keystone to fail. Taren refuses to bring Delryn to the Ridge itself, contacting the Lantern network instead. The current Lantern leadership honours the old mutual-aid pact with Durrel Ridge and approves a warded river house outside central Evermere, equipped with severance wards, healers, and an evacuation route. Before leaving, Taren writes a sealed succession plan naming Jack Durrel his successor without consulting him first, protecting Caelen and Corvin, Mike and Evan, the Lantern alliance, Mara's authority, and the wider household, with explicit instruction that Dena is to be listened to. The extraction party — Taren, Mara, Mike, Caelen, Cassien, and Garran — departs; Jack holds the Ridge's repaired Heart Ward, Corvin coordinates the retreat, Dena oversees the household.

Act IV — The Ruined Shrine and the Retriever's Chain

The party reaches a ruined border shrine near midnight, where Delryn waits, exhausted but acting under his own will, surrounded by eight Brevair soldiers marked with black Keystone throat-bindings and a hidden specialist called a Retriever. The Keystone will react violently if Delryn is dragged, teleported, compelled, or forcibly carried — he can leave only by choosing to walk, and carries poison meant to kill himself before Rhalen can reclaim him. The Retriever attacks with a Veil chain connected directly to Rhalen; Taren intercepts it with his shield and pulls the Retriever down. Rhalen speaks through the connection, declaring Taren has taken something belonging to him — Taren answers that Delryn belongs to himself. A second Radiant Reversal doesn't command or move Delryn; it creates temporary space between his identity and Rhalen's control, insulating his will and heartbeat for three days. For the first time in years, Delryn can't feel Rhalen inside the bond. Rhalen calls his name through the chain as command, intimacy, and threat. Delryn answers, "No." The reversal wounds Rhalen directly and exposes fragments of Brevair's hidden network; Taren kills the Retriever.

Act V — The Retrieval Perimeter and the Death-Command

The remaining soldiers form a magical retrieval perimeter, their bodies used as anchors despite clear signs several are resisting the commands imposed on them. The party fights to break the perimeter without treating every compelled soldier as willing — Mara shoots weapons, hands, and tether points rather than killing indiscriminately; Caelen confronts soldiers whose bodies act against their own expressed wishes; Delryn uses his remaining authority to help Lieutenant Sorn resist the Keystone himself. The perimeter collapses before Delryn can be pulled back, and Rhalen's system immediately activates a death-command through his body — black geometry spreading toward his heart. Mike strips the poison from Delryn's hand; Taren spends part of his Lay on Hands keeping his heart stable; Cassien exposes the command-thread; Caelen holds back the outer pattern; Garran anchors Delryn through personal memories belonging to neither Rhalen nor the ritual. Rhalen argues that Delryn's original choice binds him forever — Taren answers that choice must be permitted to happen again, and Delryn declares he chose Rhalen once, not forever. The death-command severs; the soldiers' throat-bindings fracture with it. Taren orders the survivors to surrender at Silver Bastion for treatment and questioning rather than punishment.

Act VI — The Secret Severance of the Keystone

The surviving Keystone keeps trying to measure Delryn's distance and rebuild its route through him; mounted patrols begin tracking the pulse. Sheltering in an abandoned Ridge-allied hunting lodge, Taren privately tells Cassien the remaining Keystone must be destroyed completely, regardless of Brevair's civic systems — and orders him not to tell Delryn or the rest of the party the full intent. Cassien objects that this turns Delryn into a weapon without informed consent, but agrees to assist, making clear the decision and its consequences belong to Taren alone. He tells the others they're only suppressing the pulse to prevent tracking — true, but incomplete. Inside the sealed lodge room, Cassien draws the remaining Keystone root from Delryn's chest; Delryn realises the ritual is more than temporary suppression and asks what Taren is doing. Taren tells him they're bringing him back to the Light — true, but not the whole truth; Delryn trusts the answer and stops resisting, never told the entire national structure is being destroyed through him. Taren channels Luminara's fire through every remaining branch of the network, reaching Brevair's gates, courts, grain houses, military commands, estates, and offices at once. Officials, guards, servants, and soldiers are abruptly released from compulsions many didn't fully understand they carried; some institutions keep functioning through ordinary authority, others collapse or divide. Rhalen tries to hold the network together and is struck by the returning Light. The Keystone is destroyed entirely. Delryn's heart stops during the severance; Taren and Cassien restore it before the last thread disintegrates. He survives with a branching scar over his sternum and no active tether remaining. Cassien agrees to preserve the account of what happened for now, but does not absolve Taren of the deception.

Act VII — The Lantern River House

With the Keystone pulse gone, the mounted patrol loses the party's trail. At an authenticated Lantern bridge, a wounded Lantern warden and a second, false watcher — revealed as an infiltrator carrying a Brevair signal bracelet — nearly compromise the crossing; Mike and Garran intercept the infiltrator before he can kill the wounded warden, and Taren destroys the signalling device before it can reveal their location. The party reaches the old Lantern river house without further pursuit; Lantern healers take Delryn into a prepared severance chamber, and the extraction is formally completed. Taren advances to Paladin Level 9.

Act VIII — The One-Way Passage and Rhalen's Death

After the others sleep, Taren takes Cassien outside the Lantern wards and asks him to reopen the Heart Ward as a passage into Brevair — he intends to kill Rhalen personally before another structure can be built around him. Cassien has preserved a fragment of Rhalen's magical signature in blackened violet glass, enough to guide the ward toward the last place Rhalen directly touched the Keystone. He teleports himself and Taren back to Durrel Ridge — Jack notices their return, but Cassien acts before he can be stopped — and turns the repaired Heart Ward outward into a one-way passage. Taren crosses alone; the route seals behind him. Rhalen stands in the ruined Keystone chamber with no network, soldiers, or living conduit left to protect him. He accuses Taren of freeing Delryn through deception and destroying Brevair through a choice Delryn was never allowed to understand. Taren doesn't argue the point — he states he came to kill Rhalen, overwhelms him before he can restore his defence, and kills him with a critical strike and an invocation of Luminara's radiance. Taren takes Rhalen's cloak and escapes through a palace window unseen; Brevair's guards find the body but never identify the killer.

Act IX — Delryn's Custody

Back at the river house, Delryn reads Rhalen's death in Taren's armour, sword, and manner before being told. He asks whether Rhalen suffered — Taren answers the death was quick, that Luminara is merciful. Delryn acknowledges he loved Rhalen, and just as firmly refuses to let that love, or victimhood, or the severance erase his own complicity: he helped build the regime and won't accept the kindness of being declared innocent. Taren orders him transferred to protected custody at Silver Bastion rather than immediate punishment — medical treatment first, formal accounting after. Garran volunteers to accompany him as a witness, accepting Bastion custody and judgment for his own actions too.

Act X — The Return to Durrel Ridge

Taren, Caelen, and Mike travel home unopposed as rumours of Brevair's fall outrun any official account — that Delryn killed Rhalen, that Brevair's own soldiers rebelled, that Luminara struck the palace personally. Taren refuses to correct any of them. At the gates, Jack and Jorah receive him, Corvin reunites with Caelen, Evan with Mike — and Dena interrupts any attempt at a formal military welcome, ordering everyone into the kitchens to eat before discussing Brevair at all. She asks whether Taren did what he believed was right, or merely what he'd decided was necessary. He answers that Brevair's people weren't free and now have the chance to choose, and that he didn't act for glory — refusing banners, statues, or any official account naming him as Rhalen's killer, letting the contradictory rumours stand.

Act XI — The Succession of Durrel Ridge

Once the household settles, Dena returns Taren's sealed will. Recognising that naming Jack without consulting him was unfair, Taren calls Jack and Jorah into Dena's kitchen and tells him the truth. Jack initially questions why a cadet-branch Warden of Meaning should inherit Taren's place at all; Taren makes clear the succession is meant as a choice, not an entitlement of blood. Jack agrees on his own terms — the arrangement stays known within the household rather than announced immediately, the document distinguishes what transfers automatically from what needs Ridge confirmation, Jack may name an alternative if circumstance makes him unsuitable, and any future he accepts includes Jorah. The protections for Caelen and Corvin, Mike and Evan, Mara, the Lantern alliance, and the household remain; Dena's instruction to be listened to stays one of the strongest clauses. The sealed death instruction becomes an open, amendable household agreement. Taren tells Jack that although they're cousins, he considers him his brother — Jack answers that Taren is his brother too, and accepts responsibility without treating succession as ownership.

World State After Campaign B15

Geography. Durrel Ridge remains secure and becomes the confirmed continuity-house of the northern Light resistance. Silver Bastion, fully restored, now also serves as a protected custody, recovery, and testimony site for those emerging from Brevair's system. Brevair remains geographically intact but loses the hidden magical structure connecting its palace, gates, granaries, courts, military routes, and civic offices. Rhalen's inner palace survives physically but loses its central Veil authority. The Lantern river house remains a functioning extraction and severance site outside central Evermere.

Politics/Institutions. Rhalen's uncrowned rule ends; Brevair no longer has a single hidden command structure capable of forcing civic and military obedience through Delryn. Older magistrates, officers, households, and local powers begin reasserting authority — some institutions continue functioning ordinarily, others fragment, close, or fall into conflict. Durrel Ridge deliberately refuses to annex Brevair or choose its successor government. Taren's role in Rhalen's death stays publicly unconfirmed, protected by conflicting rumours he declines to correct. Delryn will face formal accounting after recovery rather than being declared wholly innocent or automatically condemned. The Ashen Hand survives as a possible internal witness network, its future structure unresolved. Jack is recognised within the household as Taren's intended successor — succession now a negotiated household and institutional process rather than a private bloodline assumption.

Magic. The Keystone is permanently destroyed; Delryn no longer carries any active command-route, location tether, death-command, or civic control structure. A person's earlier consent is confirmed not to create permanent magical ownership — Rhalen's attempt to treat one past choice as an eternal claim is rejected by Delryn and broken through Light. The campaign is equally clear that Light itself can be used coercively when truth or consent is withheld: Taren's destruction of the Keystone frees countless people, but he achieves it partly by keeping Delryn from understanding the full scope of what was being done through him. Neither side of that tension is allowed to fully absolve the other.

The Durrel Line. Closes both Taren's own B9–B15 arc and the Rhalen–Brevair conflict introduced in B10. Jack Durrel becomes Taren's acknowledged household successor; Taren reaches Paladin Level 9. Taren and Jack openly recognise each other as brothers as well as cousins — the clearest statement yet that the Ridge's continuity runs on chosen family and negotiated agreement, not just bloodline.

Canon status: follows the source account directly; no PG-13 reframing was needed for this campaign. This completes Timeline B's current roster, B1–B15.
