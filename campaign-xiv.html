Timeline A · Campaign XIV

The Dominion Cycle

Second Cyberpunk Era — ends in the Cataclysm

7920–8040 ARV Primary location: The Citadel of the Vale

Opening World State

Still pre-Concord but firmly cyberpunk: neon, nodes, and civic cults of order. Roughly five centuries after the Quiet Republics of Campaign XIII, Rhett of the Vale rises among fractured city-states — Veylaris still trades, Evermere wanes, the Citadel of Inheritance stands half empty — preaching that motion breeds decay, and that a world exhausted by choice is ready to be told what stillness feels like.

Party & Key Figures

  • Rhett of the Vale Disappeared into the Northern Wilds; presumed dead
    Emperor; Architect of Stillness; founder of the Dominion. Consort to Niall Argent, creator of the Twin Chains.
  • Niall Argent Alive by tradition; mythic figure in Renewal Age lore
    Witness and Consort; bearer of the Pattern Complete; first Warden of Motion. Founded the Sanctum of Renewal after Rhett's disappearance.
  • The Veil (entity) Active elemental presence
    Sentient force of balance, reclaimed from Dominion captivity through Niall's mercy and Rhett's collapse; manifests as auroras and storms in the Age of Renewal.
  • Artisans of the Pattern (collective) Survived the fall
    Guild of crafters who expanded the Pattern Complete and forged the Twin Chains; reformed as caretakers of relic fragments under the Collegium of Renewal.

Act-by-Act

Act I — The Accord Made Flesh

Rhett inherits a fractured world and sets out to weld it into unity. His central creed — "Stillness is mercy" — promises peace without struggle, and with Niall Argent standing as his Witness, he remakes the world into the Dominion: an empire where order itself becomes religion. Through Niall, Rhett forges the Pattern Complete — armour of black leather and living silver, a literal map of the empire worn upon a single body — and through it, the Dominion's pulse beats in rhythm with the Veil's own last whisper. Cities march to that cadence; rebellion simply ceases; prayers align themselves with law. For a full generation, the world genuinely believes it has found perfection.

Act II — The Chain of Faith

As the years pass, the Veil itself falls silent. Niall, desperate to spare the people despair at that silence, begins quietly whispering new "revelations" of his own. Rhett receives them as divine command, answering each one with another layer of adornment — mantles, crests, bands, scripture hammered directly into metal. The Dominion enters what becomes known as its Age of Adornments, when every city starts mirroring the Witness's own vestments and every citizen wears fragments of imitation leather and thread. Order hardens into ritual. Faith becomes geometry.

Act III — The Twin Chains

When the last pulse of the Veiled Heart finally wanes, Rhett senses the lie underneath Niall's revelations. He confronts him, learns the truth, and — rather than breaking from it — chooses to preserve it. Two chains are forged upon the Witness's collar: silver for the Veil, iron for mankind. Publicly they symbolise harmony; privately, Rhett declares, "Iron will outlast silver." From here forward he rules through fear of collapse, turning monuments into doctrine. The empire gleams. It does not breathe.

Act IV — The Age of Unity

In his final decades, Rhett remakes the Pattern once more — the old spiral straightened into a single line, the Heartline, running directly from Niall's chest to the imperial crest itself. Authority now flows only one way: upward. The Dominion reaches its true zenith here, a single rhythm encompassing every province at once. Peace reigns, absolute and airless. Niall stands beside him as a living icon, silent witness to a world that has quite thoroughly forgotten how to move.

Act V — The Veil Reclaimed

When even the River's hum finally ceases, the Veil wakes. It returns not as wrath but as release: towers crack, moss spreads, wind and rain return to patternless, ordinary life. The twin chains melt into light. Rhett watches the empire he spent a lifetime perfecting dissolve into weather; Niall calls it mercy. The Veil reclaims what was bound, unmaking the Dominion without a single drop of bloodshed. Rhett vanishes into the northern forests afterward — some say seeking forgiveness, others simply silence. Niall wanders south with the now-extinguished Heart, making sure the new republics rising in the Dominion's place never again worship stillness the way Rhett's empire did.

Aftermath — The Age of Renewal

The Dominion's ruins become the soil of rebirth. Free cities rise where its towers fell; the Veil lives on now as storm, breath, and memory rather than empire. No divine hum guides the world anymore — only human rhythm does. Rhett's empire endures as cautionary scripture; Niall's name endures as a quiet blessing offered to travellers. The world calls them the Last Emperor and the Witness Who Let It Move. Their story ends where all Amoran cycles do: perfection yielding to motion, love yielding to legend.

World State After Campaign XIV

Geography. The Citadel of the Vale stands as Rhett's seat, built atop the ruins of Inheritance Proper. Evermere's own Citadel converts into the Collegium of Doctrine. The Southern Marches become home to the Sanctuary of Renewal, founded by Niall after Rhett's fall. Menge's thawed lands again become the empire's forge and frontier, its auroras glowing steady silver at the height of Dominion power.

Magic. Architectural Resonance — magic expressed through geometry and alignment rather than emotion — is this era's dominant system, requiring perfect stillness to sustain. After the fall, magic reverts to natural, unpredictable flow; the last fragments of the Pattern dissolve into ley-light that directly seeds the next age's renewal. A theological shift follows the same reversal: the Dominion Creed "To move is to err" inverts completely into the Renewal Creed, "To err is to move."

The Durrel Line. No Durrel figures appear directly this campaign — Rhett and Niall represent a fresh, entirely unrelated dyad pattern rather than a lineage continuation, demonstrating once again that the commanding/anchoring structure first established in Campaign I can recur without any blood connection to the founding line at all.

Institutions. The Sanctum of Renewal becomes the philosophical heart of the Age of Renewal and the direct prelude to Campaign XV's Third Cyberpunk Age. XIV becomes the cautionary prequel to the Neon/Concord work that follows — a demonstration of how even consent-coded bonds can slide into empire when left unbounded, providing exactly the case law and institutional trauma that later makes Concord so wary of anything resembling a throne.

Canon status: follows the source account directly, including its full five-act structure and thematic framing (Order vs. Movement, Faith as Architecture, Love as Mirror, Cycle Renewed).
