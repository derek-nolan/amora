Timeline A · Campaign XIV

The Dominion Cycle

Late Pre-Neon Civic Experiment

7920–8040 ARV Primary location: The Citadel of the Vale

Opening World State

Still pre-Concord but firmly cyberpunk: neon, nodes, and civic cults of order. Roughly five centuries after the Quiet Republics, Rhett of the Vale rises among fractured republics preaching that motion breeds decay.

Party & Key Figures

  • Rhett of the Vale
    Emperor; Architect of Stillness; founder of the Dominion. Consort to Niall Argent, creator of the Twin Chains. Disappeared in the Northern Wilds; presumed dead.
  • Niall Argent
    Witness and Consort; bearer of the Pattern Complete; first Warden of Motion. Founded the Sanctum of Renewal after Rhett's disappearance.
  • The Veil (entity)
    Sentient force of balance, reclaimed from Dominion captivity through Niall's mercy and Rhett's collapse.
  • Artisans of the Pattern
    Guild of crafters who expanded the Pattern Complete and forged the Twin Chains; survived the fall as caretakers of relic fragments.

Act-by-Act

The Accord of Stillness

Rhett's creed — "Stillness is mercy" — promises peace without struggle. With his lover Niall Argent, he remakes the world into the Dominion, an empire where order itself becomes religion. Every citizen wears sigils echoing the Pattern Complete.

The Pattern and the Twin Chains

The Pattern Complete, forged from black leather and living silver, binds Niall's body to Rhett's command lattice, fusing love with governance. When Rhett discovers Niall's gentle falsehoods meant to preserve faith, he forges the Silver and Iron Chains — to the empire, harmony; to Rhett, proof of dominion.

The Fall

During his final decades, the Veil itself begins to weaken under Rhett's rigid harmonics. Rhett disappears into the Northern Wilds. When the Veil reclaims the world, magic reverts to natural flow — unpredictable but alive.

Final World State

Niall founds the Sanctum of Renewal in the Southern Marches, teaching pilgrims to move again — walking, dancing, breathing out of rhythm. The Dominion Creed "To move is to err" inverts into the Renewal Creed "To err is to move," marking the spiritual rebirth of Amora's people. XIV becomes the cautionary prequel to the Neon/Concord work: proof that even consent-coded bonds can slide into empire when unbounded — the case law and trauma that later makes Concord so wary of "thrones."
