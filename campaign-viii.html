Timeline A · Campaign VIII

The Codifier of Order

Age of Theocracy II — "Through Discipline, Purity"

c. 5900 ARV Primary location: The Gate

Opening World State

Still the Theocracy Age, but later now — the Gate has become a city of laws layered upon laws, each one meant to close a gap the last one left open. Into that thicket steps Sirian, a legalist convinced that clarity itself might be enough to tame the theocracy's worst excesses.

Party & Key Figures

  • Sirian PC The legalist who writes the Gate Codex; motto "Through Discipline, Purity." Believes clarity of law can restrain the theocracy's excesses from within.
  • Councillors, inquisitors, and jurists
    The full institutional apparatus Sirian's Codex has to negotiate, and eventually gets weaponised by.

Act-by-Act

The Writing

Sirian sets out to write the Gate Codex, believing that if the theocracy's laws are simply made clear enough, their worst excesses can be tamed without needing to dismantle the theocracy itself. The campaign tracks the actual writing, negotiation, and drafting process in close detail — the slow, unglamorous work of turning belief into statute.

The Weaponisation

Once written, the Codex doesn't stay Sirian's alone. The party discovers, gradually and then all at once, that clarity doesn't automatically produce mercy — once a rule exists in writing, it can be picked up and used by whichever faction currently holds power, regardless of what Sirian originally intended it for.

The Long Shadow

The Codex outlives the theocracy that produced it by a wide margin. Long after the Wardens themselves fall, its doctrines keep getting quoted — by courts seeking legitimacy and by tyrants seeking cover in equal measure.

World State After Campaign VIII

Geography. The Gate is confirmed as "a city of laws layered upon laws" — its identity for the rest of the Theocracy Age and well beyond.

Magic/Law. The Gate Codex is written and formally adopted, becoming one of the most cited — and most misused — legal documents in Amoran history.

The Durrel Line. No Durrel figures appear this campaign.

Institutions. The Gate Codex becomes later ages' ultimate cautionary tale about "neutral" law — proof that discipline can simply be a prettier word for control, depending entirely on who's holding the pen.
