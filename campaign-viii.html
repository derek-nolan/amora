Timeline A · Campaign VIII

The Codifier of Order

Age of Theocracy II

c. 5900 ARV Primary location: The Gate

Opening World State

Still the Theocracy Age, but later — the Gate is now a city of laws layered upon laws. Sirian inherits a theocracy already convinced of its moral purpose from Campaign VII, and sets out to complete the Gate Codex.

Party & Key Figures

  • Sirian — Completed (doc)
    Codifies the Gate Codes and hardens order, believing a complete Codex will make the system fair rather than arbitrary. Motto: "Through Discipline, Purity."
  • Alaric — Completed (doc)
    Paladin of Light, married to Lorian; lives inside the consequences of Sirian's ambition.
  • Lorian — Completed (doc)
    Scholar of Silence, married to Alaric.

Act-by-Act

A Codex for Fairness

Sirian doesn't see himself as a tyrant — he sees inconsistency and arbitrary judgement, and believes a complete Gate Codex will fix it. Alaric and Lorian live the consequences as faith, marriage, and scholarship are all forced into legal categories.

Drafting and Weaponisation

The campaign follows the drafting, interpretation, and weaponisation of the Gate Codes. Every clarification removes one official's discretion while giving the state another permanent precedent.

Clear Law, Not Merciful Law

Sirian hardens order in the name of purity; Alaric and Lorian confront how easily intimate loyalty can be made evidence, leverage, or heresy. The age proves that clear law is not automatically merciful law.

World State After Campaign VIII

Geography. The Gate is confirmed as "a city of laws layered upon laws" — its architecture now inseparable from its legal apparatus.

Magic. No new magic system; the campaign's danger is entirely legal/institutional, a direct escalation of Campaign VII's warning about discipline without limits.

The Durrel Line. No Durrel figures this campaign — the pattern continues purely institutionally.

Institutions. The Codex outlives its authors and the theocracy itself. Long after the Wardens fall, its doctrines are quoted by courts, inquisitors, reformers, and tyrants alike. Later ages read this as the ultimate cautionary tale about "neutral" law: discipline can be a prettier word for control. This directly seeds Campaign X, where Serik Thorne — an inquisitor turned reformer — has to dismantle exactly this kind of "responsible" legal inheritance.

Canon status: follows the character register directly.
