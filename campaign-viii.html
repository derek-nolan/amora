Timeline A · Campaign VIII

The Codifier of Order

Age of Theocracy II — "Through Discipline, Purity"

c. 5900 ARV Primary location: The Gate Central legacy: The Gate Codex

Opening World State

Campaign VIII inherits the Warden state built in Campaign VII at full institutional weight. The Gate is now a city of laws layered upon laws: Executor precedent, inquisitorial rulings, magical restrictions, doctrinal exceptions, and generations of local interpretation. The problem is no longer the absence of law. It is that there is so much of it that enforcement depends on whoever has the authority to decide which rule matters most.

Sirian enters this world as the figure later remembered as the Codifier of Order. The surviving summaries preserve two different readings of him. One describes a legalist who believes clearer law can restrain the theocracy's excesses; another later reconstruction casts him as a Veilbearer-like inquisitor who turns faith into surveillance. The campaign's end-state supports both readings more strongly than it proves either private motive.

Party & Key Figures

  • Sirian PC status unresolved
    The Codifier of Order. Writes the Gate Codex under the creed "Through Discipline, Purity." Later sources disagree over whether he is best understood as reformist legalist, authoritarian inquisitor, or the campaign's Taren-side successor figure.
  • Councillors, inquisitors, and jurists
    The institutional actors who negotiate, amend, interpret, and ultimately weaponise the Codex. Their individual names have not survived in the accessible record.
  • The Gate Codex
    Not a person, but effectively the campaign's enduring antagonist and legacy: an attempt to make law neutral enough to restrain arbitrary power that instead becomes usable by any later authority able to quote it.

Act-by-Act

The Problem of Contradictory Law

The Gate has accumulated enough doctrine that two officials can often justify opposite decisions using different precedents. Sirian's project begins from a real problem: discretionary enforcement gives inquisitors enormous power. Codification promises to reduce that discretion by stating offences, procedures, punishments, and exceptions clearly.

Writing the Gate Codex

The campaign tracks the slow conversion of belief into statute. Councillors bargain over wording. Jurists try to reconcile contradictory Executor rulings. Inquisitors resist clauses that would constrain their field authority. Magical practices are classified, prohibited, licensed, or folded into approved ritual. The creed "Through Discipline, Purity" becomes attached to the finished work.

Clarity Without Mercy

The central discovery is that written clarity does not guarantee justice. A vague rule can be abused through discretion; a precise rule can be abused through consistency. Once the Codex exists, punishment can be carried out with less personal cruelty and still produce a more efficient system of control. Faith ossifies into ritual law, and the Inquisition expands across the cities under rules that now appear impersonal.

The Codex Is Weaponised

Whatever Sirian intended, the text no longer belongs to him once adopted. Rival factions learn to quote the same clauses against each other. The state can call surveillance procedure rather than suspicion. Heresy becomes an administratively legible category. Later lineage records remember Sirian as the figure who manipulates faith into surveillance; the more sympathetic campaign summary remembers him as the man who discovered too late that neutral law does not remain neutral in unequal hands.

The Long Shadow

The Warden Theocracy eventually falls, but the Gate Codex survives it. Courts cite it because it is old. Tyrants cite it because it is useful. Reformers cite it because some procedural protections really were attempts to restrain abuse. Campaign VIII therefore leaves one of Timeline A's most durable lessons: a law can outlive both the crisis that created it and the moral intent of its author.

World State After Campaign VIII

Geography. The Gate is fully established as a city of law and archive, its authority reaching far beyond its walls through Executor and inquisitorial circuits.

Magic/Law. The Gate Codex binds state and religion together in a formal legal structure. Magic is regulated through written classifications rather than ad hoc Warden judgement, but the result is not automatically more humane.

The Durrel Pattern. No literal Durrel recurrence is securely established. Later archivists cast Sirian as a Veilbearer-like successor because the Codex converts protective law into scalable control. That is an interpretive continuity model rather than proof of reincarnation.

Institutions. The Codex outlives the theocracy. Its survival turns Campaign VIII into the bridge between overt religious government and the later legal-technological cultures that inherit its obsession with written authority.

Canon status: Sirian, the Gate Codex, the creed "Through Discipline, Purity," the writing/negotiation/weaponisation of the Codex, and its survival beyond the Warden Theocracy are confirmed in the surviving summaries. Unresolved: Sirian's original player-character status and private motive. The site deliberately keeps both the reformist and authoritarian readings visible rather than choosing one without the missing campaign chat.
