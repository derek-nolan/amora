The Second Measure

Ancient Binder History / The Setting of the Eight

Core Premise

B17 is the payoff campaign for the mysteries B16 opened. Where B16 produced too many simultaneous questions at once — the eight Seals, the missing centre of the survey apparatus, the War of Ash and Glass, the Oath Wars, the Western branch, black-gold, and the deeper Rimeholt network — B17 narrows its focus deliberately. Nick wants to understand the Seals themselves: how their Warden traditions emerged, why all eight belong to Vanmere, and how the ancient wars intersected with their history. It establishes the first coherent history of how the Great Seals were set, dates Tarenus Durrel and the Shattering in relation to that process, explains why the Western Icewardens exist, and closes Nick's major personal threads without exhausting every wider Great Seal mystery — several are deliberately left for later protagonists.

Party & Key Figures

  • Nicholas "Nick" Soren PC Human Sorcerer 3, Western Icewarden Custodian. Enters B17 still twenty, shy, dryly funny, and academically curious — the campaign substantially reduces the uncertainty around the office he inherited without turning him into a grand institutional leader.
  • Jarek Flint
    Sorcerer 4; Western Way-hand; carpenter and Nick's partner. His warm-gold magic is fully independent after B16's severance. B17 closes his family history, confronts his secret conditioning of Nick, and ends with his engagement to Nick.
  • Professor Halden
    Nick's senior Collegium mentor, now in his sixties. Becomes the principal modern scholar normalising the ancient Ridge calendar into ARV, repeatedly insisting on separating direct evidence from inference.
  • Ser Nora Durrel
    New this campaign — a young Durrel paladin/Warden at Durrel Ridge, direct descendant of Jack Durrel's later line. Becomes the living Durrel witness to the recovery of Tarenus's real history.
  • Maraine Soren
    Nick's mother in Rimeholt. Closes the campaign by learning why her family came west and supporting Nick's decision to preserve both the measure and its purpose.
  • Garran Flint and Mrs Flint
    Jarek's parents in Highever. Their family history supplies the final context for Deren Flint's real fate and Jarek's own severance.
  • Maelin Harrow & Sabren Orrick
    Administrator of the Harrow Collection and a reputable Highever antiquities broker, respectively — both cooperative rather than adversarial once the oath-chain's archaeological context is established.
  • Tamsin Brack, Oren Voss & Esmond Krail
    Nick's Collegium circle, continuing from B16 — Tamsin as practical field safeguard, Oren and Esmond receiving Nick's full disclosure about the B16 killings.

Act-by-Act

Act I — A New Level, the Same Home

B17 opens only days after the Rimeholt return. Nick is now Sorcerer 3; Jarek is Sorcerer 4 and remains the more magically advanced of the pair. The early scenes are deliberately domestic — the workshop has become a genuine shared home, and Nick and Jarek are no longer negotiating whether they are together. They are. The research direction sharpens too: rather than chase every mystery B16 opened at once, Nick narrows his focus to the Seals themselves — how their Warden traditions emerged, why all eight belong to Vanmere, and how the ancient wars intersect with their history.

Act II — Jarek Flint Goes Home

Before the next expedition, Nick and Jarek fulfil a promise made during B16 and properly visit Jarek's childhood home — Nick is the first partner Jarek has brought to his parents. The visit resolves Deren Flint's real family history: Deren was Garran Flint's older brother and a legitimate former Western Way-hand who carried the same kind of foreign black-gold attachment that later clung to Jarek. Elara Soren severed it with his consent; Deren survived for years afterward and eventually died of an unrelated ordinary illness. Garran was the brother who told Deren to stay away, frightened of what had attached to him — and his conclusion once he learns what happened to Jarek is simple: his brother was never lying about his magic. For Jarek, the visit removes a false explanation of his own: his power isn't a disease inherited from a doomed uncle. The foreign claim was real; so was the Way-hand magic beneath it. One could be cut away without killing the other.

Act III — The Bastion Comparison

Back in Highever, Nick and Halden compare the Western archive against Silver Bastion records. The Bastion stays careful about jurisdiction — Nick is recognised as Western Custodian but makes no claim over the northern Frost Seal, the Frostborn, or Heartwarden authority. Senra Merith's restricted genealogy work independently confirms Bastion remembers western Frost households carrying genealogical and custodial material away from the north, with no language of expulsion or apostasy in the record; a Soren secondary household is present, but later notes explicitly prohibit tracing beyond a named bearer, and Nick honours that privacy rather than turning lineage into entitlement. Ivena Dorr's technical comparisons strengthen a recurring eightfold architecture: Silver Bastion's Heart chamber is remembered with eight positions and eight channels, and a separate older diagram uses eight peripheral Seal positions around an empty central fitting. B17 doesn't yet solve the centre — it does establish the geometry isn't accidental.

Act IV — The Western Oath-Fort

The first major field expedition sends Nick, Jarek, Tamsin, and the scholarly team toward a western oath-fort tied to late Oath Wars material — a damaged military-religious complex of pale older walls, later black volcanic rebuilding, and signs that glass once melted across stone. Recent tracks and blood confirm others have been inside. The expedition encounters three glasshounds — six-limbed creatures whose wounded bodies build toward an explosive white internal charge; cold suppresses the reaction while heat accelerates it. Two rupture; a third is killed while sufficiently chilled to preserve the body for Collegium study. The encounter gives Nick a practical discovery alongside the combat: his cold magic isn't merely thematic here — it's the safest way to control a genuinely dangerous physiological reaction.

Act V — The Seven Refusal

Below the fort lies a crypt arranged around six faceless statues and a central dead paladin, representing seven oath traditions — Redemption, Crown, Hearth, Devotion, Sanctuary, Liberty, and Fellowship — that participated in a common refusal during the Oath Wars. The central body is Ser Auren Valeth, a Fellowship paladin; the principal inscription records that when vow was turned against vow, seven refused the "righteous slaughter" and one carried their refusal to the grave. Auren would not raise his sword against people beside whom he had already held a wall; he asked each representative to speak their actual oath and found none of the oaths required what their masters were demanding. A hidden Crown inscription adds one of the campaign's most important qualifications: "Velis did not command this. Men spoke in his name. We obeyed them until obedience became the sin." The event is given the modern working name the Seven Refusal — one documented moment where oath-bound paladins refused to let hierarchy override the vows they'd actually sworn, not the whole of the Oath Wars compressed into one battle.

Act VI — Glass Through Armour

Auren's armour preserves physical evidence anchoring the Oath Wars beside the War of Ash and Glass: a literal shard of fused glass driven through it. The western sky had burned glass, and the crypt was receiving refugees while the oath conflict was still active — establishing overlap between the two wars without collapsing them into one. The Oath Wars concern competing paladin oath traditions, authority, and conscience; the War of Ash and Glass concerns sorcerous Houses, warlock covenants, and magical pressures driven catastrophically beyond control. At this site, their human consequences meet.

Act VII — The Oath-Chain and the Antiquities Trail

The crypt also holds a seven-link iron oath-chain tied to the seven traditions. Rather than loot it, the party traces its provenance through the Highever antiquities market — recovered by two treasure-hunters and placed into legitimate private acquisition channels, tracing through solicitors to the Harrow Collection. Once Maelin Harrow understands the archaeological context, the Collection withdraws its acquisition interest; Silver Bastion later purchases the chain properly. Sabren Orrick also buys surplus glasshound material for the party at market price, split fairly among the six original field participants.

Act VIII — The Harrow Collection and the Empty Centre

The Harrow Collection turns out to be exactly what it claims — a private family historical trust assembled over generations, not another hidden magical society. Its value is provenance and comparison: a drawing of eight Seal symbols arranged around a human-shaped empty centre, annotated "THE CENTRE DOES NOT HOLD. THE CENTRE CONNECTS." This strengthens the recurring eight-around-centre pattern without identifying the Bearer of No Seal, proving a ninth Seal, or turning the centre into a Way-hand office. A separate warning against camping at a place called the Western Refuge cross-compares with a Silver Bastion inventory reference — real evidence, but only two source streams, not independent confirmation. The Western Refuge stays unresolved, left for later Amora history rather than forced into B17.

Act IX — To Durrel Ridge

The evidence increasingly points toward Durrel Ridge and the ancient Heartbinder line. Until arrival, the Highever party assumes the assigned Ridge paladin escort is a man, since the correspondence never gives a gender — at the gate they meet Ser Nora Durrel instead, a young woman of Jack Durrel's later line. This corrects the family record without rewriting B15: Jack and Jorah were a couple, and no children were established during their own campaign era — later continuity through surrogacy created descendants, including Nora's line. She isn't a secret superior heir to the Ridge, just a Warden with her own legitimate branch history, and her real importance is historical rather than mechanical: a living Durrel present when the older Durrel story changes.

Act X — Tarenus, Binder of Heart

At Durrel Ridge, the expedition examines older levels beneath the better-known Warden estate — a lower chamber again showing eight positions and eight inward-running channels; Nick's Frost inheritance wakes the Frost position, and the centre responds with warm Heart-like behaviour. Tarenus Durrel's tomb provides the first decisive personal correction: TARENUS DURREL — BINDER OF HEART — FIRST WARDEN OF HIS HOUSE. His shield carries the mark of the Oath of Devotion. Tarenus wasn't merely an ancestral Heart-associated figure — he was already a Heartbinder and a Devotion paladin before any of it. An earlier inscription records his insistence that Heart be kept out of the "quarrel of the sworn," since the oaths were the paladins' own and Heart must never be made a weapon between them. B17 still doesn't invent a specific Oath Wars battle for him — it places him securely in the late Oath Wars/War of Ash and Glass world and shows the principles he held, leaving exact battlefield participation unresolved.

Act XI — The House Records

The central historical breakthrough comes from substantially intact records sent to the House of Heart by older Binder Houses. Frost reports that cold no longer keeps its bounds and answers Heart's question — it may be set, but not alone: "FROST SHALL NOT BE SET ALONE. STONE AND FLAME MUST ANSWER THEIR OWN PRESSURES. STORM MUST BE BROUGHT WITHIN MEASURE. LIFE, LIGHT AND SHADOW MUST EACH GIVE THEIR LIMIT. WHEN THE SEVEN ARE SET, HEART MAY TAKE THE MEASURE BETWEEN THEM." Stone reports the deep places no longer bear their load and answers simply: "It must." Flame reports fire feeding where nothing burns, consents to permanent setting, and supplies the earliest near-origin of the word Seal itself: when the bound is set and the limit holds, mark the place as sealed. Together the three records overturn Nick's first simple theory — the Houses didn't arise from the Seals. The Binder traditions already existed. The Great Seals were the permanent answer once older local binding methods could no longer contain world-scale pressure.

Act XII — The Setting of the Eight

A decisive Ridge plate, headed RECORD AFTER THE SETTING, removes the need for further speculation: Frost, Stone, and Flame were set within measure; Life, Light, and Shadow were set to their Houses' own limits; Storm answered last, but Storm answered. When seven held, Heart was set between them: "HEART SHALL NOT COMMAND THE SEVEN. HEART SHALL KEEP THEIR MEASURE AGAINST ONE ANOTHER, THAT NO ONE PRESSURE DRIVE THE REST FROM THEIR LIMITS." This is the cleanest original definition of Heart yet recovered — a balancing relationship among the pressures, never their sovereign. The plate closes: "TARENUS DURREL, BINDER OF HEART AND SWORN OF DEVOTION, ACCEPTS THE FIRST WATCH OF HEART. THOSE WHO COME AFTER SHALL KEEP WHAT THE BINDERS HAVE SET." The Binder/Warden distinction now has historical depth: Binders predate the Great Seals and perform the work of setting; the Warden role emerges as the enduring custodial responsibility to keep what the Binders established.

Act XIII — The Shattering Dated

The ancient Ridge material uses a calendar no one in modern Highever recognises. Halden cross-dates it against astronomical and historical anchors, normalising it into the modern ARV calendar — the negative numbering is a modern scholarly convenience; nobody at the time called their own dates negative ARV. The resulting chronology (roughly -960 to -900 ARV, from the Oath Wars' main period through the Great Seal setting cycle to the Shattering and the Western households' separation) produces a major structural conclusion: the Great Seals existed for roughly eight years before the Shattering. The Shattering didn't create the Seal system — it struck a newly completed one, disrupting the ability of Wardens to measure, report on, and in some cases reach their Seal sites. The reverse of the plate confirms Heart answers, with Frost, Flame, and Stone; reports from Life, Light, Shadow, and Storm are incomplete — not converted into destroyed, and not established as having caused the Shattering. See the Chronology page for the full dated sequence.

Act XIV — The Second Measure

The campaign's greatest Nick-specific discovery follows when the Ridge record is compared against the Western archive. The post-Shattering Frost instruction is blunt: "KEEP THE MEASURE WHERE THE ICE CANNOT TAKE IT. IF THE NORTH CANNOT ANSWER, THE RECORD MUST. THREE HOUSEHOLDS WEST BY AGREEMENT. NO CLAIM UPON THE SEAL. NO ABANDONMENT OF FROST. MEASURE DIVIDED SO MEMORY CANNOT FALL IN ONE PLACE." A further line settles the relationship between the surviving traditions: northern households remain with the Seal; western households keep the second measure; blood of Frost remains one kinship though office divides. Nick's branch was never exiled, disgraced, or apostate — it was deliberately created as a post-Shattering redundancy system. Three households travelled west carrying duplicated measures, verification standards, and containment knowledge while explicitly renouncing any rival claim to the northern Frost Seal. Rimeholt later grew out of that western settlement and forgot why it had been founded. For Nick, the answer becomes personal: the Western Custodian exists so that if northern custody, access, or memory ever fails, Frost still has a trustworthy baseline against which the world can be measured.

Act XV — Jarek Removes What He Never Needed

The ancient Binder language forces Jarek to recognise an uncomfortable similarity in his own private reasoning — he's told himself his hidden enchantments don't create Nick's feelings, only strengthen what's already there, and the Binders likewise describe regulating pressure that already exists rather than creating it. But Nick isn't an elemental pressure — he's a person, and he never consented to the hidden reinforcement. Without confessing, Jarek completely removes every hidden conditioning effect he's placed on Nick. Nothing remains, and Nick never learns anything happened. What follows matters enormously to Jarek: Nick doesn't change. He still reaches for Jarek, still relaxes into his touch, still enjoys the same private dominance, restraint, leather, containment, chastity, and being chosen for in the consensual spaces where he genuinely wants Jarek to lead — the affection, submission, and kink were Nick's before the magic and remain Nick's after it. Jarek discovers he never needed the enchantments at all. He doesn't become morally clean, and he doesn't confess — his choice stays private: he stops doing this one specific wrong while remaining possessive, dominant, and morally grey. While Nick is mostly asleep at the Ridge, Jarek whispers, "Gods, I'm going to marry you one day," believing Nick doesn't hear. Nick hears enough.

Act XVI — The Bastion Packet

Once the Ridge work is coherent, the party stops chasing fragments and records what's actually proved. Nick insists the Silver Bastion packet distinguish direct evidence from strong conclusion from unresolved question. Nora authenticates the Ridge provenance, Tamsin contributes western watch-site measurements, Halden handles palaeography and relative dating, and Nick supplies the Western comparisons while protecting restricted family material. Three copies are maintained — Durrel Ridge, Highever Collegium, and Silver Bastion. Nick signs the Western contribution in his own right: Nicholas Soren, Custodian, Western Icewarden Line.

Act XVII — What Kind of Dangerous Man?

Back in Highever, Nick convenes his B16/B17 circle and gives Halden, Oren, Esmond, and Tamsin the complete circumstances of his three B16 killings — the first an ordinary combat death, the second and third deliberate executions of a captured, interrogated operative and Vale himself, each judged too dangerous to release alive. Nick doesn't disguise either death as confusion, nor decide the only moral response is total repentance. B17 has shown him repeatedly choosing not to kill when trespassers and armed opponents can be handled without lethal force, so his conclusion sharpens: he wasn't becoming someone who kills because killing is easier — he genuinely judged those specific men too dangerous to remain alive, and accepts he could have been wrong. Esmond remains unconvinced the first execution was necessary; Tamsin says she might have stopped him for that one and is less certain about Vale; Oren is hurt most by the earlier withholding but values being told without being cornered. Halden gives the core institutional warning: being capable of lethal force doesn't disqualify Nick from custodianship — believing the office makes his judgement automatically correct would. The result is accountability, not absolution, and not a slide toward indiscriminate cruelty either.

Act XVIII — Silver Bastion Answers

The Bastion accepts the central historical revisions as sufficiently evidenced — Binder traditions predating the Great Seals, the Seals set during the War of Ash and Glass before the Shattering, Heart established last to keep the other seven measures, and Tarenus already a Heartbinder and Devotion paladin before taking the first permanent Heart watch. Most importantly for Nick, Senra formalises the Western branch in institutional language: a deliberately separated preservation branch of the older Frost custodial kinship, carrying no portion of the Frost Seal and acquiring no northern authority by doing so. Nick authorises Silver Bastion to preserve a complete authenticated copy of the non-private Western custodial corpus — protected genealogies and explicit do-not-trace branches remain protected — so the old failure of memory can't happen in only one place again.

Act XIX — Home to Rimeholt

The campaign deliberately closes away from another ruin. Nick and Jarek travel to Rimeholt to tell Maraine what the family finally knows — the opposite of B16's return, with no coded emergency and nothing hostile beneath the town. Maraine reads the Western departure record and immediately understands its emotional meaning: their ancestors weren't running away, they were sent west by agreement because Frost needed its memory to survive catastrophe. Nick adds a plain account to Elara's archive — no puzzle, no scattered clues — recording why the Western branch exists, what the second measure is, why blood doesn't compel office, and why the reason for preserving the measure must survive alongside the procedures themselves: "The Western Custodian shall preserve not only the measure, but the reason for preserving it. The measure must be capable of surviving me." Maraine supports that future while holding onto the lesson of her own life — whoever comes after Nick must be allowed to choose; a future child, relative, or recognised bearer is a person before they're a Custodian.

Act XX — The Golden Snowflake

Nick's existing frost and crystal tattoos, done in Rimeholt before he ever understood their Icewarden significance, gain a single golden snowflake worked into the older frostwork — Frost is Nick, gold is Jarek, and the symbolism is intentionally personal rather than institutional. Every hidden enchantment is gone, and Nick is voluntarily placing a permanent symbol of Jarek into his own skin by choice.

Act XXI — The Proposal

Before the tattoo, Nick secretly shows Maraine a ring and swears her to secrecy — he's been planning since Durrel Ridge, having heard Jarek's whispered intention. On a picnic outside Rimeholt, Nick reveals he heard the Ridge whisper and refuses to let "one day" belong only to Jarek's timetable. He proposes first. Jarek says yes, then admits he'd been planning to ask Nick himself. The inversion matters to their relationship — the more submissive, less possessive partner is the one who freely initiates the permanent commitment, becoming the strongest possible proof to Jarek that control was never necessary to keep Nick beside him.

Act XXII — The Future They Choose

The final morning returns to the Soren kitchen. The Western archive is secure, Maraine is home, and Nick and Jarek are engaged. Jarek proposes one more decision: when Nick finishes at the Collegium, would he consider moving back to Rimeholt? There's enough carpentry work to relocate his workshop, and Highever would stay close enough for family and colleagues while their own home could stand near Maraine and the Western archive. Nick answers: "If you're there I can live anywhere. But I'd love that." The plan isn't an immediate relocation or an immutable prophecy — Nick finishes school first — but at B17's close, they genuinely intend to build a future in Rimeholt together.

World State After Campaign B17

Historical Understanding. Ancient Binder Houses predate the permanent Great Seal system. The War of Ash and Glass pushed magical pressures beyond the capacity of local, temporary binding. Frost, Stone, Flame, Life, Light, Shadow, and Storm were set first; Heart was set last, between them, to keep their measure against one another rather than command them. Tarenus Durrel was already a Heartbinder and Devotion paladin before accepting the first permanent Heart watch. The Great Seals were functioning roughly eight years before the Shattering, which damaged reporting, access, and measurement across the network without yet proving any specific Seal destroyed. The Western Frost branch was deliberately established shortly afterward to preserve a second measure.

Institutional/Archival. Silver Bastion formally accepts and preserves the Western Icewardens as a legitimate preservation branch with no northern Seal claim. Non-private Western custodial material is now intentionally distributed among Rimeholt, Highever Collegium, Silver Bastion, and relevant Ridge cross-records, while protected family branches and do-not-trace instructions remain protected. The seven-link oath-chain is under legitimate Silver Bastion custody; the Harrow Collection remains a cooperative private historical trust, not a hidden faction.

Relationship/Personal. Nick and Jarek are engaged. Jarek's hidden conditioning of Nick has been completely removed, with Nick remaining unaware it ever existed. Nick and Maraine's relationship is stable and active; Nick intends to finish his Collegium education before the two of them relocate to Rimeholt together.

Open threads carried forward. B17 deliberately closes Nick's major inherited questions without finishing the wider Great Seal story. Left open for later Amora history: the ultimate identity and origin of the black-gold phenomenon and its connection (if any) to "the bound"; the original builders and full network of the deeper Rimeholt transit infrastructure; the identity of the Bearer of No Seal at the empty centre of the eightfold apparatus; the exact location of the Western Refuge; the current location and condition of the Frost Seal itself and the other Great Seals, especially Life, Light, Shadow, and Storm; the exact trigger of the War of Ash and Glass; the full military and political history of the Oath Wars; and the precise extent of Tarenus's own participation in its fighting.

Canon status: follows the source account closely — a genuinely dense archaeological/lore campaign with minimal need for reframing. This site's mature, non-graphic editorial standard continues to apply: Jarek and Nick's private dynamic is described clearly enough to understand what's consensual and what Jarek privately corrects without narrating explicit content.
