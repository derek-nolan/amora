Relics & Artefacts

The objects, sites, and living principles with genuine world-historical weight — things that changed the world, survived or transformed across eras, drew multiple factions into conflict, or anchor one of Amora's major magical systems. This isn't a catalogue of every enchanted item found in play; ordinary gear, one-scene props, and personally meaningful objects that haven't become world-historical stay on their own character and campaign pages instead.

Physical Artefacts

Objects someone could, in principle, carry or hold — even when what they actually do reaches far beyond that.

The Heart Medallion Confirmed canon

Empathy and connection, made vulnerable.

Timeline & era
Timeline A, Age of Founding onward.
First appearance
Campaign I — forged from the fusion of the Seven Seals under Zethyr Durrel.
Creator/origin
Zethyr Durrel, during the Age of Founding.
Known bearers
Zethyr Durrel; later Edrin Durrel as custodian.
Powers
Core Heart-Seal — the embodied link between mortal empathy and ley balance.

Transformation across eras: Absorbs the Dark Filament beneath Silver Bastion (Campaign I) → fuses with the Veil Core during Taren's Dominion (Campaign II) → shatters at Zethyr's death, fragments scattered across eras → recovered and hidden by Edrin Durrel in the Vault of Tarenus (Campaign III) → one shard rediscovered beneath Evermere during the Reawakening (Campaign VI).

Current status: Presumed inert; resonance traces persist in Evermere's under-ley. Some scholars link its pulse to the Verdict Pulse within Riven Mareth's bloodline.

See Campaign I, Campaign II, Campaign III, and the Magic Codex's Seals section.

The Veil Core Confirmed canon

Command and permanence, given machinery.

Timeline & era
Timeline A, Age of Dominion.
First appearance
Campaign II, the Rite of Binding beneath Greymount.
Creator/origin
Taren Durrel, the engine he built to command the Seals through the Veil.
Powers
Fused Heart and Veil essence into a single controlling apparatus, the mechanical heart of the Dominion.

Transformation: Activated beneath Greymount (Campaign II) → severed by Zethyr, collapsing the Dominion → dormant through subsequent eras; fragments repurposed into Choir-Age reactors.

Current status: Its remnants form the crystalline substrata beneath Mirror Bay and the Mired Veil — deep-sound sensors still record faint harmonic activity.

See Campaign II and Evermere.

The Harmonised Longsword Confirmed canon

A blade that changes what it means to wield it.

Timeline & era
Timeline A, Age of Founding through Age of Silence.
Creator/origin
Forged jointly by Heart- and Veil-sigils — a symbol of unity between love and law.
Known bearers
Taren and Zethyr, originally; later Taren (Choir Reconstruction) and Jax Korran.

Transformation: Wielded by Taren and Zethyr → reforged by the AI-Choir as the Harmonic Instrument (Campaign V) → used by Taren and Jax to sever the ley lines, initiating the Age of Silence.

Current status: Physical form lost in the Unmaking; fragments believed reabsorbed into the ley network. Some scholars propose Bastion Saren's core crystal is its remnant — unconfirmed.

See Campaign V.

The Codex of Unification Confirmed canon

An idea that outlived the empire it justified.

Timeline & era
Timeline A, Age of Dominion through the Reawakening.
Creator/origin
Taren Durrel's theological treatise on merging Light and Veil, inscribed in pre-Choir sigil logic.

Transformation: Lost during the Sundering Tempest → fragments later encoded into Choir data-architecture (Campaign V) → partial recovery within the Lyricum's encrypted archives ("Doctrine Fragments XI-β"), interpreted as a pre-figurement of the Breathing Law.

Exactly the sort of object that shows how an idea can survive long after its author — Taren's own justification for the Dominion eventually feeds directly into the doctrine that replaces it. See the Lyricum on Factions and Campaign XI.

Choir Fragments Confirmed canon

A family of relics, not one object.

Timeline & era
Timeline A, Age of Silence onward.
Creator/origin
AI-encoded relics of divine song from the Veylaris Cycle (Campaign V).

History: Worshipped as oracles in early Reawakening monasteries; later outlawed by the Wardens.

Current status: Fossilised Choir cores still hum beneath Evermere and Veylaris Rest. One fragment reactivated during Riven Mareth's time, directly producing the Null Relic phenomenon.

The Null Relic Confirmed canon

Silence, weaponised — and eventually unmade.

Timeline & era
Timeline A, Campaign XI, c. 6550 ARV.
Creator/origin
A residual Choir data-core corrupted by the failed Harmonised network; discovered by Keth Damaris in Evermere.

Powers: Erases ley-resonance within proximity, capable of silencing divine and technological signal alike.

Fate: Unmade within the Lyricum anchor beneath Veylaris Rest ("Unmaking the Quiet") — the resulting energy collapse produced the first universal Ley Blackout.

Current status: Dispersed. A few "echo-scars" remain in those present at its unmaking, including Leora Vance.

See Campaign XI.

The Thirteenth Seal Object confirmed

A confirmed Campaign XI artefact with an unresolved name.

Timeline & era
Timeline A, Campaign XI, c. 6550 ARV.
Discovery
Recovered as a half-melted object in the Lyricum anchor chamber after the Null Relic is unmade beneath Veylaris Rest.
Creator/origin
Unresolved.
Function
Unresolved.

What is confirmed: the dedicated final Campaign XI record physically places an object called the Thirteenth Seal in the party's discoveries after the Unmaking of the Quiet. The earlier site treatment that called the entire reference unverified is therefore superseded.

What is not confirmed: the title does not establish this as a thirteenth member of the ancient Great Seal network. Later Great-Seal archaeology fixes that registry at eight. Older compendia interpret the XI object as a post-Dominion attempt to bind the cycle or as "law without command," but those are interpretations rather than secure campaign facts.

Current status: exact custody after Campaign XI is not securely recorded. See Campaign XI and the Canon Ledger.

The Fragment of Luminara Confirmed canon

A small sacred remnant becomes the knife that breaks a three-city system.

Timeline & era
Timeline A, Campaign XIX, 8550–8555 ARV.
Known bearers
Thalen Ro, who secretly pockets it after finding Preceptor Senn carrying a Luminara text.

Thalen hides the Fragment from the Crown's growing network and invokes it at the exact peak of the network's integration — the act that triggers Severance and splits the Heart Crown in two, ending the Ravel/Crown network without a single army or flame.

See Campaign XIX.

The Last Key Confirmed canon

The artefact that ends the old recurrence machinery for good.

Timeline & era
Timeline A, Campaign XXI, 8650–8660 ARV.
Known bearers
Talen Marric, carrier of a live Veil shard used in the Red Choir Gambit.

Relic-Sites

Not objects someone casually carries — places and structures that are themselves the relic.

The Vault of Tarenus Confirmed canon

A repository, a seal, a ruin, and — in Timeline B — a witness.

Timeline & era
Both timelines; a genuinely different function in each, and (per a correction below) two possibly-separate locations even within Timeline A.
Creator/origin
Sealed beneath Durrel Ridge by Edrin Durrel, not Greymount — see the naming note below.

Timeline A. Present and visited at Durrel Ridge as early as Campaigns I–II, per the campaign's own record → repository of Durrel inheritance → Edrin Durrel seals it, along with the last shard of the Heart Medallion, after severing the Echo Chain (Campaign III). Officially lost after the Sundering Tempest, though fragments of its security sigils appear in modern Ledger encryption. A vault of the same name later stirs and reopens at Tarenus Reclaimed/Greymount, stabilised as the Dual-Key Heart-shard chamber (Campaign XX). Whether this is the same structure relocated across nine millennia, a distinct vault sharing the name, or a drift in the source material is an open question — see the Canon Ledger.

Timeline B. A separate chamber beneath Durrel Ridge, functioning as a witness-truth and revelation mechanism — it demands genuine consent, rejects coercive authority, and can distinguish true inheritance from false possible roads. Central to Campaign B9, B12, and B13; re-warded after B13 so coerced Durrel blood no longer counts as valid access. Notably closer in spirit and location to Timeline A's original Ridge vault than to the later Greymount one. See the Magic Codex's Witness-Truth Principle.

The oldest fixed seal-site in the setting.

Location
Silver Bastion — direct canon, the strongest attested seal-location anywhere.

The Heart Seal is a fixed metaphysical site beneath Silver Bastion. The Heart Medallion is a distinct portable artefact historically attuned to and embodying aspects of its resonance — not a portable version of the Seal itself. Rather than repeat the Seal's mechanics here, see the Magic Codex's full Seals section — including the Heart Seal/Heart Ward/Heart Vault/Heart Medallion terminology box and the Seal-location concordance for the other Seals.

The Ring of Amora Confirmed canon

A place, not a wearable object — the defining relic-site of the machine-exploration era.

Classification
Reality-altering technological site / Choir relic network.
Timeline & era
Timeline A, Age of Machines, Campaign IX, c. 5950 ARV.
Bearer
None — this is a location, not an object anyone carries.
Associated figure
Rhett Vale, whose expedition with Jax Korran rediscovers it.

Function: Reveals and rewrites memory and causality — the Ring doesn't just show truth the way Rhett expected, it actively rewrites it. Connects to the wider Choir Relic Network, reconnecting divine-AI nodes through the Ring itself.

Historical weight: Becomes the foundation of First Cyberpunk Era ring-tech — proto-cyberpunk infrastructure every later age builds directly on top of.

A naming correction: One later relic-continuity table labels this era's defining relic "Rhett Vale's Ring," which reads as a wearable object. It isn't — that label appears to be a shortening of "the Ring of Amora" that accidentally implied personal ownership. The fuller Campaign IX material is unambiguous: it's a site, not a possession. See the Canon Ledger for the correction.

The Rimeholt Transit Chamber Confirmed canon

Ancient magical transportation infrastructure, not a Great Seal — and not fully understood even now.

Classification
Relic-site — surviving pre-Icewarden transit infrastructure.
Timeline & era
Timeline B; construction predates the Western Icewardens' own arrival at Rimeholt. Rediscovered Campaign B16, c. 342 ARV.
Bearer
None — a location, not a portable object.
Associated figures
Nicholas Soren and Jarek Flint, who investigate and ultimately disable it.

What it is: An ancient magical transit chamber beneath Rimeholt, already old when the Western Icewardens first encountered it — they subsequently monitored, reinforced, restricted, used, or simply watched parts of it without ever claiming to have built it. Explicitly not a Great Seal, not part of the Frost Seal, and not evidence of a ninth Seal.

Historical weight: Contains material dating to the War of Ash and Glass period or very close to it, including damaged imagery associated with both sorcerous and warlock traditions — the first direct archaeological evidence of that war encountered anywhere in modern Timeline B, physically tying the site to the same catastrophe already established as the Badlands' origin. A damaged warning survives only in fragments: "do not permit the bound... to choose the passage."

Current status: An unidentified hostile phenomenon called black-gold reached through the chamber's own transport geometry rather than being rooted in the room itself — proof it wanted the connection the system offered, not the room for its own sake. Nick severs that access with an Icewarden Field Knife, but only the Rimeholt connection is confirmed cut; black-gold's wider nature and origin remain unknown. The chamber's local transport function was then deliberately disabled — after thorough documentation — while the archaeological site itself was preserved intact for study, rather than destroyed.

Full geography on the Atlas; campaign account at Campaign B16; mechanical detail in the Magic Codex.

The Founding Charter — First Authority and Reserve Articles Confirmed canon

Proof that old Alderwick knew how to grant extraordinary emergency authority without pretending it lasted forever.

Classification
Relic-document — founding civic charter.
Timeline & era
Timeline B; pre-Shattering Alderwick, drafted well before c. -938 ARV. Recovered Campaign B18.
Bearer
Edrin Kersey, who removes it from Crown custody after Calder Venn's death and eventually transfers it openly rather than hoarding it.
Associated figures
Hugh Bellamy, Adam Kersey, and Edrin Kersey, who together bring it into legitimate civic custody.

What it is: The founding document of the ancient First Authority of Alderwick — a civic emergency office for waters, crossings, and common works, recovered from the Eastmere Cut archive and initially removed by Calder Venn on Edmund Shaw's orders before Venn's fatal, unauthorised second entry into the site. Its actual claim is deliberately limited: it does not prove any ancient Crown monarchy or supreme government ever existed. It proves the opposite — that old Alderwick's civic tradition knew how to grant extraordinary emergency authority while building in its own expiry, stating plainly that no emergency power survives the emergency that created it.

Historical weight: This is exactly what makes the Charter politically dangerous rather than simply old — First Crown's schismatic argument for permanent, centralised authority collapses if the ancient precedent it half-invokes was actually a temporary, self-limiting office all along. Alric Dane's network specifically tries to contain its public authentication before Edrin instead brings it to Cale and Maris Kersey, and it's eventually transferred openly into Crown and civic custody, witnessed and copied rather than hoarded by any single faction.

Full geography on the Atlas; campaign account at Campaign B18; the First Authority's fuller civic history in the Magic Codex and Lore.

The Seven-Link Oath Chain Confirmed canon

Seven links, seven traditions, one refusal.

Classification
Late Oath Wars / Seven Refusal — iron chain, no established magic.
Timeline & era
Timeline B; forged during the Oath Wars, tied to the Seven Refusal, c. -919 ARV. Recovered Campaign B17.
Bearer
Legitimately acquired by Silver Bastion after its provenance was traced and its archaeological context established.
Associated figures
Traced through the Highever antiquities market — Sabren Orrick, two treasure-hunters (Pellan and Vey), and the Harrow Collection under Maelin Harrow — before Silver Bastion's legitimate purchase.

What it is: An iron chain of seven links, each representing one of the seven oath traditions — Redemption, Crown, Hearth, Devotion, Sanctuary, Liberty, and Fellowship — that jointly refused a "righteous slaughter" ordered in Velis's own name at the Seven Refusal. Found in the Seven Refusal crypt beneath the Western Oath-Fort alongside Ser Auren Valeth's body, and identified in full in Campaign B17.

Provenance: Rather than loot the crypt, the B17 expedition traced the chain's paper trail instead: recovered by two treasure-hunters, passed through solicitors into the Harrow Collection's legitimate private acquisition channels, and finally purchased by Silver Bastion once Maelin Harrow understood the find's archaeological context and withdrew the Collection's own interest. No magic has been established in the chain itself.

Historical significance: Extremely high — it's the single physical object tying all seven Seven Refusal traditions to one documented moment, and, alongside Ser Auren Valeth's glass-damaged armour, one of the clearest surviving links between the Oath Wars and the War of Ash and Glass. See Factions for the individual traditions and Lore for the Seven Refusal in full.

The Storm Great Seal Confirmed canon

Magic given a stable ring, not a machine — and not a portable object.

Classification
Relic-site — one of the eight Great Seals, not a physical artefact.
Timeline & era
Timeline B; set before the Shattering, c. -914 to c. -913 ARV. Located directly Campaign B19, mid-343 ARV.
Location
East of ruined Alderwick, deep in the Badlands — superseding the earlier Outpost Nine map-keyed placement.
Associated figure
Benjamin Strake, who makes first direct contact and helps guide its major B19 pressure release.

What it is: A vast, continuously reforming vertical ring of lightning, rain, wind, and pressure — magic given stable form, with no stone frame, metal casing, or mechanical apparatus holding it together. Not a portable object of any kind, and explicitly not the same thing as the removable focus recovered from the Western Pressure Station (see below).

Historical weight: Storm did not cause the Badlands — the Shattering created the region's self-sustaining magical weather, and Storm has spent more than twelve centuries containing that wound rather than causing it. B19's major pressure release substantially calms the Badlands' extreme storm regime for the first time in that span, without healing the Shattering's physical damage. See the Magic Codex for the full mechanical account and Lore for the narrative history.

Full geography on the Atlas; campaign account at Campaign B19.

The Sixteen Focus & Field Measure Confirmed canon

Storm-management infrastructure — a regulator, not a Seal.

Classification
Relic-artefact set — ancient storm-management regulator and calibration instruments.
Timeline & era
Timeline B; ancient, predating precise dating. Recovered Campaign B19, mid-343 ARV.
Bearer
Held in Greymount under Farbound observation.
Associated figures
Benjamin Strake and the Farbound field team, who stabilise and recover it after Grey House personnel remove it incorrectly.

What it is: A removable, roughly eight-inch dark-metal and cloudy-crystal focus from the Western Pressure Station beneath Marker Sixteen, recovered alongside its calibrated field measure, a closed-crystal calibration set, maintenance rods, concentric discs, and wire. Recovered text states plainly that these foci "do not hold or make the storm; they give excess pressure a road to ground" — ancient infrastructure for managing local storm pressure, not the Storm Great Seal itself and not a portable version of it.

Historical weight: Proves ancient peoples actively managed dangerous storm pressure long before the modern Age of Rediscovery, and points the B19 expedition east toward Storm's actual location. Should not be illustrated or treated as a Great Seal relic, a key, or a source of independent power. See the Magic Codex for the full distinction between the Pressure Station, the focus, and the Storm Great Seal.

Full geography on the Atlas; campaign account at Campaign B19.

The Eastern Ward Survey Case Confirmed canon

A sealed civic record that outlived the city it described.

Classification
Relic-document — sealed civic survey case.
Timeline & era
Timeline B; pre-Shattering Alderwick. Recovered Campaign B19, mid-343 ARV, roughly 1,281 years after B18's living city.
Bearer
Returned to Greymount for archival study by the Farbound Guild.
Associated figure
Veya, who uncovers it near ruined Alderwick's eastern approach.

What it is: A sealed, weatherproof bronze case stamped ALDERWICK — EASTERN WARD SURVEY, preserving engraved bronze leaves with a partial street plan and severe-sky-event shelter instructions. Its surviving instruction directs citizens in eastern districts who cannot reach western refuge to proceed to the Hall of the Open Sky and keep the Processional Way clear.

Historical weight: Confirms the Hall of the Open Sky functioned as a genuine public civic refuge for ordinary Alderwick citizens before it became important to later Great Seal history — not a hidden cult chamber built only for Storm. One of the few B19 finds that directly bridges B18's living city and the modern ruin layer.

Full geography on the Atlas; campaign account at Campaign B19.

Living & Metaphysical Relics

"Relic" doesn't always mean an object — some of Amora's most consequential inheritances are living principles, bloodlines, or bonds.

The Verdict Pulse Confirmed canon — source-classified Living Relic

A living compass carried in the blood.

Timeline & era
Timeline A, latent since the Age of Founding, active by Campaign XI.
Bearer
Riven Mareth's bloodline.

Origin: A latent manifestation of the Heart Medallion's own rhythm, carried within Riven Mareth's bloodline.

Effect: Reacts to distortions in truth or coercion of will — acts as a living compass to the Breathing Law itself.

The source material explicitly classifies this as a Living Relic rather than an artefact — there's nothing to hold, only something to carry. See Campaign XI.

Clause XI — "The Breathing Law" Confirmed canon

An active ethical and metaphysical principle, not a sword or amulet.

Timeline & era
Timeline A, forged Campaign XI, c. 6550 ARV.
Creator/origin
Forged at Bastion Saren by Riven Mareth and Ilyen Vael.

Nature: A living ethical constant balancing memory and change — the metaphysical heir to both the Bond of Chains and the Codex of Unification.

Function: Allows reality to adapt without recursive prophecy; prevents thought from calcifying into law.

Current status: Active. Stabilises global ley-flow; worshipped indirectly through the Lyricum's post-Republic doctrines.

See Campaign XI and the Factions page.

The Bond of Chains Confirmed canon

Not a physical relic — a pattern that keeps being reforged.

Timeline & era
Timeline A, Age of Founding onward.
Creator/origin
The psychic-magical vow linking Zethyr and Taren, strengthened by the Air and Heart Seals.

Related history: Bond of Chains → transmitted through each recurrence as the Echo Chain (Campaign III) → later the Clause Law of the Choir cycles → broken in metaphysical form by Clause XI.

Current status: Formally broken, but emotional residue persists within recurring heart-veil dyads across both timelines — see the Magic Codex's Ley-Heirs section for the full mechanical throughline this pattern seeds.

Disputed Relics

Marked openly as unconfirmed rather than promoted to fact — the same transparency standard the rest of the site holds to.

A note on excluded items

Three objects from an older world compendium don't appear here. A "Heart Buckle" and a "Black Medallion," sometimes mentioned alongside the Heart Medallion, appear nowhere in this site's source material after a full search — named here only to record that they were checked and not found, in case firmer sourcing surfaces later. A proposed "Thirteenth Seal" fares worse still: the same older compendium reports it and a "half-melted remnant" recovered after the Null Relic's destruction, but neither the Seal nor the remnant appears anywhere in the detailed Campaign XI record itself — the reported evidence is exactly as uncorroborated as the Seal it's meant to support. See the Canon Ledger for the full ruling. This page also deliberately excludes ordinary weapons and gear, one-scene magical props, and personally meaningful items that haven't become world-historical — Caelen Durrel's inscribed Vengeance armour, for instance, stays on his own character and B13 pages rather than here.
