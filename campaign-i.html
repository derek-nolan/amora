Timeline A · Campaign I

The Heartwardens

Age of Founding / Age of Light

First seven months of the Year of the First Seal Primary theatres: Silver Bastion, Highever; Evermere; Menge

Opening World State

Campaign I begins in a Vanmere that is politically established but still poorly understood magically. Highever is prosperous, diplomatic, and largely peaceful, governed through elected leadership under Prime Minister Killian Mare. Its colleges draw promising warriors, priests, mages, and specialists from across the region, and Silver Bastion stands at the centre of its paladin tradition.

Beyond Highever, the continent is much less secure. Menge is a difficult northern kingdom ruled by Queen Maeve and her ailing husband Roderick, with no heir and growing rumours of a dangerous Icebinder movement. Brevair, ruled by Emperor Trainor Fell, is isolationist, expansionist, and actively interested in destabilising neighbouring powers; Highever has become the principal diplomatic obstacle to that ambition. The Badlands already exist as a feared magical wasteland whose true origin is unknown to the people of this era.

Silver Bastion also sits over something far older than its current institutions understand. The permanent Heart watch predates this campaign, but the later order had lapsed and its deeper purpose was only partly remembered. Zethyr and Taren therefore revive the Heartwardens rather than create the tradition from nothing. They believe they are recovering or establishing the structure by which the world's great magical forces can be kept in balance. Later history proves that both the Great Seals and older Warden traditions predate them by centuries. Campaign I is therefore best read as an age of rediscovery, even though its own characters often believed they were standing at a beginning.

Party & Key Figures

  • Zethyr Durrel PC
    Paladin whose instinct is toward compassion, restraint, and protection. Becomes High Commander of the revived Heartwardens. His surviving creed is "Through Heart, Balance." The Seal investigations, the psychic bond, and the campaign's final moral fracture are experienced primarily through him.
  • Taren Durrel
    Gifted Heartwarden captain and Paladin of the Crown, later of the Veil. His competence, certainty, and attraction to command initially make him an ideal partner to Zethyr. His relationship with Zethyr is genuine, romantic, and ultimately marital, while already containing the imbalance Campaign II will exploit.
  • Seraphine Vale
    Grand Marshal of the Paladin Council and senior authority at Silver Bastion. Represents the institutional Heartwarden tradition before Taren reshapes it.
  • Arienna Lume
    Magister of the Collegium Arcanum and descendant of the Flame Binder/Warden tradition. Joins the Seal investigations and eventually the Heartwardens, linking the order's religious and martial work with serious magical scholarship.
  • Aldric Fen
    Heartwarden chaplain, providing the spiritual and pastoral voice of the revived order.
  • Lyra & Thane
    Young initiates of Silver Bastion whose later careers under the Veil make their presence here an important piece of tragic continuity.
  • Orell
    Antagonist whose plot dominates the campaign's final months. His exact office, motives, allies, and detailed scheme are no longer recoverable from the surviving record and are left unresolved rather than reconstructed.

Act-by-Act

Months One & Two — Beneath Silver Bastion

The campaign opens with Zethyr and Taren serving at Silver Bastion as the Heart tradition is being restored to active purpose. Their first great discovery lies beneath the Bastion itself: the Heart Seal. Investigation rather than war defines these first months. The pair descend into old spaces beneath the fortress, encounter structures their own order only partially understands, and establish that the Heart is not simply a relic but part of a wider system of forces requiring active balance.

Contact with the Heart produces the first form of the bond between Zethyr and Taren. What begins as heightened awareness of one another becomes a psychic connection capable of carrying emotion, intention, and eventually will. At this stage it is experienced as extraordinary closeness rather than control.

Month Three — The First Balancing

The first major balancing ritual succeeds. The bond between Zethyr and Taren becomes perceptible beyond private sensation, and the Heart Medallion reawakens.

The triumph also contains the campaign's most important hidden danger. Beneath Silver Bastion they encounter the first shadow later remembered as the Dark Filament, and some part of it enters the Heart Medallion. Neither man yet understands what has happened. The accompanying Vision of Balance presents Light and Shadow as interdependent rather than simply opposed. Nothing about that revelation requires Dominion. Its tragedy lies in how differently it can be interpreted: Zethyr sees balance as restraint; Taren will increasingly see balance as something that can be imposed.

Month Four — The Wider Seal Hunt

With the Heart temporarily stable, the campaign opens outward. Three additional powers are investigated and stabilised under the names preserved by the period record: Aether, Flame, and Stone. Arienna's Flame heritage becomes directly relevant, and the investigations reveal that individual magical traditions already exist around different forces rather than every Seal belonging to one universal Heartwarden order.

The northern crisis leads into Menge and the tradition later understood as the Icewardens. Campaign I's records remember the hostile branch as Icebinders, associated with attacks and instability in the north. Later B16–B17 archaeology clarifies that the Icebinders were the fallen form of an older Icewarden lineage rather than the original neutral custodians of Frost. This wider Seal hunt is one of the campaign's central pieces of exploration: the party is discovering that Vanmere's magical landscape has its own histories, custodians, and failures.

Month Five — Evermere

After months spent confronting ancient powers, Evermere becomes a rare interval where the story focuses on the two men rather than the Seals. Zethyr and Taren marry.

Their relationship is neither secret nor treated as a moral problem. Their love is genuine, and the campaign never needs to reinterpret that love itself as the cause of what follows. The danger lies in the magical structures accumulating around it. The bond strengthens, with the surviving record attributing part of the connection to the power then called the Air/Aether Seal, later reinforced through Heart. What began as emotional connection is becoming capable of transmitting command and surrender as readily as affection. Later historians call this the period in which the first Chains of the Veil began forming unconsciously. Zethyr and Taren themselves would not yet have understood it that way.

Months Six & Seven — Orell

The final movement turns away from archaeological discovery and toward human threat. Orell's plot is uncovered. Its detailed machinery has been lost, but its effect has not: ending the plot requires Zethyr and Taren to take a life for the first time. The surviving record does not establish whether Orell himself is the person killed, and this page does not invent that answer.

The act breaks something in the moral certainty with which they began. Until this point the pair have repeatedly been rewarded for acting together: the Heart responds, Seals stabilise, crises end, their order grows in prestige, and their own bond deepens. Orell's defeat introduces a more dangerous lesson — that violence carried out through the same unity can also appear necessary, effective, and righteous.

Taren leaves the campaign increasingly willing to ask what else decisive power might solve. Zethyr leaves it still devoted to the man he loves, but with the first evidence that sharing another person's will can carry him somewhere he would not have gone alone. The Dominion does not exist yet. But its emotional architecture does.

World State After Campaign I

Geography. Silver Bastion stands as the operational centre of the revived Heartwardens in Highever. Evermere is established as the site of Zethyr and Taren's marriage. Menge remains a northern frontier destabilised by the Icebinder crisis. Brevair remains an expansionist political threat under Emperor Trainor Fell rather than a settled ally.

Magic. The campaign generation restores practical access to Heart and stabilises several other pressures under the terminology available to it. The Heart Medallion is active, with the Dark Filament hidden inside it. Flame and Frost-associated traditions establish that the Heartwardens are part of a much larger magical history rather than the origin of all Seal keeping.

The Durrel Line. Zethyr and Taren are married and psychically bound. In the surviving Timeline A record this relationship becomes the founding pattern from which later Durrel recurrences are understood, even though B17 later proves that Heartbinding and the Great Seals themselves are much older than either man.

Institutions. The Heartwardens have been revived as a paladin and custodial order. Orell's plot has been definitively ended, though its surviving details are fragmentary. The Dominion is not yet born, but the bond, the Dark Filament, and Taren's growing belief in decisive order establish the conditions from which Campaign II emerges.

Canon status: reconstructed from surviving campaign-era summaries, the original setting record, and later continuity material. The seven-month sequence, the Heart investigation, the first balancing ritual, Aether/Flame/Stone work, the Evermere marriage, Arienna, Seraphine, Aldric, Lyra and Thane, Orell's final plot, and the first taking of life are all preserved in the surviving early record. Orell's exact office, motive, network, and whether he personally died in the final confrontation remain unresolved. The site's later 0–27 ARV dating is an archival normalisation used by later summaries; the campaign-era chronology describes the played story itself as seven months. Later understanding: Campaigns B16–B17 recover a current registry of Heart, Flame, Stone, Storm, Life, Shadow, Light, and Frost and establish that all eight Great Seals were permanently set centuries before Campaign I. Campaign I's Aether/Air and other legacy names therefore remain valid historical terminology for what this generation believed or recorded, not proof of additional Great Seals outside the later eight. The Icebinders are likewise now understood as the fallen form of the Icewarden lineage rather than the original neutral Frost custodians.
