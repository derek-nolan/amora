Core Premise

Those Left Beyond is the second directly played Oath Wars prequel, returning to the deep-history era roughly ten years after The Crown Within and bringing Edmund Shaw back into direct play as more than an off-stage name. Twenty-two-year-old Alderwick runner Alec Verran works for the Circle — an opaque but practical organisation interested in routes, weather, ruins, trade, and celestial observation — with no manifested class magic of his own, until a Greymount assignment exposes an ancient transit apparatus built from eight positional components and an Observation Anchor. He willingly accepts a pact with the cold, intimate presence known as the Whispering Frost, while his lifelong best friend Finn Carrow spontaneously manifests spatial Sorcery in the same crisis and begins training with House Saye. The investigation follows a trail of falsified crossings, retained travellers, and a formally severed fourth relation to ancient Belisar on Nareth Isle, where the campaign resolves the two-century-old Receiving House IV disaster, gives former route worker Cairn Morren's grief a true ending, and exposes Shaw's hidden programme of testing forced Way crossings on prisoners, deserters, and the expendable in the name of "protecting" a fracturing country. Alec ends the campaign as a formal Circle member, Finn's boyfriend, and the person who chose to execute a defeated Shaw rather than risk him trying again.

Where this sits relative to B18, and to B19-B20. B21 is played after B20 but is chronologically a second deep-history prequel, roughly 1,271 years before B19-B20's mid-343 ARV present and about ten years after B18's own c. -938 ARV setting. Several later landmarks remain future history that B21's own characters cannot know: the Seven Refusal is roughly nine years ahead (c. -919 ARV); the Great Seal setting cycle is roughly fourteen to fifteen years ahead (c. -914 to -913 ARV); the Shattering is roughly twenty-three years ahead (c. -905 ARV); and Rimeholt does not yet exist as the later Western Icewarden settlement. The Oath Wars remain active and fragmented, and the separate conflict later named the War of Ash and Glass is already underway, though contemporary characters have no reason to use that later historian's label. B21 does not alter Benjamin Strake's modern story in any way — it adds deep-history canon, not a new chapter of the Age of Rediscovery.

Party & Key Figures

  • Alec Verran PC
    Human, Level 1 → 3, non-practitioner Circle runner turned Warlock. Twenty-two, working-class, charismatic, and already deeply embedded in the Circle's everyday work before he has any formal magical office. Drawn to danger, forbidden knowledge, and the feeling of being deliberately chosen — the campaign makes him more honest about that appetite rather than turning it into secret villainy.
  • Finn Carrow
    Twenty-two, Alec's best friend since roughly age two and eventual boyfriend. A Carrow Freight worker who manifests spontaneous spatial/force Sorcery during the Greymount crisis and begins training with House Saye without joining it. Their romance is genuine and rooted in a lifelong bond, not a Saye operation.
  • The Whispering Frost
    Colloquial name for Alec's patron — ancient, cold, intimate, and explicitly older than the known Way network. Its true nature remains unresolved; it is not identified as the Frost Great Seal, an Icewarden power, or any other known entity. Once loved former pact-holder Cairn Morren.
  • Mara Vey
    Forty-seven, Human Warlock and Alderwick Circle leader. Practical and morally grey — she knowingly treats Alec as a non-consenting experimental variable early on, later admits that probable agreement isn't consent, and writes an explicit informed-consent protection into his formal Circle membership terms.
  • Silas Kern
    Twenty-six, an experienced Warlock with a separate amber-working patron who becomes Alec's protective older-brother figure — feeding him, challenging his recklessness, and protecting his agency rather than competing for his affection.
  • Rhydian Saye
    Twenty-five, disciplined House Saye Sorcerer and dangerous but respected ally. Alec explicitly rejects the idea that wartime necessity gives Rhydian permission to use him, and Rhydian accepts the distinction without becoming harmless.
  • Cairn Morren
    Historical Belisari route worker and former Whisper pact-holder, living nearly two centuries before B21. Survived the original Nareth disaster, refused to abandon the missing, later severed his own pact to protect others, and died after a normal mortal life.
  • Mera Vann
    Traveller lost on an Evermere-to-Belisar crossing roughly two centuries before B21. Cairn identifies her retained route-state during the climax, and the party recovers her alive into c. -928 ARV.
  • Edmund Shaw Antagonist
    Founder and principal architect of First Crown, and the same figure whose hidden influence reached Crown institutions in B18. By B21 he is financing forced Way crossings on prisoners, deserters, and the expendable to identify bloodlines statistically likelier to survive displacement — protection and control fused into one doctrine.

Act-by-Act

Act I — The Runner Before the Pact

B21 opens in Alderwick with Alec already useful to the Circle but not formally initiated — Mara Vey trusts him with movement, messages, and practical work precisely because he's socially capable and non-magical, and the Circle itself is established as broader than an occult cabal, its lunar observation sitting beside trade routes, freight, weather, ruins, and ordinary civic information. Finn is already woven into Alec's life through Carrow Freight and a friendship that began in toddlerhood; each has privately wanted more since roughly age fifteen or sixteen, but neither has crossed that line.

Act II — Greymount and the Eighth Position

Circle work draws Alec into investigating an ancient Greymount apparatus that uses eight positional components and an Observation Anchor to make prepared places "agree" through geographic and celestial relation. Alec eventually retains the eighth component, which becomes the physical object multiple factions begin planning around. Mara also crosses a line here — she knowingly treats Alec as a non-consenting experimental variable, and when challenged later admits that expecting his agreement was not the same as obtaining permission.

Act III — The Whispering Frost

During the Greymount crisis, Alec encounters the cold presence known as the Whispering Frost and chooses a pact. The campaign never identifies the patron as Frost itself, a Great Seal, or any other known entity — the Whisper explicitly remembers the Way's builders as mortals and describes itself as older than their road. Alec's magic takes the form of black-blue force edged with frost, Armor of Agathys, Hex, and a Book of Shadows marked OPENED IN COLD; the relationship stays intimate and occasionally dominant, but consent remains a hard boundary — the Whisper can perceive want without that want becoming blanket permission.

Act IV — Finn Carrow Awakens

Finn manifests spontaneous Sorcery in the same emergency — magic that pushes, braces, and senses spatial misplacement rather than expressing through a conventional element — and helps stabilise a dangerous structural failure through practical action. House Saye notices, and Finn accepts training from Rhydian Saye without committing to membership; B21 deliberately stops short of making Saye recruitment his destiny.

Act V — The False Sky

A black calibration sphere forces the Greymount apparatus to accept a deliberately falsified celestial condition. Corren Bale crosses during the experiment, experiences hours while barely a minute passes outside, and returns beneath the wrong stars with only one visible moon and a hostile attachment in tow — establishing the campaign's central technical danger: a damaged route can select a place-state, temporal state, or possible version that satisfies enough of the relation to be accepted, and seven positions do not safely close the system.

Act VI — The True Conjunction

At the real conjunction, Greymount, Highever, Evermere, and the severed fourth relation become dangerously difficult for the old calculation to distinguish, briefly exposing Alec and the others to an impossible view of Amora from above and a distant, densely lit future. B21 treats these as evidence of route-state instability rather than a controllable window through history — the party cannot simply choose a year to visit.

Act VII — Sella Returns

Sella Morren reappears after vanishing from the Evermere River Office thirty-six years earlier, physically unchanged and certain only about two hours have passed. Derran Voel is separately displaced and returns with incompatible memories, including an older version of Sella twenty-nine years beyond the present — evidence that the damaged Ways can hold mutually inconsistent route-states, which B21 deliberately doesn't resolve into one clean theory.

Act VIII — A New Level and an Old Love

The Greymount crisis brings the first B21 milestone: Alec reaches Warlock 2 with Pact of the Tome, Agonizing Blast, and Fiendish Vigor, and Finn reaches Sorcerer 2. Alec and Finn finally stop pretending their six years of mutual attraction don't exist, sharing a first official date on a moonlit hill and agreeing that neither the Circle nor House Saye should become a hidden institutional secret capable of changing the other's choices without warning — a sincere, intentionally naive promise the campaign doesn't mock.

Act IX — The Evermere Paper Trail

The post-conjunction investigation moves through Evermere, where document broker Tomas Rell and salvage worker Vessa Marr initially look like possible conspiracy centres but resolve into morally compromised, limited links — paid, not directing. The real organiser is watching the information network from a distance, confirmed to know that Alec kept the eighth component and left Evermere with the Circle group.

Act X — The Bridge

The wider wars stay visible as the party travels — at a blocked bridge, Crown and Sanctuary personnel protect refugees and a deserter who fled after men were executed for refusing to burn a village storehouse. Alec chooses not to intervene because reaching Nareth is urgent, hating the choice; the campaign never reveals that things worked out to excuse him, establishing that moral compromise can be a refusal to act as readily as an overtly dark decision.

Act XI — West to Nareth

The expedition sails toward Nareth Isle, a large inhabited island roughly two to three good sailing days from the western coast, with Belisar on its sheltered southeastern side and harsher, rocky uplands to the west. As the island approaches, the Whisper reacts with unexpected grief, recognising Nareth through a former pact-holder rather than abstract Way knowledge — fragments of black stone, salt, refusal, and a name: Cairn Morren.

Act XII — Belisar

Belisar is established as an old maritime city already ancient by c. -928 ARV, civic and mercantile in identity rather than secretly built around the Way station, with Receiving House IV lying far away in western Nareth. Master Sounder Letha Marr grants supervised access to restricted records and assigns surveyor Nera Voss to the western expedition — Belisar has its own law and memory, and mainland Houses and oath disputes don't automatically override it.

Act XIII — Cairn Morren

Belisar's records reconstruct the original Receiving House IV disaster nearly two centuries earlier — travellers returning years out of sequence, a major retrieval attempt that sent twelve after two missing people and returned six of uncertain identity, and Cairn Morren among the survivors insisting the missing were still alive beyond the road. Belisar quarantined the site; Cairn resigned Hall service and spent years reconstructing who was lost. The Whisper finally recognises him as a former pact-holder it loved, its memory of him incomplete only because it has survived far longer than mortal memory holds together.

Act XIV — The Road That Was Cut

The records distinguish Belisar's old civic quarantine from a later, formal severance. Forty-four years before B21, Elias Vey and other former custodians tried to restore Nareth to the wider Way calculation, causing simultaneous failures at Highever and Evermere; surviving custodians then withdrew the Nareth baseline and formally cut the fourth relation. Mara Vey inherits a genuine family connection to that history through Elias without inheriting automatic guilt. Eight years after the cut, Sella vanished when Evermere equipment began seeking the lost relation again — and a separate House Saye expedition disappeared in western Nareth only four years before B21, its fate still unresolved.

Act XV — Edmund Shaw

Fresh tracks, a dead broker, and written orders signed E. Shaw at Receiving House IV finally expose the organiser — the same First Crown figure whose hidden influence reached Adam Kersey in B18, now insulated behind a decade of intermediaries. His immediate target is Cairn Morren's master record of the lost: names, routes, dates, and circumstances Shaw wants not primarily to rescue anyone, but to identify descendant lines statistically more likely to survive unstable Way displacement.

Act XVI — Protection Through Control

Shaw has already moved from volunteers to prisoners, deserters, and the condemned. His argument isn't empty — a restored Way network really could move grain, refugees, and messages around closed roads and checkpoints — but he accepts every authoritarian implication of the same tool, arguing that central control is preferable to fragmentation even when it means treating unwanted people as expendable. He produces a genuine Cairn document as consent; the handwriting is real, but Cairn's actual consent was only ever for controlled rescue work, never for lineage profiling or forced experimentation.

Act XVII — Receiving House IV

Alec, Finn, Silas, and Nera infiltrate the inner station while Rhydian and Orsen secure the outer approach. Alec opens the ambush with a devastating Eldritch Blast; the battle deliberately stops becoming a damage race once Shaw is critically wounded, and Alec has already told him to talk before facing judgement. Under Rhydian's sword, Shaw explains the financing chain, the forty-four-year severance, and the strategic endgame before Alec decides whether he lives.

Act XVIII — Mera Vann

The party realises Cairn doesn't need to be extracted — Shaw's portable frame can temporarily complete a receiving relation with Alec's eighth component while Cairn, still in his own time, uses his own list to choose one of the people he was trying to rescue. He selects Mera Vann, lost on an Evermere-to-Belisar transfer three years before the old closure; Alec uses the Whisper's recognition, Cairn's selection, and Finn's spatial instinct to distinguish her actual lost crossing from competing retained route-states, and Mera falls through alive into c. -928 ARV — roughly two centuries after her interrupted journey, by Belisar's reckoning.

Act XIX — The Death of Edmund Shaw

With Mera safe and the route closed, Shaw is defeated, critically wounded, and capable of being taken alive. Alec has heard the full programme explained and decides Shaw will never stop looking for another apparatus, another route, another population he can justify using. Frost runs down Alec's arm and Eldritch Blast kills Shaw at close range — the campaign records this plainly as an intentional execution, not self-defence and not an accident. Rhydian immediately recognises the historical uncertainty this creates: First Crown's earliest reports already show fracture.

Act XX — Closed, Not Destroyed

The party doesn't erase Receiving House IV — Belisar closes the physical site again, but the ancient station survives, while Shaw's portable forced-selection frame and black calibration sphere are dismantled so his exact programme can't simply resume. Alec removes the eighth component and keeps it; Shaw's copied operational material is divided among Belisar, the Circle, and House Saye so no single institution leaves with the complete packet.

Act XXI — Cairn Lived

Mera returns to Belisar and learns what became of Cairn — that he survived, came home, resigned Hall service, spent years trying to account for the missing, and eventually died long before her recovery. The Whisper receives an equally important correction: Cairn didn't vanish helplessly into the dark, but deliberately ended their pact to protect others, then lived an ordinary mortal life. What the Whisper remembered as an unresolved disappearance was a protective act followed by a real ending.

Act XXII — The Circle Asks Properly

Back in Alderwick, Mara reviews the Nareth operation and makes Alec state plainly that Shaw could have been captured — she names the act an execution without immediately telling Alec whether she thinks it was right. She then offers him formal Circle membership, this time with written terms first: confidentiality, a right to refuse field assignments, no Circle ownership over his pact or the eighth component, and a new informed-consent clause born directly from her earlier mistake. Alec reads the terms and says yes almost immediately.

Act XXIII — Home, Finally Said Aloud

Alec and Finn return to Carrow Freight and tell Finn's mother they're together; Mrs. Carrow reveals their attraction had been obvious to her since their teens. The romance closes not with House Saye, the Circle, or the Whisper, but with the family kitchen — the two of them having spent nearly their whole lives choosing each other in ordinary ways before finally calling it what it was. News of First Crown's fracture keeps moving through Alderwick, its outcome still unknowable.

World State After Campaign B21

Alec Verran. Warlock 3, formal Circle member under written consent protections, keeper of the eighth Way component, and the person who deliberately executed Edmund Shaw after his defeat and full confession. His pact, Book of Shadows, and the eighth component remain his own — the Circle does not own any of them.

Finn Carrow. Sorcerer 3, openly Alec's boyfriend, continuing House Saye training without having joined the House. His magic remains spatial/force Sorcery in narrative identity regardless of any mechanical chassis used to run it.

The Whispering Frost / Cairn Morren. The Whisper's true nature remains unresolved — it is not the Frost Great Seal, an Icewarden power, a proto-Seal, or Jarek Flint's black-gold entity, and "Great Old One" is Alec's Level 3 mechanical chassis only, not a canonical identification. Cairn Morren's own history is now closed: he survived the original Nareth disaster, deliberately severed his pact to protect others, and died after an ordinary mortal life.

Edmund Shaw / First Crown. Shaw is dead, executed by Alec after his forced-Way lineage-survival programme was exposed and defeated. First Crown survives him but fractures into competing succession responses — councilist, anti-personalist, and harder Shaw-doctrine factions among them — with the eventual outcome deliberately left open for later Oath Wars history.

Nareth / Belisar / Receiving House IV. Nareth Isle and Belisar are established as real, inhabited, civically independent geography rather than a bare map label. Receiving House IV is closed again by Belisar but not destroyed — the ancient Way infrastructure survives in fragmented form, while Shaw's portable forced-selection apparatus and black calibration sphere are removed from play.

Mera Vann. Recovered alive into c. -928 ARV from a crossing roughly two centuries earlier — proof that at least one traveller left beyond could be recovered, not a guarantee that every missing person on Cairn's list remains reachable.

The ancient Ways. Confirmed as mortal-built transit infrastructure using eight positional components and celestial/geographic observation, and explicitly separate from the Great Seals and from the B20 Inner Way. The much later Rimeholt transit mechanism belongs to the same broad ancient tradition, but B21's characters do not found the later Western Icewarden Way-hand office.

Open threads carried forward. What First Crown becomes after Shaw; the identity of the unnamed woman who questioned Sella before her disappearance; the fate of House Saye's missing third Nareth expedition; the long-term custody of Alec's eighth component; whether other names on Cairn's list remain recoverable; and the true nature and long-term aims of the Whispering Frost.

Canon status: follows the source account closely. B21 is a deep-history prequel and does not alter the modern mid-343 ARV state established by B19-B20 — see the Canon Ledger for the hard separation between the ancient Ways, the Great Seals, and B20's Inner Way, and the Magic Codex for the Way's eight-position mechanics and the Whispering Frost's unresolved identity, and the Atlas for Nareth Isle, Belisar, and Receiving House IV.
