Timeline A · Campaign XXII

The Shattered Quiet

4CE begins — 1,000-year jump

9650–9660 ARV Primary location: Caelwick Frontier Post, East Vanmere

Opening World State

A year after Talen Marric's Severance unspooled the Heart-Veil, ending the ley-heir cycle and returning destiny to mortals, the world hasn't fully stabilised. The Null Compact — Consent, Reversibility, Witness — spreads unevenly across the frontier republics; old Dominion corridors lie as dormant ruins that most people now walk past without a second thought. The great powers no longer listen for hums beneath the stone. The map is reverting to a quieter, medieval world defined by roads, rivers, toll-gates, and appetite, not ley-lines. And yet the Severance was a ritual, not an erasure — sites it never fully reached still hold sub-surface charge. Historians will later call these Quiet Faults. Caelwick, a muddy little charter town nobody outside East Vanmere could place on a map, sits on top of one without knowing it.

Party & Key Figures

  • Arden Vale PC Co-discovered the Shard Chamber; forms half of the unintended shard-pattern imprint
    Quick-tongued, clever wanderer with a survivor's instinct; accidentally aligned with the traits Taren's ancient pattern once sought — loyalty, desire, responsiveness, and a dangerous willingness to follow someone stronger.
  • Caelen Marrith Paladin, Captain of the Charter Guard
    Charismatic and composed, with an instinct for leadership — and something darker: a quiet hunger for order, for shaping a world drifting after the Severance. Treats the frontier as a place to rebuild structure, not merely keep peace.
  • Lysa Thorne
    Charter Warden Scout; practical, skeptical, the first to note the strange behaviour of Caelen around the shard chamber.
  • Kett Ironrun
    Dwarf merchant-archivist; keeper of odd relics salvaged from collapsed Dominion sites, skeptical but curious. Serves as guide to the ruins.

Act-by-Act

Act I — The Frontier Inquiry

A routine patrol report — subsidence near the old grain cellars — turns into something stranger once Caelwick's Charter Guard actually digs. Beneath the muddy streets, Arden, Caelen, Lysa, and Kett find the roof of a scriptorium vault that no civic record so much as mentions: a collapsed Dominion-era archive, its entrance long since built over by a trade-republic outpost that had no idea what it was standing on. Roads muddy, guild charters pinned with sunset dates that no one has bothered renewing in years — Caelwick is the kind of place where paperwork lapses quietly and nobody minds. That lapse turns out to matter later. For now, the four of them stand over a hole in the ground looking at something that should be impossible a year after the Severance: light, faint and crystalline, still moving down there.

Act II — The Shard Chamber

Kett is the one who identifies the walls — Dominion administrative sigils, half melted, the kind that once meant a scriptorium answered directly to central command. At the chamber's heart sits the Last Shard Node, a crystalline matrix designed to mirror a wielder's will into structured command — one of the few instances of that Dominion technology that the Severance simply never reached. It should be inert. It isn't. When the group studies it as a group, nothing happens. The moment Caelen steps close enough to touch the housing, the node answers with a low pulse, keyed to nothing any of them can name. Arden notices first, in the offhand way you notice a friend getting good at something new — how quickly, how naturally, Caelen starts talking about the relic like he already understands its grammar.

Act III — The Pattern Wakes

Deeper levels of the vault hold what Kett's notes later call Scribe-Lattice Script — a partially corrupted Dominion scripting language that doesn't just record, it tries to complete. Fed by residual Pale Concord Residue baked into the scriptorium's walls, a harmless-seeming leftover harmonic dust from the Concord ages, the script begins reconstructing behavioural logic around the group as they explore — quietly, incrementally, drawing its shape from whichever will in the room is strongest. That will is Caelen's. Not because he's an echo of anyone. Not because destiny selected him. Because the world, as the Nulls warned back at the close of Campaign XXI, mirrors intent — and Caelen wants order badly enough that the ruin has something to answer.

Act IV — The Charter Gap

It's Lysa who spots the real problem, and it isn't magical: Caelwick's patrol charter lapsed months ago and nobody renewed it. That Null Charter Fracture means Caelen's patrol is operating with no legal oversight whatsoever — no one back at the republic capital even knows what they've found, let alone that they're still down there. Caelen doesn't raise the alarm. He starts making decisions — what to log, what to leave out, which passages to reseal and which to keep open — that quietly diverge from anything resembling standard civic duty. Nothing dramatic. Just a captain deciding, privately, that this discovery is his to shape.

Act V — The Withdraw & Debrief

The team surfaces for the first time in two days, filthy and unsettled in a way none of them can quite articulate. Caelen is calm, almost serene, turning the day's finds over in his hands like a man solving a puzzle he already half-expected to solve. He's the only one the relic ever answered. He knows it. Arden, watching him across the debrief fire, notices the shift without naming it yet: Caelen has gotten more protective of the group's plans, more decisive about what happens next, more certain of things he had no particular reason to be certain about a week ago.

Act VI — The Pumps & Second Descent

A second trip below, meant to be a routine survey of structural stability, finds the Under-City Pumps — ancient Veil-era water engines, retrofitted centuries ago with Dominion stabilizers, dormant and harmless for as long as anyone's kept records — cycling on their own, responding to the shard's low, steady pulse. Caelen doesn't read this as a malfunction. He reads it as proof: the ruin isn't a collapse to be catalogued, it's a system that's been waiting, patiently, for someone to give it structure again.

Act VII — The First Signature

The node records something none of Kett's Dominion references have a name for: a pattern imprint built from nothing but proximity, trust, and shared action in the ruins — not bloodline, not a spoken command, not ritual. A dyad-like resonance, forming by accident between Caelen and Arden simply because they were there, together, often enough. It is not a ley-heir. It is not the old cycle reborn under a new mask. It is something the Dominion's own scriptorium never got to finish building: a system rebuilding itself around the nearest strong will it can find.

Act VIII — Decision at Caelwick

Back above ground, Caelen proposes something bigger than a local survey: if Dominion shard remnants survived the Severance here, they've likely survived elsewhere too, on other continents entirely. He wants to know what else is still out there. The team agrees, tired and curious and not yet alarmed. Nothing about the proposal sounds dangerous. Over the following months, the two of them go on behaving as if nothing extraordinary happened at Caelwick — but the relay recorded a pattern, however small, and small was enough. Historians centuries later will name this the beginning of the Shattered Quiet: not a decree, not a chant, not an heir — Dominion logic reawakening purely through accident, affection, and intent, exactly as the Nulls said it could. A thousand years on, that flicker of a pattern survives through forgotten archival circuits and dormant Dominion nodes scattered across a changed world — and it wakes Caelen and Arden again, in an age neither of them will recognise, not as echoes, not as heirs, but as survivors of a pattern older than either of them ever understood.

World State After Campaign XXII

Geography. Caelwick Frontier Post is confirmed as a rainswept charter outpost built atop layered foundations — the current frontier town on the surface, Veil-era pump networks beneath it, and the collapsed Dominion scribe-vault beneath that. Its population resumes normal trade, entirely unaware a sub-surface shard still dreams below their grain cellars.

Magic. Dominion logic is rekindled, accidentally — not the cycle, not the heirs, but a remnant algorithm still able to mirror will. This directly complicates Campaign XXI's claim that the recursion was over: the Severance didn't prevent recurrence by intent, only by ritual mechanism, and the gap between those two things is exactly where Caelwick's Quiet Fault lives. Historians later identify this as the first of multiple such sites where the Severance never fully took.

The Durrel Line. No Durrel figures appear; Arden and Caelen's imprint is explicitly framed as new — "not a ley-heir, not a chain, something new" — the first major pattern-recurrence in the record with no claimed lineage connection to the Durrel name at all.

Institutions. The Null Compact is proven stressed by this campaign — the accidental activation shows the Null ethic wasn't enough, on its own, to prevent the world from mirroring a strong enough will. Caelen's story ends in death and Severance in Campaign XXIII, but his shard-coded echo survives the thousand-year jump, carrying the Caelwick imprint into the Fourth Cyberpunk Era.

Canon status: follows the source account directly; this campaign is set at the threshold before the actual 1,000-year jump — the full reinstantiation in 4CE happens at the opening of Campaign XXIII.
