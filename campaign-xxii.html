Timeline A · Campaign XXII

The Shattered Quiet

Late post-Severance drift — before the 1,000-year jump

Shortly after Campaign XXI; the 1,000-year jump follows this campaign Primary location: Caelwick Frontier Post, East Vanmere

Opening World State

A year after Talen Marric's Severance unspooled the Heart-Veil, ending the ley-heir cycle and returning destiny to mortals, the world hasn't fully stabilised. The Null Compact — Consent, Reversibility, Witness — spreads unevenly across the frontier republics; old Dominion corridors lie as dormant ruins that most people now walk past without a second thought. The great powers no longer listen for hums beneath the stone. The map is reverting to a quieter, medieval world defined by roads, rivers, toll-gates, and appetite, not ley-lines. And yet the Severance was a ritual, not an erasure — sites it never fully reached still hold sub-surface charge. Historians will later call these Quiet Faults. Caelwick, a muddy little charter town nobody outside East Vanmere could place on a map, sits on top of one without knowing it.

Party & Key Figures

  • Arden Vale PC Co-discovers the Shard Chamber and forms half of the unintended shard-pattern imprint.
    Quick-tongued, clever wanderer with a survivor's instinct; accidentally aligned with traits the old Dominion systems once rewarded — loyalty, desire, responsiveness, and a willingness to follow someone stronger.
  • Caelen Marrith Paladin, Captain of the Charter Guard.
    Charismatic and composed, with an instinct for leadership and a quiet hunger for order. Treats the frontier as a place to rebuild structure, not merely keep peace.
  • Lysa Thorne
    Charter Warden Scout; practical, skeptical, and the first to notice how quickly Caelen's behaviour changes around the shard chamber.
  • Kett Ironrun
    Dwarf merchant-archivist; keeper of relics salvaged from collapsed Dominion sites, skeptical but curious. Serves as guide to the ruins.

Act-by-Act

Act I — The Frontier Inquiry

A routine patrol report — subsidence near the old grain cellars — turns into something stranger once Caelwick's Charter Guard actually digs. Beneath the muddy streets, Arden, Caelen, Lysa, and Kett find the roof of a scriptorium vault that no civic record mentions: a collapsed Dominion-era archive, its entrance long since built over by a trade-republic outpost that had no idea what it was standing on. Caelwick is the kind of place where paperwork lapses quietly and nobody minds. That lapse matters later. For now, the four of them stand over a hole in the ground looking at something that should be impossible so soon after the Severance: faint crystalline light still moving below.

Act II — The Shard Chamber

Kett identifies the walls as Dominion administrative sigils, half melted, the kind that once meant a scriptorium answered directly to central command. At the chamber's heart sits the Last Shard Node, a crystalline matrix designed to mirror a wielder's will into structured command — one of the few instances of that Dominion technology the Severance never reached. It should be inert. It isn't. When the group studies it together, nothing happens. When Caelen steps close enough to touch the housing, the node answers with a low pulse. Arden notices how quickly Caelen begins talking about the relic like he already understands its grammar.

Act III — The Pattern Wakes

Deeper levels hold what Kett later calls Scribe-Lattice Script — partially corrupted Dominion scripting that does not merely record; it tries to complete. Fed by residual Pale Concord material baked into the walls, the script begins rebuilding behavioural logic around the explorers, drawing its shape from the strongest will in the room. That will is Caelen's. Not because he is an echo, and not because destiny selected him. The ruin is simply capable of mirroring intent, and Caelen wants order badly enough to give it something to mirror.

Act IV — The Charter Gap

Lysa spots the real problem, and it isn't magical: Caelwick's patrol charter lapsed months ago and nobody renewed it. Caelen's patrol is therefore operating without the oversight the civic system assumes exists. He does not immediately raise the alarm. Instead he starts making private decisions about what to log, what to omit, which passages to reseal, and which to keep open. Nothing dramatic — just a captain quietly deciding the discovery is his to shape.

Act V — Withdraw and Debrief

The team surfaces after two days, filthy and unsettled. Caelen is calm, turning the finds over like a man solving a puzzle he half expected to find. Arden notices that he has become more protective of the group's plans, more decisive about what happens next, and more certain of conclusions he had no reason to hold a week earlier.

Act VI — The Pumps and Second Descent

A second survey finds Under-City Pumps — old water engines retrofitted centuries earlier with Dominion stabilisers — cycling on their own in response to the shard's pulse. Caelen does not read this as a malfunction. He reads it as proof the ruin is a system waiting for somebody to give it structure again.

Act VII — The First Signature

The node records something none of Kett's references names: a two-person pattern imprint built from proximity, trust, and shared action — not bloodline, spoken command, or ritual. A dyad-like behavioural signature forms between Caelen and Arden simply because they have been there, together, often enough. It is not a Ley-Heir and not the old cycle reborn. It is a system rebuilding itself around the nearest strong will it can find.

Act VIII — Decision at Caelwick

Back above ground, Caelen proposes expanding the survey. If Dominion shard remnants survived the Severance here, others may have survived elsewhere. The group agrees, curious and not yet alarmed. Historians later name this the beginning of the Shattered Quiet: Dominion logic reawakening through accident, affection, and intent rather than decree or inheritance. The small imprint persists in dormant archival circuits. A thousand years later, when a new grid finally closes the missing circuit, that imprint will wake Arden and Caelen again in an age neither man ever saw.

World State After Campaign XXII

Geography. Caelwick Frontier Post is confirmed as a rainswept charter outpost built atop layered foundations — the contemporary frontier town on the surface, older pump networks beneath it, and a collapsed Dominion scribe-vault below those. Daily life resumes above a site whose historical importance nobody yet grasps.

Magic. Dominion logic is rekindled accidentally — not the old cycle and not the heirs, but a remnant algorithm still able to mirror will. The Severance ended recurrence by the old ritual mechanisms; Caelwick demonstrates that it did not make behavioural recurrence impossible.

The Durrel Line. No Durrel figures appear; Arden and Caelen's imprint is explicitly new, with no claimed lineage connection to the Durrel name.

Institutions. The Null Compact is proven incomplete as a safeguard against systems people do not recognise as systems. The pattern sleeps after Caelwick, migrates forward through dormant infrastructure, and survives the thousand-year gap into the Fourth Cyberpunk Era.

Canon status: follows the detailed Caelwick account. This campaign is the pre-4CE origin of the behavioural imprint; the 1,000-year jump occurs between XXII and XXIII, and the full Quiet Fault reinstantiation opens Campaign XXIII. Older concise summaries that place Arden and Caelen's 4CE awakening inside XXII compress the two campaigns together.
