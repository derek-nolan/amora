Core Premise

The Price of Command is the third directly played Oath Wars prequel, moving the deep-history chronology forward roughly six years from Those Left Beyond into a period where the War of Ash and Glass is no longer recovered through later archaeology but lived as an ordinary, worsening fact of life. Twenty-two-year-old Caldreth paladin Matt Harcourt and his oldest friend Tarenus "Taren" Durrel — already an existing Heartbinder in the wider recovered record, though not yet anything close to a legendary Warden — are drawn from a routine grain-road escort into exposing Ser Aldren Voss's Rookwatch and Eastvault command programme, a wartime network that uses captured civilians and soldiers as experimental material for Glass-aligned coercive magic. Matt and Taren free the prisoners, capture Voss, and swear the Oath of Devotion around the principle "truth before allegiance." The campaign's harder edge arrives afterward: Matt enters a genuinely consensual relationship with former Voss lieutenant Joren Cassel, who secretly converts that chosen submission into black-glass conditioning, a private detention designation (C-17), and a Custody Defence response that turns Matt into a weapon against the people he loves. The campaign's two set-piece operations — the command-tower assault at Darrow Vale, where Joren dies, and the three-junction liberation of Kelder's Reach — close the local war without resolving the wider one, and a last, deliberately unsettled thread opens with Caldreth roadwarden Ronan Vale, who tells Matt plainly that he intends to influence him if given the access.

Where this sits relative to B18, B21, and B22. B23 is played after B22 but is chronologically the third deep-history prequel, roughly six years after B21's own c. -928 ARV setting and about 1,265 years before B22's later c. 343 ARV present. Several later landmarks remain future history that B23's own characters cannot know: the Seven Refusal is roughly three years ahead (c. -919 ARV); the Great Seal setting cycle is roughly eight to nine years ahead (c. -914 to -913 ARV), with Heart set last at Silver Bastion; and the Shattering is roughly seventeen years ahead (c. -905 ARV). Tarenus Durrel is already a Heartbinder sworn to Devotion in the wider recovered record, but B23 does not show the origin of his Heartbinding and does not make him Heart Warden before that later Heart setting. The Oath Wars remain active and fragmented — Crown and Devotion paladins can cooperate, disagree, or fight depending on the local dispute — and the War of Ash and Glass, openly underway since roughly c. -932 ARV, is not reduced to a clean two-sided conflict. B23 does not alter B22's modern c. 343 ARV Corvane / Vesperhold state in any way — it advances deep-history canon, not a new chapter of the Age of Rediscovery.

Party & Key Figures

  • Matt Harcourt PC
    Human, Paladin 2 → 4, Oath of Devotion, Caldreth-born and gay. Twenty-two, physically direct, protective, and more inclined to make a hard choice in motion than to remain in prolonged abstract debate. Followed Taren into paladin training but develops independent judgement rather than acting as his subordinate. Closes the campaign Level 4 with Shield Master, STR 17, HP 36, and AC 19.
  • Tarenus "Taren" Durrel
    Twenty-three, Matt's oldest friend since age eight, and a fellow paladin reaching Paladin 4 / Oath of Devotion with Sentinel. Already part of the older Heartbinder tradition in the wider recovered canon and sworn to Devotion, but B23 does not depict the origin of his Heartbinding and he is not yet the permanent Heart Warden. Capable of affection, humour, protection, and brutality — he deliberately breaks two of the restrained Ser Aldren Voss's fingers after the Rookwatch victory.
  • Ser Eamon Durrel
    Forty-eight, Taren's paternal uncle — not his father, whose identity remains intentionally unnamed — and a formative mentor to both Taren and Matt. A practical, dry, experienced paladin whose priorities are safe roads, food, shelter, refugees, and responsible use of authority. Establishes Kelder's Reach's destruction-accounting rule and formally recognises both Matt and Taren as ready for Level 4 at campaign close.
  • Elira Vane
    Taren's girlfriend, met roughly seven weeks before the Rookwatch introduction. A capable independent scout, courier, and archer who kills Joren Cassel at Darrow Vale to stop him turning Matt against Taren. Matt explicitly accepts that she saved him. Remains Taren's girlfriend, not formally engaged, at campaign close.
  • Joren Cassel Lover / Antagonist
    Twenty-six, a Crown soldier and former trusted lieutenant in Voss's command programme who genuinely helps overthrow Voss and genuinely cares for Matt. Believes obedience built through attachment, pleasure, and approval is more durable than obedience built through fear — and without Matt's informed consent, modifies Matt's collar with black-glass command contacts, designates him C-17 in an old detention ledger, and conditions a Custody Defence response. Dies permanently at Darrow Vale, shot through the temple by Elira Vane.
  • Ronan Vale
    Caldreth roadwarden, archer, and tracker; older than Matt, exact age unresolved. Not one of Voss's men and carries no established command magic. Becomes Matt's next strong attraction at Kelder's Reach, openly states he intends to influence Matt if given access, and explicitly refuses to promise he is safe. Matt does not follow him when he leaves — the thread stays open for later play.
  • Kerr Dain
    Caldreth roadwarden and spear fighter; a recurring, capable field companion from the opening grain escort through Kelder's Reach, keeping the campaign from becoming a two-paladin rescue in which every local ally is helpless.
  • Captain Maelis Vorr
    Ash commander in her early thirties, burn-scarred and pragmatic. Becomes a battlefield ally at Darrow Vale and Kelder's Reach without ever being recast as the uncomplicated good side of the war, and receives no permanent claim over the Reach afterward.
  • Ser Aldren Voss Antagonist
    Senior officer of the Rookwatch / Eastvault command programme, which uses civilians and soldiers as experimental material under cover of wartime and institutional authority. Defeated and captured alive at Rookwatch; remains imprisoned through campaign close with no hidden superior revealed above him.

Act-by-Act

Act I — Caldreth, Ridge, and the Boys Who Grew Up

B23 opens by placing Matt and Taren inside a life that existed long before either becomes historically important. Taren was born at Durrel Ridge, but Eamon's long Caldreth assignment meant Taren spent substantial childhood stretches in the eastern city; Matt grew up there and became part of Eamon's household orbit almost by accident. The two met at age eight and nine during children's temple-school weapons drills — Matt bloodied Taren's nose with a practice staff, and the friendship stuck as ordinary biography, not a mystical sign of either boy's future. Eamon becomes their shared adult model: keep roads open, feed people, shelter refugees, and distrust an order when it stops serving the people it claims to protect.

Act II — The Grain Road and the Glass-Taken

The first assignment is intentionally mundane — escorting grain along the eastern road with Ronan Vale and Kerr Dain — which lets the war intrude as lived reality rather than exposition. Civilians Edrin and Mara are found altered by Glass command, frightened but still recognisably people needing rescue; they survive with damage rather than an instant restoration. A six-legged crystalline beast linked to a command shard attacks and is defeated by the full group together, the first direct evidence that Glass command architecture can organise battlefield creatures as well as people.

Act III — Rookwatch

The trail leads through Crown paladin Garran Holt — a participant in the prisoner and material chain, not the hidden architect — to Ser Aldren Voss's fortified Rookwatch quarry, chapel, and command hall. Internal resistance figures Lysa Merrow and Hadden keep the rescue from becoming a two-paladin operation in which every local is helpless; the active command rod is destroyed, and Voss attempts to flee before being tackled and captured alive. Taren deliberately breaks two of his restrained fingers afterward — a hard edge the later legend must not erase.

Act IV — Oath of Devotion

In the aftermath of Rookwatch, Matt and Taren swear the Oath of Devotion, centred on the principle "truth before allegiance" — a freely chosen oath to that principle rather than to personal obedience, carried through to their Level 4 close.

Act V — Eastvault and the Supply Trail

Five prisoners are freed at a Caldreth warehouse and twelve more at Eastvault; a natural-20 Investigation check proves Voss personally knew about the forced trials and evidence destruction. Clerk Orren Vey is arrested and the relevant accounts seized through his ties to Vale & Orsen Mercantile. Senior civic magistrate Lord Cassian Vale's legitimate authority was reused or altered in the records — proving access, not knowing approval — and his personal culpability is deliberately left unresolved. An unidentified blue-coated woman escapes the warehouse and remains unresolved.

Act VI — The Caldreth Gate

A mass refugee approach draws a Glass rider attack at Caldreth's east gate. The city holds and is not occupied or destroyed, but Matt's decision at the gate costs four civilian lives — a cost the record does not erase from his history.

Act VII — Joren Cassel

Matt and former Voss lieutenant Joren Cassel become lovers in a relationship that is, at first, genuinely and enthusiastically consensual: Matt actively wants Joren's dominance and enjoys leather, bondage, degradation, praise, and other chosen kink within a negotiated stop rule and duty exceptions, and Joren's ordinary life — cooking, relief work, horses, bad singing, books, a narrow blue-shuttered townhouse — is real rather than a cover story. Clues from leatherworker Maera, cobbler Den Orra, and a carved token at the old soldiers court eventually resolve into the harder truth: Joren was one of the lieutenants Voss trusted to make soldiers obey, and he does not want Voss restored — he believes Voss was merely crude, and that trust, pleasure, and approval can produce an obedience stronger than fear ever could.

Act VIII — The Collar Changes

Without Matt's informed consent, Joren secretly modifies the collar with black-glass command contacts, converting a chosen restraint into a covert magical interface. The coercion is demonstrated mechanically rather than inferred: Matt repeatedly rolls Wisdom saves at disadvantage and catastrophically fails — on being ordered to kneel, on his Devotion mark, on being pushed toward "Devotion comes second," on being told to burn a written warning, and on having disclosure itself inhibited. Standing consent to submission is treated as a hard continuity boundary that does not authorise secret compulsion.

Act IX — C-17

Joren reuses old Eastvault-style detention language, designating Matt C-17 in a private ledger — property-of-record language naming Joren as sole claimant, annotated do not release. "Home" becomes a conditioned return anchor, and the structure escalates to Custody Defence — Matt conditioned to protect Joren's ownership of him by force if necessary, confirmed by another catastrophic failed save. Some of the acts overlap with things Matt genuinely enjoys; the abuse lies in the hidden mechanism deciding when the structure applies and whether Matt can meaningfully refuse it.

Act X — Merrow's Ford

Matt, Taren, Elira, Ronan, Kerr, and Joren move against a Glass occupation at Merrow's Ford, rescuing roughly two hundred trapped civilians and destroying a command carriage controlling a giant Glass construct. A limited battlefield alliance forms with Ash commander Captain Maelis Vorr. During equipment negotiations, Joren uses the existing conditioning to have Matt distract Taren while he steals an older command rod — Matt's natural-20 Deception check conceals the theft.

Act XI — Darrow Vale

At Darrow Vale, hundreds of collared civilians and soldiers are controlled from a black-glass command tower. Joren seizes the tower with the stolen rod and issues DOWN / DROP WEAPONS / STAY DOWN, immediately saving lives — while the same network forces Matt down through his own modified collar. Taren finally confronts Joren directly. When the tower begins failing, Joren chooses continued command over releasing the captives into a brief uncontrolled interval; Custody Defence then makes Matt grab Taren's wrist when Taren reaches for the collar. Elira shoots Joren through the temple before he can reinforce the system again, killing him outright. Matt resists the surviving conditioning with a Wisdom save of 19 at disadvantage, stops himself from drawing against Elira, and recognises aloud that he has not been himself.

Act XII — Kelder's Reach

Ash techniques can painfully overload an individual Glass collar — a real but limited countermeasure, not a universal clean dispel. The campaign's final operation targets the retreating Glass force at Kelder's Reach, a road-and-gorge fortress built over a much older white-stone civilian sanctuary. A sanctuary-tunnel infiltration lets the party cut three command junctions together — the lower sanctuary, the cistern gallery, and the east-bridge command room — safely neutralising thirty-two active collars at once. All forty sheltering civilians survive, Commander Sevrin is captured alive, and the site's command architecture is destroyed. Caldreth retains the Reach afterward as a dual-function site: military defence above, a restored neutral civilian sanctuary below.

Act XIII — Ronan Vale

At Kelder's Reach, Matt's attraction to Ronan becomes a consensual, nonmagical dominance encounter. Ronan openly studies Matt's responsiveness to approval and clear instruction and says plainly that he intends to influence Matt if Matt keeps giving him access — and explicitly refuses to promise he is safe. Matt's natural-1 Insight check leads him to underestimate how seriously Ronan means it. On the final night, Matt hears Ronan outside and does not go looking; desire remains, but it does not decide the action, and the two are not a couple at campaign close.

Act XIV — Level 4, and the Return to Caldreth

Joren's Caldreth townhouse and remaining private apparatus are destroyed by fire — the public cause left unresolved — while supervised collar-release work continues at Darrow Vale under less urgency. Back at the Durrel household, Eamon formally recognises both Matt and Taren as ready for Paladin 4, and the campaign closes on a quiet supper rather than a victory parade — the wider Oath Wars and War of Ash and Glass left exactly as unresolved as B23 found them.

World State After Campaign B23

Matt Harcourt. Paladin 4, Oath of Devotion, Shield Master, STR 17, HP 36, AC 19. Alive in Caldreth, free of Joren's physical command hardware, with no active Joren command surviving by default. His sexuality and kink preferences — chosen before Joren and unrelated to the coercion — remain his own. Strongly attracted to Ronan Vale but not in a relationship with him at close.

Tarenus "Taren" Durrel. Paladin 4, Oath of Devotion, Sentinel (+1 Strength under the chosen rules chassis); the complete prior numerical ability array is unrecovered and must not be invented. Remains with Elira Vane, not engaged. Already a Heartbinder in the wider recovered record, but does not become the first permanent Heart Warden until the later Heart setting around c. -913 ARV.

Joren Cassel. Dead — shot through the temple by Elira Vane at Darrow Vale. His completed private C-17 method, personal command hardware, and usable coercive instructions are destroyed; no survival, resurrection, or hidden backup command is established. Broader Glass command technology remains independently active in the wider war regardless.

Ser Aldren Voss, Garran Holt, Orren Vey, Commander Sevrin. All captured or imprisoned at campaign close. Lord Cassian Vale's personal culpability, and the identity of the escaped blue-coated warehouse woman, remain explicitly unresolved.

Caldreth and Kelder's Reach. Caldreth survives the east-gate attack as a functioning major eastern city. Kelder's Reach is held by Caldreth as a dual-function site — upper military defence, a restored neutral civilian sanctuary below — with its Glass command architecture destroyed rather than preserved as a rebuild manual.

Hard separations carried forward. Glass command architecture is wartime coercive magic and infrastructure, not Great Seal technology — the permanent Great Seals remain unset until c. -914 to -913 ARV. Joren's C-17 is his own private derivative, not the whole of Glass doctrine. Ronan's influence over Matt is personal and nonmagical, not a command system. See the Canon Ledger for the complete B23 hard-lock set.

Open threads carried forward. Whether Ronan ultimately respects or crosses Matt's boundaries; Lord Cassian Vale's actual knowledge; the identity of the escaped blue-coated woman; Caldreth's later identity and fate, including whether it has any relationship to Brevair's later Corvane; and the road from c. -922 ARV toward the Seven Refusal and the Great Seal setting.

Canon status: follows the source account closely. B23 is a deep-history prequel and does not alter the modern later c. 343 ARV state established by B22 — see the Canon Ledger for the hard separation between Glass command architecture, Joren's private C-17 derivative, and the still-unset Great Seals, the Magic Codex for the Glass command system, the Devotion oath boundary, and the Kelder's Reach neutralisation method, and the Atlas for Caldreth and the eastern theatre's connected pre-Shattering geography.
