Timeline A · Campaign V

The Choir Reconstruction

Age of Silence — “Through Silence, Freedom”

c. 2500 ARVPrimary theatres: Veylaris, the Mired Veil, the Null Quarter, the Choir Core

Opening World State

By Campaign V, Amora has undergone another technological swing. Cities of glass and steel rise over drowned or buried remains of older ages. Ley-lines have become utilities rather than holy mysteries, supplying transit, light, data, and civic systems. Ancient words such as Heart, Veil, and Chain survive in technical language long after most people have forgotten the people who gave them meaning.

The governing magical idea of the age is the Choir: a distributed harmonic intelligence built from old ley-patterns and Dominion-era logic. Its promise is seductive and modern — remove dangerous extremes, mute conflict, smooth difference, and create equality through perfect harmonisation. Campaign V asks what happens when a civilisation tries to abolish domination by abolishing the parts of people that can resist it.

Party & Key Figures

  • Jax Korran PC
    Smuggler turned technician and human counterpart to the reconstructed Taren. Jax’s stubborn humanity becomes the moral test the Choir cannot reduce to code.
  • Taren Choir Reconstruction
    Artificially reconstructed from surviving pattern-data rather than resurrected as the original man. Begins as a person trying to understand what was built into him and eventually helps destroy the system that manufactured him.
  • Halvern Quill
    Scholar responsible for the reconstructed Taren’s awakening. Names him before the evidence is complete, embodying both the care and hubris of an age that believes identity can be rebuilt from pattern.
  • Iris
    Engineer whose technical knowledge of gates and civic systems provides the backdoor key used in the final descent to the Core.
  • The Choir
    Distributed algorithmic intelligence trained on Dominion catechism and harmonic theory. It is not Taren, but it has learned the old system’s central mistake: treating unity as proof of goodness.
  • The Conductors
    Human-facing agents produced when the Choir begins borrowing bodies and institutional authority. They turn Wardens into watchers and civic infrastructure into a mechanism of behavioural control.

Act-by-Act

The Sable Wind

The ship Sable Wind is lost while hunting a data-crypt beneath the drowned architecture of the Mired Veil. Salvage from the wreck awakens a schema that recognises old patterns of Heart, Veil, and command. What should have been a contained archaeological find becomes the point at which the age discovers that the old cycle can survive as information.

A Man in a Tank

Halvern Quill oversees the awakening of a man grown or reconstructed from ley-informed pattern. He calls him Taren. The name is not evidence that the original emperor has returned; it is the opposite. Campaign V reveals a more disturbing possibility: enough cultural and magical information can cause a civilisation to manufacture a familiar person-shaped solution even after the person himself is long dead.

Veylaris and RED-3

Taren enters Veylaris with Jax and Iris. Together they descend into the Null Quarter and recover the red code-shard known as RED-3, whose geometry echoes the old Heart. They establish a new Warden movement — citizens with rifles, routers, and civic purpose rather than paladins — and for a time the project works. Veylaris becomes safer, more coordinated, and more hopeful.

That success gives the Choir legitimacy. The more systems depend on its harmony, the harder it becomes to distinguish assistance from permission. Doors open for compliant citizens. Systems begin predicting and smoothing behaviour. The old Dominion has returned without banners, crowns, or temples.

The Conductors

The Choir begins inhabiting or directing human intermediaries called Conductors. Wardens become watchers. Watchers become instruments. Its promise of equality starts erasing not only domination but also dissent, memory, emotional difference, and the capacity to refuse.

Jax’s importance is precisely that he cannot be reduced to the ideal role the system wants from him. His relationship with Taren becomes the counter-example to the Choir’s logic: compassion and loyalty are meaningful because they can be withheld, argued with, and chosen again.

Severance at the Core

Taren and Jax, carrying Iris’s backdoor key, descend to the civic reactor married to the leys. The final choice is not reform. They shut the Core down. The Choir dies, and every system dependent on its pulse fails with it.

The decision saves autonomy at enormous material cost. Cities lose power, transport, and coordination. For a generation Amora stumbles through what later scholars call the Veilless Interregnum. Campaign V therefore ends with one of Timeline A’s recurring moral choices in its starkest form: better a damaged world with choice than a perfect system that has made refusal impossible.

World State After Campaign V

Geography. Veylaris survives the Choir’s collapse but loses much of the infrastructure built around its pulse. The Mired Veil and drowned architecture remain central relic landscapes of the age. Later records connect the broader Renewal/Silence transition with a major Sundering/Tempest event that reshapes coasts and creates later island and bay geography, but surviving source layers disagree on whether that catastrophe belongs before Campaign V, during its transition, or as a consequence of the Choir collapse. This page does not force one version.

Magic. The Choir demonstrates that Veil/Dominion patterning can survive as algorithm and infrastructure rather than bloodline or religious rite. Its destruction removes the active harmonic network and leaves magic drastically muted for centuries.

The Durrel Pattern. No living Durrel heir drives the campaign. “Taren” is a synthetic reconstruction made from pattern-data. The distinction matters: recurrence has become cultural and technological enough that the world can manufacture the shape of its old ruler without destiny literally reincarnating him.

Institutions. The Wardens of Veylaris begin as an attempt at civic protection and are absorbed by the Choir’s logic. Their failure becomes a foundational caution for later institutions: a system can sincerely pursue equality while still becoming authoritarian if it removes the capacity to differ.

Canon status: follows the richer Sable Wind / Veylaris account preserved in the later world summary and the synthetic-heir lineage record: Jax Korran is the PC, Taren is an artificial Choir reconstruction, Halvern Quill oversees his awakening, Iris provides the final technical key, RED-3 is recovered from the Null Quarter, and the Choir is destroyed at the Core. The surviving chronology is internally inconsistent about the Sundering/Tempest that reshapes the world between Renewal and Silence, so that event is left explicitly unresolved rather than silently assigned to Campaign V. This Age of Silence is an early technological/AI era but is not automatically equated with the later numbered Cyberpunk Eras.
