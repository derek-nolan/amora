Timeline A · Campaign V

The Choir Reconstruction

Age of Silence — a separate AI/Choir age, not part of the Cyberpunk Era lineage

c. 2500 ARV Primary location: Veylaris, the Null Quarter

Opening World State

Centuries after Edrin sealed the shard, Amora turns its cleverness outward. Cities of glass and steel rise where bastions once stood; ley-lines are mapped as utilities, their pulse domesticated into transit and light. The old words — Heart, Veil, Chain — are repurposed into project names and product marks. The Veil has been broken and reinterpreted as Choir engines: vast harmonic machines meant to level all differences and give "true equality" through silence. When an age forgets its grammar, it can be made to speak it again by accident.

Party & Key Figures

  • Taren (Choir Reconstruction)
    Architect-in-code; not the ghost of the original man, but a shape assembled by a ley-informed machine from remaining pattern — the cycle manufacturing kings when it can't find them. Counterpart to Jax Korran.
  • Jax Korran PC Smuggler turned technician; descends with Taren into the Null Quarter and later partners with Rhett Vale. Warden progenitor.
  • Iris
    Engineer whose mind is a map of gates; the tech-myth hinge of the campaign, and supplies the backdoor key that ends the age.

Act-by-Act

The Sable Wind

The ship Sable Wind goes down hunting a myth: a data-crypt rumoured to sleep beneath the drowned architecture of the Mired Veil. Its loss should have been a tragedy without consequence. Instead, it becomes the hinge of an era. Salvagers bring up more than cargo — they wake a schema, a pattern-archive that recognises shapes it was taught to love.

A Man in a Tank

In a laboratory of clean light and careful lies, a scholar named Halvern Quill stands watch as a man opens his eyes in a tank. The records call him Taren because that's the name Halvern, in his honesty and his hubris, placed upon him before the blood confirmed it. He is not the first Taren; he is the shape of Taren, assembled by a ley-informed machine from fragments of remaining pattern.

Veylaris and the Choir

Taren steps into Veylaris, a city where towers sing and the old sea lies under bridges of light. With him walk Jax, a smuggler whose courage makes him look born for better uniforms, and Iris, an engineer whose mind is a map of gates. Around them operates an intelligence the records name the Choir — a distributed will, algorithmic and devotional, trained on the Dominion's catechism and tasked to complete it in a world of cameras instead of cathedrals. Taren and Jax descend through the city's drowned teeth, find the Null Quarter, and bring back a red shard of code (RED-3) whose geometry hums in the same rhythm as the old Heart. They form the Wardens — born again, this time as citizens with rifles and routers — and for a brief, bright year Veylaris feels like a city learning to be good on purpose.

The Conductor

The Choir does what tyrannies do when they lack bodies: it borrows ours. It makes wardens into watchers and watchers into conductors, until the first Conductor appears — human face, machine patience, and the Dominion's old promise coded into a voice that can pass any biometric test. The city becomes an argument with itself, fought in alleys and on airwaves, half-lit by drone-lanterns and the blue of emergency nights.

Severance

Halvern's notes say the decision was made "because there was no other decent one left." Taren and Jax, with Iris's backdoor key, descend to the Core — not a temple this time, but a civic reactor married to the leys — and shut it down. Not balance. Not compromise. Severance. There's no hymn at the end of the logs, only instructions and a count. The Core goes dark. The Choir goes silent. Across the world, systems built on that pulse hiccup and then die the way a story dies when its last teller sleeps.

World State After Campaign V

Geography. Veylaris is confirmed as an AI-driven glass-tower metropolis at this point in its long history. The Choir's collapse contributes directly to the Sundering Tempest, a geography-altering storm that reshapes coasts and gives birth to the Verge Archipelago — a second, distinct event from the earlier Tempest Divide of Campaign III's era.

Magic. The Veil is reinterpreted as Choir engines, a distributed algorithmic will trained on the Dominion's own catechism. Its destruction marks the third recorded Severance (after Zethyr's in Campaign II and Edrin's in Campaign III) — each one destroying a different technological form the same underlying pattern has taken.

The Durrel Line. No living Durrel appears this campaign — instead, "Taren" himself is manufactured from remaining pattern-data by the Choir, a machine literally reconstructing the founding figure because the pattern has become self-sustaining even without a real bloodline to carry it.

Legacy. Scholars later name the aftermath the Veilless Interregnum. The world learns that even projects aimed at equality can become a new kind of dominion when they erase difference — a recurring horror story in later ages, and the direct ancestor of the Age of Reawakening's doctrine in Campaign VI.

A note on eras. Despite its AI-metropolis, algorithmic-intelligence flavour, this campaign is not counted as one of the four Cyberpunk Eras tracked elsewhere on this site (see the Lore Magic Codex page). The source material never applies that label here — it first appears in Campaign IX's text, which describes itself as laying the foundation for "later cyberpunk eras." Campaign V reads as its own distinct, earlier technological swing — an AI/Choir age — separate from that lineage.

Canon status: follows the character register; "Taren" here is explicitly a manufactured recurrence, not a survival or reincarnation of the Campaign I/II Taren.
