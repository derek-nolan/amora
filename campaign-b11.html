Timeline B · Campaign B10

The Quiet Rule of Brevair

Brevair Era

146–147 ARV Primary location: Brevair, House Valecour

Core Premise

Roughly ten years after Campaign B9. A story of private corruption within a noble house that ends as the quiet remaking of an entire city — a city does not fall to conquest if it can be taught to prefer its chains.

Party & Key Figures

  • Delryn Vale / Valecour
    Human Shadow Sorcerer; an acknowledged bastard raised within House Valecour, transformed over the campaign into the public face and keystone conduit of Rhalen's rise. The player character.
  • Rhalen Valecour
    The hidden architect of House Valecour's engineered fall and rebirth; eventual ruler of Brevair in all but title.
  • Seran Mordane
    Veil captain and Delryn's early handler; briefly seizes command of the house before being outmaneuvered and poisoned by Rhalen.
  • Malrec
    Former Veil master within the house; killed by Seran's overreach.

Act-by-Act

Seran's Hold

Seran Mordane begins a program of conditioning built through private ritual and escalating Veil saturation, reshaping Delryn from a politically vulnerable noble remnant into a highly tuned instrument.

The Revelation of Rhalen

Hidden letters reveal Rhalen Valecour, not Seran, as the true architect behind House Valecour's engineered collapse — including the deaths that cleared Delryn's path and even the terms of his own conditioning.

Malrec's Fall

A public demonstration meant to test Delryn's training becomes a succession event: Seran uses Delryn's Veil-changed shadow to kill Malrec and briefly seizes command of the house.

Rhalen's Rise

Rhalen eliminates Seran and the surviving chapter structure, outgrowing the chapter itself to become the effective ruler of Brevair without a royal title. He marries Delryn, publicly naming what was already true privately.

Final World State

Rhalen becomes Brevair's quiet administrative ruler — civic, widow, merchant, and chapel channels all function through him. House Valecour is restored as a legitimate public mask for a darker internal order. The campaign closes not with battle or divine rupture, but with something colder: a city that works, a ruler who needs no crown, and a marriage that is not redemption.
