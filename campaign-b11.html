The Fall of Silver Bastion

Highever / Evermere to Bastion's fall and the birth of the Veiled Citadel

Age of Founding, Reforged

Core Premise

After the Veil consolidation of Brevair in Campaign B10, the northern struggle shifts to Silver Bastion, Highever's sacred fortress-centre — the very location where Timeline B began in Campaign B1. What had stood as a symbol of disciplined Light faces a subtler danger than siege this time: interior corrosion through intimacy, secrecy, and devotion turned in the wrong direction. The campaign begins not with armies at the gate but with a relationship — one built first in shared missions and genuine attraction, then deepened, gradually and then irreversibly, into hierarchy, ritual, and willing descent.

Party & Key Figures

  • Gavren PC Bastion paladin whose devotion to Cameron becomes the road of his willing descent. Ends the campaign fixed beneath Cameron as his ward-knight.
  • Cameron
    Veil operative who rises through Gavren's devotion to become the Shah-aligned conqueror and ruler of Bastion — lover, handler, and eventually ruler of the Veiled Citadel.
  • Alwen Stroud
    One of Bastion's last true Light stabilisers, and the officer associated with Gavren's own earlier path into the fortress. Killed by Gavren, at Cameron's direction — the point where corruption becomes irreversible.
  • Tavian
    Practical Bastion witness-figure who notices too much; dies during the open seizure phase of the coup.
  • Pell
    Cameron's earlier Veil superior; killed by Gavren at Cameron's redirection, severing the old chain of command above Cameron and marking his first real rise.
  • Edris
    Part of the campaign's early Evermere social frame — the lighter, more human thread that, in retrospect, marks the last point the story could plausibly have turned toward a smaller, less catastrophic future.

Act-by-Act

Evermere Missions

Gavren and Cameron work together in Evermere on a string of shared assignments; repeated proximity turns what first read as chemistry into real attachment, and both men admit, over the course of these opening missions, that they like each other. A lighter social thread runs alongside it — including a date with Edris — that grounds what's coming in something more intimate than manipulation alone. Gavren's descent doesn't begin in doctrine. It begins in wanting to stay close to someone.

Return to the Regime

As the campaign moves from Evermere into Highever and Bastion, Cameron's Veil influence stops functioning as something hidden and becomes something Gavren knowingly returns to — private ritual and outright domination hardening, gradually, from isolated moments into a settled regime. The key turn comes when Gavren recognises the change happening inside himself and chooses to keep stepping toward Cameron anyway, because being with him feels worth whatever it's costing him.

Veil Alignment Deepens

Cameron discovers devotion is doing much of the work on its own, without needing much Veil technique to help it along — see the Lore page for how Shah-aligned reverence actually works as a mechanism. He begins shaping Gavren with real intent — his roles, his presentation, his prayers — while Light itself becomes harder for Gavren to summon cleanly and Shah and Veil structures start answering more readily instead. His old paladin forms survive on the surface; what answers them underneath has already changed. What began as a private imbalance hardens into a coherent hidden life. That life then turns political: Gavren starts carrying Bastion's own intelligence to Cameron, offering the fortress's closed-room truths the way he once offered devotion alone.

Pell Killed

Cameron redirects a mission to bring Gavren into a direct encounter with Pell, his own Veil superior, and has Gavren kill him. It's the first major blood-turn of the campaign — the moment the relationship stops being private corruption and becomes something bloodied and binding instead. Cameron rises; Gavren becomes bound to that rise; the old chain of command above Cameron is severed for good.

Alwen Stroud Killed

The darker, more devastating crossing comes with Alwen Stroud — not just a Bastion officer, but the man who represents the old life that first opened Gavren's future in the first place. Cameron identifies him as both Bastion's strategic spine and Gavren's personal severance point, and directs Gavren to kill him. When Gavren does, the campaign passes from corruption into something irreversible. Bastion loses one of its last true centres, and Cameron takes the act as final proof that Gavren's devotion now outweighs debt, memory, and Light itself.

The Veiled Citadel

With Alwen gone, Bastion's collapse accelerates fast. Tavian, the dry and practical witness who'd noticed too many of the wrong patterns, dies as the coup goes public within the fortress's own walls. Cameron moves cleanly from infiltration to outright takeover — remaining loyalists sorted into kneeling survivors, broken functionaries, disappearances, and removals. The main chapel is reconsecrated to Shah. Deepest of all, Cameron takes Gavren into Bastion's own ward chamber — not the original Heart Ward at Durrel Ridge, but the fortress's own major sacred nexus — and uses him as a living conduit to turn the ward itself. Bastion's old blessing architecture becomes a Veil ward; the fortress doesn't just house corruption anymore, it breathes through it. Silver Bastion is publicly renamed the Veiled Citadel in the reconsecration that follows, the old name declared formally dead, and Gavren — once Bastion's own paladin — is made to kneel before the surviving hierarchy and is fixed publicly beneath Cameron as his ward-knight. What had been private becomes public order.

The Line-Bind

The campaign closes in the newly turned ward chamber beneath the renamed Citadel, where Cameron completes a deeper binding — not marriage, not equality, but a formal line-bind under Shah joining ruler, knight, and Citadel into a single structure. The bond doesn't erase Gavren's will. It just makes separation costly. Cameron ends the campaign not as a covert manipulator but as the outright ruler of the Veiled Citadel. Gavren doesn't end as co-ruler, and doesn't end as a redeemed victim either. He ends as conquest and proof — the visible fallen son of Bastion, kneeling beneath the man who took it.

World State After Campaign B11

Geography. Silver Bastion — the founding location of all Timeline B from Campaign B1 onward — falls for the first time in this timeline's history and is renamed the Veiled Citadel. The fortress-scale ward-anchor is rerouted into a Veil ward, making any future reclamation far harder, and far more spiritually significant, than a purely military one would be.

Magic. Cameron's use of Gavren as a "living conduit" to turn the ward is a direct escalation of the conditioning-into-control pattern seen in Campaigns B5, B6, and B10 — but this is the first time it's been turned against an entire fortress's foundational magic rather than an individual or a single house.

The Durrel Line. Not directly involved — this campaign is centred on Bastion's own paladin institution rather than the Durrel Ridge storyline, though the loss of Bastion makes Durrel Ridge's importance as a remaining Light stronghold (see Campaign B9) far greater going forward — the Citadel's fall becomes the news that activates Durrel Ridge as the north's most plausible surviving centre of organised resistance.

Institutions. Silver Bastion no longer survives as a Light fortress — its command, chapel, and ward are all under Shah-aligned order. Cameron ends the campaign as the effective ruler of the Citadel and one of the major northern Shah-aligned powers of Timeline B. With Brevair lost to Veil consolidation in Campaign B10 and Bastion lost here, the Light is no longer the ruling order in the north at all. It becomes, from here forward, rebellion — setting up Durrel Ridge directly for Campaign B12, and for Campaign B14's eventual reclamation attempt.
