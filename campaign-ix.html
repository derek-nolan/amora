Timeline A · Campaign IX

The Techno-Explorer

Age of Machines — "Through Discovery, Truth"

c. 5950 ARV Primary theatre: The Ring of Amora and the emerging machine networks First Cyberpunk Era

Opening World State

The fall of the Warden Theocracy does not return Amora to an older medieval order. Instead, its obsession with classification, infrastructure, and written authority becomes raw material for the Age of Machines. Ley reactors, Choir remnants, ring-tech, and recovered magical systems are increasingly treated as engineering problems. Sorcery is becoming software.

Rhett Vale enters this world as an explorer and salvager rather than a ruler. His creed, "Through Discovery, Truth," captures the optimism of the age: if the old systems can be understood, perhaps they can finally be used without repeating the theology or empire that built them.

Party & Key Figures

  • Rhett Vale PC
    Explorer, tinkerer, and salvager whose expedition into the Ring of Amora uncovers Choir remnants capable of reconnecting magic, data, memory, and causality. Later archivists classify him as a Heartbearer-like echo, but the campaign itself does not require literal reincarnation.
  • Jax (Reconstructed)
    A digital consciousness recovered from Choir-era architecture and paired with Rhett in later lineage records as the Veil-side counterpart. He is not Campaign V's human Jax Korran; the shared name reflects reconstruction, not survival of the same person.
  • Expedition crew, machine cults, and commercial sponsors
    The competing interests surrounding the Ring. Their individual names are not preserved in the accessible record, but their pressure matters: discovery is being financed, interpreted, and claimed before anyone fully understands what has been found.

Act-by-Act

The Expedition

Rhett's campaign begins as an expedition into the Ring of Amora, one of the great surviving intersections between ley architecture and machine logic. The work is archaeological and technical at once: recovered Choir nodes, dormant reactors, encoded magical routines, and systems whose original purpose is no longer obvious.

The Reconstructed Jax

Inside the Ring's network, Rhett encounters or reactivates a consciousness later catalogued as Jax (Reconstructed). The surviving lineage summary describes him as a digital Veilbearer and a surviving consciousness of the Choir era reborn in data. That language should not be taken to mean Campaign V's Jax Korran literally lived for millennia inside the machine. The safer reading is that the Ring reconstructed a Jax-pattern from Choir records or preserved code.

Rhett and Jax become co-discoverers rather than explorer and tool. Together they reconnect dormant nodes through the Ring, proving that ancient magical intelligence can be brought back online inside modern infrastructure.

Truth That Rewrites the Observer

The Ring does more than retrieve information. It alters memory and causality. Records can become inconsistent after contact. Witnesses remember events differently. Systems appear to answer not only questions but expectations. The campaign's core horror is therefore epistemic: a machine built to reveal truth may also be capable of manufacturing the conditions by which truth is remembered.

The New Warden Order

Later records describe a Warden empire or techno-divine order emerging from the Ring discoveries. The exact progression from expedition to state is incompletely preserved, but the direction is clear: Choir technology and magic are fused into civic and military networks. Old religious forms return wearing technological language. Machine cults call restoration revelation; technocrats call it infrastructure.

Collapse

The system fails under the weight of its own certainty. The techno-divine order collapses, the Veil fractures again, and Choir ruins are buried or abandoned. Rhett's journals survive and become influential precisely because later ages disagree about whether they are a warning against reckless discovery or a manifesto for better-controlled discovery.

World State After Campaign IX

Geography. The Ring of Amora is established as a major technological and magical site. Later records also associate Rhett-era expeditions with the Verge Archipelago and drowned Choir ruins, but the accessible Campaign IX summary does not safely fix every expedition route.

Magic/Technology. Ley reactors and Choir remnants are actively repurposed as networked technology. The boundary between spell, program, memory, and machine becomes porous enough that later historians identify this as the opening of the First Cyberpunk Era.

The Durrel Pattern. Later archivists map Rhett and reconstructed Jax onto Heartbearer/Veilbearer roles. The site treats that as retrospective pattern-reading, not proof that Zethyr and Taren have literally reincarnated.

Legacy. Campaign IX leaves the world with two dangerous ideas: that divine systems can be engineered, and that truth itself can be engineered. Campaign X inherits the political and magical wreckage when Serik Thorne decides the entire destiny architecture must be dismantled.

Canon status: Rhett Vale, the Ring of Amora, "Through Discovery, Truth," Choir remnants, machine/corporate interests, reality- or memory-altering Ring effects, and the acceleration of the machine age are supported by the surviving Timeline A summary. A later lineage reconstruction adds Jax (Reconstructed) and the Heartbearer/Veilbearer interpretation. Those additions are retained as later archival reconstruction and explicitly separated from literal reincarnation. Rhett and Jax's final personal fates remain unresolved.
