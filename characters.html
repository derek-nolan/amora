Zethyr Durrel PC

IIIB1
High Commander of the Heartwardens · Lightbound paladin

The character every later "Durrel pattern" recurrence — a commanding figure paired with a devoted anchor — ultimately traces back to, though Zethyr himself is the anchor, not the commander, in the founding pair. Appointed High Commander of the revived Heartwardens by merit of discipline and clarity of judgement, not by lineage — he's not descended from Tarenus Durrel, the order's original founder; that blood runs through Taren. His founding vow beneath Silver Bastion, "Through Heart, Balance," becomes the Heartwardens' creed and, later, the exact inverse of the oath Taren carves above the black gates of Tarenus Keep.

How it began. Zethyr and Taren meet as fellow Heartwarden captains and discover the Heart Seal beneath Silver Bastion together within their first two months of service — the psychic bond is born in that same discovery, not chosen deliberately by either of them. A month later their first balancing ritual succeeds and the Heart Medallion visibly reawakens; by month four they've stabilised three further Seals (Aether, Flame, Stone) and earned joint recognition as Co-Leaders of the Heartwardens. Their marriage follows in month five, during an interlude in Evermere — and it's here, unconsciously, that what later chronicles call the "Chains of the Veil" begin forming between them, long before either recognises it as anything but love.

Personality. Devout in a quiet, private way — he still writes prayers to the Radiant One before sleeping, thanking them for the day and asking for guidance, long after Taren has stopped needing that kind of comfort. When nervous, he unconsciously traces the silver sunburst engraved on his shield, a gift from his mentor. He avoids gambling and tavern fights, carrying guilt from his youth that he never fully explains. Open-minded but quietly afraid his feelings for Taren might be read as some kind of divine test — a real tension between faith and heart that never resolves before Campaign II ends it for him.

The turn. Months six and seven of Campaign I bring the uncovering of Orell's plot and the first time either of them takes a life in its ending — a fracture in Zethyr's moral certainty that closes out the Age of Light on a far darker note than it opened on. In Campaign II, as Taren begins his private study of Veil energy, Zethyr does the one thing the marriage never fully recovers from: he yields command. Not dramatically — he simply stops contesting Taren's growing certainty, and the bond between them tips out of balance. He foresees the imbalance building toward catastrophe well before the end, and Taren ignores every warning.

IRevives the lapsed Heartwardens as High Commander; the founding ritual with Taren creates their psychic bond and unknowingly seeds the Dark Filament.
IIConsort and Co-Regent to Taren's Veiled Emperor — in practice, an instrument of his will. Foresees the coming collapse and is ignored. Ends the Dominion by severing the Veil Core himself, killing them both.
B1Unmade by a third-layer force in the opening rupture — erased, not killed, before his story can begin. Timeline B's entire divergence starts here.

Taren Durrel

IIIB1
Captain of the Heartwardens · The Veiled Emperor

House Durrel, a minor noble family from Highever's eastern provinces — a direct descendant of Tarenus Durrel, the order's original founder, generations before the Heartwardens lapsed and Zethyr and Taren revived them together. Appointed Captain of the Heartwardens alongside Zethyr's High Commander, his brilliance in the field matched only by an unquiet hunger for permanence that eventually consumes him. His creed as Emperor, "Through Shadow, Dominion," is carved above the black gates of Tarenus Keep — the deliberate, total inverse of Zethyr's own founding vow.

Personality. Proud, competitive, occasionally vain — but not cruel, not at the start. He respects strength and honesty, though he masks his own uncertainty behind wit and swagger. He and Zethyr trained side by side for years; Taren likely underestimated him early on for being middle-class and sponsored rather than born into service, but Zethyr's steady nature and skill in the field earned real respect over time. He teases constantly, rarely with any real cruelty behind it — at least until the Dark Filament and the weight of the Dominion start reshaping what "permanence" means to him.

The turn. The marriage in Campaign I's fifth month and the unconscious "Chains of the Veil" that follow it plant something in Taren that Zethyr never fully sees taking root. By Campaign II's opening months, while Highever's councils argue politics, Taren has already begun a private study of Veil energy — kept from Zethyr, kept from everyone. The Ritual of Binding beneath Greymount fuses the Veil Core to the Heart Medallion within the year, and the Dominion is born from that single secret. What follows moves fast: Aldric and Seraphine executed, the Heartwardens renamed Veilwardens, Auran Gate and Evermere conquered, and Taren crowned Veiled Emperor — his "lawful-evil turn," as later chroniclers call it, complete within twelve months of a marriage that began as an act of love.

The Dominion's peak. The Ley-Heirs — vessels of fused Heart and Veil essence meant to guarantee spiritual succession — are created in the Dominion's second year. Brevair and Greymount are annexed and Greymount renamed Tarenus. Even as Zethyr foresees the coming imbalance and warns him directly, Taren ignores it — until the Menge Auroras signal a resurgence of Light that the Veil reacts to violently, and within months Zethyr ends it the only way left to him.

ICaptain of the revived Heartwardens; the founding ritual with Zethyr creates their psychic bond.
IIRedefines Balance as control, becomes the Veiled Emperor at the head of the Dominion. Killed alongside Zethyr when the Veil Core is severed.
B1Unmade alongside Zethyr in the opening rupture — erased before either of their stories can begin.

Seraphine Vale

IIIB1B2B3B4B5
Grand Marshal of the Paladin Council · High Paladin of Silver Bastion

The clearest case of a single person's fate splitting completely between timelines. Both branches start from the same founding-era Seraphine — Timeline B diverges at 0 ARV, the exact moment of Campaign I/B1, so up to that instant she's literally the same woman in both histories. In Timeline A she's executed within decades; in Timeline B she survives far longer as the Order's ethical spine, but ultimately dies the same way — removing the strongest voice for restraint at a critical moment.

What she stands for. When the Council's leadership starts treating the six time-displaced travellers as objects of study after B1, Seraphine is the one who halts it outright — asserting a founding principle that shapes the whole Order of Anchors' culture afterward: "partners, not specimens; service, not study." It's the same instinct that later has her personally rewriting portions of Bastion's internal archive to correct the record rather than let convenient fictions stand.

The end. By B5, Shah-aligned clergy have gained real influence inside Bastion, and public worship has expanded past architectural symbolism into open doctrine. Seraphine is killed during the escalating internal conflict that follows — not a battlefield death, but a political one. Her loss removes the last institutional Luminara-aligned moral counterweight inside Bastion's leadership; paladin authority is absorbed almost immediately into Caen's sovereign command structure, with no one senior enough left to object.

IIRepresents the old Council against the Dominion; executed as the Veilwardens replace the Heartwardens.
B1High Paladin of Silver Bastion; founds the Order of Anchors after the rupture that unmakes Taren and Zethyr.
B2Oversees the Hollowrest Custodial Mandate.
B3B4Blocks the Council's attempt to classify the six travellers as research subjects; guides the Order's political and doctrinal response to the Frostborn and Veil-echo discoveries.
B5Killed amid escalating Shah-aligned internal conflict — ending the Order's direct continuity with its founding.

Personality. Composed, principled, and deeply perceptive — she balances compassion with duty so cleanly it borders on artistry, reading people quickly and kindly and always leaving room for honesty. She leads by clarity rather than force, trusts competence over hierarchy, and is acutely aware of how power corrodes intention. Soft-spoken but commanding: she listens first, asks precise questions second, and speaks last.

Edrin Durrel PC

III
Scholar-echo · Last Known Claimant of the Durrel Line

Meets Cael at the Hollow within the campaign's first two months — a scholar drawn to a paladin who carries an echo of Taren's own pattern, the Echo Chain forming between them almost as fast as it once did for Zethyr and Taren themselves. Expeditions to the Vault of Lineage and Mirror Bastion follow, surfacing ancestral memories that neither of them is fully prepared for.

The turn. By months five and six, Cael has begun claiming he is Taren reborn and starts moving toward Dominion reconstruction — and Edrin is left choosing between love and fear of what that reconstruction would cost everyone else. He chooses fear, in the end, or rather chooses to trust it: by month seven he uses the Heart Medallion to sever the Echo Chain outright, an act later scholars name the second recorded Severance. Cael vanishes at the Northern Gate the moment it happens. Edrin spends the campaign's final months writing the Final Chronicle and sealing the Vault of Tarenus — pausing the cycle rather than destroying it, and opening what history calls the Age of Equilibrium.

Cael Veyne

III
Paladin-echo of Taren

Bonds with Edrin at the Hollow through what becomes the Echo Chain — an echo of Taren's own resonance pattern living inside a man who never met the original. Ancestral memories surfacing during the Vault of Lineage and Mirror Bastion expeditions start to blur the line between echo and identity for him faster than for Edrin.

The turn. By the campaign's midpoint he's convinced himself he is Taren reborn, not merely echoing him, and begins moving toward reconstructing the Dominion in earnest — the same hunger for permanence that consumed the original Taren, resurfacing in someone who inherited only the pattern, not the person. Edrin's Severance ends it before it can go further; Cael vanishes at the Northern Gate the instant the Echo Chain breaks, his fate afterward unrecorded.

Feran Durrel PC

IV
Heartbearer · Explorer and Guardian

Becomes Coren's human counterweight at the Confluence — the partner Coren searches for who can mirror the old Zethyr role without fully surrendering to it. An explorer and guardian rather than a courtier, Feran's presence is what keeps Coren's "order without cruelty" project genuinely humane for as long as it manages to stay that way.

The arc. Feran's role across the campaign is balance, not command — a deliberate structural answer to Zethyr's fate in Campaign II, where yielding command let Taren's hunger for permanence run unchecked. Here, Feran holds his ground instead. The Confluence still half-succeeds and half-fails by the campaign's end, but the failure is softer than the Dominion's ever was, and later scholars credit Feran's counterweight for exactly that difference.

Coren Veyne

IV
The Emperor Reforged

Rises among the fractured city-states left after Campaign III's Severance, on the promise of "order without cruelty" — his creed, "Through Power, Eternity," a genuine attempt to inherit the Dominion's old imagery without repeating its full cruelty. He assembles the Confluence: old Dominion symbolism, Heartwarden ritual, and carefully limited Veil-technology, meant to give people real safety and prosperity rather than mere submission.

The verdict. Coren's project half-succeeds by any practical measure — the world under him is genuinely better off — but later ages read his reign as a warning as much as a triumph: proof that even benevolent dominion can calcify into something oppressive over time, no matter how kind its starting intentions were. The Confluence he assembles outlives his own reign, becoming raw material later reformers and scholars draw on for generations.

Jax Korran PC

V
Smuggler Turned Technician · Warden Progenitor

A smuggler who becomes a technician out of necessity when the Choir's AI-driven metropolis makes faith obsolete and machine reason takes its place. Descends into the Null Quarter alongside the manufactured Taren-shape, and forges the Harmonised Longsword with him — a weapon that, in the act of its making, helps sever the ley lines and end the Choir's reign outright.

The arc. Jax's bond with Taren is the campaign's emotional core — not romance with the original Taren, who's long dead, but with a ley-informed reconstruction assembled from remaining pattern-data, the cycle manufacturing a king when it can't find one. That the bond is real even though its object is manufactured is the campaign's central unease. Jax survives the Choir's fall and later partners with Rhett Vale, becoming, in later record, a progenitor figure for the modern Wardens founded in Campaign IX.

Taren (Choir Reconstruction)

V
Architect-in-Code

Not the ghost of the original Taren Durrel, dead for millennia by this point, but a shape assembled by a ley-informed machine from whatever pattern-data survived — the cycle manufacturing a king when it can't find one. His bond with Jax Korran is nonetheless genuine on both sides, which is exactly what makes the Choir's entire project so unsettling: a manufactured man can still love, and be loved, honestly.

The arc. Alongside Jax, Taren descends into the Null Quarter and forges the Harmonised Longsword — the weapon whose making severs the ley lines and ends the Choir's Age of Silence for good, three millennia of technological stasis beginning the moment it's done. What becomes of the reconstruction itself once the Choir falls isn't recorded.

Kael Duren PC

VI
"The Restorer"

Rediscovers a Heart Shard surviving from the very earliest age of Amoran magic, long predating the machine cults and Choir engines of the ages before him. His creed, "Through Memory, Rebirth," reframes magic entirely — not machine-bound, not empire-bound, but relationship-bound, tied to the people who hold and share it rather than any single institution.

The arc. Word of the Shard draws real pressure from rival factions who want it for power or profit rather than restoration, and Kael's campaign plays out through that tension — deciding how widely to share the rediscovered power and what safeguards to build around it, deliberately echoing Taren and Zethyr's own history while trying hard not to repeat their mistakes. Shared custody, genuine consent, and distributed guardianship all get tested here for the first time as deliberate design choices. His motto becomes the root doctrine of the Collegium Equilibria, a stabilising philosophy later ages return to whenever institutions start drifting back toward command-based magic instead of consent-based magic.

Elian Dorn PC

VII
"The Warden Executor"

Becomes the spiritual and political leader of the Wardens as the world swings back from the Collegium's scholarly ambiguity toward religious certainty. His creed, "Through Law, Salvation," defines an entire age — and founds the Executor office that becomes capital of the Wardens' Theocracy.

The arc. Elian's campaign follows the consolidation of theocratic power in real, procedural detail: codifying sins into formal doctrine, building the first inquisitorial circuits, and deciding, case by case, how far "salvation by discipline" can reasonably be pushed before it curdles into something else. As Warden decrees begin echoing old Dominion patterns more and more closely, he faces a genuine choice — reform the theocracy from within its own structures, or resist it openly from outside them. By his campaign's end, the Warden state has hardened into a rigid system that Sirian will later codify even further.

Sirian PC

VIII
The Codifier of Order

A legalist who sets out to write the Gate Codex, believing that if the theocracy's laws are simply made clear enough, their worst excesses can be tamed without needing to dismantle the theocracy itself. Motto: "Through Discipline, Purity."

The arc. Sirian's campaign tracks the actual writing, negotiation, and drafting process in close detail — the slow, unglamorous work of turning belief into statute. Once written, the Codex doesn't stay Sirian's alone: the party discovers, gradually and then all at once, that clarity doesn't automatically produce mercy. Once a rule exists in writing, it can be picked up and used by whichever faction currently holds power, regardless of what Sirian originally intended it for. The Codex outlives the theocracy that produced it by a wide margin, quoted by courts seeking legitimacy and tyrants seeking cover in equal measure long after Sirian is gone.

Rhett Vale PC

IX
The Techno-Explorer

An explorer and tinkerer by temperament who ventures into the Ring of Amora chasing exactly the kind of discovery the newly turned Age of Machines is hungry for. Motto: "Through Discovery, Truth."

The arc. Rhett's expedition frames the whole campaign as a dive into genuinely dangerous innovation — navigating ley-mechanisms, lingering Choir remnants, and commercial interests funding the exploration for their own reasons, not all of them aligned with his. What the party ultimately uncovers is unsettling: the Ring doesn't just reveal truth the way Rhett expected it to. It actively rewrites it, altering memory and even causality in ways nobody fully anticipated going in. His own journals from the expedition become something like scripture for later technocrats and reformers, and the machine age accelerates hard in their wake — laying the direct foundations for the cyberpunk eras that follow.

Serik Thorne PC

X
The Severing

A former inquisitor who emerges as a genuine revolutionary reformer once the Gate Empire collapses in fire and rebellion at the start of his campaign. Motto: "Through Choice, Freedom" — the first true rejection of ordained destiny anywhere in the record.

The arc. Serik's work is direct and structural: dismantling destiny engines built up over centuries, rewriting constitutions from the ground up, and working deliberately toward severing the Durrel echo as any kind of binding fate. His campaign culminates in the Severing proper — a metaphysical and legal break that ends the "guaranteed" return of Taren and Zethyr as central rulers of anything, the fourth recorded Severance in the record and the first framed explicitly as a legal and constitutional act rather than a personal sacrifice.

Riven Mareth PC

XI
Human Rogue ("Whisperknife") · Relic Broker · Clause Zero

Lean, early thirties, dark hair, pale eyes, weather-worn leathers — a relic broker and cipher-thief out of Veylaris Rest, unknowingly bound to the world's living law through the Mareth line: Heralds and Witnesses of Taren Durrel's forgotten law, a lineage Riven doesn't understand himself until deep into the campaign. Pragmatic, sardonic, capable of deep loyalty, and quietly haunted by an ancestry he spends the whole campaign trying not to repeat. Likes freedom, wit, quiet nights, and clean getaways; hates fanatics, destiny, and being remembered for the wrong things.

The arc. What starts as one more relic job — fleeing The Gate's collapse after uncovering relic-law tampering — becomes the campaign that forges Clause XI, the Breathing Law, at Bastion Saren, with Riven choosing restraint over power at the exact moment restraint is hardest. He carries that choice through the destruction of a genuinely dangerous null-relic, the discovery of the Twelve Herald Houses, and finally the Bridge of Mercy — where he's offered the chance to erase, merge, or stand apart from Clause Zero, and chooses to merge it with the Twelve while staying fully human. He ends the campaign remembered as Clause Zero, the Breath Between Laws — not a myth stepping out of the world, but a mortal who chose to keep living in it.

Cassian Vale

XI
Ex-Watchman Turned Mercenary · Riven's Partner

Broad-shouldered, short-bearded, calm-eyed, in practical armour — a former Veylaris Rest watchman who lost his badge defending Riven years before the campaign begins, and never once treated it as a sacrifice worth resenting. Acts as Riven's moral compass and protective anchor throughout; likes bad jokes, warm coffee, and small victories, and hates bureaucrats, zealotry, and needless heroics in roughly that order.

The arc. Cassian's role never wavers across the campaign's many turns — he's the one constant while Riven's world reshapes itself around Clause XI, the Twelve Herald Houses, and the Breath-Bridge. Injured and recovered during the Republic's Shadow, his protective instincts only sharpen through it. The rooftop scene above Verrin's workshop — where he and Riven joke about rewriting reality so Cassian becomes Riven's husband even if he'd never remember it — is the clearest window into what actually holds the two of them together.

Ilyen Vael

XI
Hidden Ledger Agent · Descendant of the Editors

Compact, ash-blonde, sharp grey eyes — a Hidden Ledger operative descended from the ancient Editors, sharp and secretive by training but pragmatic by temperament; her loyalty bends toward balance rather than faction whenever the two conflict. Likes control, quiet efficiency, and clear motives; hates ignorance, zeal, and prophecy in equal measure.

The arc. Ilyen co-authors Clause XI alongside Riven at Bastion Saren and later co-anchors the Breath-Bridge ritual with him and Leora — functioning as Riven's counterpart across the whole campaign rather than a subordinate. Marked with an echo-scar after the null-relic's destruction, her loyalty to the Ledger is tested once Verrin's more pragmatic methods enter the picture, but she never fully breaks from either her order or her growing trust in Riven's group.

Leora Vance

XI
Lyricum Scholar · Archivist of the Breath

Auburn hair, grey eyes, ink-stained hands — a Lyricum archivist who risks censure to preserve fragments of Choir law rather than let power quietly erase them. Likes research, order, and dry humour; hates censorship, superstition, and wasted time. A pragmatic idealist who documents truth without letting politics warp it, which is exactly why she ends up at the centre of the campaign's most dangerous discoveries.

The arc. Leora is the one who first names what becomes Clause Zero, documenting it methodically while Riven and the others act on it. Marked with an echo-scar after the null-relic's destruction, her research focus shifts afterward toward stabilising post-Clause-XI resonance — and by the campaign's end she's the custodian of the Clause Zero record itself, the person history will actually check when it wants to know what really happened.

Kael Durreth PC

XII
Prime Steward · Oath of Inheritance Paladin

Founds Inheritance-Reclaimed and its Harmony Network — a leygrid that mirrors civic emotion and obedience, built with genuinely good intentions to keep the Republic prosperous and united after generations of fracture. Partnered with Evren Thalos, whose historical suspicion tempers Kael's institution-building with real questions about who ends up harmed by permanence.

The legacy. Kael's own campaign closes without catastrophe, but his absence looms over everything that follows: a decade later, in Campaign XIII, his harmonic code is found still embedded in the leygrid, and whether he intended control or liberation stays genuinely uncertain — his legacy divides the Republic long after his own story ended. Commander Seraphine Vale oversees the Sentinel build-out protecting his new institutions; Jalen/Joren keeps him tied to an ordinary human life beyond councils and oaths; Cassius opens trade routes to Harn.

Evren Thalos

XII
Hidden Ledger Scholar · Co-Architect

Kael's partner and co-architect of the Republic of Inheritance — a Hidden Ledger scholar whose historical suspicion of permanent institutions is exactly what keeps Kael's Harmony Network from calcifying the way the Dominion once did. Constantly asking the one question builders of grand systems tend to avoid: who would be harmed by this lasting forever?

Rian Leth PC

XIII
Oath Guard Initiate · Stabiliser Node

Displays perfect obedience as an Oath Guard Initiate until emotion disrupts command — the first sign that the Chain's control isn't as total as the Council believes. His empathy becomes the actual key to severing the Council's grip on the Chain by the campaign's end. Meets Calen Vorr, the officer assigned to test and oversee his synchronisation, and their resonance aligns almost immediately into a genuine Anchor-Vector bond, not merely a professional one.

The arc. Through the Western Surge storm and a formal Resonance Interrogation meant to prove the Chain can enforce total obedience, Rian's empathy keeps overriding what the Chain and the Council both expect of him. He and Calen ultimately choose to sever the Council's control over the Chain while sparing its existence entirely — preserving something rather than erasing it. The epilogue finds them settled together at a forge north of the Menge Expanse, teaching that discipline isn't obedience and silence can be its own kind of freedom.

Calen Vorr

XIII
Captain · Sentinel Officer

Assigned to test and oversee Rian's synchronisation with the Chain, and becomes far more than an overseer — his protector, commanding officer, and moral compass across the campaign. When the Council orders Rian to kneel under direct command during the Resonance Interrogation, Calen intervenes and reasserts control through tone alone, overriding the Chain's own hierarchy to protect him.

The arc. Calen leads the operation that ultimately frees the Chain from Council control, and afterward founds a new doctrine of voluntary service — discipline chosen, not imposed. He and Rian settle together north of the Menge Expanse in the campaign's quiet epilogue.

Rhett of the Vale

XIV
Emperor · Architect of Stillness

Inherits a fractured world and sets out to weld it into unity under a single creed: "Stillness is mercy." With Niall Argent as his Witness, he remakes the world into the Dominion — an empire where order itself becomes religion, its pulse kept in rhythm with the Veil's own last whisper. (No PC confirmed for this campaign; both figures are given full treatment given their centrality to the story.)

The turn. As the Veil falls silent, Rhett confronts Niall over the "revelations" that had been quietly filling that silence, and rather than breaking from the lie, chooses to preserve it — forging the Twin Chains and ruling through fear of collapse from then on. He remakes the Pattern one final time in his last decades, authority flowing only upward, peace absolute and airless. When the Veil finally wakes and reclaims what was bound, Rhett watches the empire he spent a lifetime perfecting dissolve into weather, and vanishes into the northern forests — remembered afterward as the Last Emperor.

Niall Argent

XIV
Witness and Consort · First Warden of Motion

Bearer of the Pattern Complete and Rhett's Witness — desperate to spare the people despair when the Veil falls silent, Niall begins quietly whispering "revelations" of his own, answered by Rhett as divine command. What starts as mercy hardens, alongside Rhett, into the Age of Adornments' scripture-hammered-into-metal ritual.

The turn. When the Veil finally reclaims the Dominion, Niall calls it mercy rather than tragedy, and afterward founds the Sanctum of Renewal, wandering south with the now-extinguished Heart to make sure the new republics rising in the Dominion's place never again worship stillness the way Rhett's empire did. Remembered as the Witness Who Let It Move.

Dareth PC

XV
Rogue/Fixer of the Red Lattice · Civic Consort & the Voice

A Red Lattice rogue and fixer elevated into Civic Consort and the Voice — the public ritual witness who makes consent visible under Concord's new civic architecture. Works alongside Valen, the Aegis Knight turned Concord Regent whose own perimeter craft turns visibility itself into rule, the two of them anchoring the Third Cyberpunk Age's core civic innovation together.

The arc. Dareth survives the campaign's political pressure — including a blocked affidavit and Kaust Directorate remnants targeting the Red Lattice directly — supported by a found family of colleagues (Pela, Lys, Mara) and mentored by Harbor Tom, whose death by campaign's end is marked with a quiet memorial at the harbor rail. Ends the campaign as "Voice Unbound," the model every later Concord consort role gets measured against.

Valen

XV
Aegis Knight Turned Concord Regent

Rises from Aegis Knight to Concord Regent by turning perimeter craft into genuine civic rule alongside Dareth — the two of them together anchoring Neon Concord's proof-first, consent-visible approach to law. Steps down at the campaign's end to lifetime penitent service as Warden-Prime, a quiet ending for a figure who spent the whole campaign making visibility itself into the mechanism of trust.

Kaeso Vyr PC

XVI
Zethyr-Echo · Consort-Primus

An unknowing Zethyr-echo at the campaign's start, Kaeso awakens the reconstructed Taren-echo Coren and becomes the corridor's Consort — the one who pre-claims doors so the room learns tempo by following him rather than by decree.

The arc. Kaeso and Coren adopt documented, consent-receipted custody windows as their bond deepens, extending Campaign XV's civic consent architecture from a single city into genuine corridor-wide practice. By the campaign's end, the corridor breathes to Kaeso's count and releases on Coren's — a crown by practice rather than decree, publicly acknowledged as myth yet bounded firmly by appeal and public ledger.

Coren

XVI
Taren-Echo · "The Crown"

A reconstituted Taren-type echo discovered in containment, who chooses to rule by habit instead of decree — turning the corridor into infrastructure alongside Kaeso rather than building a throne. His public posture stays neutral even as the private cadence between them deepens fast.

The arc. Coren deliberately baits the Chain's own signature to draw out tracer handlers trying to exploit it, letting Cadence Court fold them with proof rather than force. By the campaign's end his role has narrowed, by his own choice, to punctuation only — a raised finger marking a period, an open palm setting the pace — while Kaeso carries the corridor's actual rhythm.

Lucen Marrin PC

XVII
Rogue, Thief · Blade for the Gray Woman's Order

A survivor of the fallen Concord grids turned blade for hire — once a street survivor, later a symbol of justice by knife. The Gray Woman binds him to an impossible contract: infiltrate the Widow's Pale Ring and expose her reach into Evermere's Council. What begins as espionage curdles into seduction, deception, and betrayal, with Lucen's own moral code fracturing between what he owes the Gray Woman and what he's starting to feel for Sorell Marcone, the Council officer he's meant to be working alongside.

The turn. When the Widow is finally captured, the Gray Woman demands spectacle and the Council demands confession; Lucen chooses a third path, ending the game in secret. Mirelle Tress is found dead in her cell, throat cut in prayer position — no one sees the hand that did it, though Sorell guesses. The revelation doesn't break them; if anything it twists them closer, love read as confession, command read as mercy. Lucen eventually withdraws to the southern vineyards, tending quiet — though the Gray Woman's mark on his wrist still burns whenever the law is broken back in Evermere.

Sorell Marcone

XVIIXVIII
Officer of Evermere's Council

Lucen's lover, torn between civic duty and the man he cannot bring himself to condemn. His own reforms, meant to purge corruption from the Council honestly, end up bleeding into the Gray Woman's separate purge of her rivals — and by the time the Widow turns up dead in her cell, he's the only one who guesses what Lucen actually did, and chooses silence as judgement rather than confrontation.

Former full-time pleasure-house worker, fixer, thief, runner, and information broker — son of Tomas and Elian Rookvale, two Ridge clerics killed defending civilians during Silver Bastion's earlier fall. Mike grew up in Highever's lower district learning to read clients, officers, locked doors, and dangerous men, surviving on charm, stealth, deception, and lethal competence. He remains unsworn throughout — never a paladin or cleric — his Light allegiance recognised as a personal choice rather than a formal oath.

The arc. Undercover at the Velvet Gate, Mike steals an occupation officer's Night Gate key and escapes Lucien Varric's first attempt to claim him, reporting the full truth rather than hiding what happened. Jack recognises his parents' names and offers him a real place at the Ridge regardless of what happens next. The last clean Saint-Ward recognises Mike as a free, unsworn, chosen Light bearer — proof sacred recognition never required institutional purity — and through him the Bastion's servant-light paths reopen. He kills Lucien, then Gavren and Cameron during the reclamation itself, opening Silver Bastion from within. Caught afterward in a working Lucien had built specifically to claim him even in death, Mike is freed by Jack and Caelen before it fully takes — treated as a victim of it, not a participant. He ends the campaign a formally recognised Ridge field scout, given his parents' old quarters, and building a chosen future with Evan.

Evan Marric

B14
Durrel Ridge Scout

Assigned to accompany Mike inside Silver Bastion, and becomes his field superior, infiltration partner, and — after real teasing built across the whole campaign — his romantic partner. Genuinely happy, and more than a little surprised, that Mike still wants a future with him once the dust settles.

The arc. Evan leads the pursuit of the fleeing occupation command after Silver Bastion falls, with Mike now formally under his field authority. Caught alongside Mike in Lucien's final working — his own protective instincts magnified into something possessive and commanding that he never freely chose — Evan is freed by the same intervention that frees Mike, and neither man lets it define what comes after. He deliberately moves his own Ridge quarters into the room next to Mike's, hands over a spare key, and the two of them share a real kiss, openly, at Caelen and Corvin's wedding.

Dena

B12B13B14
Household Anchor of Durrel Ridge

Not a warrior, not a Warden — Dena is the reason Durrel Ridge functions as a home and not just a fortress. When Jack reopens his inherited house in B12, it's Dena's care, alongside Marek's loyalty and the team's presence, that turns a private mausoleum into a lived household; she and Marek become active parts of Jack's restoration, not bystanders to it. She's confirmed as one of the campaign's key emotional anchors — the person whose attention Jorah has to earn, and does, the ordinary way: meals, rooms, jokes, trust.

The heart of the Ridge. By B13, twelve years later, Dena is the household anchor in full — keeper of the Ridge's emotional truth and Caelen's safest domestic figure. When he's rescued from Thorn House, he goes instinctively to her kitchen before anywhere else — a choice that confirms, without either of them needing to say it aloud, exactly where his safest place in the world is. He spends his first night back in her lodgings rather than alone, and she recognises the trust implied by that choice without making him explain it. The Ridge, by this point, is explicitly described as a home built by Taren, Jack, and Dena together — not a fortress that happens to have people living in it.
