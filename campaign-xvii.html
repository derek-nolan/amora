Timeline A · Campaign XVII

The Widow's Oath

Law in Shadow, Mercy in Sin — Age of Disconnection begins

8410–8415 ARV Primary location: Evermere, the Red Lattice Quarter

Opening World State

When the last neon towers of Highever finally dimmed, the proof-bound world of the cyber ages collapsed with them. Cities once ruled by optics and procedure — the whole "printer, ink, perimeter" civic architecture of Campaigns XV and XVI — revert to ink, whispers, and knives; memory of law survives now only as folklore. Evermere, rebuilt atop the drowned datacores of Highever's coast, becomes the reluctant capital of this dim rebirth — half court, half graveyard. The Red Lattice Quarter, formerly just a district, stands as the city's most infamous enclave, where oaths, flesh, and confessions all trade at equal value.

Party & Key Figures

  • Lucen Marrin PC Rogue, thief, and reluctant agent of the Gray Woman's order — a survivor of the fallen grids turned blade for hire. Once a street survivor, later a symbol of justice by knife.
  • Sorell Marcone
    Officer of Evermere's Council and Lucen's lover, torn between civic duty and the man he cannot bring himself to condemn.
  • The Gray Woman
    Courier-matron and spymaster; manipulates Evermere's decay through favours and dead letters. Contracts Lucen to dismantle the Pale Ring.
  • Mirelle Tress, "The Widow"
    Mistress of the Pale Ring, a smuggling empire disguised as charity; controls Evermere's underbelly through fear and donation.
  • The Council of Ash Veils
    Evermere's ruling senate — righteous by day, purchased by night; instrumental in both the Pale Ring's rise and its fall.

Act-by-Act

Infiltration

When the Age of Light died, its sins survived in ink. Lucen Marrin works the ruins — courier by cover, thief by need — until the Gray Woman binds him to an impossible contract: infiltrate the Widow's Pale Ring and expose her reach directly into the Council. What begins as espionage curdles quickly into seduction, deception, and betrayal. Sorell's own reforms, meant to purge corruption from the Council honestly, end up bleeding into the Gray Woman's separate purge of her rivals — and Lucen becomes both women's shared weapon, and their quiet casualty.

The Purge

Sorell's purges intensify even as the Widow consolidates her hold over the Ash Veils. Lucen uncovers Sorell's own complicity in what's unfolding, and their alliance shifts into something warier and far more intimate. Council records burn, allegiances flip overnight, and Lucen's own moral code fractures between what he owes the Gray Woman and what he's starting to feel for Sorell.

The Widow's Death

The Widow is finally captured. The Gray Woman demands spectacle; the Council demands a public confession. Lucen chooses a third path instead — ending the game in secret. Mirelle Tress is found dead in her cell, her throat cut in prayer position; no one sees the hand that did it, though Sorell guesses. The revelation doesn't break them. If anything, it twists them closer together — love read as confession, command read as mercy.

Final World State

The Pale Ring shatters; Evermere's Council survives under Sorell's pragmatic rule, a republic held together as much by blackmail, confession, and love as by any actual statute. Sorell rules Evermere from the bench; Lucen enforces its order from the shadows beneath it. The world calls it peace. They call it necessary sin. Lucen eventually withdraws to the southern vineyards, tending quiet — though the Gray Woman's mark on his wrist still burns whenever the law is broken back in Evermere.

World State After Campaign XVII

Geography. Evermere is confirmed rebuilt atop drowned datacores as a candlelit, low-tech city-state — a dramatic reversal from its neon-metropolis state under Concord. The Red Lattice Quarter becomes the era's defining location, where oaths, flesh, and confession now function as the city's real currency.

Magic/Law. The age of optics becomes an age of whispers — oaths are signed in blood, not ink. Without Concord's cameras, morality becomes entirely personal; justice exists only between people who remember what proof used to feel like. This is the collapse of the Third Cyberpunk Age and the true dawn of the Age of Disconnection. Former couriers, smugglers, and agents form new guilds that replace the old Concord grids with purely human networks.

The Durrel Line. No literal Durrel appears, but Lucen and Sorell's bond is explicitly framed as echoing the old Durrel pattern — authority and devotion intertwined until mercy and obedience become the same act — not a literal recurrence of Taren and Zethyr, but the same underlying shape reasserting itself in a much darker key.

Institutions. The Pale Ring is shattered; Evermere's Council survives, but as something closer to a private arrangement than a real government. Lucen's Widow's Oath becomes a whispered covenant across the city: justice in the spaces the law can't reach — reframing dominion-style control into street-level violence and mercy, and grounding later magical crises in very human costs. Technology's fall restores real consequence: every oath sworn from here forward draws real blood.
