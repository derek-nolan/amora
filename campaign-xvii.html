Timeline A · Campaign XVII

The Widow's Oath

Age of Disconnection begins

8410–8415 ARV Primary location: Evermere, the Red Lattice Quarter

Opening World State

When the Concord Grids collapsed and the last neon towers guttered out, the Third Cyberpunk Age ended not in fire but in silence. Cities once ruled by visible law fell dark; proof became parchment, and power reverted to hands and knives. Evermere, rebuilt atop the drowned datacores of Highever's coast, becomes the reluctant heart of this medieval twilight — half bureaucracy, half shrine. The Red Lattice District is rebranded the Red Lattice Quarter, remaining Evermere's most infamous sanctuary: a den of information, flesh, and confession.

Party & Key Figures

  • Lucen Marrin Survives — withdraws to the southern vineyards
    Streetwise rogue and reluctant agent of the Gray Woman's order. Once a street survivor, later a symbol of justice by knife. Echoes the Durrel archetype of surrender tempered by agency. Lover of Sorell Marcone.
  • Sorell Marcone Survives — presides over Evermere's reformed Council
    Evermere Council officer and Lucen's lover, torn between law and the man who breaks it. Rules by confession and personal trust after the Pale Ring's collapse.
  • The Gray Woman Alive; remains in the shadows
    Head of an intelligence guild disguised as couriers; contracts Lucen to dismantle the Pale Ring. Influence wanes as Evermere's lantern law rises.
  • Mirelle Tress, "The Widow" Executed by Lucen; death staged as suicide
    Crime matron of the Pale Ring, disguising vice as charity; controls Evermere's underbelly through fear and donation.
  • The Council of Ash Veils (collective) Intact under Sorell's new leadership, morally tainted
    Evermere's ruling oligarchy — half-righteous, half-corrupt; instrumental in both the Pale Ring's rise and its fall.

Act-by-Act

Infiltration

Lucen Marrin, a streetwise rogue and part-time sex worker, lives off the refuse of fallen empires until a Gray Woman contract draws him into the Pale Ring's inner circles. The Widow, Mirelle Tress, masks her empire of contraband relics behind charitable ledgers and civic reform. To dismantle her power, Lucen and his handler Sorell weave a double game: law by day, knives by night.

The Purge

The investigation curdles into civil inquest. Council records burn, allegiances flip, and Lucen's own moral code fractures between love and necessity. When the Gray Woman proposes sacrificing innocents to root out the Widow, Lucen turns executioner himself — staging the Widow's death in prison and keeping the order secret, even from Sorell.

The Widow's Death

What follows is aftermath, not triumph. Sorell pieces together the truth: the man he loves has become the city's unseen blade. Their reconciliation is half-confession, half-submission — a fragile peace where command and surrender blur into faith. Together they rebuild Evermere's street order, neither holy nor lawful, merely human.

World State After Campaign XVII

Geography. Evermere is confirmed rebuilt atop drowned datacores as a candle-lit, low-tech city-state — a dramatic reversal from its neon-metropolis state under Concord. The Red Lattice Quarter becomes the era's defining location.

Magic/Law. The age of optics becomes an age of whispers — oaths are signed in blood, not ink. Without Concord's cameras, morality becomes personal; justice exists only between people who remember what proof felt like. This is the collapse of the Third Cyberpunk Age and the dawn of the Age of Disconnection.

The Durrel Line. No literal Durrel appears, but Lucen and Sorell's bond is explicitly framed as echoing the Durrel pattern — command and surrender tempered by consent, mercy born from control — not a literal recurrence of Taren and Zethyr, but the same underlying shape.

Institutions. The Pale Ring is shattered; Evermere's Council survives under Sorell's pragmatic rule, a republic held together by paper promises and quiet threats. Couriers, smugglers, and ex-agents form new guilds that replace Concord's machines with human networks. Lucen's Widow's Oath becomes a whispered covenant: justice in the spaces the law can't reach — reframing dominion-style control into street-level violence and mercy, grounding later magical crises in very human costs.
