Timeline A · Campaign XVII

The Widow's Oath

Age of Disconnection begins

8410–8415 ARV Primary location: Evermere, the Red Lattice Quarter

Opening World State

After Concord's grids fail, the Third Cyberpunk Age ends in silence, not fire. Cities once ruled by visible law fall dark; proof becomes parchment, and power reverts to hands and knives. Evermere, rebuilt atop the drowned datacores of Highever's coast, becomes the reluctant heart of this medieval twilight.

Party & Key Figures

  • Lucen Marrin
    Streetwise rogue and reluctant agent of the Gray Woman's order; infiltrates the Widow's Pale Ring and becomes entangled in Sorell Marcone's reforms and affection.
  • Sorell Marcone
    Evermere Council officer and Lucen's lover; a pragmatic leader who believes in law through mercy.
  • The Gray Woman
    Manipulative spymaster and head of the Courier Guild; contracts Lucen to dismantle the Pale Ring.
  • Mirelle Tress, "The Widow"
    Crime matron of the Pale Ring, disguising vice as charity; controls Evermere's underbelly through fear and donation.
  • The Council of Ash Veils
    Evermere's ruling oligarchy — half-righteous, half-corrupt; instrumental in both the Pale Ring's rise and its fall.

Act-by-Act

Infiltration

Lucen Marrin, drawn into the Pale Ring's inner circles by a Gray Woman contract, works a double game against the Widow's empire of contraband relics masked behind charitable ledgers — law by day, knives by night.

The Purge

Council records burn, allegiances flip, and Lucen's own moral code fractures between love and necessity. When the Gray Woman proposes sacrificing innocents to root out the Widow, Lucen turns executioner himself.

The Widow's Death

Lucen stages the Widow's death in prison and keeps the order secret, even from Sorell. What follows is aftermath, not triumph — Sorell pieces together the truth that the man he loves has become the city's unseen blade.

Final World State

The Pale Ring shatters; Evermere's Council survives under Sorell's pragmatic rule, a republic held together by paper promises and quiet threats. Lucen withdraws to the southern vineyards, though the Gray Woman's mark on his wrist still burns when law is broken. Law After Light: without Concord's cameras, morality becomes personal — justice exists only between people who remember what proof felt like.

Canon status: follows the character register; Lucen and Sorell's bond is explicitly framed as a Durrel-pattern echo (command and surrender tempered by consent), not a literal recurrence of Taren and Zethyr.
