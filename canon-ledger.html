Working canon

Canon Ledger

Amora's source documents disagree with themselves in places — deliberately, in most cases, since names and dates genuinely changed as the campaigns were played. This page records every settled call and every question still open, so the rest of the site doesn't have to explain itself every time. For a plain record of when things changed on the site itself — new campaigns, new pages, structural fixes — see Site Updates instead.

Confirmed canon

Directly stated or unambiguously fixed by the source material.

Confirmed canon

ARV runs continuously; "CE" means Cyberpunk Era

ARV is the single dating system across both timelines. "CE" is not Common Era — it labels the recurring cyberpunk-state swings in Amora's history (the world moves between medieval and futuristic states more than once, most notably the Third Cyberpunk Age and the Fourth Cyberpunk Era). See the Chronology page for the full count, including the specific ARV numbers assigned to fill placeholder dates like "82XX ARV."

Confirmed canon

Campaign XII cast

The concise Timeline A summary conflates Campaign XII with Arden and Caelen; the fuller chronology corrects this. This site follows the fuller version: Kael Durreth, Evren Thalos, Seraphine, Jalen/Joren, and Cassius are the Age of Inheritance cast. Arden and Caelen enter much later in Campaign XXII, The Shattered Quiet; the 1,000-year jump to 4CE occurs between XXII and XXIII rather than inside XII or XXII.

Confirmed canon

Late Timeline A source priority — Campaigns XXII–XXV

The dedicated late-Timeline-A records settle several points that older compact summaries compress or contradict. XXII takes place one year after Talen Marric's Severance in the post-network medieval drift; Arden and Caelen leave the Caelwick imprint there, and the roughly 1,000-year gap falls between XXII and XXIII. XXIII is the 4CE reinstantiation era, with the corrected final account of Spirefall — Caelen is deliberately assassinated by the Rainshelf Rebels with a single analogue round, not killed by conflicting grid protocols. XXIV destroys the specific fragment called the Presence; XXV later reveals that a separate Caelen-linked behavioural shard-core had survived inside the temporal fracture, so those two remnants are not the same object. Campaign XXV is itself the final Timeline A campaign: I–XXIV are the pre-fracture continuum it closes, while XXV is the hinge that carries the six travellers into Timeline B.

Arrival-location source conflict: the older XXV dossier pictures the six arriving at Silver Bastion's training grounds. Later played Timeline B material in Campaign B1 places their actual arrival in the Heart chamber during early rites, while Taren and Zethyr are still paladin candidates. Both agree on physical arrival at Silver Bastion during the pair's training years; B1 takes priority for the precise room.

Confirmed canon

Tempest Divide and Sundering Tempest are two separate events

Not a duplicate record of the same storm — two distinct geography-altering events, roughly 1,200 years apart (~975 ARV and ~2200 ARV).

Confirmed canon

Corrections from a source cross-check (August 2026)

A pass against the source material caught several errors, now fixed: Seraphine Vale's death during Campaign B5 (the site had incorrectly kept her alive with "nominal authority"); Campaign B2's date (was showing 1 ARV, confirmed 0 ARV); Brevair's genuinely original opening relationship with Highever (Brevair is already an isolationist, expansionist empire under Emperor Trainor Fell while Highever rallies opposition; the timelines diverge in what happens to that rivalry afterward); the order of Severance's first use (Zethyr enacts it in Campaign II; Edrin names and formalises it in Campaign III — the Lore page had this backwards); and Campaign I's opening line, which claimed "no Seals beyond the Heart" while the same page later describes stabilising four of them.

Confirmed canon

Lucien Varric's final status

Killed by Mike Rookvale in Campaign B14, Act IV — wounded through his ring hand and stripped of the conditioning working sustaining his hold before death. His Officer Chain and personal influence network are broken with him. He leaves behind one final working, built specifically to claim Mike even after his own death; it's discovered and broken by Jack and Caelen before it fully takes hold.

Confirmed canon

Campaign B14's title, dates, and full act structure

Confirmed and built out in full: The Bastion Reclaimed, Late c. 162 ARV, eight acts running from Mike's Velvet Gate infiltration through Silver Bastion's reclamation to the Ridge household's acceptance of him and Caelen and Corvin's wedding. Timeline B continues into Campaign B15.

Confirmed canon

Campaign B15 — "The Crownless War": locked canon

Confirmed and built out in full: Late c. 162 ARV, closing both Taren's own B9–B15 arc and the Rhalen–Brevair conflict introduced in B10. This completed Timeline B's full roster at the B15 close, B1 through B15 — Timeline B has since continued through B19. The following points are locked:

Taren survives and reaches Paladin Level 9. Rhalen is physically dead — no copy, successor-body, or hidden continuation of him exists. The Keystone is permanently destroyed. Delryn is alive and completely severed from it; his victimisation does not erase his own complicity, and he does not automatically become Brevair's ruler. Taren deliberately withheld the full purpose of the final severance from Delryn; Cassien knew the full intent and objected before agreeing to help. Brevair remains sovereign and geographically intact — Durrel Ridge, Silver Bastion, and The Lantern do not annex it. Jack is Taren's intended, consensual successor, not an automatic heir by blood; Taren remains Warden of the Ridge and has not abdicated. Jorah is part of any future Jack accepts. Silver Bastion is the agreed protected destination for Delryn, Garran Vey, and the compelled Brevair survivors. The campaign ends at home, without a hidden new attacker or an immediate romance hook.

Confirmed canon

Deep History added: the Oath Wars, the War of Ash and Glass, and the Badlands' true origin

A dedicated source document on pre-campaign history was added to Lore in full: the Aureth/Velis traditions associated with the Oath Wars, the separate War of Ash and Glass between sorcerous bloodlines and warlock covenants, and the Shattering that collided them — which is now the established origin of the Badlands, replacing the old setting's vaguer "mages' experiment" rumour (that rumour still stands as the in-world folk explanation; the Shattering is the buried truth beneath it).

Unresolved: whether the Aureth/Velis separation itself literally triggered the Oath Wars, represents a later simplification of a more complex fracture, or was one strand among several is explicitly not settled canon — see Lore and Campaign B18, which shows the war's ground-level factions (Crown, Sanctuary, Liberty, and the traditions of the Seven Refusal) as distinct positions rather than simple proxies for one legacy or the other.

Continuity lock: the Oath of the Veil that Taren creates when he turns the Heartwardens into the Veilwardens in Campaign II is not the same thing as Velis's ancient authoritarian tradition, even though they rhyme thematically. Taren may have echoed or rediscovered older ideas, but any Oath of the Veil zealots found among later Oath Remnants are later arrivals to the pattern, not survivors of the original war.

Naming fix: an older Campaign IV-era summary's reference to a "Badlands of Menge" is a different, later-blighted part of Menge — not the primary Badlands, which predate Taren entirely. That northern scar doesn't yet have its own settled name on this site.

Confirmed canon

Mirror Bay, not Evermere Bay

Previously listed as a site choice between two equally-attested names. That was wrong — the maps themselves label it Mirror Bay directly, so there was never actually a naming decision to make. Reverted on Relics; nowhere else on the site had picked up the Evermere Bay variant.

Confirmed canon

The Great Seals and Warden Lineages, restored to prominence

Superseded in part by Campaign B17's fuller definition below — kept here for the record rather than deleted. This site previously used "Binder" loosely and let "Icebinder" function as the neutral umbrella name for the whole Frost lineage, with Timeline B's Order of Anchors treated as having fully exonerated them. A dedicated canon-recovery document restores the fuller structure: every Great Seal has its own Warden lineage, and "Binder" names inherited affinity while "Warden" names the fuller custodial duty and institution. Locked canon: the Icebinders are specifically the fallen branch of the Icewardens, not a neutral name for the whole Frost lineage — a distinction this site now keeps consistently. The Order of Anchors' Campaign B2 conclusion that the Icebinders were "never a cult" is now presented as their own conclusion, and as a documented overcorrection built on real but incomplete evidence, rather than settled narrative fact. Corrected across Magic Codex (a full rewrite of the Seals section using a four-part Seal/Warden-lineage/Warden-order/fallen-form model), Factions (new Icewardens and Icebinders entries, plus a Seal Wardens umbrella), Atlas, and Campaigns B2 and B3. "Binder" here named inherited affinity in general terms; Campaign B17 (below) later gives it a precise, technical definition — a practitioner/custodian of a pre-Seal House tradition capable of binding and measuring pressure — that refines rather than replaces this entry's Warden-lineage structure.

Confirmed canon

The eight-Seal registry: Storm, Life, Shadow, and Light replace Aether, Veil, and Balance

Campaign B16 introduces a large relief apparatus surveying exactly eight Great Seals, and a Warden statue confirming the current working registry as Heart, Flame, Stone, Storm, Life, Shadow, Light, and Frost — each with its own Warden lineage. This explicitly supersedes an earlier working list this site had adopted, which included Aether/Air, Veil, and Balance in place of Life, Shadow, and Light. Rather than treat the older names as a ninth and tenth Seal, they are retired from the active registry; the older material describing them (Zethyr's Balance association, the Veil lineage's fusion with Heart under the Dominion, Aether blessing Taren and Zethyr's union) is not erased from the historical record, but no longer presented as part of the current eight. Corrected across the Magic Codex. The apparatus's own missing central component is explicitly not evidence of a ninth Seal; a separate, unidentified figure — "bearer of no Seal, keeper of the roads between them" — remains an open thread, not attached to any existing office.

Confirmed canon

Heart Seal location — resolved conflict, not an open question

Previously carried in this ledger's Open section as unresolved wording. It no longer is. Older baseline material places a "Heart Seal" in the Durrel family crypt. Repeated later material — the Campaign I/B1 record placing the fixed Seal beneath Silver Bastion, and now Campaign B16 explicitly, when Nick Soren's formal visit confirms the Heart Seal was never transferred to Durrel Ridge, even after Silver Bastion's own fall — settles the question under the site's source-priority rules. Durrel Ridge contains the Heart Ward, Heart Vault, and Heart-linked custodial structures; it does not contain the Great Seal itself. The older crypt sentence is retained as contradictory legacy wording, not used to invent a relocation event that was never played. See the Magic Codex's Heart terminology box for the full Seal/Ward/Vault/Medallion distinction.

Confirmed canon

Campaign B16 — additional locked canon

Beyond the Seal registry and Heart Seal confirmation recorded separately above, the following are locked from Campaign B16: Blood versus recognition — Western Icewarden blood only preserves the possibility of the Custodian office; recognition, not bloodline proximity, determines who actually holds it. Custodian and Way-hand are confirmed as a specific paired Western office, not a template to assume onto other Seal lineages. Maraine Soren's non-presentation was a deliberate, legitimate choice on her part, not a failure to inherit — the same principle Elara's own recorded warnings insist on. Vale is dead, killed by Nick after capture and interrogation; his black-gold connection withdrew downward through the quarry floor rather than transferring to Nick or Jarek on his death. "First Road" is an unverified term — it originates with Vale specifically, has no independent corroboration, and Elara herself treated it as suspect; it should not be repeated as an attested name for the buried complex. Rimeholt's Western infrastructure did not build the deeper complex — the Western Icewardens encountered an already-old structure and variously monitored, reinforced, restricted, used, or simply watched parts of it; construction style inside the outermost chamber is visibly different from later Western work. The Rimeholt transit mechanism is disabled, not destroyed — its local transport function was deliberately rendered inoperable while the archaeological chamber itself was preserved for study. Black-gold's wider nature and origin remain genuinely unresolved — only its Rimeholt access is confirmed severed, not the phenomenon itself.

Confirmed canon

Campaign B17 — locked canon

Campaign B17 establishes an unusually large set of hard locks for future Amora writing; the following hold unless a later campaign explicitly changes them. The dated ancient sequence — all eight Great Seals were successfully set before the Shattering; Heart was set last, between the other seven, to measure their relationship rather than command them; Binder traditions predate the Great Seals and could perform local, temporary binding before permanent Setting; Warden watches arise specifically to keep what the Binders set. Tarenus Durrel was already a Heartbinder before accepting the first Heart watch, and is a Devotion paladin securely dated to the Seal-setting/War of Ash and Glass era — his exact Oath Wars combat role remains unproven and should not be invented. The Shattering occurs after the Seal setting and doesn't become a Seal-caused event without new evidence; Heart, Frost, Flame, and Stone answer in the immediate post-Shattering report, while Storm, Life, Light, and Shadow's status is incomplete, not failed. The Western Icewardens are confirmed as a deliberate post-Shattering preservation branch keeping a second Frost measure — not exiled, not abandoning Frost, carrying no Seal portion, and holding no northern Seal authority; northern and Western legitimate Frost custodians share an older kinship, though the exact Nick/Caen genealogy remains unproven and shouldn't be assumed. Blood creates possibility, not automatic office — recognition and consent remain distinct from ancestry throughout. Jarek's conditioning of Nick is completely removed in B17 with no residue; Nick remains unaware it ever existed, and his love and consensual private preferences remain independently genuine both before and after. Nick's golden snowflake tattoo is nonmagical. The recurring eightfold centre is still not a ninth Seal and is still not identified as a Way-hand or any other known office. Glasshound cold-suppression/heat-acceleration field rules are established; their origin remains unknown. Black-gold remains unidentified and must not be retroactively made the cause of the Great Seals, the Shattering, or the War of Ash and Glass.

Confirmed canon

Campaign B18 — locked canon

Campaign B18 is a deep-history prequel, c. -938 ARV, and establishes its own set of hard locks. Historical boundary — B18's characters cannot know any future history: the War of Ash and Glass hasn't begun (roughly six years ahead), the Seven Refusal is roughly nineteen years ahead, the Great Seals don't exist (their setting cycle is roughly twenty-five years ahead), Rimeholt doesn't exist, and the Badlands don't exist — the Alderwick Basin is fertile, densely settled farmland. Nothing in B18 should be written as foreshadowing these events with in-world certainty. Edmund Shaw genuinely approved the Barrowfen pressure campaign — this is not retroactively excused by Alric Dane's later disobedience. Shaw explicitly did not order the Westfield refugee wagon arson, ordered Calder Venn to withdraw the instant the crossed-circle mark appeared, and was unwilling to sanction seizing the Founding Charter without examining its provenance — Dane countermanded him directly on the last point. Shaw did not know Hugh Bellamy's name until days before the campaign's climax. Shaw's own response to Dane's exposure is deliberately unresolved and must not be settled by a later campaign without new play. The First Authority is confirmed as ancient civic emergency infrastructure for waters, crossings, and common works, not a throne and not a Great Seal system — see the architecture lock in the Magic Codex: it must not be merged with the crossed-circle reserve's builders, the later Rimeholt transit network, or B17's eight-around-centre apparatus without direct new evidence. The crossed-circle reserve's builders, exact function, and later fate remain unresolved. Adam Kersey's espionage and his private consensual dynamic with Hugh are locked as entirely separate — the prior secrecy must never be read backward as evidence the intimacy itself was coercive, in either direction. Both Hugh and Adam end B18 as Level 3 Oath of the Crown paladins. Ancient geography: at c. -938 ARV, the landmass later known as Brevair remains connected to the principal Vanmere mainland by a broad, inhabited land bridge through Alderwick; separation occurs during the Shattering (c. -905 ARV), which severs the connection and begins Brevair's later geographic isolation. See the Atlas and Lore.

Confirmed canon

Nareth Isle and Belisar — confirmed geography beyond Vanmere

Campaign B16 establishes the first confirmed inhabited geography beyond Vanmere's own shores: Nareth Isle, a large island roughly two to three days' sail west-southwest of Vanmere's western coast, and its harbour city Belisar on the island's sheltered southeastern coast. The island's absence from earlier Vanmere maps reflects those maps' own coastal focus, not a late discovery — sailors could always reach it. No larger Nareth political system (kingdom, republic, or otherwise) has been established, and none should be invented until future play requires it. Corrected across Atlas and Lore, which previously and incorrectly stated nothing beyond Vanmere's shores was established.

Confirmed canon

Campaign IX restores Jax (Reconstructed) as Rhett Vale's co-discoverer

The live site previously named only Rhett plus generic background groups. Multiple project documents independently identify a second principal figure: Jax, a reconstructed digital Veilbearer consciousness surviving from the Choir age, confirmed as Rhett's co-discoverer and paired principal figure rather than a background assistant. Not the same person as Jax Korran, Campaign V's human PC — the name is shared, nothing else is. Added to Campaign IX. A Heartbearer/Veilbearer archetype framing pairing Rhett and Jax appears in some sources; this site treats it as interpretive continuity, not literal reincarnation of Zethyr and Taren. Confirmed canon

The Vault of Tarenus (Campaign III) is beneath Durrel Ridge, not Greymount

This site had placed Edrin Durrel's sealing of the Vault of Tarenus beneath the ruins of Greymount, on the assumption that a vault called "of Tarenus" belonged at the city that later takes his name. The source material says otherwise, explicitly: "Edrin sealed the Vault and the last shard of the Heart Medallion beneath Durrel Ridge." Corrected across the site — Campaign III, Durrel Ridge, Greymount, and Relics & Artefacts now all agree. This also confirms Timeline A's Durrel Ridge held its ancestral Vault as early as Campaigns I–II, well before Campaign III's sealing — a detail the compiled summary documents don't cover directly, confirmed instead by the creator's own account of the campaign.

A related open question: Campaigns XIX–XX describe a "Vault of Tarenus" stirring and reopening at Tarenus Reclaimed, the later name for Greymount. Whether this is the same structure somehow relocated or rebuilt across roughly nine thousand years, an unrelated vault that happens to share the name, or a drift between source documents written at different times is genuinely unresolved. This site presents both without forcing a resolution — see Greymount and Relics & Artefacts.

Confirmed canon

The Ring of Amora is a place, not a wearable object

A relic-continuity table in one source document labels Campaign IX's defining relic "Rhett Vale's Ring" — a label that reads as a wearable object someone owns. The fuller Campaign IX material is unambiguous that this is the Ring of Amora, a reality-altering technological site Rhett Vale and Jax rediscover, not a personal artefact. The table's label appears to be an accidental shortening of the place name. Corrected across the site: "Rhett Vale's Ring" removed entirely; the Ring of Amora given its own proper Atlas entry and Relics & Artefacts classification as a relic-site.

Confirmed canon

The Storm Seal's location is corrected: east of ruined Alderwick, not Outpost Nine

Prior to Campaign B19, Outpost Nine was the site's best available map-keyed guess for the Storm/Tempest Seal, inferred alongside Vyrn's Spring and Hollowrest Citadel rather than confirmed. B19 locates the Storm Great Seal directly, through firsthand contact: its true manifestation zone lies in the Badlands east of ruined Alderwick. Outpost Nine remains a real hard-weather post associated with the northern storm tradition — it simply was never the Seal's location. Corrected across Atlas and Magic Codex; this is the only Storm-related change made — B16 and B17's own eight-Seal registry and Storm's confirmed successful setting are unaffected.

Confirmed canon

The Storm Great Seal is not the Western Pressure Station, the focus, or the Primary Measure

Campaign B19 draws a firm line between three separate things that share the same general subject. The Storm Great Seal is magic given stable form — a vast, continuously reforming ring of lightning, rain, wind, and pressure, with no stone frame, metal casing, or mechanical apparatus. The Western Pressure Station beneath Marker Sixteen, its removable eight-inch focus, and its lower Primary Measure distribution chamber are ancient storm-management infrastructure — a regulator, not the Seal itself, and not a template for a continent-wide repair-node quest. See Magic Codex and Relics & Artefacts.

Confirmed canon

B18 and B19 Alderwick are two layers of the same place, not one

Campaign B18's living, c. -938 ARV Alderwick — a fertile basin city of eighty to a hundred thousand people — was already published canon before B19 began; B19 does not retroactively make it unpublished or newly discovered. B19 physically rediscovers the same ground roughly 1,281 years later as a vast ruin inside the Badlands — Nine Arches and the southern reservoir survive in shattered form, and an Eastern Ward civic case and the Processional Way lead to the Hall of the Open Sky. B19 makes only a partial modern survey: most of B18's named districts (Crown Hall, the Deep Market, the First Authority reserve sites among them) are not re-located on the ground and should not be treated as confirmed modern ruin coordinates until a future expedition reaches them. Both layers are preserved side by side on the Atlas rather than one overwriting the other.

Confirmed canon

The post-Shattering "report incomplete" note is a failed transmission, not proof Storm failed

Campaign B16 and B17's archival record left Storm's status after the Shattering marked incomplete, distinct from Frost, Flame, and Stone's confirmed post-Shattering responses. B19 resolves what that gap meant: Storm's own preserved memory shows a badly injured Storm Warden crawling into contact and attempting to report in the immediate aftermath, her transmission rupturing repeatedly before completing. The report was incomplete because the communication failed — not because the Seal failed, was destroyed, or went silent. Storm continued holding throughout.

Confirmed canon

The Badlands' storm regime is permanently calmer after B19 — not healed, not safe

A major pressure release during Campaign B19 substantially breaks the Badlands' centuries-old, self-sustaining extreme magical storm regime — a permanent world-state change, not a temporary clear-weather scene. This is a bounded change: the Shattering's physical damage remains exactly as catastrophic as before (glassed terrain, fused valleys, shattered cities, altered creatures, and dangerous ordinary weather all persist), the fertile pre-Shattering Alderwick Basin is not restored, and large newly visible areas are not thereby certified safe. See the Atlas for the corrected map treatment.

Confirmed canon

Ben's B19 "not a Warden" status was accurate then, not permanent — corrected by B20

At the close of B19, Ben had contacted the Storm Great Seal but had not been recognised as its Warden — the site's existing B19-era wording (Characters, Magic Codex, and the B19 page itself all state plainly that Ben "is not a Storm Warden" and returns to Greymount "not a Warden") is correct as a description of that moment and is not being rewritten or deleted here. B20 moves that state forward, not backward: only when Ben returns weeks later, after the crisis has passed, and brings his companions into the Seal's corridor does it recognise him directly and specifically as its current Storm Warden. Do not backdate Warden recognition into B19 — the B19-era pages describe an earlier true state that B20 has since superseded, exactly the way this ledger has handled other superseded interpretations elsewhere (see the Great Seals/Warden Lineages entry above).

Confirmed canon

Warden office is not proof of Warden lineage (B20)

B20's Storm Great Seal recognition of Ben is direct Great Seal choice, made on demonstrated action and custody — finding Storm under strain, understanding rather than breaking it, relieving pressure safely, and returning without an emergency forcing the contact. It is explicitly not proof of hereditary bloodline. The ancient Storm Warden lineage remains a wholly separate, unresolved genealogical question — it may survive intact, be fragmented, or be extinct — and Ben's parents remain ordinary Farbound delvers, not retroactive ancestors. Office and lineage can now diverge in the modern era: holding the Storm Warden office does not by itself say anything about who, if anyone, still carries the original line.

Confirmed canon

The eight-around-empty-centre geometry is now witnessed, not just inferred — still not a ninth Seal

B16 and B17 (both above) established the recurring eightfold pattern from documentary and architectural evidence only. B20 is the first time it is directly witnessed in the field: inside Storm's own contact, with Nick and Nora present, eight equal positions appear around an empty centre — Storm active, Frost and Heart answering through them, five other positions present but quiet. This upgrades the pattern's evidentiary status; it does not resolve it. The centre remains exactly as unresolved as before and must not be treated as a ninth Great Seal, a Way-hand office, or a throne, and Nick's Frost response and Nora's Heart response do not make either of them a new Great Seal Warden.

Confirmed canon

The Inner Way's black star-pool is a real threshold — exactly three routes verified

B20 confirms the Hall of the Open Sky's black star-pool as an active physical magical threshold, not a documentary curiosity. Only three traversals are verified: Hall to an isolated ridge waystation, ridge waystation to an eastern Badlands pool, and eastern pool back to the Hall. This is a confirmed three-leg circuit, not evidence of a complete or unlimited transit network — the Inner Way's builders, its full route map, whether all legs are bidirectional, how many other pools exist, and its relationship to other ancient networks all remain unresolved. Do not extend it into a fast-travel system beyond these three legs without further play.

Confirmed canon

No "Blackglass" — a discarded out-of-character term, not a faction

No "Blackglass" faction or organisation exists anywhere in Amora canon. The name surfaced as a discarded out-of-character suggestion during play around B20's Deep Market incident and was never adopted in-world. The masked scavenger Ilan Crewe, encountered near Deep Market, is an independent individual with no organisation behind him — not a Blackglass agent, and not evidence one exists. This entry exists specifically so the discarded term is never mistaken for established canon later.

Open

Deep Market's small clawed scavengers are unidentified — not kobolds

The small clawed creatures B20's party encounters scavenging the outer Deep Market ruin are deliberately left an unidentified species. Kobolds do not exist anywhere in Amora canon and must never be used to name, identify, or describe these or any other creature on this site. Their taxonomy is an open thread, not a placeholder waiting to be filled with a stock D&D species.

Confirmed canon

Timeline A's later Tempest Divide will not occur in Timeline B

A narrow, specific cross-timeline lock, not a general claim about how far Timeline A and Timeline B now diverge. Timeline A's later Tempest Divide (c. 975 ARV; see the Tempest Divide/Sundering Tempest entry above) is tied to accumulated Storm Great Seal burden interacting with Auren Delyth's Outpost Nine experiment. Because B19 substantially relieved and stabilised the Storm Great Seal, that specific catastrophe will not occur in Timeline B. This does not heal the Badlands, erase the Shattering's damage, or imply that any other later Timeline A event is thereby prevented — it applies only to the established Tempest Divide itself.

Confirmed canon

Campaigns I–II dates corrected to the played chronology

The surviving campaign-era chronology describes Campaign I as seven months and Campaign II as beginning in the eighth month, with the Dominion arc unfolding over roughly the following two years — not the many-decades gap the site previously implied. An older summary had normalised those campaigns to 0–27 ARV and 28–55 ARV; site tiles, tables, and reference pages now display the corrected play-accurate dates (0 ARV for Campaign I; 0–2 ARV for Campaign II) throughout.

Corvane is a name this site invented, not one found in any source document — chosen to tie the capital to the Corvett family who feature heavily across the Brevair-adjacent B8–B13 campaigns. (Timeline A's version of the capital, renamed under the Dominion in Campaign II, is called Durrelion — a separate, source-attested name for a separate history's version of the same city.)

Editorial reconstruction

Any "Dur-" name is a Durrel derivative

Names like Durreth (Age of Inheritance) and Durren (Age of Reawakening) are treated as the Durrel line's name drifting across the centuries between campaigns, rather than coincidental or unrelated names. This is an inference applied consistently, not a source-stated rule — any close variant (Dur-, Durr-) found in the source material is respelled to keep the double-R core (e.g. Duren → Durren).

Editorial reconstruction

Four Cyberpunk Eras, not two

Only the Third and Fourth were named directly in the source. Reconstructed from two textual clues — Campaign XII's note that the world rebuilds "after machine/cyber ages" (plural), and Campaign XV's reference to "the Cataclysm" that precedes it — a First (Campaigns IX–XI) and Second (Campaigns XIII–XIV) era are now labelled explicitly. Campaign XII's old "Pre-4CE Pivot" tag is dropped in favor of "Post-First-Cyberpunk Medieval Interlude" — the old label was a symptom of the same conflation error that misplaced Arden and Caelen in that campaign. Confirmed canon

The Thirteenth Seal — object confirmed, meaning unresolved

The dedicated final Campaign XI record confirms that, after the Null Relic is unmade beneath Veylaris Rest, the party physically recovers a half-melted object identified as the Thirteenth Seal. That supersedes this site's earlier assumption that the name survived only in a later relic-continuity table. What it does not confirm is a thirteenth ancient Great Seal: the recovered B16/B17 registry remains exactly eight — Heart, Flame, Stone, Storm, Life, Shadow, Light, and Frost. The XI artefact's creator, function, age, and reason for the name remain unresolved. It is therefore an attested Campaign XI relic with disputed interpretation, not an addition to the ancient Seal system. See Campaign XI and Relics & Artefacts.

Sirian's private motive (Campaign VIII)

The live account presents Sirian as a reformist legalist whose Gate Codex gets weaponised despite his intent. Other sources characterise Sirian less charitably — closer to a lawful inquisitor consolidating surveillance through faith than a genuine reformer. Both readings fit what the Codex actually does once written. The Architect and the disputed final blow (Campaign X)

All high-level sources agree the final Veil Shard is destroyed in Campaign X; they disagree on the exact hand that strikes it. The live account follows Serik Thorne's campaign leadership without asserting he personally struck it (now noted explicitly on Campaign X). A separate, older continuum table introduces the Architect — a provisional final synthetic Taren-echo said to test Serik's refusal. The reconstructed Campaign X page now includes that figure with the provenance visible: an editorial/archival reconstruction, not confirmed original-play cast. A further "Burning Fall" account (Sirian and Alaric opening a remnant key, Lior and Jalen exposing an emperor through records, Sevrin Mareth renouncing Elian, Lior breaking the shard beneath the Collegium) survives in one older summary document but conflicts with the accepted Serik-led campaign frame and is not silently merged into it. The exact physical hand that destroys the final Veil Shard remains unresolved.

Campaign VIII — the PC's actual identity is not safely confirmed

This site currently presents Sirian as Campaign VIII's player character. That should be treated as unconfirmed, not settled. Later reconstruction material identifies Sirian as the Veilbearer/Taren-side successor figure in the broader lineage — a role incompatible with the PC always occupying the Zethyr/Heart-side position across the rest of Timeline A. If that reading holds, VIII's actual PC is a distinct, currently unnamed figure, and Sirian belongs in the antagonist role instead. This needs recovery from the original campaign chat rather than a guess — the site is not correcting this without that source, and flags it here rather than letting an uncertain reconstruction quietly become canon.

What is confirmed: the Choir Reconstruction figure recorded as Taren is explicitly a manufactured reconstruction, not the historical man, and Jax occupies the human counterpart position opposite him. Campaigns VI and VII — no antagonist reconstructed, deliberately

Neither campaign has a securely named antagonist on this site, and that's an intentional gap, not an oversight to fix on sight. Campaign VI's surviving record doesn't preserve a clearly named Taren-side counterpart to Kael Duren — only "rival factions" wanting the Heart Shard for power rather than restoration. Campaign VII similarly preserves its institutional story (a hardening Warden theocracy) better than its individual opposing cast. Both are left without an invented villain rather than have one assigned to fill the slot.

Campaign B19 does not solve every Storm/Warden question

B19 locates the Storm Great Seal, confirms it survived the Shattering, and eases a major share of its modern burden — but several questions it deliberately leaves open remain open, not quietly resolved. The exact initiating cause of Storm's comparatively recent increase in strain before B19 is unidentified; Grey House's focus removal added burden but did not create the underlying problem. The modern status, survival, and organisation of any Storm Warden lineage after the injured post-Shattering Warden seen in memory is unknown — Benjamin Strake is a Storm Walker, not that lineage, and B19 does not establish whether one still exists. The exact geographic relationship between the Hall of the Open Sky and the deeper Storm contact zone east of Alderwick, and between the Hall and the separate Shelter of the Open Sky, are both explicitly unresolved. None of these should be treated as answered simply because B19 answered the questions it did answer.
