Working canon

Canon Ledger

Amora's source documents disagree with themselves in places — deliberately, in most cases, since names and dates genuinely changed as the campaigns were played. This page records every settled call and every question still open, so the rest of the site doesn't have to explain itself every time.

Confirmed canon

Directly stated or unambiguously fixed by the source material.

Confirmed canon

ARV runs continuously; "CE" means Cyberpunk Era

ARV is the single dating system across both timelines. "CE" is not Common Era — it labels the recurring cyberpunk-state swings in Amora's history (the world moves between medieval and futuristic states more than once, most notably the Third Cyberpunk Age and the Fourth Cyberpunk Era). See the Chronology page for the full count, including the specific ARV numbers assigned to fill placeholder dates like "82XX ARV."

Confirmed canon

Campaign XII cast

The concise Timeline A summary conflates Campaign XII with Arden and Caelen; the fuller chronology corrects this. This site follows the fuller version: Kael Durreth, Evren Thalos, Seraphine, Jalen/Joren, and Cassius are the Age of Inheritance cast. Arden and Caelen enter later, in the Shattered Quiet/4CE arc (Campaigns XXII onward).

Confirmed canon

Tempest Divide and Sundering Tempest are two separate events

Not a duplicate record of the same storm — two distinct geography-altering events, roughly 1,200 years apart (~975 ARV and ~2200 ARV).

Confirmed canon

Corrections from a source cross-check (August 2026)

A pass against the source material caught several errors, now fixed: Seraphine Vale's death during Campaign B5 (the site had incorrectly kept her alive with "nominal authority"); Campaign B2's date (was showing 1 ARV, confirmed 0 ARV); Brevair's genuinely different starting relationship with Highever in each timeline (Timeline A: diplomatic, becomes a crown ally; Timeline B: isolationist empire under Emperor Trainor Fell, not an ally); the order of Severance's first use (Zethyr enacts it in Campaign II; Edrin names and formalises it in Campaign III — the Lore page had this backwards); and Campaign I's opening line, which claimed "no Seals beyond the Heart" while the same page later describes stabilising four of them.

Confirmed canon

Lucien Varric's final status

Killed by Mike Rookvale in Campaign B14, Act IV — wounded through his ring hand and stripped of the conditioning working sustaining his hold before death. His Officer Chain and personal influence network are broken with him. He leaves behind one final working, built specifically to claim Mike even after his own death; it's discovered and broken by Jack and Caelen before it fully takes hold.

Confirmed canon

Campaign B14's title, dates, and full act structure

Confirmed and built out in full: The Bastion Reclaimed, Late c. 162 ARV, eight acts running from Mike's Velvet Gate infiltration through Silver Bastion's reclamation to the Ridge household's acceptance of him and Caelen and Corvin's wedding. Timeline B continues into Campaign B15.

Confirmed canon

Campaign B15 — "The Crownless War": locked canon

Confirmed and built out in full: Late c. 162 ARV, closing both Taren's own B9–B15 arc and the Rhalen–Brevair conflict introduced in B10. This completes Timeline B's full current roster, B1 through B15. The following points are locked:

Taren survives and reaches Paladin Level 9. Rhalen is physically dead — no copy, successor-body, or hidden continuation of him exists. The Keystone is permanently destroyed. Delryn is alive and completely severed from it; his victimisation does not erase his own complicity, and he does not automatically become Brevair's ruler. Taren deliberately withheld the full purpose of the final severance from Delryn; Cassien knew the full intent and objected before agreeing to help. Brevair remains sovereign and geographically intact — Durrel Ridge, Silver Bastion, and The Lantern do not annex it. Jack is Taren's intended, consensual successor, not an automatic heir by blood; Taren remains Warden of the Ridge and has not abdicated. Jorah is part of any future Jack accepts. Silver Bastion is the agreed protected destination for Delryn, Garran Vey, and the compelled Brevair survivors. The campaign ends at home, without a hidden new attacker or an immediate romance hook.

Confirmed canon

Deep History added: the Oath Wars, the War of Ash and Glass, and the Badlands' true origin

A dedicated source document on pre-campaign history was added to Lore in full: the Aureth/Velis schism and the Oath Wars it caused, the separate War of Ash and Glass between sorcerous bloodlines and warlock covenants, and the Shattering that collided them — which is now the established origin of the Badlands, replacing the old setting's vaguer "mages' experiment" rumour (that rumour still stands as the in-world folk explanation; the Shattering is the buried truth beneath it).

Continuity lock: the Oath of the Veil that Taren creates when he turns the Heartwardens into the Veilwardens in Campaign II is not the same thing as Velis's ancient authoritarian tradition, even though they rhyme thematically. Taren may have echoed or rediscovered older ideas, but any Oath of the Veil zealots found among later Oath Remnants are later arrivals to the pattern, not survivors of the original war.

Naming fix: an older Campaign IV-era summary's reference to a "Badlands of Menge" is a different, later-blighted part of Menge — not the primary Badlands, which predate Taren entirely. That northern scar doesn't yet have its own settled name on this site.

Confirmed canon

Mirror Bay, not Evermere Bay

Previously listed as a site choice between two equally-attested names. That was wrong — the maps themselves label it Mirror Bay directly, so there was never actually a naming decision to make. Reverted on Relics; nowhere else on the site had picked up the Evermere Bay variant.

Confirmed canon

The Great Seals and Warden Lineages, restored to prominence

This site previously used "Binder" loosely and let "Icebinder" function as the neutral umbrella name for the whole Frost lineage, with Timeline B's Order of Anchors treated as having fully exonerated them. A dedicated canon-recovery document restores the fuller structure: every Great Seal has its own Warden lineage, and "Binder" names inherited affinity while "Warden" names the fuller custodial duty and institution. Locked canon: the Icebinders are specifically the fallen branch of the Icewardens, not a neutral name for the whole Frost lineage — a distinction this site now keeps consistently. The Order of Anchors' Campaign B2 conclusion that the Icebinders were "never a cult" is now presented as their own conclusion, and as a documented overcorrection built on real but incomplete evidence, rather than settled narrative fact. Corrected across Magic Codex (a full rewrite of the Seals section using a four-part Seal/Warden-lineage/Warden-order/ fallen-form model, plus two previously-missing Seals — Veil and Balance), Factions (new Icewardens and Icebinders entries, plus a Seal Wardens umbrella), Atlas, and Campaigns B2 and B3.

Confirmed canon

The Sundering Tempest was not caused by Campaign V's Choir collapse

This site previously claimed the Choir's collapse in Campaign V "contributes directly" to the Sundering Tempest. The site's own dating makes that impossible: the Sundering is dated to c. 2200 ARV, before Campaign V's c. 2500 ARV. Corrected on Campaign V and Chronology (which also moves the Sundering's row to its correct chronological position, before Campaign V rather than after it) — both events are real and both are now presented without a causal claim neither can support.

Confirmed canon

Cael, not Cael Veyne

Campaign III's paladin-echo had no recorded surname in the source material — "Veyne" was carried over in error, likely by pattern-matching to other Veyne-surnamed figures elsewhere in the setting. A project-chat correction confirms he was known simply as Cael. Fixed on Campaign III and Characters (the anchor ID there is unchanged to preserve existing links; only the displayed name changed).

Confirmed canon

Arienna Lume, not Arienna Voss

Previously listed here as a site-invented choice between two unattested names. A project-chat correction specifically identifies Lume as the name for Campaign I's Flamewarden — this wasn't an arbitrary pick between equals after all. Corrected across the site (Campaigns I–II, Characters, Atlas, Factions, Magic Codex); Voss is retained everywhere as the recorded variant.

Confirmed canon

Campaign IX restores Jax (Reconstructed) as Rhett Vale's co-discoverer

The live site previously named only Rhett plus generic background groups. Multiple project documents independently identify a second principal figure: Jax, a reconstructed digital Veilbearer consciousness surviving from the Choir age, confirmed as Rhett's co-discoverer and paired principal figure rather than a background assistant. Not the same person as Jax Korran, Campaign V's human PC — the name is shared, nothing else is. Added to Campaign IX. A Heartbearer/Veilbearer archetype framing pairing Rhett and Jax appears in some sources; this site treats it as interpretive continuity, not literal reincarnation of Zethyr and Taren. No romance between Rhett and Jax, no expedition crew names or fates, and no specific final fate for either character are established in the recovered record — none are invented here.

Confirmed canon

The Vault of Tarenus (Campaign III) is beneath Durrel Ridge, not Greymount

This site had placed Edrin Durrel's sealing of the Vault of Tarenus beneath the ruins of Greymount, on the assumption that a vault called "of Tarenus" belonged at the city that later takes his name. The source material says otherwise, explicitly: "Edrin sealed the Vault and the last shard of the Heart Medallion beneath Durrel Ridge." Corrected across the site — Campaign III, Durrel Ridge, Greymount, and Relics & Artefacts now all agree. This also confirms Timeline A's Durrel Ridge held its ancestral Vault as early as Campaigns I–II, well before Campaign III's sealing — a detail the compiled summary documents don't cover directly, confirmed instead by the creator's own account of the campaign.

A related open question: Campaigns XIX–XX describe a "Vault of Tarenus" stirring and reopening at Tarenus Reclaimed, the later name for Greymount. Whether this is the same structure somehow relocated or rebuilt across roughly nine thousand years, an unrelated vault that happens to share the name, or a drift between source documents written at different times is genuinely unresolved. This site presents both without forcing a resolution — see Greymount and Relics & Artefacts.

Confirmed canon

The Ring of Amora is a place, not a wearable object

A relic-continuity table in one source document labels Campaign IX's defining relic "Rhett Vale's Ring" — a label that reads as a wearable object someone owns. The fuller Campaign IX material is unambiguous that this is the Ring of Amora, a reality-altering technological site Rhett Vale and Jax rediscover, not a personal artefact. The table's label appears to be an accidental shortening of the place name. Corrected across the site: "Rhett Vale's Ring" removed entirely; the Ring of Amora given its own proper Atlas entry and Relics & Artefacts classification as a relic-site.

Normalised terminology

Both variants exist in the source; the site picked one and uses it consistently.

Normalised terminology

Durrel, not Durell

Both spellings occur throughout the source material; Durrel is the majority usage (roughly 6:1) and is used everywhere on this site, including the several Timeline B passages that use Durell.

Normalised terminology

Taren Durrel, Kellan / Caen Vance, Lio Serrin / Vareth

Kellan/Caen and Lio/Vareth are same-person renamings the source Chronicle already flags. This site uses Taren Durrel exclusively (dropping the "Taren Veyne" variant found in some source passages), Caen (from his introduction onward), and Vareth (from Campaign B5 onward), noting the earlier name at first mention on a character's own page.

Normalised terminology

Kael Durreth

Spelled Durreth, not Durrel or Dureth — the Age of Inheritance sits far enough down the Durrel line that the name has drifted through the intervening centuries. Treated as the same lineage as the founding Durrel line, not a coincidence or separate family.

Site-created names

The source left these genuinely unnamed or unresolved between two options; this site chose one to keep the material usable, not because the choice is attested.

Site-created name

Brevair's Timeline B capital: Corvane

The source atlas leaves this explicitly "TBD." Corvane is a name this site invented, not one found in any source document — chosen to tie the capital to the Corvett family who feature heavily across the Brevair-adjacent B8–B13 campaigns. (Timeline A's version of the capital, renamed under the Dominion in Campaign II, is called Durrelion — a separate, source-attested name for a separate history's version of the same city.)

Editorial reconstruction

Inferred from textual clues rather than stated outright. Reasonable, but genuinely a step beyond what the source says directly.

Editorial reconstruction

Any "Dur-" name is a Durrel derivative

Names like Durreth (Age of Inheritance) and Durren (Age of Reawakening) are treated as the Durrel line's name drifting across the centuries between campaigns, rather than coincidental or unrelated names. This is an inference applied consistently, not a source-stated rule — any close variant (Dur-, Durr-) found in the source material is respelled to keep the double-R core (e.g. Duren → Durren).

Editorial reconstruction

Four Cyberpunk Eras, not two

Only the Third and Fourth were named directly in the source. Reconstructed from two textual clues — Campaign XII's note that the world rebuilds "after machine/cyber ages" (plural), and Campaign XV's reference to "the Cataclysm" that precedes it — a First (Campaigns IX–XI) and Second (Campaigns XIII–XIV) era are now labelled explicitly. Campaign XII's old "Pre-4CE Pivot" tag is dropped in favor of "Post-First-Cyberpunk Medieval Interlude" — the old label was a symptom of the same conflation error that misplaced Arden and Caelen in that campaign. See the Lore and Magic Codex pages for the full breakdown.

Thorn House's exact location

Only ever placed "within the Brevair sphere / Brevair road network." Left unpinned — its page will describe it relative to Corvane rather than with fixed coordinates.

Open

Thirteenth Seal — unverified legacy reference

An older world compendium proposes a post-Dominion "Thirteenth Seal" and reports a half-melted remnant recovered after the Null Relic's destruction. Neither the Seal nor the recovery appears in the detailed Campaign XI record. It is therefore not confirmed canon and should not be treated as an established relic unless supported by an earlier campaign chat or explicitly adopted. Deliberately omitted from Relics & Artefacts rather than presented as a disputed entry with its own evidence — the reported evidence is exactly as uncorroborated as the Seal itself, and a "disputed" listing risked implying more substantiation than actually exists.

Open

Sirian's private motive (Campaign VIII)

The live account presents Sirian as a reformist legalist whose Gate Codex gets weaponised despite his intent. Other sources characterise Sirian less charitably — closer to a lawful inquisitor consolidating surveillance through faith than a genuine reformer. Both readings fit what the Codex actually does once written. This site presents the stated intent and the authoritarian result side by side on Campaign VIII and Sirian's Characters card, rather than settling private motive without direct chat evidence.

Open

The Architect and the disputed final blow (Campaign X)

All high-level sources agree the final Veil Shard is destroyed in Campaign X; they disagree on the exact hand that strikes it. The live account follows Serik Thorne's campaign leadership without asserting he personally struck it (now noted explicitly on Campaign X). A separate, older continuum table introduces "the Architect" — a provisional final synthetic Taren-echo said to test Serik's refusal — without corroboration from any other source; this figure is deliberately not added to the site as confirmed content. A further "Burning Fall" account (Sirian and Alaric opening a remnant key, Lior and Jalen exposing an emperor through records, Sevrin Mareth renouncing Elian, Lior breaking the shard beneath the Collegium) survives in one older summary document but directly conflicts with the accepted Serik/Lior/Iria campaign frame and isn't merged into it. Recorded here rather than silently adopted or silently discarded.

Open

Heart Seal / Durrel Ridge legacy conflict — unresolved wording

The dominant Campaign I/B1 record places the fixed Heart Seal beneath Silver Bastion, repeatedly — the rupture in B1 happens in its Heart Seal chamber, and the early historical account explicitly says "the Heart Seal beneath Silver Bastion." One early setting brief separately says the Seal was "found in vault in family crypt" at Durrel Ridge. No source records the Seal being transferred to the Ridge, including after Silver Bastion's fall in B11 — what Cameron corrupts there is confirmed as the fortress's own separate ward-anchor, not the Heart Seal, and what Jack restores in B14 is the same ward-anchor, not a returned Seal. Current usage therefore distinguishes the Silver Bastion Heart Seal from Durrel Ridge's own Heart Ward, Heart Vault, and Heart-linked relics — see the Magic Codex's terminology box. The old contradictory sentence is recorded here rather than pretending it never existed, and rather than inventing a relocation event that was never played.

Aureth and Velis vs. Luminara and Shah

Whether Aureth and Velis are the same figures as Luminara and Shah under older names, direct predecessors, or something else entirely is not established anywhere in the source material. This site does not assume they're identical — treated as a genuine gap rather than silently merged.
