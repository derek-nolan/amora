The Frostborn Inheritance

Core Premise

Weeks after Hollowrest, deliberate frost geometry begins forming in Caen and Lio's quarters. Resonance testing isolates Caen as the source. The discovery reveals that Caen carries a surviving Frostborn / Icewarden inheritance distinct from the fallen Icebinder branch. Crucially, this ancestry is not the same thing as his role as Lio's Thread: one is inherited custodial history, the other is a Timeline B resonance function formed through relationship and experience. Caen happens to carry both.

Act-by-Act

Act I — The Frost-Sigil in Silver Bastion

Frost appears overnight in Caen and Lio's quarters, forming deliberate geometric patterns rather than Hollowrest's chaotic environmental bleed. The sigils react only to Caen. A dormant Binder door opens and identifies him as a returning steward. The Order recognises this as lineage-level custodial magic rather than an Anchor/Thread anomaly.

Act II — The Road North & Vyrn's Spring

Old Frost markers answer to Caen and lead the party toward Vyrn's Spring, a surviving enclave of Frostborn descendants. Their existence proves the Frost tradition survived outside the fallen Icebinder branch. Caen is offered history, training, and ancestral memory, and completes the Frostborn Imprint Rite to integrate the inheritance safely with the life he already has rather than replace it.

Act III — Recognition & Return

Caen claims his Frostborn heritage with Lio as his stabilising partner. The Spring entrusts him with ancestral sigils and establishes a stable communication link with Silver Bastion. Back at the Bastion, the Frostborn Vault accepts Caen's imprint and the Order expands its records to distinguish inherited custodial traditions from its own newly imported resonance roles.

World State After Campaign B3

Geography. Vyrn's Spring enters the record as a surviving northern Frost enclave. Silver Bastion gains a sealed Frostborn Annex tied to Caen's access.

Magic. Caen demonstrates that a person can simultaneously carry an inherited Icewarden/Frostborn tradition and occupy an Anchor/Thread resonance role without those becoming the same category. Later B17 research clarifies the older historical sequence further: each pressure had its own House and Binder practice before the Great Seals were set; permanent Warden watches followed Setting.

The Durrel Line. Still absent from the narrative directly. Lio's Anchor role is not evidence that he carries Durrel Heartbinder ancestry, and Caen's Thread role is not itself a bloodline.

Institutions. The Order begins cataloguing hereditary custodial traditions alongside — not inside — its Anchor/Thread training. Caen becomes the first clear case where those two kinds of identity coexist in one person.

Canon status: follows the source account; this campaign is where the Kellan → Caen renaming becomes explicit. Later terminology lock: Frostborn/Icewarden is an inherited custodial lineage; Anchor and Thread are relational resonance roles. They can coexist, as they do in Caen, but should not be listed as equivalent bloodline categories. B17's House → Binder → Setting → Seal → Warden model supersedes the early Order's broader lineage theory.
