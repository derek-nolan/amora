Highever

Realm and capital region

Highever is the political territory surrounding its central fortress, Silver Bastion — see that entry below for the fortress's own detailed history. This entry covers the wider realm: its status, government, and role in the world at large, independent of who currently holds the Bastion itself.

Current State — Timeline B, c. 342 ARV (post-B17)

Stable, with Silver Bastion returned to Light control since B14. Its Collegium is the confirmed origin point of the Western Icewarden route network's eastern node, and the Harrow Collection has now confirmed itself as a genuine — if cooperative — private archive rather than a hidden order, helping tie Highever's own private collections back to the ancient western sites B17 uncovers.

Silver Bastion

also Obsidian Bastion (Dominion era) · The Veiled Citadel (post-B11) — located within Highever, above

The single most contested structure in the entire chronicle — the same physical fortress changes hands, names, and purpose more than anywhere else in Amora. It's also where both timelines begin: Campaign I and Campaign B1 open in the same chamber, on the same night, with radically different outcomes. The Heart Seal's location here is direct canon — the strongest fixed seal-site in the setting; see the Magic Codex for the full seal-location concordance.

Current State — Timeline B, c. 342 ARV (post-B17)

Fully restored to the Light and standing as its own institution again since Campaign B14. Nick Soren's B16 visit confirms the Heart Seal remains here, never moved to Durrel Ridge, and B17 formally confirms the Bastion accepts Nick's status as Western Custodian while recognising the northern Frost Seal and Heartwarden authority both remain entirely outside his claim — jurisdiction respected on both sides.

Evermere

Repeated rebuild site — sanctuary, capital, and port by turns

Evermere is destroyed and rebuilt more times than anywhere else in Timeline A, and separately becomes Timeline B's most detailed civic setting from Campaign B6 onward. It's also the interpretive map-key location for the Flame Seal — Arienna Lume's line are Flamewardens; see the Magic Codex for the confidence level on this placement.

Current State — Timeline B, c. 342 ARV (post-B15)

Stable, with The Lantern still operational nearly eight decades after its founding — an institution that has genuinely outlived every one of its original leaders, now run under successor leadership honouring the same mutual-aid pact rather than the founders themselves personally persisting.

The Western Oath-Fort

Ruined military-religious complex · site of the Seven Refusal

A damaged military-religious complex somewhere in the western Highever interior route network — its exact continental coordinate isn't fixed. Its fabric preserves more than one construction phase: older pale-grey masonry survives beneath later black volcanic rebuilding, and surfaces show the physical effects of ancient glassfall, not metaphorical damage. Represents the late Oath Wars with direct overlap into the War of Ash and Glass period. First identified and explored in Campaign B17; ruined and archaeologically recoverable in 342 ARV, not an active fortress. Recent scavenger movement and blood confirmed the site was still being entered shortly before the expedition arrived.

Current State — Timeline B, c. 342 ARV (post-B17)

Ruined and no longer contested — its glasshound threat cleared, its Seven Refusal crypt formally documented and its oath-chain relocated to legitimate Silver Bastion custody. Remains an active archaeological site for future study rather than a settlement of any kind.

Rimeholt

Inland town · former Western Icewarden refuge

A modest, ordinary town in the northwestern Highever interior — west-northwest of Highever City, well north of Gloamford, and separate from the established Evermere-Gloamford travel cluster. Green woodland, farmland, and streams surround it; despite its Frost-associated name, it is not an icy settlement. Roughly a two-day overland journey from Highever City, along older woodland roads distinct from the Gloamford/Evermere route. First appears as a real location in Campaign B16, though it was Nicholas Soren's home well before then.

Current State — Timeline B, c. 342 ARV (post-B17)

Home again, in the fullest sense — Nick and Jarek return not to another crisis but simply to see Maraine, and the town's Western archive is now complete and properly explained rather than a puzzle scattered across unmarked warnings. Nick and Jarek are engaged, with a stated intention to eventually relocate Jarek's workshop here once Nick finishes at the Collegium.

also Tarenus Keep · Citadel of Inheritance · Inheritance Reclaimed · Free Citadel

This city changes its own name more often than any other location in Amora — five distinct identities across Timeline A alone, each marking a different political regime built on the same ground. It's also the interpretive map-key location for the Stone/Earthbound Seal; see the Magic Codex for the full seal-location concordance and confidence levels.

Current State — Timeline B, c. 342 ARV (post-B6)

Stable and quiet — its buried vault systems remain dormant and undisturbed since a Luminara resistance cell's investigation confirmed no ley-field distortion or conduit reactivation. No campaign has returned here since.

Brevair

Timeline A capital renamed Durrelion · Timeline B capital named Corvane

Brevair's starting relationship with Highever is genuinely different between the two timelines, not the same premise playing out two ways. Timeline A begins diplomatically engaged and becomes a crown ally through the Heartwardens' work; Timeline B begins as an isolationist, militarised rival under Emperor Trainor Fell.

Current State — Timeline B, c. 342 ARV (post-B15)

Sovereign and geographically intact, but without its former hidden command structure — the Keystone that let Rhalen Valecour rule through Delryn is permanently destroyed. Older magistrates, officers, and local powers are reasserting authority; Durrel Ridge deliberately declined to annex Brevair or choose its successor government, so the outcome remains genuinely unsettled.

Durrel Ridge

Ancestral estate, Warden seat, and continuity house

The ancestral home of the Durrel line, predating Campaign I itself. Its family crypt houses statues of the first Heartwardens and an ancestral Heart Vault tied to the Durrel line's custodianship of Heart. Tarenus Durrel, Taren's distant ancestor, is entombed here — Campaign B17 corrects an earlier assumption that Tarenus founded the Heartbinder tradition: he was already a Binder before Heart was ever set, and the Ridge preserves history from before the permanent Great Seal system existed at all. The fixed Heart Seal itself is sited at Silver Bastion, not here; see the Magic Codex for the distinction between the Seal, the Ridge's own Heart Ward, and its Heart Vault. By the time Zethyr and Taren arrive at Silver Bastion, the later institutional order built on that legacy had lapsed; they revive it rather than start it fresh. The Ridge and its Vault of Tarenus were present in Timeline A from early on — visited during Campaigns I–II, per the campaign's own record — though the compiled summary documents this site otherwise draws from don't cover that visit directly; see the Canon Ledger. It becomes one of Timeline B's most important recurring settings from Campaign B7 onward — the site that carries the Durrel line's institutional weight for the second half of that timeline's current record.

Current State — Timeline B, c. 342 ARV (post-B17)

Secure, and confirmed as the strongest surviving archive for the original House of Heart's role in the Great Seal setting — Ser Nora Durrel, of Jack Durrel's later line, stands as the Ridge's current living witness to that history. Its Heart Ward is fully repaired after B15's breach; Jack Durrel holds the estate as Taren's named, consensual successor rather than an automatic heir — succession here is now a negotiated household process, not a bloodline assumption.

The Badlands

One of Vanmere's four principal regions — a magical wasteland

A wild-magic desert at the interior of Vanmere, distinct from Menge, already ancient by 0 ARV — proof neither the Dominion nor any later calamity created it. Officially avoided and shunned by every institution on the continent, yet consistently, quietly explored anyway.

Public account

The story ordinary people tell: centuries ago, a small group of mages conducted a catastrophic experiment that reached too far and burned an entire region into wasteland. This rumour is also why mages became objects of general suspicion throughout Vanmere — the popular memory of the Badlands and the popular memory of mage-suspicion are the same story, told from two angles.

Buried history

The true origin, as established in Lore: the Badlands are what remained after the War of Ash and Glass — a conflict between sorcerous bloodlines and warlock covenants — ended in the Shattering, when the sorcerous houses drew on Vanmere's foundational ley structure while the covenants anchored vast pacts into that same network at the same moment. The collision ruptured ley-lines, burned and glassed the terrain, and destroyed the houses and covenants fighting to control it. Popular memory simplified this entire war into the single rumour of "a mages' experiment" — the public account above is that simplification, not a fabrication from nothing.

What the region is like

Wild-magic storms, violently shifted stone, shattered towers, buried vaults, pre-cataclysmic ruins, and artefacts described as "too powerful to exist." Expeditions into the Badlands are notorious for how few people return from them — yet nobles and mages continue to secretly sponsor scavengers and relic hunters despite the region's official shunned status, precisely because of what still might be found there.

Later events damaged the Badlands further without creating them. Taren's Dominion used parts of the region for exile and penal settlements, mined Veilstone there, and diverted ancient ley-rivers to feed imperial infrastructure (Campaign II onward). Centuries later, the Sundering Tempest's aftershocks fractured the region further into glass canyons, now occupied by nomads, scavengers, and relic hunters (~2200 ARV). See the Canon Ledger for the separate "Badlands of Menge" naming issue — a different, later-blighted part of Menge, not this region.

Nareth Isle

Belisar — an inhabited island beyond Vanmere's own map frame

A large, long, north-south island roughly two to three days' sail west-southwest of Vanmere's western coast — the first named inhabited land beyond the frame of every existing Vanmere map. Its absence from earlier maps reflects their own coastal focus, not the island's late discovery. Distinctly shaped: a broad, blunt northern end narrowing gradually south, ending in a southern hook that curls eastward to shelter a natural harbour. First named in Campaign B16.

Current State — Timeline B, c. 342 ARV (post-B16)

Unchanged since first named — Maraine Soren has since returned to Rimeholt, and no campaign has revisited the island directly. Its own political structure, beyond Belisar as a harbour city, remains entirely unestablished.
