Timeline B · Campaign B5

The Living Reliquary

Betrayal, Sovereignty, Devotion, Myth-Making, Veil Instability

Age of Founding, Reforged

3–4 ARV Primary location: Bastion, Greymount, Brevair

Core Premise

Campaign B5 opens with catastrophe: the Six — future-born temporal anchors whose knowledge had guided every stabilisation effort since Campaign B1 — are executed within Bastion under orders sanctioned by the High Justicar, justified as prevention of a projected metaphysical fracture tied to their continued existence. The truth proves darker. Through interrogation, ledger cross-reference, and Veil resonance analysis, Lio and Caen uncover a Shah-aligned infiltration within Bastion's own hierarchy — the "fracture" narrative was manipulated from the start. The deaths of the Six weren't prevention. They were exploitation. Seraphine Vale, the Order's founder and its clearest moral authority since Campaign B1, dies in the same wave of consolidation that follows — and with her goes the last stabilising tie to Timeline A, and the last person with the standing to check what happens to Caen next.

Party & Key Figures

  • Lio Serrin PC Renamed Vareth this campaign. Uncovers, with Caen, the Shah-aligned infiltration behind the Six's execution, and orchestrates the covert fall of Brevair's Emperor.
  • Caen Vance
    Investigates alongside Lio; receives a Veil imprint at Greymount that becomes the hidden turning point of the campaign, and authors the Sovereign Reliquary Codex by its end.
  • Seraphine Vale Dies during this campaign
    Founder of the Order of Anchors since Campaign B1; her death removes the strongest institutional advocate for restraint and marks the point at which Bastion can no longer plausibly claim uninterrupted continuity with the original Order.

Act-by-Act

The Assassination of the Six

The Six are executed within Bastion on the High Justicar's authority, ostensibly to prevent a fracture their continued presence was said to be causing. Lio and Caen's investigation exposes that story as cover — a Shah-aligned faction had infiltrated Bastion's hierarchy and manipulated the fracture narrative specifically to justify removing the Six. The revelation shatters internal trust within Bastion; the institution that formed to protect people has just killed six of the people who helped found it, on the word of infiltrators.

Purge and Consolidation

The High Justicar is detained. Several arcanists die resisting arrest or during collapse events triggered by exposed ritual interference. Seraphine — the Order's founder and, until now, its clearest institutional check on runaway power — dies in the same period, and with her gone, strategic control over Bastion passes to Caen by default rather than by any formal transfer. Bastion enters a quiet purge phase: hidden Shah networks dismantled, the Cult Ledger cross-referenced against internal command structures, internal dissent suppressed efficiently rather than theatrically. For the first time in Timeline B, Bastion's leadership moves with real, decisive moral ambiguity — and no one senior enough is left to object.

Greymount and the Conduit

Seeking the deeper source of the manipulation, Lio and Caen travel to Greymount and uncover Veil-infused conduit architecture beneath Rillhaven, built for sustained resonance imprinting. During the attempt to destroy it, Caen receives a Veil imprint — a form of magical conditioning that begins subtly reshaping how he experiences authority and control. The conduit is damaged but not fully eradicated, and the Veil does not leave him unchanged.

The Emperor of Brevair

Drawing on intelligence about Timeline A's history — knowledge known only through the Six, now dead — Lio initiates a covert destabilisation of Brevair's Emperor, Trainor Fell, believing him Shah-aligned in both timelines. The assassination is engineered through noble rivalry manipulation rather than open war; the Emperor falls, and Brevair fractures internally while Bastion publicly stays uninvolved. Privately, the balance of continental power shifts for good. This is Timeline B's first decisive use of knowledge imported from a future that no longer exists anywhere else.

The Birth of the Living Reliquary

As Bastion stabilises politically, the dynamic between Caen and Lio shifts. Lio is renamed Vareth, and the relationship between Sovereign and Reliquary evolves into a sustained, Veil-aligned resonance state — ritual pauses folded directly into governance itself. Bastion's inner clergy begin referring to Vareth as the "Living Reliquary." What began as private structure becomes public myth: devotion made visibly embodied, hierarchy made visibly present. The Sovereign no longer rules alone — he rules beside a stabilising axis the whole court can see.

Territorial Alignment & the Era of Consecration

Under Caen's leadership, border regions voluntarily align with Bastion for protection, refugee corridors are formalised, and clerical education expands Shah's doctrine of order through structure rather than conquest — Bastion's stability attracts alignment on its own. Internally, though, Bastion grows colder. The Sovereign-Reliquary state stops being something entered and exited ritually and becomes continuous instead: Bastion's sigils begin responding to Caen and Vareth's mere proximity without being invoked, atmospheric density rising during council pauses, clergy reporting the Veil feels "closer" whenever the two of them stand together. The structure has stopped being symbolic. It's become infrastructural — and the Veil has started responding without anyone commanding it to.

The Grand Council Fracture

During a multi-realm council meant to reassure foreign envoys, Caen enacts a routine ritual pause — and it doesn't resolve normally. Bastion's foundational sigils activate simultaneously; architectural resonance patterns mirror proto-Ley structures; stone cracks in geometric formations without collapsing. Witnesses describe a sensation of temporal overlap. No one is hurt, but the message is unmistakable: the Veil has integrated the Sovereign-Reliquary structure into long-term recurrence architecture, and the pattern has begun seeding itself beyond anything Caen actually intended.

The Codex of Recurrence

In response, Caen authors what becomes known as the Sovereign Reliquary Codex, formalising containment as transformation, naming as identity refinement, governance through embodied devotion, and the necessity of sustained consecration for structural stability. The Codex is sealed within Bastion's western arch. The myth is no longer accidental — it's recorded.

World State After Campaign B5

Geography. Bastion stands politically stable on the surface. Greymount's Veil conduit beneath Rillhaven is damaged but not destroyed — a lingering threat for future campaigns. In Brevair, Emperor Trainor Fell's fall produces internal instability there and shifts regional power toward Bastion — see the Atlas for Brevair's fuller chronology.

Magic. A proto-Ley-Heir resonance signature is embedded into Bastion's architecture — the first time Timeline B develops its own version of the succession-through-bonded-pairs mechanism that defined so much of Timeline A's history. Shah's Veil now exhibits genuinely autonomous behaviour, moving beyond anything Caen consciously intends. Caen believed he'd stabilised instability through permanence; the evidence instead suggests the Veil used that permanence to encode recurrence. The era of the Ley-Heirs proper hasn't begun yet in Timeline B — but the pattern now exists.

The Durrel Line. Still no Durrel figures, but this campaign matters precisely because it shows Timeline B independently developing a Ley-Heir-equivalent structure around Caen and Vareth — proof that erasing Taren and Zethyr in Campaign B1 didn't prevent the underlying pattern (bonded pair as succession mechanism) from re-emerging on its own, under new names. The assassination of the Six removes the last stabilising tie to Timeline A; in their absence, Timeline B doesn't avoid repetition — it reshapes it.

Institutions. Seraphine Vale's death ends the Order of Anchors' direct continuity with its Campaign B1 founding — everything from here forward happens under leadership she never approved. Clergy factions begin forming around how to interpret the new doctrine, and the Living Reliquary myth spreads well beyond Bastion's own walls, four campaigns into a timeline that was supposed to have broken this exact pattern for good.

Canon status: follows the source account directly; this is where Lio Serrin is renamed Vareth, and where Seraphine Vale dies — confirmed explicitly multiple times in the source material's character register and campaign summaries. One secondary source describes her as surviving in a diminished role instead; the site follows the death, per direct confirmation.
