Timeline B · Campaign B5

The Living Reliquary

—

3–4 ARV Primary location: Bastion, Greymount, Brevair

Core Premise

Campaign B5 opens with catastrophe: the Six — future-born temporal anchors whose knowledge had guided early Timeline B stabilisation since Campaign B1 — are executed within Bastion under orders from the High Justicar, justified as preventing a projected metaphysical fracture. It's the single biggest loss the Order of Anchors has suffered so far, and it happens at the hands of its own leadership. Seraphine Vale, the Order's founder and ethical spine since Campaign B1, dies during this same campaign — removing the strongest institutional advocate for restraint at exactly the moment the Order needs one most.

Act-by-Act

The Assassination of the Six

Through interrogation and Veil resonance analysis, Lio and Caen uncover that the "fracture" narrative used to justify the Six's execution was manipulated — their deaths were exploitation dressed up as prevention. The revelation shatters internal trust within Bastion; the institution that formed to protect people has just killed six of the people who helped found it.

Seraphine's Death

In the consolidation that follows the Six's execution, Seraphine Vale — the Order's founder and its clearest moral authority since Campaign B1 — is killed. Her death isn't incidental to the plot; it's the campaign's real turning point. With her gone, there's no longer anyone senior enough, or trusted enough, to check what happens to Caen and Lio next.

Greymount and the Conduit

Seeking the deeper source of manipulation, Lio and Caen find Veil-infused conduit architecture beneath Rillhaven. During its attempted destruction, Caen receives a Veil imprint — a form of magical conditioning that begins subtly reshaping how he experiences authority and control. The conduit is damaged, but not fully eradicated.

The Living Reliquary

With Seraphine no longer able to provide institutional counterweight, Lio is renamed Vareth. Caen's imprint deepens into a sustained dynamic between them — an intensifying, ritualised form of devotion and control that Bastion's clergy begin describing in reverent terms, calling Vareth the "Living Reliquary." What starts as an isolated magical injury becomes something the institution around them starts treating as sacred infrastructure — a shift that almost certainly wouldn't have gone unchallenged had Seraphine survived.

The Grand Council Fracture

During a council meant to reassure foreign envoys, a ritual pause triggers Bastion's foundational sigils to activate simultaneously — proof the Veil has integrated the Caen/Vareth structure into long-term recurrence architecture, seeding itself beyond Caen's intent or awareness.

World State After Campaign B5

Geography. Bastion stands politically stable on the surface. Greymount's Veil conduit beneath Rillhaven is damaged but not destroyed — a lingering threat for future campaigns. In Brevair, Emperor Trainor Fell is assassinated during this same period, producing internal instability there and shifting regional power toward Bastion — see the Atlas for Brevair's fuller chronology.

Magic. A proto-Ley-Heir resonance signature is embedded into Bastion's architecture — the first time Timeline B develops its own version of the succession-through-bonded-pairs mechanism that defined so much of Timeline A's history. The Veil now exhibits autonomous behaviour, moving beyond anything Caen consciously intends.

The Durrel Line. Still no Durrel figures, but this campaign is significant precisely because it shows Timeline B independently developing a Ley-Heir-equivalent structure around Caen and Vareth (Lio) — proof that erasing Taren and Zethyr in Campaign B1 didn't prevent the underlying pattern (bonded pair as succession mechanism) from re-emerging on its own, under new names.

Institutions. Seraphine Vale's death ends the Order of Anchors' direct continuity with its Campaign B1 founding — everything from here forward happens under leadership she never approved. Caen authors the Sovereign Reliquary Codex, formalising what he believes is stabilisation through permanence — though the pattern now exists independent of his intent, and there's no one left with Seraphine's standing to challenge it. The era of the Ley-Heirs proper has not yet begun in Timeline B, but the seed is planted here, four campaigns into the timeline.

Canon status: follows the source account directly; this is where Lio Serrin is renamed Vareth, and where Seraphine Vale dies — confirmed explicitly multiple times in the source material's character register and campaign summaries.
