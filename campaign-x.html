Timeline A · Campaign X

The Severing

Post-Veil Reformation — "Through Choice, Freedom"

c. 6450 ARV Primary location: The Gate (collapsing), continent-wide

Opening World State

The Post-Veil Reformation, and the true climax of the "old" Durrel cycle. The Gate Empire collapses in fire and rebellion at this campaign's start; republics and technocracies across the continent all sit atop layers of Veil, Choir, and machine remnants left behind by every age that came before them.

Party & Key Figures

  • Serik Thorne PC Revolutionary reformer; motto "Through Choice, Freedom." Leads the effort to dismantle destiny engines and decisively sever the Durrel echo as binding fate.
  • Lior & Iria
    Civic allies serving as Serik's co-rebels and moral equals — his personal leverage, and his reason for restraint when restraint is the harder choice.
  • Old-order holdouts and machine priests
    The remaining institutional resistance to dismantling the destiny engines.

Act-by-Act

Collapse

The Gate Empire collapses in fire and open rebellion as the campaign opens — the Theocracy's long-accumulated laws finally giving way under their own weight. Serik Thorne, once an inquisitor, emerges as a genuine revolutionary reformer.

Dismantling Destiny

Serik's creed — "Through Choice, Freedom" — articulates the first true rejection of ordained destiny anywhere in the record. The party's work is direct and structural: dismantling destiny engines built up over centuries, rewriting constitutions from the ground up, and working deliberately toward severing the Durrel echo as any kind of binding fate.

The Severing

The campaign culminates in the Severing proper — a metaphysical and legal break that ends the "guaranteed" return of Taren and Zethyr as central rulers of anything. This is the fourth recorded Severance in the record, and the first framed explicitly as a legal and constitutional act rather than a personal sacrifice.

World State After Campaign X

Geography. A half-deserted Gate becomes an archive city, mined for relics rather than ruled from — its imperial function permanently ended.

Magic. The age of divine and dynastic inevitability ends here. Magic, law, and history all become genuinely contingent from this point forward, opening space for later echoes of the Taren/Zethyr pattern to be read as choices people fall into, not destiny they're bound by.

The Durrel Line. This is the pivotal turning point for the whole lineage's meaning: Timeline A's later campaigns still echo Taren and Zethyr repeatedly, but from here forward as recurring cycles people choose or fall into, never again as hard, guaranteed destiny.

Institutions. Republics and technocracies across the continent begin rebuilding on genuinely contingent foundations rather than destiny-engine infrastructure — setting the stage directly for Campaign XI's Age of Clause and its culture of doubt, transparency, and earned trust.
