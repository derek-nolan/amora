Timeline A · Campaign X

The Severing

Post-Veil Reformation — "Through Choice, Freedom"

c. 6450 ARV Primary theatres: The collapsing Gate order and continent-wide machine remnants Closes the First Cyberpunk Era's old destiny architecture

Opening World State

Campaign X opens in the wreckage of the Warden-machine order inherited from Campaign IX. The Gate's authority is collapsing, old inquisitorial structures are losing their monopoly, and republics and technocracies across Amora still sit on top of centuries of Veil, Choir, and machine systems designed to preserve, predict, or enforce preferred futures.

Serik Thorne begins on the inside of that world. Later records describe him as an inquisitor who becomes a revolutionary reformer and as the symbolic Heartbearer of the final old cycle. His creed — "Through Choice, Freedom" — is not simply opposition to one ruler. It is opposition to the idea that any system should be allowed to make human futures inevitable.

Party & Key Figures

  • Serik Thorne PC
    Former inquisitor turned revolutionary reformer. Leads the attempt to dismantle destiny engines, break the legal inheritance of the old Gate order, and end the Durrel pattern as binding fate rather than recurring cultural memory.
  • The Architect Antagonist
    A final synthetic Veilbearer in later lineage records — a Choir/Warden construct retaining a Taren-like command pattern. The surviving source does not establish the Architect as literal Taren reincarnated; it is the last machine expression of the old will-to-order.
  • Civic allies, old-order holdouts, and machine priests
    The coalition around Serik and the forces defending the inherited system. Individual names are not secure in the surviving Campaign X summaries and are not invented here.

Act-by-Act

The Gate Burns

The old Gate order gives way through revolt rather than orderly reform. Institutions built in Campaigns VII and VIII have already survived far longer than the people who created them; now their archives, enforcement systems, and machine successors are being attacked, abandoned, or claimed by competing successor states.

Dismantling Destiny

Serik's campaign is structural. The party targets engines and legal mechanisms that make inherited patterns self-reinforcing: systems that predict behaviour, preserve old command architectures, or turn historical precedent into inevitability. Constitutions and civic orders have to be rewritten at the same time the magical infrastructure beneath them is being dismantled.

The Architect

Later lineage material identifies the campaign's final synthetic opponent as The Architect, a construct carrying the last significant Taren-like will pattern inside Choir/Warden machinery. The name survives more clearly than the encounter's exact scene sequence. Its role is nevertheless important: Campaign X is not merely a revolt against dead institutions but a confrontation with a system still capable of producing a commanding intelligence from the accumulated residue of those institutions.

The Final Veil Shard

The campaign culminates in destruction of the final Veil Shard. Sources agree on the outcome and on Serik's leadership; they do not securely preserve whose hand physically destroys the shard. This page therefore treats the destruction as the party's victory rather than inventing a single final strike.

The Severing

The metaphysical consequence is larger than destroying one artefact. The conduits that had once carried Ley-Heir recurrence and later synthetic echoes collapse as a binding system. Prophecy loses its enforceable architecture. The Severing is simultaneously magical, political, and constitutional: the world stops treating an old pattern as something the future is obliged to repeat.

World State After Campaign X

Geography. The Gate survives as a diminished archive-city and relic site rather than the capital of a continent-spanning sacred state. Old Warden and Choir facilities remain scattered across Amora, but no single network commands them.

Magic. The last divine algorithms and the final Veil Shard are destroyed. Magic returns to the people in a less centrally governed, more unstable form. Later echoes can still occur as reconstruction, culture, technology, or choice, but not because a functioning Ley-Heir system guarantees their return.

The Durrel Pattern. Later archives say the old Durrel cycle "concludes" here. That does not mean Timeline A never again produces Taren/Zethyr-like pairs — it plainly does. It means the world is no longer metaphysically required to generate them as ordained heirs. Later recurrences are better understood as recreated patterns, social repetition, surviving artefacts, or people choosing familiar roles.

Institutions. Republics and technocracies begin rebuilding without destiny-engine authority. That unstable freedom creates the setting Campaign XI inherits: a culture increasingly obsessed with reversible law, evidence, doubt, and whether memory can be preserved without being allowed to command.

Canon status: Serik Thorne, "Through Choice, Freedom," rebellion against Inquisition/Choir remnants, destruction of the final Veil Shard, and the Severing of guaranteed recurrence are supported across the surviving Timeline A summaries. The Architect comes from the later lineage reconstruction and is retained as the final synthetic Veil-side antagonist with that provenance made explicit. The exact final combat sequence and the individual who physically destroys the shard are unresolved.
