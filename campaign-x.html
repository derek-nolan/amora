The Severing

Post-Veil Reformation — First Cyberpunk Era

c. 6450 ARV Climax of the "old" Durrel cycle

Opening World State

Republics and technocracies all sit atop Veil, Choir, and machine remnants — the world has layered republic, theocracy, machine faith, and Veil residue on top of one another across nine campaigns. This is the climax of the old Durrel cycle: everything built since Campaign I is about to be tested at once.

Party & Key Figures

  • Serik Thorne — Completed (doc)
    Inquisitor turned reformer; breaks the last shard. Learned exactly how the system justified itself. Motto: "Through Choice, Freedom."
  • Lior — Completed (doc)
    Co-rebel with Serik; his moral equal.
  • Iria — Completed (doc)
    Personal leverage in Serik's bargain, and proof that no abstract victory is worth sacrificing the people caught beneath it.

Act-by-Act

From Inquisitor to Reformer

Serik Thorne begins as an inquisitor — trained within exactly the kind of legal apparatus Sirian built in Campaign VIII — and becomes a reformer once he learns exactly how the system justifies itself. The struggle moves through records, courts, defections, street resistance, and finally open collapse.

Refusing the Compromise

Old authorities try to preserve the last Veil shard as a tool that can be made responsible under new ownership. Serik and Lior refuse — their revolution isn't an attempt to place a better ruler above the machine, but to remove the machine's right to choose rulers at all.

The Final Shard

The final shard is destroyed. Divine and dynastic inevitability ends, and magic becomes power without a script. The Severing doesn't create peace — it creates responsibility. This is the fourth recorded Severance in Amora's history (after Zethyr's, Edrin's, and the Choir Core's), and the first to be explicitly framed as a political rather than personal act.

World State After Campaign X

Geography. No single location is transformed so much as the entire layered structure of prior ages — Gate-era law, Choir-era infrastructure, and Machine-era networks — loses its claim to inevitability all at once.

Magic. The last Veil shard, the direct descendant of the Dark Filament first absorbed in Campaign I, is destroyed. This closes out the entire "old Durrel cycle" — every subsequent Timeline A campaign deals with magic and lineage as choice and pattern rather than active divine/dynastic mechanism.

The Durrel Line. No living Durrel appears, but this campaign marks the formal end of "dynastic inevitability" tied to the name — from here forward, Durrel recurrences (Campaign XIII's Speaker Lira Durrel, the later Age of Inheritance) are understood in-world as cultural pattern, not literal destiny.

Institutions. The age of divine and dynastic inevitability ends. Magic, law, and history become contingent, opening space for later Echoes to be choices, not mandates. Timeline A's later campaigns still echo Taren and Zethyr — but as cycles people fall into, not hard destiny.
