Timeline A · Campaign XXI

The Last Key

Late Concord Afterglow

8650–8660 ARV Primary location: Evermere, Sable Gate Cisterns

Opening World State

A generation after the Vault Unbound; the world edges toward a partial medievalisation again — tech receding, institutions ossifying, frontier conflicts brewing. Evermere runs on habit: press rites, neat ledgers, clocks that keep time as if the world still hummed. The Collegium's Unbirth project aims to print a final chant to end recurrence; beneath Sable Gate, the Nulls argue the world shouldn't be destroyed or enthralled, only taught to choose.

Act-by-Act

The Unbirth Plates

Rumours of sanctioned erasures draw Talen and Seren to the Nulls below Sable Gate, where they learn the Unbirth plates target the Heart-Veil itself.

The Red Choir Gambit

At Annex V, Talen speaks a counter-verse, Rook inverts the runes, and Seren pins the fighting core — dusting the plate and turning chant into static. In the ash, the ley traces an open-ring: recognition of intent, not obedience.

Severance Spoken

At the pedestal rite, the shard shows that the world mirrors the actor's intent. In council, Talen chooses: "No chains, no heirs, only choice." The Heart-Veil unwinds; the metronome stops; the future returns to mortals. This is the seventh and most explicitly doctrinal Severance in the record — the first to be spoken as a formal philosophical statement rather than performed as a desperate act.

World State After Campaign XXI

Geography. Evermere's Annex V is re-tooled as the Hall of Paper. Sable Gate Cisterns are confirmed as the Null council's ritual floor.

Magic. No new ley-heirs are made; the Taren/Zethyr recursion recedes to legend, not machinery — the campaign's central claim is that the cycle of recurrence, traced from Campaign II through every echo since, is genuinely over as a mechanism, though the pattern itself (per Campaign XXII's opening) is about to prove it can still recur without any mechanism at all. Magic becomes personalised: small workings answer intent and focus, while grand rites require community consent or heavy prices.

The Durrel Line. No Durrel figures directly, though the Severance Talen speaks explicitly addresses "heirs" as a category — the clearest textual statement yet that the Durrel-descended Ley-Heir mechanism is being deliberately ended, not merely interrupted.

Institutions. Central authority dissolves into self-governing provinces; law is issued as revocable charters that sunset unless renewed. The Null Compact (Consent, Reversibility, Witness) spreads as civic baseline. Historians mark the incoming epoch as the Age of Forgotten Kings — a deliberate reversion toward a truer medieval world where borders are human, not hummed. This inaugurates that age and sets up Campaign XXII: maps redraw around roads, rivers, oaths, and appetites, not leylines; would-be rulers must persuade, not resonate.

Canon status: follows the character register directly; this campaign closes the Concord Afterglow era and sets up the eventual 1,000-year jump into 4CE.
