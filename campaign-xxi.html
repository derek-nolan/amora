Timeline A · Campaign XXI

The Last Key

Threshold of the Age of Forgotten Kings

8650–8660 ARV Primary location: Evermere — Annex V, Sable Gate

Opening World State

A generation after the Vault Unbound, the world still runs almost entirely on habit — clocks that keep unnervingly precise time, ledgers that think they're law, and people who've long since mistaken calm for consent. In Evermere, the Collegium's "Unbirth" project attempts to end recurrence for good by printing a final chant through sanctioned presses at Annex V. Beneath the Sable Gate cisterns, a broken order — the Nulls — argue the cycle shouldn't be destroyed at all, only taught to choose.

Party & Key Figures

  • Talen Marric PC Custodian; carrier of a live Veil shard, wears old Custodian/Dominion "atonement gear." Led the Red Choir inversion and speaks the Severance.
  • Seren Valor
    Former Warden Captain — protective, possessive, and the one who says "steady." Steadies Talen through the Severance's aftershock.
  • Rook Halvern
    Ex-Custodian vows-master; Null contact and ritual technician. Inverts the rune-logic during the Red Choir Gambit.
  • Councilor Avar
    Null council lead in Evermere; convenes the quiet ring-rite for the Severance.
  • Collegium Annex V
    The press guild whose presses were meant to enable the Unbirth rite.

Act-by-Act

The Unbirth Plates

Answering rumours of sanctioned erasures, Talen and Seren trace a relay from the basilica vaults all the way to Annex V. In the cisterns beneath the Sable Gate they court the Nulls — not really an order, more a memory that simply refuses to die — and learn the Unbirth is a print-based kill-switch aimed squarely at the Heart-Veil itself. The Null ethic — Consent, Reversibility, Witness — reframes Talen's own private vows with Seren as a choice rather than a chain.

The Red Choir Gambit

A coded dead-drop leads them to Sable Gate and the Nulls' own contact, Rook. Together the three attempt the Red Choir Gambit: carry a Veil Core Fragment to the plate altar itself and invert the printing chant mid-run. Talen speaks the counter-verse, Rook inverts the rune-logic, and Seren pins the fighting core — three clean successes in a row that dust the plate and turn the Unbirth into harmless static. The city exhales. In the ash, the ley leaves behind a sign: the Null sigil — two circles with a deliberate break — recognition of intent, not obedience.

The Pedestal Question

At the Null council, Talen submits the shard to the pedestal's question — "What do you want of me?" — and receives a vision in answer: the cycle exists only because the world forgets. It will always mirror the actor's intent, never merely their words. With the Collegium regrouping and the whole world listening, the council asks Talen to make the actual decision: let the pattern sleep, sever it outright, or teach it.

Severance

In the cistern chamber, the Nulls raise a quiet ring-rite. Talen hesitates just long enough to feel the ley's new rhythm settle — two notes no longer at war with each other — and then speaks a single line: "No chains, no heirs, only choice." The Heart-Veil doesn't shatter. It unwinds. Threads lift like spider-silk; the old heartbeat simply stops. Bells fail without any compulsion behind them; ledgers remain merely ink; the entire Ley-Heir mechanism collapses. Seren steadies Talen through the aftershock. The room doesn't kneel. It agrees. The world's future returns, fully, to mortals.

World State After Campaign XXI

Geography. Evermere's Annex V is re-tooled as the Hall of Paper. Sable Gate Cisterns are confirmed as the Null council's ritual floor.

Magic. No new ley-heirs are made; the Taren/Zethyr recursion recedes to legend, not machinery — the campaign's central claim is that the cycle of recurrence, traced from Campaign II through every echo since, is genuinely over as a mechanism, though the pattern itself (per Campaign XXII's opening) is about to prove it can still recur without any mechanism at all. Magic becomes personalised: small workings answer intent and focus, while grand rites require community consent or heavy prices.

The Durrel Line. No Durrel figures directly, though the Severance Talen speaks explicitly addresses "heirs" as a category — the clearest textual statement yet that the Durrel-descended Ley-Heir mechanism is being deliberately ended, not merely interrupted.

Institutions. Central authority dissolves into self-governing provinces; law is issued as revocable charters that sunset unless renewed. The Null Compact — Consent, Reversibility, Witness — spreads as civic baseline. Historians mark the incoming epoch the Age of Forgotten Kings — a deliberate reversion toward a truer medieval world where borders are human, not hummed. This inaugurates that age and sets up Campaign XXII directly: maps redraw around roads, rivers, oaths, and appetites, not leylines; would-be rulers must persuade from here forward, not resonate.

Canon status: follows the source account directly; this campaign closes the Concord Afterglow era and sets up the eventual 1,000-year jump into 4CE.
