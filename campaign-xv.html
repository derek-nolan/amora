Timeline A · Campaign XV

Neon Concord: The Regent and the Voice

Third Cyberpunk Age

8210–8215 ARV Primary location: Highever, Inheritance Reclaimed (Tarenus)

Opening World State

Decades after the Cataclysm of Campaign XIV reshaped maps and institutions. After centuries oscillating between crown, creed, and clause, the city learns to keep order by proof on camera rather than prophecy or panic — this is the source material's own explicitly-named "Third Cyberpunk Age," the first of the four cyberpunk eras this site names directly rather than reconstructs.

Act-by-Act

The Black Ledger

A Verris Blackbook surfaces: Dripline payments, flare crews, and a House attempt to legalize remote audit copies via a "3-B good-cause affidavit." The Red Lattice faces a compliance squeeze. Dareth works rooms on camera; Valen holds the perimeter; Rell blocks 3-B in committee.

Card + Law + Aegis

The Concord Charter passes; badge unification aligns Wardens and Aegis. Valen is Regent de facto; Dareth is named Civic Consort & Voice as the Addendum formalizes proof-first ritual. The Concord Charter codifies Card + Law + Aegis: on-premise consent is baseline; any copy requires judge's ink; Sanctuary remains invite-only. The Concord Addendum establishes five optics that stabilize crowds: Witness Hand, Line-of-Sight, Three Counts, Exit Shown, and Auric Anoint.

The Golden Knife & the Crown

Stability warrants freeze Regency slush; Vesc counsel admits authoring 3-B. A blackout strike fails when a portable, judge-inked feed restores Line-of-Sight. Daylight ceremony confers the Regency. An assassination attempt (choir-knife) is beaten on rooftops and canals. Echo Burn — a plaza resonance test — splits the crowd between Voice and Regent; Valen's cadence carries. The Highever Crown / Chorus-Key is seized under shared registry by optics, not decree.

Neon Concord Finale

Underharbor tribunal exposes Old House finance and the cost of the Regent's consolidation. In the private rite, Dareth must choose to Yield the crown forever or end the count. He speaks: "Return my voice." The Chorus-Key binds to the Voice alone; the plaza holds to law without a throne. Valen steps down, accepting lifetime penitent service as Warden-Prime emeritus, barred from the Voice's periphery unless summoned. Dareth later learns Valen ordered Harbor Tom's "quiet hold" — he cannot kill him, and consigns him to ink and service instead.

World State After Campaign XV

Geography. Highever functions as the ley-crown and civic rite hub; Inheritance Reclaimed (Tarenus) as the administrative/commerce core — the two-city structure that Campaign XVI extends into a full corridor.

Magic/Law. The Concord Charter and Addendum (Five Optics) become baseline municipal law. Unified Cadence combines Wardens and Aegis into one procedural sequence. This is not a new magic system so much as a new civic technology for managing the same underlying leader/consort pattern the Durrel bond established millennia earlier — proof before power, replacing miracle and mandate.

The Durrel Line. No Durrel figures directly, but the campaign is explicitly framed as "a civic descendant of Dominion pageantry" — the same leader/consort grammar first established by Zethyr and Taren, reinterpreted here as proof before power rather than metaphysical compulsion.

Institutions. Warden-Prime is reframed as procedural service, not throne; the Regent's office is functionally dissolved. Old Houses are indicted on paper — no martyrs, no raids. This campaign inaugurates the mature Third Cyberpunk Age that Campaign XVI extends from city to corridor and coast, and which Campaign XVII will eventually see collapse.

Canon status: follows the character register; this campaign inaugurates the mature Third Cyberpunk Age.
