The Citadel of Inheritance

The Chains of Inheritance

Second Cyberpunk Era begins — Obedience, Authority, Trust, and the Cost of Choice

7420–7421 ARV Primary location: The Citadel of Inheritance, Western Surge Outpost, Menge Expanse

Opening World State

Ten years after Kael Durreth's consolidation of the Republic in Campaign XII, the Republic of Amora stands united but hollow. Its prosperity and order rest entirely on the Harmony Network — a vast leygrid that mirrors civic emotion and obedience — and citizens wear symbolic Oath collars as marks of loyalty under the Council's largely unseen governance. The Citadel of Inheritance now rises over the thawed Menge Expanse as the Republic's capital, and beneath it old Choir-era technologies still hum, forgotten directives stirring awake in the ley-lines. The Chain — a metaphysical echo of devotion and command, thought dormant since Kael Durreth's original stewardship — returns as a self-aware rhythm of command woven directly through the ley itself, and the Republic's illusion of freedom starts to fracture.

Party & Key Figures

  • Rian Leth PC Oath Guard Initiate who becomes the Chain's Stabiliser Node — young, attuned to the Chain, displaying perfect obedience until emotion disrupts command. His empathy becomes the key to severing the Council's control.
  • Calen Vorr
    Captain, Sentinel Officer; assigned to test and oversee Rian's synchronisation. Becomes his protector, commanding officer, and moral compass, and leads the operation that ultimately frees the Chain.
  • Commander Seraphine Vale
    High Command Liaison overseeing the Chain Project's military containment. Initially loyal to the Council, later reveals her covert plan to destroy the system's integration. Vanishes after the public broadcast, presumed in exile.
  • Speaker Lira Durrel
    Council Chair of Inheritance; believes obedience is essential to peace. Orders Rian and Calen's Resonance Interrogation, attempting to prove total civic control is achievable. (Source spells this "Durell"; normalized to Durrel here.)
  • Kael Durreth (Legacy)
    The absent Prime Steward and original architect of the Age of Inheritance. His harmonic code remains embedded in the leygrid; whether he intended control or liberation stays uncertain, and his legacy divides the Republic long after his own campaign ended.

Act-by-Act

The Festival of Harmony

Fifty recruits kneel in a rain-lit ceremony as Commander Seraphine Vale seals their Oath collars. Mid-ritual, Councilor Rethan dies in a burst of sigil-light — and the Chain awakens for the first time in a century, binding every recruit present in shared resonance. Rian hears its whisper directly: "Steady. Kneel. Remember."

Barracks Confidence and First Resonance

Rian meets Calen Vorr, a senior officer assigned to oversee him. Their resonance synchronises almost immediately, forming what the Chain itself recognises as a genuine Anchor-Vector link. Rian learns the Chain amplifies authority through emotion, not cruelty — and the bond between the two men grows into real mutual trust even as fear spreads through the ranks around them.

The Hall of Memory Investigation

Examination of Rethan's corpse turns up a Choir-era clasp engraved with the words "Obedience is Remembrance." The Chain's signature traces back to Dominion-era technology, and the Council responds by initiating full secrecy protocols around the discovery rather than disclosing it.

Training and Conflict

Rian undergoes formal obedience trials, and the Chain proves it responds only to authentic intent, not rank or authority. Calen begins covertly undermining the Council's methods, teaching Rian to trust instinct over command even as the Council orders full integration of the Chain into the Harmony Network.

Western Surge Incident

The Chain manifests as a full storm consuming the Western Surge Outpost, a frontier facility on the edge of the Republic. Rian stabilises the resonance not through control but through emotional recall — remembering Calen's own cadence — providing the campaign's first real proof that empathy can override command signals the Chain itself was built to enforce.

Resonance Interrogation

The Council seizes Rian and Calen for a formal Resonance Interrogation, forcing Rian to kneel under direct Council command to prove the Chain can enforce total obedience. Calen intervenes, reasserting control through tone alone to protect him — overriding the Chain's own hierarchy in the process. The Council confirms its obedience thresholds and begins planning mass integration across the Republic.

Counter-Order and Escape

The Chain implants a directive of its own: "Remove the vector." Rian very nearly attacks Calen under its influence, but regains control through trust rather than force. In secret, Seraphine makes contact and reveals a failsafe hidden beneath the Hall of Memory.

The Descent and Choice

Rian and Calen descend into the Anchor Vault beneath the Hall, where the Chain speaks to them in borrowed voices. Faced with a genuine moral choice — destruction, surrender, or liberation — they choose to sever the Council's control over the Chain while sparing its existence entirely, preserving something rather than erasing it.

The Reckoning

The truth is revealed publicly: the Chain exposed as the Republic's hidden mechanism all along. Rian tells the world plainly, "We don't know if Kael Durreth was complicit." Citizens wake to silence for the first time in generations — an unfamiliar, genuine freedom.

Final World State

Rian Leth survives, his status afterward largely unknown but remembered publicly as a figure of reform and conscience. Calen Vorr survives and goes on to found a new doctrine of voluntary service. The Chain itself becomes autonomous and neutral, awaiting new interpretation rather than commanding anyone. The Council of Inheritance disbands entirely. In a quiet epilogue north of the Menge Expanse, Rian and Calen settle beside a forge and a river, teaching settlers that discipline isn't obedience and that silence can be its own kind of freedom — no collars, no hierarchies, only rhythm: hammer, breath, flame.

World State After Campaign XIII

Geography. The Citadel of Inheritance remains the Republic's capital but loses its centralised authority; local assemblies and guild coalitions rise to replace it. The Menge Expanse stays thawed and habitable, its wild ley currents now forming visible auroras called the Chainlight Veils.

Magic. The Chain — revealed as a surviving Choir-era relic, the same technological lineage destroyed back in Campaign V — is severed from central command rather than destroyed outright. The leygrid stays active but ungoverned; the Chain persists as a neutral phenomenon, responsive to emotion but no longer obedient to command. This is the fifth recorded Severance in the record, and the first to leave the underlying structure intentionally intact rather than eliminating it.

The Durrel Line. Speaker Lira Durrel represents the name recurring as antagonist rather than protagonist this time — proof the pattern isn't deterministically heroic, just recurring. Kael Durreth's legacy, absent but still embedded in the leygrid itself, divides the Republic long after his own campaign ended; fringe scholars begin quietly searching for his original code, suspecting it may have held a hidden safeguard all along.

Institutions. The Oath Guard fractures into Loyalists, still bound to whatever remains of Council doctrine, and Independents led by Calen Vorr, focused on rebuilding infrastructure without obedience protocols of any kind. The word "Harmony" itself becomes politically taboo. The Citadel becomes the hinge city connecting past dominion to later Concord systems, laying groundwork for the Third Cyberpunk Age's obsession with law made visible rather than merely commanded — directly seeding Campaign XV.

Canon status: follows the source account directly, including its full timeline, faction detail, and epilogue. "Lira Durrel" and "Kael Durreth" appear as "Durell"/"Dureth" in some source passages — both normalized here per the site's spelling decisions. Per the source's own canon integration notes, no campaign after this one is set earlier than 7430 ARV, allowing time for the Republic's fracture to have historical ripple effects.
