The Emperor Reforged

Age of Renewal

c. 1900–2000 ARV Primary location: The Confluence, rebuilt Silver Bastion

Opening World State

Long after Edrin: the world has tried republics and reforms, and old symbols are returning in new clothes. The Age of Renewal begins with a promise that Amora can recover the strength of empire without recovering its cruelty. Silver Bastion rises again for a time, as does the temptation it represents.

Act-by-Act

The Confluence Rises

The Age of Renewal begins with a promise that Amora can recover the strength of empire without recovering its cruelty. Coren Veyne becomes the public face of that promise: charismatic, commanding, and convinced that old symbols can be made clean by better intentions. Feran Durrel, the Heartbearer beside him, is not merely a consort or witness — he's an explorer, a guardian, and the human counterweight through whom Coren tries to prove that power and love can rebuild rather than consume.

Chronicle and Resistance

Halvern Quill chronicles the return of Silver Bastion and the strange convergence of old Heart and Veil structures, while Selora and other resistance-minded scholars watch the new order for signs of repetition. The rebuilt Evermere kingdom prospers, Highever becomes a pilgrimage ruin, and the Verge Archipelago emerges from a world already reshaped by catastrophe. Yet the Confluence gathers authority around Coren faster than its safeguards can grow.

The Pattern Recognised

Coren seeks order without cruelty; Feran seeks discovery without surrendering his conscience. Their bond is pragmatic, admiring, and deeply personal, but it carries the old danger — one man increasingly treats unity as proof of legitimacy, while the other keeps hoping devotion can steer ambition. The age doesn't simply repeat Taren and Zethyr; Coren and Feran make different choices, for different reasons, in a world that knows the old cautionary tale. That's what makes their failure important. Amora learns that benevolence doesn't neutralise dominion — it can merely make dominion easier to accept.

World State After Campaign IV

Geography. Silver Bastion rises again as the Confluence's seat. Evermere prospers as a rebuilt kingdom. Highever becomes a pilgrimage ruin rather than a functioning centre of power. The Verge Archipelago is confirmed as a lasting feature of the post-Tempest map.

Magic. No new magical system is introduced this campaign — the Confluence works by gathering old Heart and Veil symbolism together under Coren's authority rather than creating anything new. That reuse of old power, rather than invention, is precisely the age's warning.

The Durrel Line. Feran Durrel carries the line as Heartbearer and consort to Coren — a further generation past Edrin, still shaping political power through the same commanding/anchoring pattern, now consciously aware of the history it risks repeating.

Institutions. Coren's project half-succeeds: infrastructure and peace improve, but the pattern is recognisable — one will, many lives shaped around it. The Confluence of old symbols Coren assembles becomes raw material for later ages' experiments in law and magic.

Placement in canon. Follows Campaign III's Severance precedent — Coren represents the opposite temptation, restoration rather than refusal. Later ages read him as a warning: even benevolent dominion can calcify into oppression. This campaign closes the mythic-age cluster (Campaigns I–IV) before Amora begins translating its oldest temptations into systems: code, theology, archives, civic law, and machine infrastructure.

Canon status: follows the character register directly; no unresolved names in this campaign.
