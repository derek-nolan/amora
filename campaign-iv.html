Timeline A · Campaign IV

The Emperor Reforged

Age of Renewal — "Through Power, Eternity"

c. 1900–2000 ARV Primary location: Evermere, Tarenus, the Confluence

Opening World State

Long after Edrin Durrel's Echo Chain and its Severance in Campaign III, the world has already tried republics and reform once — and old symbols are starting to creep back into fashion, wearing new clothes. Into that opening steps Coren Veyne, the "Emperor Reforged": charismatic, ambitious, and genuinely convinced he can inherit the Dominion's old imagery without repeating its exact cruelty.

Party & Key Figures

  • Coren Veyne
    Emperor Reforged; rises on the promise of "order without cruelty" and the creed "Through Power, Eternity." Assembles the Confluence — a synthesis of old symbols and limited Veil-tech meant to give people safety and prosperity without the Dominion's full weight.
  • Feran Durrel PC Heartbearer; explorer and guardian who becomes Coren's human counterweight — the partner Coren searches for who can mirror the old Zethyr role without fully surrendering to it.
  • Allies, rivals, and would-be consorts
    A rebuilt Evermere and Tarenus provide the political stage Coren's ascent plays out on.

Act-by-Act

The Ascent

Coren rises through symbols, law, and carefully limited Veil-technology rather than conquest — using each to give people genuine safety and prosperity, at least at first. His search for a partner who can share the burden without simply submitting to it leads him to Feran, and the two begin building something neither empire nor republic: a kinder power structure assembled from the bones of the old one.

The Confluence

What Coren actually builds is the Confluence — a working synthesis of old Dominion symbolism, Heartwarden ritual, and careful technological restraint. Infrastructure improves. Peace holds. And yet the underlying pattern remains recognisable to anyone who knows the old histories: one will, many lives quietly shaped around it, however gently.

The Half-Success

Coren's project half-succeeds by any practical measure — the world under him is genuinely better off than it was. But later ages read his reign as a warning rather than a triumph: even benevolent dominion can calcify into something oppressive over time, no matter how carefully it starts out kind. The Confluence itself, the specific synthesis of symbols Coren assembles, becomes raw material generations of later scholars and reformers draw on for their own experiments in law and magic.

World State After Campaign IV

Geography. Evermere and Tarenus are both rebuilt and serve as Coren's political stage; the Confluence itself becomes a recognised body of doctrine and symbolism rather than a physical location.

Magic. Veil-technology is deployed here in carefully limited, restrained form for the first time since Campaign II's Dominion — proof the old power can be wielded with real restraint, even if restraint alone doesn't prevent the underlying pattern from recurring.

The Durrel Line. Feran Durrel represents the Heartbearer role opposite Coren's Emperor — a continuation of the commanding/anchoring dynamic first established in Campaign I, now explicitly framed as a search for balance rather than pure devotion.

Institutions. The Confluence becomes a lasting body of political and magical doctrine, cited and adapted by later ages even after Coren's own reign ends — a recognisable ancestor of ideas that resurface across the Second Cyberpunk Era.
