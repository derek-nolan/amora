Timeline A · Campaign VII

The Warden Executor

Age of Theocracy I — "Through Law, Salvation"

c. 5800 ARV Primary location: The Gate Central institution: Executor Office / Warden Inquisition

Opening World State

After the rediscovery of older magic in Campaign VI, Amora swings away from the Collegium's uncertainty and back toward religious certainty. Spellcraft is increasingly treated as something that should be licensed, interpreted, and disciplined. The Gate becomes the capital of the emerging Wardens' Theocracy, while the new Executor Office and its Inquisition turn moral judgement into state machinery.

Elian Dorn rises inside that transition. The surviving campaign summary calls him the Warden Executor; a later lineage reconstruction describes him as an "Executor of Faith" and a Heartbearer-like archetype. That later label is interpretive rather than proof of a literal Durrel recurrence. What is secure is more important: Elian believes law can save people from the chaos left by earlier magical ages, and he helps build a state that increasingly treats obedience as evidence of virtue.

Party & Key Figures

  • Elian Dorn PC
    "The Warden Executor." Spiritual and political leader of the Wardens; associated with the founding of the Executor Office and the authorisation of the Inquisition. His creed, "Through Law, Salvation," captures both the campaign's idealism and its danger.
  • The Executors
    The new office that converts Warden doctrine into enforceable law. Initially framed as guardians against magical abuse; increasingly empowered to define heresy, investigate citizens, and silence forbidden practice.
  • Inquisitors, archivists, and underground dissidents
    The people who make the system function — or quietly resist it. Individual names have not survived in the accessible record, so this page does not invent a supporting cast.

Act-by-Act

Founding the Executor Office

Campaign VII is about institution-building rather than a single relic hunt. Elian and the Wardens formalise the Executor Office at the Gate and authorise the inquisitorial structure that will define the age. Law and faith are deliberately fused: a Warden judgement can become a civic judgement, and magical regulation becomes a matter of salvation rather than merely safety.

Codifying Sin

Elian's motto — "Through Law, Salvation" — becomes the argument for increasingly detailed regulation. The campaign deals with codifying sins, building investigatory circuits, regulating magical practice, and deciding what should happen to pre-Choir or pre-Warden knowledge the new state regards as dangerous. A later magical history summarises the period as Grace through Regulation: miracles become licensed incantations, while heresy becomes punishable by silence.

When Protection Starts to Resemble Dominion

The central tension is not whether every law is malicious. Many of the dangers the Wardens respond to are real. The problem is what happens when one institution decides it alone can distinguish safe knowledge from corrupt knowledge, proper devotion from heresy, and disciplined citizens from dangerous ones. Warden decrees begin to echo old Dominion patterns even though the language is salvation rather than empire.

Reform or Resistance

The campaign's surviving summary frames the choice clearly: reform the theocracy from within its own structures, or resist it from outside. The record does not preserve a single dramatic scene that settles that argument. Instead, the end-state shows the answer the institution itself reached — the Warden state hardens rather than loosens.

The Precedent Problem

Elian's age leaves behind more than inquisitors. It leaves precedent. Archives and edicts from Campaign VII survive long after the theocracy, repeatedly cited by later judges, reformers, and tyrants. That legacy is what makes Campaign VIII possible: Sirian inherits not an empty state but a century of accumulated rules that appear contradictory, discretionary, and ripe for codification.

World State After Campaign VII

Geography. The Gate is the capital of the Wardens' Theocracy. The Executor Office is an institution based there; it is not itself a city or capital.

Magic/Law. Spellcraft is increasingly institutionalised as law. Veil-associated practices and older magical traditions are rewritten into bureaucratic formulae, while forbidden knowledge is subject to inquisitorial control.

The Durrel Pattern. No confirmed Durrel descendant or literal Ley-Heir drives this campaign. Later archivists classify Elian as a Heartbearer-like successor because he recasts older ideals of protection into law; that is retrospective interpretation, not proof of reincarnation.

Institutions. The Executor Office and Inquisition are entrenched. The state is now strong enough that Campaign VIII's question is no longer whether theocracy will exist, but whether its immense body of law can be made coherent without making its control even stronger.

Canon status: the strongest surviving sources agree on Elian Dorn, the Gate, the Executor Office, the Inquisition, the creed "Through Law, Salvation," and the consolidation of a Warden Theocracy. One compact source phrases the founding as "Elian and Dorn," but the dedicated campaign locator and character register consistently treat Elian Dorn as one person; this page follows that reading rather than inventing a second founder. Named supporting NPCs and exact scene-by-scene play are not recoverable.
