Timeline A · Campaign VII

The Warden Executor

Age of Theocracy I

c. 5800 ARV Primary location: The Gate

Opening World State

The world swings back from the Reawakening's scholarly ambiguity toward religious certainty. The uncertainty of distributed custody frightened many people more than the old darkness had — Kael Durren's Collegium Equilibria proved that magic could be chosen rather than commanded, but choice without structure feels like exposure to a frightened population. The Gate becomes capital of the Wardens' Theocracy.

Party & Key Figures

  • Elian Thorne — Completed (doc)
    Paladin and early Executor; founds the Executor office at the Gate with Dorn Kael. Motto: "Through Law, Salvation." Becomes both spiritual and political authority.
  • Dorn Kael — Completed (doc)
    Explorer and Executor figure; helps transform expeditionary necessity into permanent institution.

Act-by-Act

Founding the Executor Office

Elian Thorne and Dorn Kael answer the age's fear by founding the Executor office at the Gate, believing discipline can preserve the fragile return of magic. Their circuits of investigation protect settlements from relic cults, predatory sorcery, and the chaos of unregulated ley-work — at first, genuinely useful work born of genuine danger.

Power Gathers

Elian becomes both spiritual and political authority, while Dorn helps turn expeditionary necessity into permanent institution. Inquisitors, archivists, and local officials learn to speak of salvation through law. The language is different from Taren's Dominion, but the direction of travel is familiar.

Discipline Without Limits

The campaign's central struggle isn't whether law is needed, but whether any institution built to save people can accept limits on its own certainty. Reformers work inside the order; dissidents build routes around it. By the end of the age, the Wardens have become a theocracy, and the tools created for emergency protection have become the ordinary machinery of obedience.

World State After Campaign VII

Geography. The Gate becomes capital of the Wardens' Theocracy — the first time this city is named as a major seat of power in the record.

Magic. No new magical system is introduced; instead, the campaign shows institutional discipline being layered on top of Kael Durren's chosen-magic doctrine from Campaign VI, gradually converting free choice back into structured obedience.

The Durrel Line. No Durrel figures directly, though the pattern the line originated — devotion hardening into hierarchy — plays out institutionally rather than personally this campaign.

Institutions. The Warden state hardens into a rigid system that will later be codified by Sirian in Campaign VIII. Archives and edicts from this time survive long after the theocracy falls, haunting later republics with "precedent" — a direct link forward to Campaign X's Post-Veil Reformation, which has to dismantle exactly this kind of institutional residue.
