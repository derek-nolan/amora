Timeline B · Campaign B10

The Quiet Rule of Brevair

A city does not fall to conquest if it can be taught to prefer its chains

Age of Founding, Reforged

146–147 ARV (~10 years after B9) Primary location: Brevair, House Valecour

Core Premise

A story of private corruption within a noble house that ends as the quiet remaking of an entire city. Delryn Vale enters the campaign as a politically vulnerable acknowledged bastard inside a broken Brevair, surviving within House Valecour's Veil household. What begins as survival becomes transformation — into the public face, private instrument, and eventually keystone conduit of Rhalen Valecour's rise. By the campaign's end, Brevair hasn't been conquered. It's been made to function too well under corruption to imagine refusing it.

Party & Key Figures

  • Delryn Vale / Valecour PC Human Shadow Sorcerer; an acknowledged bastard raised within House Valecour, transformed over the campaign into the public face and keystone conduit of Rhalen's rise.
  • Rhalen Valecour
    The hidden architect of House Valecour's engineered fall and rebirth; eventual ruler of Brevair in all but title, and Delryn's husband by the campaign's end.
  • Seran Mordane
    Veil captain and Delryn's early handler; recognises both his political usefulness and his susceptibility to control. Briefly seizes command of the house before being outmanoeuvred and poisoned by Rhalen.
  • Malrec
    Former Veil master within the house; killed by Seran's overreach during a demonstration meant to test Delryn's conditioning.
  • Lady Ilyra Venn, Brother Caldus, Captain Vey, Marr
    The Veil chapter's inner council — political stabiliser, disciplinarian, enforcer, and archivist respectively. All poisoned by Rhalen once he no longer needs the chapter as cover.

Act-by-Act

Act I — House Valecour / Seran's Hold

Delryn enters the campaign inside Seran Mordane's private orbit — a Veil captain who recognises both his political usefulness and his vulnerability to deeper control. Seran begins a sustained program of conditioning, built through private ritual, controlled environments, and escalating Veil influence; he doesn't simply seduce Delryn, he studies and reshapes him deliberately. Delryn's Shadow Sorcery begins responding differently under that influence — colder, more obedient, more tied to Seran's presence specifically. Seran is training him not just for private submission but for future use in elite rooms and civic negotiation, and Delryn slowly starts to recognise that Seran's ambitions reach well past ordinary service within the house — he wants Malrec's place.

Act II — The Revelation of Rhalen

Delryn discovers Seran was never the true architect. Hidden letters found in the old music room and revelations from the west wing reveal Rhalen Valecour — not dead, not absent, but the long-hidden intelligence behind the house's engineered collapse. Seran and Rhalen had been aligned from the beginning: they planned Delryn's father's death together, and Delryn himself was never the true end point of Valecour succession, only its public face. Rhalen even reveals Delryn wasn't his father's bastard at all, but a taken-in blood relation hidden inside a more politically convenient story. Rhalen begins privately claiming Delryn in his own right — a colder, more strategic hold, distinct from Seran's more devotional approach. Delryn's loyalty splits between the two men for the first time.

Act III — Malrec's Fall

Malrec tests Seran publicly, in a demonstration meant to assess how far Delryn's conditioning has actually progressed. It becomes a succession event instead. On Seran's cue, Delryn's Veil-altered shadow strikes, and Seran uses the moment to kill Malrec outright and openly overreach, claiming both Delryn and the room in front of witnesses. For a brief window, Seran looks poised to become the new master of the house — and the house itself now sees Delryn as something more dangerous than novice stock: a strategic dependent, fully capable of lethal action under the right hand's direction.

Act IV — Rhalen's Coup

Seran's triumph doesn't last. Rhalen, already embedded far deeper in the house's architecture than anyone realised, reveals he's been quietly shaping Delryn's responses from underneath Seran's visible control the entire time — and moves to remove Seran exactly as efficiently as Seran removed Malrec. He poisons him. As Seran dies, Delryn runs to Rhalen rather than staying at his deathbed, and Seran understands, too late, that the deeper design moved past him a long time ago. Rhalen openly claims the room, the house's future, and Delryn's direction. The campaign shifts here from a house-corruption story into a state-formation story.

Act V — The Dark Architecture

With Seran gone, Rhalen stops merely using the house's existing structures and starts building new ones — deepening Delryn's dependency, expanding his own control over the house's physical and political architecture alike, and turning Valecour into a graded map of who answers to whom. Two private spaces at the centre of this become permanent fixtures: one built around appetite, routine, and directed control, the other built around reduction and total obedience without comfort. Delryn's responses within them feed back into Rhalen through the bond itself, making him steadily more invested — and more dangerous — the deeper it goes.

Act VI — The Chapter Dies

Rhalen eliminates what remains of the Veil chapter's inner council — not through chaos, but through poison and patience. The surviving core, including Ilyra, Caldus, Vey, and Marr, are gathered under the guise of consolidation and quietly killed. Delryn is made to witness it, cementing exactly who Rhalen is once there's no higher structure left above him. Rhalen formally abandons any pretence that the chapter was ever the real end goal — it was scaffolding, nothing more. With the chapter gone, Brevair itself becomes the true object of rule, and the city, not the house, is now the actual field of play.

Act VII — The Keystone Bind

The campaign's deepest private architecture culminates in a permanent structural bond joining Delryn's public role, his private conditioning, his altered shadow magic, and his civic usefulness into one convergent whole. Delryn stops being merely an instrument and becomes the central conduit through which Rhalen's entire mode of rule actually functions — public rooms across Brevair begin resolving back to Rhalen more efficiently specifically because they run through Delryn first. Rhalen can no longer honestly treat him as replaceable.

Act VIII — Rule of Brevair, and Marriage

With Brevair effectively his, Rhalen no longer needs the chapter, and increasingly no longer needs the fiction that what exists between him and Delryn is purely strategic. He proposes formal marriage — not to soften the bond, but to give lawful, permanent shape to what the private architecture has already made true. Brevair accepts them publicly as a symbol of stability; widow houses, merchant channels, chapel funds, and guard structures all quietly reroute through Rhalen's systems underneath that public face. In one unguarded scene, Rhalen allows himself real vulnerability with Delryn for the first time, revealing that what he feels has genuinely outgrown strategy alone — though his instinct, even then, is to answer it with more control rather than less. The wedding itself plays publicly as stability and privately as full political annexation.

World State After Campaign B10

Geography. No new territory is openly conquered. Brevair's capital and House Valecour are established as major Timeline B locations for the first time, geographically intact but functionally remade under Rhalen's systems — the house's inner chambers become permanent, hidden infrastructure for future campaigns. (This site names Brevair's capital Corvane — see the Canon Ledger — since the source atlas leaves it explicitly undetermined.)

Magic. Delryn's Shadow Sorcery becomes fully Veil-aligned and integrated into the house's own architecture — control that once lived in individual rooms has expanded into something closer to a whole-chapter-wing system. The bond between Rhalen and Delryn becomes one of the most powerful, and most dangerous, relational architectures anywhere in Timeline B.

The Durrel Line. Not directly involved — this campaign centres entirely on House Valecour and the Veil chapter operating within Brevair, geographically and narratively separate from the Durrel Ridge storyline of Campaign B9.

Institutions. Rhalen becomes Brevair's quiet administrative ruler — civic, widow, merchant, and chapel channels all function through him without his ever needing a royal title. House Valecour is restored as a legitimate public mask for a darker internal order, and the marriage between Rhalen and Delryn formally legitimises their shared position within Brevair's political structure. The campaign closes not with battle or divine rupture, but with something colder: a city that works, a ruler who needs no crown, and a marriage built on a foundation neither partner is free of.

Canon status: follows the source account, with terminology adjusted to keep the site's content at a general-audience level while preserving the political and coercion-vs-consent themes central to the story. The source material is significantly more explicit in its depiction of the private control dynamics between Delryn, Seran, and Rhalen than reflected here — see the Canon Ledger for the site's approach to this material.
