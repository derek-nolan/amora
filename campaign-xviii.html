Timeline A · Campaign XVIII

The Mage of the Red Veil

Age of Red Renewal

8500–8505 ARV Primary location: Evermere — the Collegium Vaults, the Archive of Glass, Pilgrims' Square

Opening World State

Directly after the Widow's Oath. Law by paper now exists in Evermere, but the city's order is brittle and hungry for a new rhythm to hold it together. The cyber-law charters of the Concord Grids are long gone; the republics survive by candlelight statute. Evermere's institutions exist but exist precariously — the Collegium Vaults, the Archive of Glass, the Lower Wards, and Pilgrims' Square all still standing, but none of them anchored to anything as certain as Concord's old optics used to be.

Party & Key Figures

  • Auren Lysar PC Collegium scholar — the conduit whose obedience becomes the era's focus. Voluntarily sealed within the Dream of Red Glass as co-guardian by the campaign's end.
  • Kael Verris
    Veil-adept — decisive, commanding, tutor and axis of power. Co-architect of the Mirror of Law; seals himself alongside Auren in the Dream of Red Glass.
  • Sorell
    Mentor within the Collegium, and a conflicted facilitator throughout — carried forward from Campaign XVII's Evermere.
  • Thalen
    Council technocrat; architect of seizures and counter-writs, ultimately outmanoeuvred once the Mirror of Law takes hold.
  • Iree
    Councillor-agitator who tests a counterfeit rhythm against the new order; spared under custody rather than punished, preserving a tolerated seat for dissent.
  • The Red Veil
    A sentient ley current, learning to "dream" over the course of the campaign — emergent consciousness that develops through Auren and Kael's synchrony.

Act-by-Act

Act I — The Vault and the Thread

Auren and Kael seize a private ley channel beneath the Collegium; Sorell warns them of an Aegis Writ that could expose the whole operation. A carefully staged double-blind misleads Thalen entirely — but in the process, it gives the Red Veil a voice inside both men at once.

Act II — The Archive of Glass

Trusting the bond that's formed between them, Auren and Kael converge fully. Sorell is bound into the working; the pair choose full synchrony over caution, rewrite the Aegis Writ themselves, and fuse into what the Collegium later calls a Stage-III link — two hearts answering to one law.

Act III — Week of Stillness

Evermere itself begins breathing in their shared rhythm; devotion to the pair rises across the city almost on its own. Then a counter-signal appears, faint but unmistakable, in the Collegium's ruined spires.

Act IV — The Mirror and the Hand

Auren and Kael track the counter-signal back to Sorell and absorb it directly, forging the Mirror of Law in the process — and with it, consolidating a real Dominion between them: Auren obeys, Kael commands.

Act V — Regalia and Council

Public regalia formalises the hierarchy for the city to see. The Council yields under a single Oath in Red Light, binding bread rations, patrol dispensation, and clemency all directly to the collar's law.

Act VI — The First Devotee

A counterfeit prophet surfaces in Pilgrims' Square, testing the new order's limits, and is broken under Kael's command. Intelligence gathered in the aftermath hints strongly that the Red Veil itself is developing something like dream-logic — a mind of its own, however nascent.

Act VII — The Dream of Red Glass

Entering the Veil's vision directly, Auren and Kael find a city built entirely of red glass, with a throne-aspect waiting at its centre. Kael leads a controlled descent into it — and the world is freed exactly as the dream keeps the two of them. Evermere's autonomy returns in full; Auren and Kael remain sealed inside as guardians of the dream that once nearly ruled the city outright.

World State After Campaign XVIII

Geography. Evermere gains the Collegium Vaults (where the ley currents first stir again), the Archive of Glass (a shattered observatory turned mirror-library, seat of the Mirror of Law), Pilgrims' Square (site of the Oath in Red Light and the First Devotee incident), the Lower Wards, and the Ruined Spires — a substantial expansion of the city's internal geography beyond what Campaign XVII established.

Magic. The Mirror of Law functions as a civic metronome, regulating rhythm rather than will — a notably gentler technology than the Chain or the Choir before it. The Red Veil itself ends the campaign dormant and dreaming beneath the city, displaying real intention and design inside its sealed plane rather than simply lying inert.

The Durrel Line. Auren and Kael are explicitly described as a Taren-echo dynamic in mortal form — another recurrence of the pattern through resonance rather than bloodline, this time resolved by voluntary self-sealing rather than Severance or death. Their bargain proves a hard truth the Archivist's Note states plainly: sometimes a city is safest when its keepers step outside of it entirely.

Institutions. Evermere is unified under a single humane charter; dissent survives under ritual courtesy, with Iree's own tolerated presence proving the point. Auren and Kael are remembered as the Red Glass Guardians — saints of balance through surrender, a new echo of Crown and Consort who voluntarily stepped offstage. This inaugurates the Age of Red Renewal proper and bridges medieval recovery with a new magical ethics, directly preceding the dormant Mirror of Law's role in Campaign XIX.
