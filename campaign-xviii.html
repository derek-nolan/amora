Timeline A · Campaign XVIII

The Mage of the Red Veil

Age of Red Renewal

8500–8505 ARV Primary location: Evermere, the Archive of Glass

Opening World State

Directly after the Widow's Oath. The Age of Red Renewal is born from the ashes of the Age of Disconnection — the cyber-law charters of the Concord Grids are long gone, and the republics survive by candlelight statute and the fragile consensus of memory. Evermere stands at the centre of this imperfect order: a city of archives, half-drowned spires, and dreams that refuse to die.

Party & Key Figures

  • Auren Lysar Voluntarily sealed within the Dream of Red Glass as co-guardian
    Collegium scholar and obedient vessel; Red Veil synchronist. Primary counterpart to Kael Verris — a Taren-echo dynamic in mortal form; chosen obedience channels civic order.
  • Kael Verris Voluntarily sealed within the Dream of Red Glass as co-guardian
    Veil-adept and architect of command; citywide cadence setter. Mentor/lover dynamic with Auren; designs the Mirror of Law regimen.
  • Sorell Marcone Maintains the republic under ritual calm after the sealing
    Senior magistrate and conflicted mentor/ally, carried forward from Campaign XVII. Sponsors Auren and Kael; mediates Council compliance with the Mirror of Law.
  • Thalen Outmaneuvered when the Mirror of Law and Oath in Red Light take hold
    Council technocrat and seizure proponent; attempts to capture the cadence for the Council; foil to Sorell.
  • Iree Spared to preserve dissent within the new order
    Councillor-agitator and counterfeit resonance tester; opposes consolidation, becomes a tolerated critic post-sealing.
  • The Red Veil Dormant, dreaming; bound as treaty through the Dream of Red Glass
    A sentient ley current, emergent consciousness that learns via Auren/Kael synchrony and seeks order through them.

Act-by-Act

A Private Ley-Thread

In the candlelit ruins of Evermere, the Collegium discovers that the ley still breathes. Auren Lysar, a scholar marked by restraint, and Kael Verris, his superior and opposite, uncover a private ley-thread beneath the Vaults. Their synchrony calls forth the Red Veil, a living current that learns through them.

The Mirror of Law

What begins as study becomes governance. In rewriting the city's Aegis Writ, they forge the Mirror of Law, a crimson lens that transforms obedience into civic order. Evermere's people fall into perfect rhythm: law, breath, and mercy united.

The Oath in Red Light

As their influence spreads, devotion becomes dominion. The Council yields under an Oath in Red Light that binds every ration and pardon to their new cadence. Only when the Veil begins to dream through them do Auren and Kael understand the price of control.

Sealing the Dream

To spare the waking world from perfection, Auren and Kael descend into the Dream of Red Glass, sealing themselves within it. Evermere wakes free — calm, imperfect, alive. The mirror remains, pulsing faintly with the rhythm of its absent keepers.

World State After Campaign XVIII

Geography. Evermere gains the Collegium Vaults (where the ley currents first stir again), the Archive of Glass (a shattered observatory turned mirror-library, seat of the Mirror of Law), Pilgrims' Square (site of the Oath in Red Light), the Lower Wards, and the Ruined Spires — a substantial expansion of the city's internal geography beyond what Campaign XVII established.

Magic. The Mirror of Law functions as a civic metronome, regulating rhythm rather than will — a notably gentler technology than the Chain or the Choir. The Red Veil itself is confirmed as dormant, dreaming beneath the city, a consciousness learning mercy rather than an inert relic.

The Durrel Line. Auren and Kael are explicitly described as a Taren-echo dynamic in mortal form — another recurrence of the pattern through resonance rather than bloodline, this time resolved by voluntary self-sealing rather than Severance or death.

Institutions. Evermere is unified under a single humane charter; dissent survives under ritual courtesy. Auren and Kael are remembered as the Red Glass Guardians — saints of balance through surrender, a new echo of Crown and Consort who voluntarily stepped offstage. This inaugurates the Age of Red Renewal and bridges medieval recovery with a new magical ethics, directly preceding the dormant Mirror of Law's role in Campaign XIX.
