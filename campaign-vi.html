Timeline A · Campaign VI

The Restorer

Age of Reawakening — “Through Memory, Rebirth”

c. 5500 ARVPrimary theatre: Evermere Collegium and its buried Choir-era ruins

Opening World State

Thousands of years after the Choir collapse, civilisation has rebuilt on top of systems most people no longer understand. The Age of Silence is remembered through broken machines, dead infrastructure, and stories about a time when magic itself seemed to vanish. Evermere has become a centre of scholarship precisely because so many layers of the old world survive beneath it.

Campaign VI begins when archaeology stops being safely historical. Beneath the Evermere ruins, Kael Duren uncovers a surviving Heart Shard — a fragment linked to the original Medallion tradition. Its return proves that the oldest magical structures were never wholly erased by Choir, Severance, or time.

Party & Key Figures

  • Kael Duren PC
    Archaeologist-priest, distant blood descendant of the old Durrel line, and the figure later remembered as the Restorer. His central problem is not how to seize the Heart Shard, but how to let magic return without creating another single point of control.
  • Evermere Collegium scholars
    Researchers, archivists, and custodians who help authenticate the Shard and debate whether rediscovered magic should be studied openly, restricted, or distributed through shared stewardship.
  • Rival claimants
    Political, mercantile, and religious interests that see the Heart Shard as power, legitimacy, or profit rather than a dangerous inheritance.
  • The missing counterpart
    The lineage record notes an unknown or unmanifested Veilbearer possibility around this age, but no stable surviving campaign account identifies such a figure. The absence is retained as an archival gap rather than filled with a reconstructed NPC.

Act-by-Act

The Heart Shard

Kael’s discovery reintroduces active ley resonance to a world that has spent centuries treating the great magical systems as history. The Shard reacts as an ancient Heart object rather than a Choir machine. Its existence reconnects the present to the first campaigns without requiring a new Taren/Zethyr recurrence.

Kael’s creed — “Through Memory, Rebirth” — is not a call to restore the old world exactly. Memory is valuable because it gives the living enough information to choose differently.

Who Owns Returning Magic?

The campaign’s central conflict is custody. Scholars want time to understand the Shard. Rival factions want access. Some see a chance to rebuild civilisation faster; others see proof that divine or dynastic authority should return with magic.

Kael’s response is shaped by the entire preceding history. Taren centralised power because he believed his judgement was uniquely necessary. Edrin hid power because he feared recurrence. The Choir tried to distribute power so completely that individuality disappeared. Kael searches for a fourth option: shared custody without shared mind.

Relationship-Bound Magic

The returning ley does not behave like Choir infrastructure or imperial command. Kael’s work increasingly suggests that old magic responds to relationship, vow, trust, and chosen stewardship. Later summaries describe the campaign as one of the first deliberate experiments with consent, distributed guardianship, and multiple custodians holding pieces of authority rather than one central bearer.

This is not yet the modern resonance system of Timeline B, and later ages should not retroactively impose Anchor/Thread terminology here. The significance is ethical and institutional: magic can be powerful without requiring a sovereign.

The Collegium Equilibria

Kael’s work becomes the root of the Collegium Equilibria tradition. The Shard is not converted into a throne or a universal engine. Instead, the rediscovery becomes the basis for study, guarded access, and a doctrine that magic should follow choices and vows rather than decree.

The reawakening also has unintended consequences. Once ley resonance returns to the world, later movements will reinterpret it through religion and law. Campaign VI therefore succeeds on its own terms while opening the conditions from which the Warden theocracies of the next age can eventually emerge.

World State After Campaign VI

Geography. Evermere’s Collegium becomes a major centre for recovered magic and archaeological study. Beneath it remain layers of Choir-era technology distinct from the older Heart Shard Kael recovers.

Magic. A fragment of the original Heart Medallion tradition is active again. Ley resonance begins returning after the long silence. The campaign establishes that ancient magic can be approached through shared stewardship and relationship rather than machine totality or imperial command.

The Durrel Line. Kael is recorded as a distant blood descendant rather than a direct recurrence of either founder. The old pattern is present as history and inheritance, but the surviving campaign record does not confirm a paired Taren/Zethyr echo in this generation.

Institutions. The Collegium Equilibria grows from Kael’s work. Its doctrines of study, distributed custody, and choice become stabilising precedents used by later Collegium traditions — even as later religious institutions selectively reinterpret the same return of magic into law and hierarchy.

Canon status: the detailed lineage record and the existing campaign page place Kael Duren and the Heart Shard at the centre of Campaign VI. Some compact chronological directories instead compress the wider “Return of the Ley” and later Warden resurgence into this same slot, naming figures associated with the following theocratic age. Those summaries are treated here as broad era-level compression rather than a reason to replace Kael’s campaign. The Shard’s rediscovery, Kael’s distant Durrel descent, the rebirth of ley resonance, and the Collegium Equilibria are the strongest surviving Campaign VI-specific points.
