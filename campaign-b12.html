Timeline B · Campaign B12

The Warden of Meaning

Home reclaimed honestly, and the difference between desire chosen in truth and desire renamed by corruption

Age of Founding, Reforged

150 ARV Primary location: Durrel Ridge, Tarenus's Vault

Core Premise

Jack Durrel begins the campaign in the aftermath of Marcus's death — no longer trapped beneath the immediate threat that shaped his recent life, but not yet fully restored to ease either. He returns to the Ridge carrying grief, relief, damage, and a real hunger to find out whether life after Marcus can be genuinely real rather than just survived. With Silver Bastion lost to Cameron in Campaign B11, Durrel Ridge's importance as a Light stronghold has never been higher. The campaign opens not with a fortress assault but with return: can Jack truly inhabit his own inheritance, rather than merely stand near it?

Party & Key Figures

  • Jack Durrel PC Durrel heir, Ridge operative, and survivor of Marcus's shadow. Named Warden of Meaning by campaign's end.
  • Taren Durrel
    Ridge leadership, carried from Campaign B9. His reserve toward Jack — present even after Marcus's death — finally breaks down once Jack shares what the Vault showed him.
  • Jorah
    Arrives during the campaign and is gradually allowed into Jack's private orbit; survives the scrutiny of the Ridge, Dena's attention, and Marek's loyalty. Confirmed real and structurally important by the Vault's revelation.
  • Marek & Dena
    Jack's core circle. Dena becomes a key emotional anchor and continuity figure for the household; both prove themselves as much a part of Ridge life's practical architecture as its field operations.
  • Marcus
    Confirmed dead in the true line before the campaign's present, but structurally vital as the road Jack's life might have taken — the Vault's later vision draws its force entirely from showing what he would have made of Jack and the Ridge had he lived.
  • The cadet-branch Durrel child
    A surviving seven-year-old, brought to the Ridge from a destroyed holding late in the campaign — the next generation of the Durrel story, now physically present at the Ridge.

Act-by-Act

Act I — Jack Comes Home / the House Reopens

The campaign opens with return, not crisis. Jack re-enters Ridge life after Marcus's death and begins the difficult work of letting life feel warm again — Dena's care, Marek's loyalty, the team's presence around him. A major early turning point is his decision to reopen the house at the Ridge that had sat emotionally closed since Marcus, and before that since it was first left to him. What could have remained a private mausoleum becomes a communal house instead: the team moves in, bedrooms are claimed, domesticity and ordinary humour start to return. This isn't filler — it's the campaign's first real answer to the Veil. Home has to be reclaimed in truth before it can be defended in war. Jorah arrives during this same phase and is gradually let into Jack's private orbit.

Act II — Scouting, Household Life, and Jorah

The campaign's middle stretch balances two rhythms at once: active Ridge work and the slow deepening of household trust. Jack and the team keep scouting and running operations around the Ridge's perimeter, proving this isn't a static healing interlude — the Ridge is still a resistance centre, and everyone living under its protection is also part of its practical function. Jorah becomes central here, not as a romantic addition to the cast but as someone who survives real scrutiny — the Ridge's, Dena's, Marek's — and earns his place the ordinary way: meals, rooms, jokes, trust, the slow possibility of chosen intimacy that doesn't feel like manipulation, a deliberate contrast to the coercive dynamics that defined Campaigns B10 and B11.

Act III — Marcus Dies / the Old Threat Breaks

Marcus's death is confirmed in the true line, and it's one of the campaign's major thresholds — not simply removing a villain, but changing the entire emotional and strategic climate of the Ridge. With him gone, the old immediate route of manipulation and fear is broken for good. The house can be reopened honestly. Jorah can exist in Jack's life without being instantly overshadowed by the possibility of Marcus reclaiming the same ground. Taren's old distance from Jack begins losing its justification. The campaign doesn't pretend Marcus's damage simply disappears with him, but it locks in something crucial: one road is now closed for good.

Act IV — Tarenus's Vault

Late in the campaign, Jack descends into Tarenus's Vault with Jorah and is shown not an arbitrary nightmare, but a true possible future: Marcus surviving, taking Jorah's place, entering the reopened house and Ridge life through false intimacy, and slowly corrupting Jack through home, hierarchy, surrender, and private trust. The vision's point isn't that desire itself is dangerous — it's that the Veil's deepest power is the ability to rename real desire into false law. Tarenus — the same Vault entity Taren Durrel reclaimed back in Campaign B9 — confirms Marcus is dead in the true line and that Jorah is exactly who he says he is, then reveals a buried truth of the Durrel line itself: its deepest function was never simply rule or paladin authority. It is the power to define and defend meaning inside bonds, vows, surrender, and witness. Jack is named a Warden of Meaning.

Act V — Taren and Jack Align

After the Vault, Jack tells Taren what he saw — Marcus taking Jorah's place, the danger that had hovered unseen over the reopened house, and Tarenus's revelation. He leaves out anything unnecessarily intimate, but gives Taren the truth that actually matters. Taren, who'd stayed more distant with Jack than he should have even after Marcus's death, finally stops treating him as a lingering risk and starts treating him as exactly what the mission needs — one of the Ridge's own answers to the Veil. Then the campaign makes its final move: a surviving seven-year-old cadet-branch Durrel child is brought to the Ridge from a destroyed holding. Dena receives them first; Taren acknowledges them formally. The Ridge stops being only a site of defence and becomes, visibly, the place where the next generation will actually be raised.

World State After Campaign B12

Geography. Durrel Ridge re-centres as command-centre, lived household, sacred continuity-house, and the most plausible surviving Light stronghold after Bastion's fall in Campaign B11 — its importance has grown directly proportional to what's been lost elsewhere. Jack's inherited house at the Ridge changes from symbolic inheritance-space into a genuinely lived household.

Magic. Tarenus's Vault demonstrates a new capability — showing a true possible future, not just responding to bloodline and witness as it did in Campaign B9. This expands what's understood about the Vault's nature significantly.

The Durrel Line. Jack Durrel is named Warden of Meaning — a title distinct from Taren's Warden role established in Campaign B9, confirming the Durrel line is developing multiple specialised roles rather than a single hereditary office. Taren inherits the Ridge and Warden-seat; Jack inherits the truth-knife inside corrupted bonds — their roles no longer compete, they complement. The cadet-branch child brought to the Ridge at the campaign's end represents the next generation directly, and can now age into a future protagonist while being meaningfully shaped by Taren, Jack, Dena, and the household around them.

Institutions. The arrival of the cadet-branch child locks in the next generational handoff: the Ridge is now raising what comes next, not merely defending what remains. The campaign's central thesis becomes explicit here: the Ridge wins not only through military resilience, but by staying genuinely inhabitable — domesticity, warmth, humour, and chosen family aren't lesser than the war-story material, they're the actual field corruption gets resisted or invited on.
