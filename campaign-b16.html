Core Premise

Roughly 180 years after the Crownless War closes the Ridge Arc, B16 opens the next major historical phase of Timeline B — moving the central mystery away from the immediate Light-Veil struggles of the Ridge/Brevair era and toward the Great Seals themselves, their individual Warden lineages, forgotten pre-modern infrastructure, and the deep history of the Oath Wars and the War of Ash and Glass. It introduces Nicholas "Nick" Soren, an ordinary Collegium student who discovers his family descends from an unfallen Western branch of the Icewardens that separated from the northern tradition before the branch that became the Icebinders fell, long ago given over entirely to preservation rather than power. Inheritance without destiny; blood versus authority; knowledge before opening; the difference between willing submission and manufactured compliance; deciding what kind of dangerous person one becomes.

Party & Key Figures

  • Nicholas "Nick" Soren PC Human sorcerer, twenty years old, studying at Highever's Collegium. Begins the campaign believing his fascination with ice geometry is a personal quirk; ends it recognised as Custodian of the Western Icewarden branch.
  • Jarek Flint
    Highever carpenter, Nick's lover, and — after a hidden foreign claim is severed — the recognised Western Way-hand. Genuinely in love with Nick; also secretly conditioning him through enchanted personal items, entirely apart from their consensual dominance/submission relationship, which is real and independently Nick's own.
  • Professor Halden
    Senior Collegium archaeologist and Nick's mentor. Believes evidence and ownership matter more than institutional possession or destiny.
  • Oren Voss, Esmond Krail & Tamsin Brack
    Nick's Collegium friends — sociable, methodical, and practical respectively — who become the independent witnesses Elara's own philosophy insisted on.
  • Maraine Soren
    Nick's mother; bearer of Icewarden blood deliberately never presented for recognition. Loving and protective without controlling Nick's choices.
  • Elara Soren
    Nick's deceased grandmother and the previous Western Custodian. Dead before the campaign begins, but her records, warnings, and deliberate withholding shape nearly everything Nick discovers.
  • Deren Flint
    Jarek's uncle and a former legitimate Way-hand, severed from a foreign attachment by Elara decades earlier.
  • Edric Harl
    Retired Rimeholt surveyor, kidnapped by Vale's operation; his decades-old surveys confirm Elara knew of the deeper complex and deliberately kept it buried.
  • Vale
    Outsider leader of a hostile Rimeholt cell, hunting the buried complex using modular black-gold tools and a corrupted claim on Way-hand authority. Killed by Nick after capture.

The Story

The Forgotten Warden

Nick begins B16 as an ordinary Highever Collegium student, his fascination with ice geometry read as personal aesthetic preference rather than anything stranger. That changes when a Warden statue recovered during rebuilding in 339 ARV reacts to him directly — a Frost-associated sigil fragment linking to his own tattoos, imagery of eight distinct Seals arranged around an empty human-shaped centre, and two fragments of text: another comes, not yet remembered. Professor Halden becomes directly involved; Nick's friends Oren, Esmond, and Tamsin start noticing something has changed. The statue's imagery establishes B16's central magical correction — the current Seal registry is Heart, Flame, Stone, Storm, Life, Shadow, Light, and Frost, each with its own Warden lineage — and reinforces that the Icebinders were never the Frost tradition's neutral original guardians, only their fallen form.

Jarek Flint

Nick meets Jarek in a Highever tavern; Jarek crosses the room first, sensing something unusual about Nick without being able to name it. Their relationship develops quickly into something genuinely mutual — Nick discovers a real, entirely his own enjoyment of yielding control to Jarek, restraint and being dressed or physically contained among the things he wants and can still say no to, which he does once, respected instantly. But as Jarek makes or supplies Nick's clothing and restraints, he begins layering small enchantments into them — weighting Nick's already-genuine feelings of safety, calm, and the pull to return to Jarek when frightened, rather than creating those feelings from nothing. Nick never consents to the added influence and doesn't know it exists. Jarek's love is real: he cooks for Nick, protects him, imagines a shared future. The manipulation is equally real. Neither cancels the other.

The Western Road

Nick's investigation moves beneath the Collegium, and the secrecy it demands costs him something real — Oren's genuine hurt at being made to feel unreasonable for noticing obvious lies, repaired only once Nick tells the truth and Halden responds not with confiscation but with one simple rule: nobody works alone with dangerous artefacts. Clues then carry Nick west, into a concealed network of routes and archives that answers directly to his own magic: Icewarden lineage acknowledged. Living line unbroken claim. His family isn't merely interested in frost magic — they descend from an unbroken Western branch of the Icewardens, who split from the northern line before its fall, carrying no portion of the Frost Seal and choosing preservation over power instead. Recognition, not blood alone, determines the Custodian; Nick is eventually formally recorded as Custodian, Western Branch, with no claim over the northern tradition. His grandmother Elara, the previous Custodian, shaped nearly everything he finds — including a deliberate choice to never present his own mother Maraine for recognition, proof that custodial blood creates possibility, never obligation.

The Way-hand

At Return VI, Nick and Jarek discover Jarek's uncle Deren was once a legitimate Way-hand before a foreign attachment compromised him decades earlier — severed by Elara, with his cooperation, and survived. The same evidence increasingly suggests Jarek's own power is entangled with, not wholly derived from, a foreign claim of its own. Jarek chooses severance; Nick helps stabilise it. The claim is confirmed cut — but Jarek doesn't lose his magic, and the system recognises him: Way-hand appointment valid. What the severance also proves is harder: the enchantments affecting Nick don't disappear, meaning Jarek was shaping them himself all along, not channelling something else's influence. Understanding this, he continues anyway — some of his later conditioning growing more deliberate now that he can no longer blame an outside voice. The enchantments cannot manufacture Nick's sexual consent, which remains entirely his own throughout; what they weight instead is broader trust and dependence, narrowing his choices without replacing who he is.

Eight Seals

Nick presents himself formally at Silver Bastion as Custodian of the Western branch, explicitly claiming no authority over the northern Frost Seal or Heartwarden standing — a distinction the Bastion accepts without argument. The visit confirms directly that the Heart Seal remains beneath Silver Bastion, never transferred to Durrel Ridge even after the Bastion's own later fall, and clarifies Heart's own role as moderation rather than command. Research back in Highever then uncovers a huge relief apparatus surveying exactly eight Great Seals — its missing central component explicitly not evidence of a ninth. A further fragment from the original Warden statue names an unresolved figure: bearer of no Seal, keeper of the roads between them. A final clue points somewhere deeply personal — Nick's own hometown, Rimeholt.

Homecoming

Rimeholt is revealed as a former Western Icewarden refuge, its households and craftspeople expanding over generations into an ordinary green town that entirely forgot its own founding purpose. Before the final descent, Maraine's letters reach Nick — you were never born owing the dead anything — alongside an operational warning that someone has been searching for the Western line since before his birth. Travelling toward Rimeholt, Nick and Jarek are ambushed; Nick kills his first attacker in combat, then deliberately kills the second after capture and interrogation, a line Jarek doesn't pretend is the same as the first. Reunited with his mother, Nick watches her interrogate Jarek socially rather than magically about what he actually wants — and warm to him once he answers plainly. A black-gold marker planted in the family house by local handyman Bren Talwick is discovered; Nick offers him a way out rather than killing him, and lets him go with a warning: this is Icewarden territory.

Vale's Cell

Nick traces the hostile network to an old mill, dismantling its relay and rescuing surveyor Edric Harl, kidnapped for refusing to hand Vale everything he knew about a deep alignment Elara had once deliberately struck from the public record. At North Quarry, Nick and Jarek defeat Vale himself — who used black-gold power directly, unlike the modular cores his operatives relied on. Interrogated, Vale confirms he found the buried structure before Nick ever mattered to him personally, and speaks of a "First Road" with no independent corroboration anywhere in the record. Judging him too dangerous to release, Nick kills Vale after the interrogation — his third death in under two days, and the one that makes Jarek tell him plainly he's become dangerous and now has to decide what kind of dangerous man he intends to be. When Vale dies, his black-gold connection doesn't transfer to Nick or Jarek; it withdraws downward, toward whatever lies beneath.

Beneath Rimeholt

Nick and Jarek investigate only the buried complex's outermost chamber before deliberately stopping, summoning witnesses rather than pressing on alone — a concealed stair beneath Elara's own garden shed carries her explicit warning: deep access requires witnessed opening. Her final letter separates three things people keep trying to collapse into one — blood that can find, a Custodian who can recognise, and authority that permits passage — and asks Nick to bring people who value the record over the discovery, who might physically stop him if necessary. He obeys. When Halden, Oren, Esmond, and Tamsin arrive and the group finally descends together, a witness mechanism distinct from both Custodian and Way-hand authority confirms their presence and allows inquiry before demanding any opening at all — vindicating Elara's whole philosophy that knowledge doesn't require passage. The deeper room proves to be an ancient transit chamber, not a Seal of any kind, containing material dating to the War of Ash and Glass and a damaged warning: do not permit the bound... to choose the passage. A faint black-gold trace still reaches through the chamber's own geometry rather than being rooted in the room — proof the phenomenon wanted the connection, not the room itself. Nick severs it, feeling something distant resist for a moment before the link snaps. Only the Rimeholt access is confirmed cut, not the phenomenon.

What Home Means

Judging the risk of future exploitation greater than the value of preserving a functioning ancient mechanism beneath his mother's garden, Nick chooses to destroy its function — after Halden and Esmond thoroughly document everything — while keeping the archaeological chamber intact for study. Before leaving, he finally learns where Maraine had been living: Belisar, a harbour city on Nareth Isle, the first named inhabited land beyond Vanmere's own map frame, chosen specifically because the family had no contacts there. Back in Highever, Halden orders everyone to write independent accounts before any shared theory forms, protecting the record from any single perspective hardening into fact. The campaign closes at home, in the workshop and loft Nick and Jarek now share in practice — Jarek taking control of the evening in the way Nick genuinely wants, while quietly reinforcing the very enchantments the campaign has spent thirty-five acts examining. Jarek sees the magic respond. Nick doesn't. The love is real. The domestic life is real. The submission is genuinely desired. And the contradiction at the heart of it all remains exactly that — fully intact, and entirely unexposed.

Full Campaign Record — all 35 acts

Act-by-Act

Act I — The Forgotten Warden

An ordinary Collegium student, Nick becomes fixated on a Warden statue recovered during rebuilding in 339 ARV. It reacts to him — a Frost-associated sigil fragment linking directly to his own tattoos — and presents imagery of eight distinct Seals arranged around an empty, human-shaped centre, with fragments reading "Another comes" and "Not yet remembered." Professor Halden becomes directly involved as Nick's friends Oren, Esmond, and Tamsin start noticing something's changed. The statue's imagery establishes the current working registry: Heart, Flame, Stone, Storm, Life, Shadow, Light, and Frost — each with its own Warden lineage, superseding older, incompatible accounts — and reinforces that the Icebinders were never the neutral original Frost guardians, only their fallen form.

Act II — Jarek Flint

Nick meets Jarek in a Highever tavern; Jarek crosses the room first, having sensed something unusual about Nick without being able to identify it. Physical attraction is immediate and their relationship develops quickly — Jarek a working carpenter rather than a scholar, practical, confident, and comfortable with magic Nick finds more dangerous than anything taught at the Collegium. Nick initially understands him, for practical purposes, as a warlock.

Act III — A Real Dynamic, and a Line Crossed Beside It

Nick and Jarek's relationship becomes intimate quickly. Nick genuinely enjoys a private submissive role with Jarek — restraint, leather, being dressed or physically contained by him, letting Jarek take control when invited — none of it created by magic; it's real and entirely his own. He also remains capable of saying no, and does, in a scene where Jarek immediately respects it without argument. But as Jarek makes or supplies clothing and restraints for Nick, he begins layering small enchantments into them — reinforcing feelings of safety in what Jarek chose, calm at his touch, and a pull to return to him when frightened or uncertain. The magic develops alongside their genuine relationship, which gives Jarek an easy way to rationalise what he's doing: Nick already likes these things, so Jarek tells himself he's only deepening what's already there. Nick never consents to the added influence and doesn't know it exists. Jarek's feelings are genuine — he cooks for Nick, protects him, imagines a future with him — and the manipulation is equally genuine. Neither erases the other.

Act IV — The Hidden Collegium Work

Nick's investigation moves beneath the Collegium into older structures, recovering objects connected to Jarek's magic. He conceals the full extent of his discoveries from Halden and his friends, fearing institutional control — a secrecy that genuinely damages his friendship with Oren, who resents being made to feel unreasonable for noticing obvious lies. When Nick admits what he's done, Halden doesn't confiscate his discoveries or remove him from the investigation — he imposes one simple principle instead: nobody works alone with dangerous artefacts.

Act V & VI — The Western Route and the Western Icewardens

Clues direct Nick west, toward a concealed network of routes, markers, and archive sites responding directly to his Frost-linked magic. An archive acknowledges him outright: Icewarden lineage acknowledged. Living line unbroken claim. His family isn't merely interested in frost magic — they descend from an unbroken Western branch of the Icewardens, who split from the northern line before its fall, carrying no portion of the Frost Seal and no claim to Vyrn's Spring, choosing preservation over power instead. Recognition, Nick learns, isn't inherited by blood alone: blood continues possibility; recognition determines Custodian. He's eventually formally recorded as Nicholas Soren, Custodian — Western Branch, with no authority over the northern Frost Seal or the wider Icewarden tradition.

Act VII — Elara Soren and the Family Record

Nick learns his grandmother Elara was the previous Western Custodian, and that his mother Maraine carried the bloodline but was deliberately never presented for recognition — bearer-capable, not presented, by design, choice preserved. Custodial blood creates possibility, not obligation. The records also reveal collateral Soren descendants Elara deliberately obscured, with explicit warnings against pursuing them: kinship is not authority; authority is not safety; do not confuse absence of recognition with absence of blood. Nick chooses not to go hunting distant relatives simply because they might carry the blood.

Act VIII — Return VI, Deren Flint, and the Way-Hand

The Western network reveals a second office alongside the Custodian: the Way-hand, who opens, releases, moves, and returns, where the Custodian preserves, verifies, and braces — neither commanding the other. At Return VI, Nick and Jarek discover Jarek's uncle Deren was once a legitimate Way-hand before a foreign attachment compromised him decades earlier; Elara severed him from it, with his cooperation, and he survived. The same evidence increasingly suggests Jarek's own power is entangled with, rather than wholly derived from, a foreign claim. Jarek chooses severance; Nick helps stabilise it. The foreign claim is confirmed severed — but Jarek doesn't lose his magic. A deeper power remains genuinely his own, and the system recognises him: Way-hand appointment valid.

Act IX — What Severance Doesn't Undo

Severing the foreign claim reveals something important: the enchantments affecting Nick don't disappear, proving Jarek was shaping them himself all along, not merely channelling something else's influence. Understanding this, Jarek continues anyway — some of his later conditioning becomes more deliberate, since he can no longer blame an outside voice. The enchantments cannot manufacture sexual consent; Nick's desire for Jarek and their submission dynamic are real and remain his own throughout, and he can still resist anything that strongly conflicts with his values. What the magic does instead is weight broader trust, dependence, comfort, and decision-making — narrowing Nick's choices without replacing his personality. That distinction matters and stays intact through the rest of the campaign.

Act X — Silver Bastion and the Great Seals

Nick presents himself formally at Silver Bastion as Custodian of the Western Icewarden branch, explicitly claiming no authority over the northern Icewarden tradition, the Frost Seal itself, or Heartwarden standing — a distinction the Bastion accepts. This visit confirms directly that the Heart Seal remains beneath Silver Bastion, never transferred to Durrel Ridge even after the Bastion's later fall; Durrel Ridge holds important Heart-related structures and traditions, but not the Seal itself. Heart's own function is clarified as moderation, not command — it balances the wider Seal network rather than ruling it. The Bastion's own surviving Frostborn material gives only a faint response to Nick, reinforcing the campaign's central principle: identity is not authority.

Act XI — The Eight-Seal Survey Apparatus

Research uncovers a huge relief apparatus surveying Vanmere and the eight Great Seals — not a Seal itself, its missing central component explicitly not evidence of a ninth. Further work with the original Warden statue yields a new fragment: when eight stand apart, one must walk between — not warden of the ninth, bearer of no Seal, keeper of the roads between them. This figure's identity remains unresolved and must not be assumed to be the Western Way-hand simply because both involve movement. A final clue — "the road was kept where the name was forgotten" — points somewhere deeply personal: Nick's own hometown, Rimeholt.

Act XII & XIII — Rimeholt, and the Old Threat

Rimeholt is revealed as a former Western Icewarden refuge — its original Icewarden households, spouses, and craftspeople expanding over generations into an ordinary green western town that forgot entirely why it carried its own name. Before the final descent, Nick receives Maraine's letters: you were never born owing the dead anything, alongside an operational warning — do not take the black-and-gold road, even if it knows my voice. Someone had been searching for the Western line since before Nick's birth.

Act XIV — Ambush on the Road

Travelling toward Rimeholt, Nick and Jarek are ambushed by two men carrying instructions identifying Nick by description, route, and Elara's witness ring. Nick kills the first attacker in combat with Ray of Frost — his first kill — then deliberately kills the second after capture and interrogation, judging the risk of release too high. The interrogation identifies Vale's Rimeholt operation and its doctrine: blood finds the road; Hand opens. Jarek doesn't pretend the two deaths are the same.

Act XV & XVI — Reunion, and the Informant in the House

Nick reaches the Soren house and reunites with Maraine, who interrogates Jarek socially rather than magically about his intentions; he answers plainly that he wants an ordinary life with Nick, and she gradually warms to him. Investigation of the house uncovers a black-gold marker planted by local handyman Bren Talwick, working for Vale, who also stole family papers. Nick offers him a chance to leave Rimeholt and abandon Vale for good rather than killing him — and releases him, warning him plainly: this is Icewarden territory.

Act XVII & XVIII — The Old Mill and Edric Harl

Nick traces Vale's local infrastructure to an old mill, collapsing its black-gold relay network with his growing Custodian authority and capturing four operatives along with maps, coded communications, and payment records. The party rescues Edric Harl, a retired surveyor kidnapped by Vale after refusing to hand over everything he knew — decades earlier, Elara had found his old surveys of a deep buried alignment and deliberately ordered the depth struck from his public work, telling him: if anyone comes asking for the deepest line, don't tell them where it goes — ask them what they think opens it.

Act XIX & XX — North Quarry and Vale's Interrogation

Nick and Jarek assault Vale's excavation base at North Quarry, where most operatives rely on modular black-gold cores rather than integrated power — Vale himself doesn't need one. Defeated and interrogated, Vale confirms his research into Rimeholt's buried structure began before Nick became personally important to him; he found the site first, then went looking for people who might help open it. He speaks of a "First Road" — a term with no independent corroboration, one Elara herself treated as suspect — and describes failed attempts to create "false Hands" from corrupted copies of Way-hand authority. None survived.

Act XXI & XXII — Vale's Death

After the interrogation, Nick judges Vale too dangerous to release and kills him — his third death in under two days. Jarek doesn't tell him the deaths don't matter; he tells him, in effect, that Nick has become dangerous and now has to decide what kind of dangerous man he intends to be. The question stays open at campaign's end. When Vale dies, his black-gold connection doesn't leap to Nick or reclaim Jarek — it withdraws downward, toward the deeper buried structure. Whatever Vale's relationship to the phenomenon was, he wasn't its entirety.

Act XXIII & XXIV — The Outer Chamber and Elara's Guidance

Nick and Jarek investigate only the buried complex's outermost chamber before deliberately stopping — the construction is visibly different from later Western reinforcement, proving the Western branch encountered and modified an existing structure rather than building it. Rather than proceed, Nick summons his friends and Halden. Beneath Elara's garden shed, a concealed Western stair carries her own warnings: deep access requires witnessed opening; if pursued, do not descend. A letter from Elara draws the line between blood that can find, a Custodian who can recognise, and authority that permits passage — three things people keep trying to collapse into one. Her instruction is direct: bring witnesses who will disagree with you; bring someone who values the record more than the discovery; bring someone who will physically stop you if necessary. Nick obeys. The deeper door stays closed for now.

Act XXV & XXVI — An Ordinary Day, and the Witnesses

While waiting for his friends, Nick spends a deliberately ordinary day showing Jarek his hometown — the school, the market, the bridge, the fields — giving Jarek his first real sense of a life beyond his Highever workshop, though he doesn't decide to move. When Halden, Oren, Esmond, and Tamsin arrive, Nick tells them everything, including that he's killed three people, and the group methodically separates what's confirmed from what's merely Vale's own interpretation before anyone descends.

Act XXVII & XXVIII — The Witnessed Descent and the Transit Chamber

The six descend fully equipped, Maraine remaining above as contact. A witness mechanism distinct from both Custodian and Way-hand authority confirms the presence of genuine outside witnesses and allows inquiry before demanding any opening — vindicating Elara's whole philosophy that knowledge doesn't require passage. The deeper room proves to be an ancient magical transit chamber — not another Seal, not a hidden Frost Seal, not evidence of a ninth. The Western Icewardens encountered it already old, and variously watched, reinforced, restricted, or simply left parts of it alone.

Act XXIX — War of Ash and Glass Evidence

Inside the complex, Halden identifies material dating to the War of Ash and Glass or very close to it, including damaged imagery associated with both sorcerous and warlock traditions — dating the site without identifying who actually built it. A damaged warning survives only in fragments: do not permit the bound... to choose the passage. This ties the Rimeholt complex physically to the same catastrophe already established as the Badlands' true origin, without yet explaining the current black-gold phenomenon.

Act XXX & XXXI — What Black-Gold Wanted, and the Purge

A faint black-gold trace remains inside the chamber, reaching through the transport geometry itself rather than rooted in the room — proof the phenomenon wanted the connection the transit system offered, not the room for its own sake. What lies on the other side stays unknown. Using an Icewarden Field Knife and the same "does this belong?" principle he's used throughout, Nick severs the connection. Something distant resists for a moment — no voice, no name, no definite form — then the link snaps. Only the Rimeholt access is confirmed severed, not the phenomenon itself.

Act XXXII — Destruction of the Transit Mechanism

Judging the risk of future exploitation greater than the value of preserving a functioning ancient transport mechanism beneath his mother's garden, Nick decides to destroy it — but only after Halden and Esmond thoroughly document everything first. Nick severs the magical channels, Jarek breaks the mechanical joins, and Tamsin ruins the alignment components by hand, leaving the archaeological chamber intact for study while rendering its function permanently inoperable. Even Halden agrees the destruction is justified.

Act XXXIII — Maraine and Nareth

Before leaving, Nick finally asks where his mother had been living. Maraine reveals she spent much of her absence on Nareth Isle, west-southwest of Vanmere, in the harbour city of Belisar — chosen precisely because Elara had no known contacts there, letting her simply be Maraine Soren rather than a Custodian's daughter. She returned to Rimeholt because she missed home. Nick promises to visit, now that he knows where she's been.

Act XXXIV & XXXV — Return to Highever, and Home

The journey east is deliberately uneventful; back in Highever, Halden orders everyone to write independent accounts before constructing any shared theory, deliberately preventing any one perspective from becoming unquestioned truth. Nick and Jarek return to the workshop and loft they now share in practice. The ending is domestic: after the danger and revelation, Nick experiences the workshop as home, and Jarek takes control of the evening in the consensual way Nick genuinely enjoys. Beneath the tenderness, Jarek quietly reinforces the hidden enchantments he has built throughout — Jarek sees the magic respond; Nick doesn't. The campaign closes on its own central contradiction, unresolved and unexposed: the love is real, the domestic life is real, the submission is genuinely desired, and Jarek is nevertheless still secretly shaping the emotional architecture through which Nick experiences trust and safety. That remains a live thread, not a settled one.

World State After Campaign B16

Geography. Rimeholt is established for the first time as a major location — a former Western Icewarden refuge turned ordinary town, with the Soren house, the old mill, and North Quarry as its key sub-locations, and a forgotten Western route network connecting it back toward Highever through Return VI. Nareth Isle and its harbour city of Belisar are established as the first named geography beyond Vanmere's own map frame. The Badlands' origin in the War of Ash and Glass gains its first physical archaeological confirmation, far from the Badlands themselves.

Magic. The current Great Seal registry is confirmed as Heart, Flame, Stone, Storm, Life, Shadow, Light, and Frost — each with its own Warden lineage — superseding an earlier, incompatible working list. The Icewarden line is confirmed to include a third branch beyond the fallen Icebinders and the northern Frostborn: the Western Icewardens, who chose preservation over power generations before the northern fall. The Custodian and Way-hand offices are established as a specific Western pairing, not a template for every Seal lineage. A hostile, unidentified black-gold phenomenon is confirmed to interact with recognised Warden infrastructure by seeking connection and passage rather than raw power — its Rimeholt access is severed, its wider nature and origin still unknown.

Institutions. Silver Bastion confirms the Heart Seal was never relocated to Durrel Ridge, even after the Bastion's own fall — a standing clarification for how the two sites relate going forward. The Collegium's archaeological ethics — evidence and ownership before either confiscation or destiny — are reinforced through Halden's handling of Nick throughout.

The Soren Line. Nicholas Soren is formally recognised as Custodian, Western Branch — inheriting a name and a history, not a destiny, in the same spirit already established for House Durrel's own repeating names. Jarek Flint is recognised as the Western Way-hand, his own magic confirmed genuine independent of the foreign claim severed from it. Beneath the recognition and the genuine partnership, an unresolved and unexposed thread continues: real love, real desired submission, and real ongoing magical conditioning Nick has not consented to and does not know exists.

Canon status: follows the source account. The violation lies elsewhere entirely: without Nick's knowledge, Jarek magically reinforces associations between himself and safety, home, relief, and yielding.
