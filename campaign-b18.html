Core Premise

B18 is the first directly played Oath Wars prequel — not an origin story for the whole conflict, but a lived, ground-level look at what that war actually felt like in one city. It gives the Oath Wars a real geography (pre-Shattering Alderwick, one of the largest cities of its era, roughly eighty to a hundred thousand people) and a real emotional core: Hugh Bellamy, a street orphan Crown Hall saved, learning the difference between gratitude and blind obedience, while his new lover Adam Kersey is secretly reporting to a schismatic intelligence network without ever having been assigned to seduce him. B18 develops Crown, Sanctuary, and Liberty from inside the conflict, introduces the First Authority as ancient civic infrastructure rather than a Great Seal system, resolves the local Dane-Holt clandestine network completely, and gives Hugh and Adam a full first arc — while deliberately leaving Edmund Shaw and the wider Oath Wars unfinished for later campaigns to pick up.

What hasn't happened yet. B18 takes place before every later Amoran landmark its own characters cannot know as future history: the War of Ash and Glass hasn't begun (roughly six years ahead, c. -932 ARV); the Seven Refusal is roughly nineteen years ahead (c. -919 ARV); the Great Seals don't exist yet, their setting cycle still twenty-five years off (c. -914 to -913 ARV); Rimeholt doesn't exist as a settlement; the Badlands don't exist — the Alderwick Basin is fertile, densely settled farmland; and the Shattering itself is roughly thirty-three years ahead (c. -905 ARV). See the Ancient Chronology for the full dated sequence this campaign sits inside.

Party & Key Figures

  • Hugh Bellamy PC
    Human Paladin, Oath of the Crown, Level 1 → 3. A street orphan Crown Hall took in — earnest, practical, and shaped by hunger he never fully stops feeling. Develops Crown's central B18 doctrine: order matters because roads, grain, hearings, and shelter actually work, not because everyone obeys.
  • Adam Kersey
    Human Crown novice, Hugh's primary romance — genuinely in love, and genuinely a secret informant for Cavan Holt's clandestine network, recruited five months before the campaign. Neither fact cancels the other.
  • Ser Roland Firth
    Crown paladin, Third Banner field officer, Hugh's protective mentor — himself a former street-poverty Crown Hall rescue, which is exactly why he recognises Hugh's habits and refuses to let gratitude become ownership.
  • Commander Voss
    Senior Crown authority handling hearings and the Dane investigation — evidence-driven, careful about lawful limits, and the clearest voice distinguishing justified action from institutional overreach.
  • Mara
    Owner of The Three Spoons and the nearest thing Hugh has to a mother — fed him, gave him useful work instead of pity, and eventually pointed him toward Crown Hall's door.
  • Alric Dane Antagonist
    Former Crown figure and head of the compromised Alderwick clandestine network — values action over legitimacy, and eventually decides Shaw's own hesitation is just one more obstacle his network is entitled to bypass.
  • Cavan Holt / "Master Cavan" Antagonist
    Adam's recruiter and field handler, a former Crown quartermaster — recruits through genuine grievances, incremental secrecy, and the feeling of being useful, not through invented lies.
  • Edmund Shaw
    First Crown commander and major Crown schismatic — real responsibility for Barrowfen's pressure campaign, but proven to have been countermanded by his own network over the Founding Charter. Deliberately left unresolved at B18's close.
  • Edrin Kersey
    Adam's uncle, one of Shaw's original thirty-nine who left Crown Hall fifteen years earlier — still connected to Shaw, but beginning to doubt whether everything done in Shaw's name is actually Shaw's order.

Act-by-Act

Act I — Crown Hall, Hunger and Belonging

B18 opens inside a Crown Hall that's functioning, imperfect, and deeply important to Hugh — rookies sleep in shared dormitory bunks, meals are regular, and field banners carry real responsibilities. Hugh still hides bread from old habit; Roland recognises the pattern because he had the same one at fourteen. Their shared history produces one of the campaign's defining lessons: Crown Hall rescuing them creates gratitude, not ownership. Adam occupies the nearest bunk and has watched Hugh for months — mostly simple attraction, though a smaller part carries the knowledge that Holt may later ask about promising younger Crown personnel.

Act II — The Barrowfen Convoy

Hugh and Adam join Roland's Third Banner on a grain convoy toward Barrowfen — a deliberately mundane assignment that becomes an ideal first test of Hugh's emerging belief that authority should exist to make necessary systems work. The convoy is ambushed; Hugh fights with javelin, Lay on Hands, Bless, and longsword, consistently favouring nonlethal outcomes. At Barrowfen the Crown schism becomes concrete: First Crown interdiction has complicated grain delivery while Crown Hall, Sanctuary, and Liberty personnel dispute authority. The campaign later confirms Shaw genuinely approved the Barrowfen pressure — a lock that matters, since discovering Dane later countermanded Shaw doesn't retroactively absolve him of choices he actually made. Hugh briefly wonders whether Sanctuary suits him better before choosing to return toward Crown.

Act III — Hugh and Adam Choose Each Other

The attraction becomes explicit during mission downtime and grows quickly through two days of leave in Alderwick. Hugh enjoys a freely-chosen submissive role with a more dominant, possessive Adam — appeal that belongs entirely to Adam because Hugh chooses it, never to be confused with institutional obedience or Adam's secret activity. Adam's attraction and love are genuine; the network never assigned the romance, and he never uses sex to search Hugh's possessions or gain access to restricted records. He does continue reporting some of Hugh's political views and field facts, which is what makes the later betrayal real even though the relationship itself isn't fabricated — he privately draws his own line around what he won't pass on, but the omissions don't authorise the rest.

Act IV — The Southern Reservoir and Older Alderwick

B18 establishes pre-Shattering Alderwick as one of the largest cities of its era — roughly eighty to a hundred thousand people, walled around a major river system, kept alive by engineered water infrastructure that survives because engineers and administrators keep it running while oath orders argue above them. South of the city, an artificial reservoir sits behind a century-old dam; beneath the later pumping works lies older fitted-stone construction, including a buried circular hall and damaged lettering reading "…DERW…" A recurring mark — three concentric squares pierced by a vertical line — begins linking old water and crossing sites. B18 later identifies this as First Authority civic marking, explicitly not a Great Seal symbol, and explicitly not automatically the same network as Rimeholt's later transit works or B17's eight-around-centre apparatus. Similar age or construction doesn't mean one civilisation.

Act V — Westfield, Forced Oath, and the Refugee Fire

Eleven disputed Crown levy-sworn from Westfield create the campaign's strongest early oath test — Helena Veyne argues Westfield's claim, Talia Renn speaks for Liberty, Emric Sol for Sanctuary. Hugh refuses to collapse the eleven into one moral category: some swore freely and broke the vow on conscience, others faced ration pressure or coercive recruitment. A forced oath is no oath at all. Official Westfield forces withdraw without violence after an hour's dispute — which matters when, after midnight, six or seven men in Crown blue but no house devices burn the refugee wagon, pursue Sister Maelin Orr, and leave the warning "A VOW BROKEN IS A LIFE FORFEIT." Evidence later ties the crew to the same hired antiquarian group moving through old Alderwick sites, directed by an unidentified grey-bearded man in an unmarked Crown cloak. Official Westfield command is cleared of the arson itself — the uniform doesn't collapse into one moral block.

Act VI — Old Merebridge

The old-site trail leads to Old Merebridge, a disused tollhouse tied to Varick Sorn's three-site commission — oil, trap lines, a sand timer, and a ratchet mechanism turn the ruin into a genuine physical puzzle rather than another research loop. Hugh tackles a fleeing suspect through a burning second-storey window to keep him alive rather than let him fall, taking minor damage — the campaign's recurring "window" joke and a real marker of his preference for capture over execution. A stone construct beneath the stable retrieves a removed bronze cylinder and restores it rather than attacking indiscriminately, and Hugh's own observation identifies the safe maintenance path — proof the old system is functional infrastructure, not a monster-filled ruin. Recovered records use the phrase "First Authority of Alderwick," deliberately ambiguous for now.

Act VII — Eastmere Cut and the First Authority

At an old drainage complex east of the city, Sorn is found alive but trapped; Calder Venn is found dead after unauthorised re-entry into failing machinery — B18 lets him stay dead. Hugh and Adam stop a dangerous floodgate sequence through careful analysis rather than force. Venn's concealed letter from Shaw proves crucial: Shaw ordered him to confirm the third site, record the mark, and withdraw immediately if a deeper crossed-circle layer appeared — instructions Venn disobeyed, which is what killed him. The archive finally defines the First Authority: "First Authority of Alderwick for waters, crossings and common works" — a civic emergency office, not an ancient monarchy. Its most important constitutional line: the Authority commands the reserve only until ordinary civic administration is restored, and no emergency power survives the emergency that created it. A second, deeper crossed-circle reserve system appears beneath the ordinary layer — only four nodes, one already decommissioned, builders and function unresolved.

Act VIII — Edrin Kersey and the Charter

Edrin brings the recovered Founding Charter to Cale and Maris Kersey rather than Crown Hall, leaving Adam a personal letter admitting he abandoned family for a cause that mattered, but not every choice made in its name was right — and that he's no longer sure everything done in Shaw's name is actually Shaw's order. The Charter's real danger is how limited its claim is: it doesn't prove ancient Crown rule, only that old Alderwick knew how to grant extraordinary emergency authority without pretending it lasted forever. It's eventually transferred openly into Crown custody, witnessed and copied rather than hoarded — which is exactly why Dane's network wants to contain its authentication.

Act IX — The Three Spoons and Hugh's Other Home

The Three Spoons deepens from a useful childhood location into Hugh's strongest non-Crown family anchor. Mara's husband once quietly left covered leftovers outside at a fixed time so street children could eat without begging; when the inn's three brass spoons were stolen, a thirteen-year-old Hugh tracked them down and bought back the third with saved odd-job money. Mara is the one who eventually told Hugh that Crown Hall had kitchens and trades work, not only paladin vows — she led with kitchens because a hungry boy would have fled from a speech about honour. During a dinner there, Hugh accidentally introduces Adam as "the man I love" and realises it's already true.

Act X — Edrin's Midnight Meeting and Adam's Betrayal

An anonymous note signed only "M" sends Adam to meet Edrin at midnight. Edrin confirms the First Crown clandestine system has become dangerously compartmentalised — Shaw genuinely approved Barrowfen but denied ordering the refugee arson and reacted strongly when confronted about it, separating Shaw's real orders from acts done by loyalists and acts done under his name without his direction. The compromised controller is identified as Commander Alric Dane. The turning point comes when Edrin quotes information about Hugh that only Adam could have supplied — "One of those things I only told you." Adam confesses rather than continuing the lie, later giving Roland and Voss a complete official statement. Holt had recruited him five months earlier after Adam complained in a tavern that Crown had become very good at explaining why nobody was responsible — a gradual recruitment through philosophical questions, not one dramatic oath. When Hugh later asks why he kept reporting after falling in love, Adam gives the campaign's clearest moral summary: "I chose to delay the cost to me."

Act XI — Hugh Does Not Break Up With Adam

Hugh is furious but doesn't end the relationship — a young relationship isn't automatically shallow, and good reasons to trust Adam don't erase the betrayal, just as the betrayal doesn't erase everything good that came before it. Roland's advice: don't make trust a vote — ten good things don't cancel one betrayal, and one betrayal doesn't erase ten good things. Hugh takes a solitary walk back to his old drainage-arch sleeping place and finds Crown Hall workers have voluntarily brought home another hungry street child, the same door open for someone else. He goes to Mara rather than Adam, telling her only what's his to tell. Her conclusion is character-defining: "I approve of consequences." At the River Steps the next day, Adam gives Hugh the full explanation without minimising it; they hold hands, laugh, and kiss when Hugh asks — their private dynamic resuming by Hugh's own choice, reinforcing rather than contradicting the line between chosen intimacy and institutional coercion.

Act XII — The Controlled Dead Drop and Cavan Holt

Adam stays under quiet operational restriction while the investigation develops — barred from restricted records but not visibly disarmed, since obvious punishment would reveal he's turned. Roland makes clear that refusing to help as bait won't worsen his discipline and agreeing won't buy leniency; Adam chooses to stay in play because he wants to decide for himself that Holt is wrong about him. A controlled message at the North Fountain dead drop leads Hugh to witness the handoff to Cavan Holt directly, confirmed by his missing left little-fingertip. Holt orders Hugh watched rather than approached, believing direct pressure only hardens him.

Act XIII — Pilgrim's Steps and Holt's Test

Holt summons Adam to an old riverside weigh-house; Hugh takes a concealed position nearby, swapping visible Crown blue for black covering while keeping his armour. From hiding, Hugh overhears Holt testing Adam's remaining access and trying to exploit his fear of losing Crown Hall — Adam pushes back, admitting Crown Hall matters desperately but refusing to let Holt define what belonging requires, and flatly refuses an order to cut Hugh loose. Holt reveals Dane's actual ideology: Shaw's continuing concern with legitimacy is a weakness, and authority that can act matters more than authority that can prove permission. In a second overheard exchange, Holt names Hugh as more dangerous than Adam ideologically — Adam wants somewhere to belong; Hugh is starting to ask what belonging is for.

Act XIV — Elian Marr and the Network Reacts

Crown Hall deliberately doesn't feed Adam impossible intelligence to make him look useful — Holt should distrust anything too perfect from a man under investigation. The network independently targets Elian Marr, the scribe connected to charter copying; Hugh spots the surveillance and intervenes, using Command and Protection to stop an attack and capture one operative while another escapes. The recovered written order is the first strong physical evidence directly connecting Holt's field operation, Copper House, and Dane in a single chain.

Act XV — Copper House

Voss, Roland, and Hugh enter Copper House with a lawful order and identify Nessa Vale, its private-surety manager, as part of the network. At her private room, Hugh's thieves'-tools instincts catch a targeted evidence-destruction mechanism — a vitriol trap wired to the door — and defeats it cleanly, recovering a full operational communications archive. Most importantly, written orders from Dane to Holt prove Shaw was unwilling to sanction seizing the Founding Charter without examining its provenance, while Dane ordered Holt to secure it anyway — direct countermand, not vague rogue behaviour. The trail now points to Gannet House.

Act XVI — Gannet House and Alric Dane

Crown confirms Dane's presence before acting; the arrest proceeds under magistrate authority. Dane tries to escape through a third-floor window onto a wet ledge — Hugh follows, grapples him, and keeps him alive through the capture despite the height. Gannet House yields Dane's unfinished letter to Shaw, capturing his core justification: "You told me to build a network capable of surviving obstruction. You cannot now object because it survived your hesitation as well." Dane has turned resilience into self-authorisation — Shaw built the apparatus, but Dane decided the apparatus itself could determine when Shaw no longer deserved to control it. Holt is arrested soon after at his Tanner's Rise lodging; Dane, Holt, and Nessa are kept separate so they can't coordinate stories.

Act XVII — Adam Learns the Network Is Gone

Only after Dane and Holt are secured does Hugh brief Adam fully — Hugh himself suggested keeping Adam ignorant of the morning's developments, since knowing too much before a possible drop could have betrayed him through a glance. Adam isn't treated as Holt's passive victim; the account preserves both the network's manipulation and Adam's own choices. Adam formally tells Voss and Roland he wants to remain in Crown Hall — not as payment for a bed, but because he now understands an oath as loyalty to values rather than to whoever stands above him.

Act XVIII — Adam's Disciplinary Review

The formal review — Voss, Roland, and Captain Merrow Vale, so Roland isn't both mentor and sole judge — states the breach plainly: Adam passed information about personnel, movements, views, and operations, concealed it from his officers, and continued after the relationship began. Crown Hall keeps him rather than expelling him, distinguishing serious discipline from permanent disposal: formal censure, six months of restricted access, no independent intelligence work — but full permission to continue paladin formation and take the Crown oath. The decision reflects that Adam now understands the difference between an oath and obedience to a person, not that catching Dane earns a reward.

Act XIX — Edrin, Shaw, and the Deliberate Loose End

Adam sends Edrin an ordinary open letter through his parents rather than rebuilding a secret channel, reporting Dane and Holt's capture and the proof of Dane's countermand. Edrin's reply refuses to resolve anything cleanly: dismantling Dane doesn't make Crown Hall right about everything, or Shaw right about everything either. He carries the message onward and leaves Shaw's response for Shaw to choose. B18 ends this thread deliberately unresolved — Shaw isn't the campaign's boss, and his future is left for a later Oath Wars return or a War of Ash and Glass prequel with different protagonists.

Act XX — Family Consequences and Found Family

At The Three Spoons, Mara confronts Adam directly — he admits he hurt Hugh, lied, and passed along things Hugh had told him in trust — and refuses to let the conversation become either absolution or permanent self-humiliation. Before Hugh leaves, Mara admits an old guilt — she and her husband once wished they could have simply taken him in as a child, but couldn't responsibly promise a future the business couldn't support. Hugh rejects the guilt outright: she gave him warmth, useful work, and eventually Crown Hall's own door, and that's enough. At the Kersey family supper, Cale refuses to let Adam use Hugh's harsher childhood as an excuse — Adam always had a home to return to — while Maris tells him love doesn't require him to become a permanent supplicant.

Act XXI — Why Hugh Chooses Crown

On Crown Hall's older wall overlooking living Alderwick, Roland asks Hugh why he himself is choosing Crown — not what the institution taught him, what Roland taught him, or what Adam believes. Hugh's answer becomes the campaign's clearest thesis: Crown gave him somewhere to stand, and being saved doesn't mean he owes it obedience forever. He almost chose Sanctuary — he understands standing between civilians and harm — but chooses Crown because he wants a system that stops the harm from reaching people in the first place. "Not order so everyone does what they're told. Order so people don't get crushed because nobody can make anything work." The people giving orders must be bound by the oath too; if Crown Hall becomes something he doesn't recognise, he wants to stay and fight for it rather than leave and build another Crown.

Act XXII — Adam's Own Crown

Adam's reasoning overlaps with Hugh's without copying it — he still believes authority must be capable of acting, having seen what jurisdictional paralysis costs at Barrowfen. What changes is his idea of responsibility: he no longer wants an oath promising he'll never doubt, but one that tells him what to do when he does. Holt and Dane taught him that a system no one can challenge eventually stops needing to be right. He remains frightened of losing belonging, but that fear no longer gets to define the oath.

Act XXIII — The Oath Ceremony

Hugh and Adam take their formal Oath of the Crown together, attended by the Kerseys, Mara and her husband, Roland, Voss, Moss, and Elian — not staged as a private romantic ceremony, but witnessed by the wider Hall. The oath is framed as binding because it's freely chosen, not because every superior will always be right; candidates accept responsibility for what their authority protects, permits, and fails to prevent. The magic answers because both men mean it. Both reach Level 3 and move into a shared two-paladin room with two separate beds — which they immediately push together. Asked where he sees them in ten years, Hugh answers: "Alderwick with you, whatever shape that may be." Pressed for one concrete detail: "probably married" — a hope, not a locked prophecy.

World State After Campaign B18

Hugh Bellamy. Level 3 Oath of the Crown paladin, based at Crown Hall in living Alderwick. His doctrine is stable: functioning protection of vulnerable people, no forced oath, gratitude without blindness, accountable authority, and reform from within while reform remains genuinely possible. Mara and her husband remain his closest found family outside Crown Hall. His relationship with Adam is intact and serious — trust is being rebuilt through conduct, not declared instantly repaired.

Adam Kersey. Level 3 Oath of the Crown paladin, formally censured with six months of active restrictions on independent intelligence access. His cooperation with the Dane investigation is relevant conduct, not an erasure of the original betrayal — he reported field facts and political views, and the romance with Hugh was genuine throughout, never assigned by the network.

Crown Hall / Third Banner. Dane's Alderwick clandestine network is fully dismantled; dispatch provenance and access-log practices receive institutional review, a practical response rather than a sweeping ideological revolution. Roland remains a model of principled Crown authority; Voss remains associated with lawful hearings and discipline.

Dane / Holt / Nessa Network. Alric Dane, Cavan Holt, Nessa Vale, and the Green Coat operative are all captured alive; one younger operative escapes and remains a loose end rather than a new command layer. Copper House survives as a legitimate institution under scrutiny, since most of its staff weren't shown to have joined Nessa's clandestine work.

Edmund Shaw / First Crown. Remains unresolved and physically off-stage. He genuinely authorised the Barrowfen pressure — B18 doesn't absolve him of that — but Dane demonstrably countermanded him over the Founding Charter. Edrin carries the evidence back toward Shaw; his response is deliberately left for later play.

First Authority / Ancient Alderwick. Confirmed as a civic emergency institution for waters, crossings, and common works — not an ancient throne, and its authority explicitly ends when ordinary civic administration resumes. The crossed-circle reserve layer beneath it is real, limited, and unresolved; no Great Seal connection is established, since the Great Seals don't exist yet in B18's own timeframe.

Open threads carried forward. Edmund Shaw's reaction to Edrin's evidence and the future of the First Crown schism; the wider Oath Wars beyond Crown Hall (other traditions, commanders, houses, battles); the identity of the grey-bearded intermediary behind the Westfield arson; the exact trigger of the Oath Wars; how the separate oath traditions arrive at the future Seven Refusal; the builders and fate of the crossed-circle reserve nodes; and the future of Hugh, Adam, Roland, Mara, the Kerseys, and Edrin if this era is ever revisited.

Canon status: follows the source account closely.
