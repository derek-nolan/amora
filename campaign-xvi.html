Timeline A · Campaign XVI

The Corridor Crown

Third Cyberpunk Age

8240–8245 ARV Primary location: Highever ↔ Tarenus ↔ Port Annex

Opening World State

Still the Third Cyberpunk Age; Concord already exists. Neon Concord taught cities to keep order by proof, not panic — paper before power, safeties in sight. This arc takes the next step: it turns that ethic into habit across an entire route, extending law from city to corridor and coast.

Party & Key Figures

  • Kaeso Vyr (Z-echo) — Highever • Red Lattice D-3
    Rogue/paladin hybrid and Consort. An unknowing Zethyr-echo at start, awakens the reconstructed Taren ("Coren"). Enters a consensual Chain-bond and serves as public Consort and field hand.
  • Coren (Taren reconstruction/echo) — Highever • Cathedral-7 (Glass Manifold)
    Veil Sovereign and Sway adept. A reconstituted Taren-type echo who re-forges the Chain with Kaeso, expanding it from rooms to crowds via Cadence rites.
  • Eryx (Keeper at the Red Lattice) — Highever • Lattice Row
    Sanctuary Keeper and Failsafe Holder. Retains "unwinding words" that can recall hazed vows if invoked.
  • Kade Loric — Highever • Neutral meet (Crown District)
    Broker, blackmail handler, and occasional Dom. Uncovers Kaeso's Lattice visits and attempts leverage.
  • Josten ("Subject J") — Antagonist, neutralized
    Tracer/financier tied to the Old House finance web and off-ledger crews. Removed during a black-box operation.

Act-by-Act

New Standards

Doors Law (the Two-Beat Bow at thresholds, turning doors into civic metronomes), Quiet Orders (eight quiet breaths baseline for halls and foyers), Festival Sixteen (synchronized release windows across Highever, Tarenus, and the Port with external adjudicators stamping the timing), Consort Annulus (an inner door-ring publicly pre-claimed by the Consort), and Cadence Court (stepside hearings using reader logs and pane forensics) all emerge as the corridor learns to inhale on the Consort's count and release on the Crown's punctuation. The method is procedural, not miraculous.

Riding the Chain

Opposition tries to "ride" the Chain's black-silver signature to stage counter-quiet flash mobs. Coren baits and folds them into Court-First on the steps — a corridor that feels inevitable without being a throne. Kade Loric, uncovering Kaeso's Lattice visits, attempts to leverage the access for influence, retaining soft leverage that moderates once Coren surfaces publicly.

Coastal Copying

Harbor satellites adopt cadence by choice; rumor becomes regulation by receipts. The myth of the two echoes — Crown and Consort — is acknowledged, yet firmly bounded by appeal, ledger, and optics: a modern re-reading of the old Zethyr/Taren bond that refuses mandate while preserving partnership. Josten, the money-trail target tied to plaza manipulation, is identified via tracer stacks and removed during a black-box operation — his fall advances Coren's consolidation and chills Old House coordination.

World State After Campaign XVI

Geography. The corridor now formally links Highever, Tarenus Reclaimed, and the Port Annex (harbor satellites) — the first time the civic structure explicitly spans coast to coast rather than being centred on a single city.

Magic/Law. Corridor Cadence — Doors Law, Quiet Orders, Festival Sixteen, Consort Annulus, and Cadence Court — is recognized as binding municipal procedure across the whole corridor, layered directly over Campaign XV's Concord baseline. Sovereignty by Practice: the Z-T Chain functions as city habit, not compulsion — rooms answer the Consort's First Step; the Crown merely punctuates.

The Durrel Line. Kaeso Vyr and Coren are explicitly framed as Zethyr and Taren echoes (Z-echo, T-echo) rather than literal Durrel descendants — the pattern recurring through resonance rather than bloodline, continuing the trend established back in Campaign III's Echo Chain.

Institutions. Appeal persists — redress windows and public ledgers remain visible; "Court-First" replaces plaza contests. The ethic of Neon Concord ("law without a throne") endures at larger scale. This campaign privileges posted standards and documented consent over spectacle, an explicit continuation of the "printer, ink, perimeter" world that followed Campaign X's Severing. It's the high watermark of proof-first civic architecture before Campaign XVII's collapse.

Canon status: follows the character register; Coren here is a separate reconstructed echo from Coren Veyne of Campaign IV.
