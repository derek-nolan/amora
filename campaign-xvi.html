Timeline A · Campaign XVI

The Corridor Crown

Third Cyberpunk Age

8240–8245 ARV Primary location: Highever ↔ Tarenus (Inheritance Reclaimed) ↔ Port Annex

Opening World State

Still the Third Cyberpunk Age; Concord already exists. After Neon Concord established the Concord Charter, its Addendum of Five Optics, and Unified Cadence as municipal baseline, this arc extends that same order by procedure rather than miracle — exporting cadence from a single city, out through the corridor, and finally to the coast. Highever and Tarenus Reclaimed share the crown by practice now, not decree, and the harbour itself is about to be brought into the same rhythm through bells, readers, and painted lanes.

Party & Key Figures

  • Kaeso Vyr PC (Zethyr echo) Consort-Primus. An unknowing Zethyr-echo at the start, awakens the reconstructed Taren and becomes the corridor's Consort — the one who pre-claims doors so the room learns tempo by following him.
  • Coren
    (Taren echo, "the Crown") A reconstituted Taren-type echo who rules by habit instead of decree, turning the corridor into infrastructure alongside Kaeso rather than a throne.
  • Eryx
    Sanctuary Keeper carrying forward the Sanctuary Architecture optics standardised citywide back in Campaign XV.
  • Kade Loric
    Liaison and pressure broker; probes the Kaeso/Coren dynamic for leverage throughout the early acts.
  • Harbor Board & Bell Guilds
    Adopt Bells Pre-Log procedures and lane painting; fight counterfeit pins at Dockline 3.
  • Reader Net (Lamppost Stewards)
    The city and port's sensor grid, used to auto-flag counterfeits and feed directly into Court-First proceedings.
  • The Tracer Coalition
    Attempts to "ride" the Chain's black-silver signature to stage counter-quiet flash-mobs; routinely baited and folded into stepside dockets instead.

Act-by-Act

Act I — Five Days Before the Glass

Kaeso Vyr is introduced in routine — Vigil Row checks, light interludes at the Red Lattice under Sanctuary rules, and Kade's earliest pressure plays. The tone throughout is procedural noir, not miracle: nothing about this corridor is meant to feel divinely ordained.

Act II — The Glass Cathedral

Coren, the Taren echo, is discovered and wakes in containment. His public posture stays neutral, but the private cadence between him and Kaeso is immediate. The pair adopt documented, consent-receipted custody windows — a halo and chime marking start and end — mirroring the consent architecture Campaign XV established, but pushing it further, toward genuine corridor-wide practice rather than a single city's rule.

Act III — Law as Metronome

The campaign's real innovations roll out here: Doors Law, the Two-Beat Bow that turns every threshold into a civic metronome; Quiet Orders, an eight-breath baseline for halls and foyers meant to prevent crush; and the Consort Annulus, an inner ring before major doors pre-claimed by Kaeso specifically so each room learns its tempo by following him. These micro-edicts are taught on-scene, repeatedly, until they stick the way traffic law does.

Act IV — Cadence Court and the Bait

Coren deliberately leaves the Chain's black-silver signature hanging at the Old Jetty as bait; tracer handlers step in to try to exploit it, and Cadence Court — informally called Plain-8 — folds them cleanly using pane forensics and reader logs. Proof beats rumour here, exactly as it did throughout Campaign XV.

Act V — Festival Sixteen

Four ceremonial arches rise across the corridor as external adjudicators stamp timed counts — two, three, four, sixteen — across Highever, Tarenus, and the Port simultaneously. People start experiencing the release windows as ordinary habit, not imposition from above.

Act VI — Corridor Crown

Deputy Cadence captains gain the authority to open Plain-8 proceedings on the Consort's nod alone. Processional lanes are painted along the quayside, and the crowd begins self-ordering the moment Kaeso pre-claims a doorway — Coren's role narrows to punctuation only: a raised finger marks a period, an open palm sets the pace.

Act VII — A Crown by Practice

By the arc's end, the corridor breathes to Kaeso's count and releases on Coren's. The myth of the two of them — quietly named "Second Light" in some quarters — is openly acknowledged under law, yet stays firmly bounded by appeal and public ledger, echoing the way Campaign XV's own Chorus-Key was archived under consent rather than decree.

World State After Campaign XVI

Geography. The corridor now formally links Highever, Tarenus Reclaimed, and the Port Annex (the harbour satellites) — the first time the civic structure explicitly spans coast to coast rather than being centred on a single city.

Magic/Law. Corridor Cadence — Doors Law, Quiet Orders, Festival Sixteen, Consort Annulus, and Cadence Court — is recognised as binding municipal procedure across the whole corridor, layered directly over Campaign XV's Concord baseline. Sovereignty by Practice: the Zethyr-Taren Chain functions here as city habit, not compulsion — rooms answer the Consort's First Step, and the Crown merely punctuates.

The Durrel Line. Kaeso Vyr and Coren are explicitly framed as Zethyr and Taren echoes (Z-echo, T-echo) rather than literal Durrel descendants — the pattern recurring through resonance rather than bloodline, continuing the trend established back in Campaign III's Echo Chain.

Institutions. Appeal persists — redress windows and public ledgers remain visible; Court-First replaces plaza contests entirely. The ethic of Neon Concord — law without a throne — endures now at a larger, corridor-and-coast scale. This campaign privileges posted standards and documented consent over spectacle, an explicit continuation of the "printer, ink, perimeter" world that followed Campaign X's Severing. It's the high watermark of proof-first civic architecture before Campaign XVII's collapse.

Canon status: follows the character register; Coren here is a separate reconstructed echo from Coren Veyne of Campaign IV.
