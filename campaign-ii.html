Timeline A · Campaign II

The Veil Ascendant

Age of Dominion — “Through Shadow, Dominion”

Later archive: 28–55 ARVPlay chronology: begins in the eighth month after Campaign I; the rise and fall unfold over roughly two yearsPrimary theatres: Highever, Greymount/Tarenus, Brevair, Evermere

Opening World State

Campaign II begins almost immediately after the seven-month Seal campaign rather than after a generation-long gap. Zethyr and Taren are newly married, the Heartwardens are respected across Vanmere, and the psychic bond between the two men has already proved capable of carrying emotion, judgement, and will. Hidden inside the Heart Medallion is the Dark Filament first encountered beneath Silver Bastion.

The political world is equally unstable. Brevair is still an expansionist empire under the ruler who once sought to undermine Highever. Rather than simply confronting Taren, the Brevair Emperor creates the Court of the Veil in an attempt to study, contain, and weaponise shadow power. That decision gives Taren exactly the institutional platform he needs. The tragedy of Campaign II is that the Dominion is not imposed on a peaceful world from nowhere; it grows out of institutions trying to use Taren before Taren learns how to use them better.

Party & Key Figures

  • Zethyr Durrel PC
    Heartwarden High Commander, Taren’s husband, later Consort and Co-Regent. He remains close to Taren partly because he believes love and proximity may still moderate him. His eventual Severance ends the first Dominion at the cost of both their lives.
  • Taren Durrel
    Heartwarden captain turned Veilward, ruler, and finally Veiled Emperor. Reinterprets Balance as control and increasingly treats the Seals, institutions, and even his marriage as structures that can be aligned beneath one will.
  • Seraphine Vale
    Grand Marshal and representative of the old Paladin Council. Her refusal to accept Taren’s remaking of the order makes her one of the clearest institutional opponents of the Dominion. Executed.
  • Aldric Fen
    Heartwarden chaplain and spiritual opponent of the new order. Executed as the old Heartwarden religious structure is dismantled.
  • Arienna Lume
    Magister of the Collegium Arcanum and Flame-line scholar. Her attempt to retain scholarly independence during the Veil Core research ends with her autonomy removed; surviving sources describe her as imprisoned in harmony, mind-stilled, or sealed into Veil infrastructure.
  • Lyra & Thane
    Former Silver Bastion initiates who survive the transition by becoming Acolytes and eventually Generals of the Veil.
  • Kellan
    Named casualty of the old order. The surviving campaign record states that Zethyr kills him, but does not preserve enough context to reconstruct the full scene or safely identify him with any other remembered early-campaign figure.
  • The Emperor of Brevair
    Founder of the Court of the Veil, initially hoping to control Taren through patronage and state power. Taren ultimately executes him and absorbs Brevair into the Dominion.

Act-by-Act

The Veil Rises

The first phase follows Taren’s movement from admired Heartwarden captain to political actor. He studies the Filament and the power now being called the Veil while councils in Highever and Brevair argue over who should control the discovery. Zethyr remains beside him, not because he fails to see the danger, but because the bond that once helped them stabilise the Seals makes separation feel like abandonment at exactly the point Taren is becoming most dangerous.

The Court of the Veil is established under Brevair’s Emperor. What begins as an attempt to weaponise shadow becomes the nucleus of a new ideology. Taren’s argument is seductively practical: the Seal crises of Campaign I proved that disorder kills; coordinated will saved lives; therefore lasting peace requires coordination strong enough that disorder cannot return.

Greymount and the Rite of Binding

Beneath Greymount, Taren performs the Rite of Binding. The Veil Core is fused with the Heart Medallion, turning the private Heart–Veil problem into a system capable of projecting command through the leys. Greymount is rebuilt as Greymount Sanctum and later Tarenus Keep.

Arienna’s fate becomes one of the campaign’s clearest warnings. She seeks autonomy to study Core overload and cognitive looping; Taren reads independence itself as disloyalty. The surviving records disagree on the exact magical procedure used against her, but agree on the result: the scholar who helped the first Seal investigations is stripped of meaningful autonomy and incorporated into the order she tried to examine.

The Heartwardens Become the Veilwardens

The old order is not merely defeated from outside; it is renamed from within. Heartwardens who submit take the Oath of the Veil and become Taren’s elite military-priesthood. Their new creed promises peace through an unseen hand. Silver Bastion is remade into the Obsidian Bastion, the Sanctum of the Veil. The Heartwarden chaplaincy is dissolved.

Seraphine and Aldric are executed. Lyra and Thane advance inside the new hierarchy. Kellan is killed by Zethyr during the collapse of the old order, a surviving fact whose context is otherwise lost. These deaths matter because the Dominion is built through the destruction or absorption of people the couple knew personally in Campaign I, not through anonymous institutional change.

Conquest, Evermere, and Coronation

Auran Gate is converted from a border crossroads into a military foundry and training centre for Veil paladins and Blackguard orders. Evermere — the sanctuary where Zethyr and Taren married — is annexed and given to Zethyr, its villas becoming their private court and a place where imperial decisions are made inside the intimacy of their bond.

Taren’s authority expands across Highever and the southern provinces. Brevair’s attempt to control him collapses completely: its Emperor is executed, the realm is annexed, and its capital is rebuilt in black stone under the name Durrelion. Taren is crowned Veiled Emperor. Zethyr becomes Consort and Co-Regent, outwardly honoured but increasingly functioning as the beloved human channel through which Taren’s will is made legitimate.

The Ley-Heirs and the Promise of Forever

The Dominion’s most consequential magical act is the creation of the first Ley-Heirs — constructed souls born from fused Heart and Veil essence, carrying both Taren’s command-pattern and Zethyr’s capacity for compassion. They are designed to solve the problem at the centre of Taren’s ideology: mortality. If the right pattern can be preserved, then order and the bond that sustains it need never end.

The Bond of Chains deepens alongside this work. A relationship that began as genuine psychic intimacy becomes both private hierarchy and public sacrament. Taren ultimately achieves the immortality he has been moving toward, but the solution makes Zethyr understand that there will be no natural end to the system if he does nothing.

Menge and the First Severance

Menge remains beyond complete Dominion control. Its auroras become associated with a resurgence of Light that Taren cannot comfortably absorb into his system; at one point he declares the region beyond redemption. The existence of something outside the imperial rhythm exposes the lie that Dominion and balance are the same thing.

Zethyr finally acts. He severs the Veil Core to stop Taren’s ascent and destroys the mechanism binding immortality, Heart, and Veil together. The act kills both men and scatters the Ley-Heirs. Later ages call this the first Severance, although the term itself is only formalised after Edrin Durrel repeats the principle in Campaign III.

World State After Campaign II

Geography. Highever’s Silver Bastion has been transformed into the Obsidian Bastion / Sanctum of the Veil. Greymount becomes Tarenus Keep, the Dominion’s southern capital and site of the Veil Core fusion. Auran Gate has become a military foundry. Evermere has shifted from sanctuary to imperial private court and legal centre. Brevair is conquered and its capital remembered under the taboo Dominion name Durrelion.

Magic. The Dark Filament and Heart Medallion are fused through the Veil Core. The first Ley-Heirs are created as constructed souls rather than ordinary descendants. Zethyr’s Severance destroys the Core’s immortal continuity and scatters those patterns rather than erasing them entirely.

The Durrel Line. Taren and Zethyr die together at the end of the first Dominion. Their marriage was real and their love remains real; what failed was the conversion of that bond into a permanent architecture of command. Later recurrences inherit the pattern because the Ley-Heirs survive them.

Institutions. The original Heartwarden structure is broken. The Veilwardens, Court of the Veil, imperial foundries, and Dominion law briefly create one of the most stable and coercive states in Amoran history. Its fall turns the first two campaigns into legend and leaves later ages arguing over whether the catastrophe came from Veil, empire, love, or the refusal to let anything end.

Canon status: reconstructed from the surviving early campaign compendium and later world summary. The source preserves the Court of the Veil, Lyra and Thane’s promotion, Arienna’s loss of autonomy, Kellan’s death by Zethyr, the Rite of Binding, Brevair’s conquest, the Ley-Heirs, Taren’s immortality, and Zethyr’s final Severance. Arienna is consistently Arienna Lume in the early record. The later 28–55 ARV span is retained as archival dating, while the play-accurate source describes Campaign II as beginning in the eighth month after Campaign I and unfolding over roughly the next two years.
