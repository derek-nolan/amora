Core Premise

Storm Walker is the campaign that finally answers the question Nicholas Soren carried out of The Second Measure: what actually happened to the Storm Great Seal after "Storm answered last, but Storm answered"? It moves the modern Great Seal arc away from Highever's archives and into the Badlands themselves, through Greymount's Farbound Guild — a professional exploration, survey, and rescue institution rather than another order of scholars. Benjamin "Ben" Strake, an experienced delver whose parents died in an ordinary Badlands expedition when he was twelve, follows a trail of sabotaged route markers and an old family survey report through a buried storm-management ruin, a subterranean sanctuary, and a field of black glass — into direct contact with Storm itself. The campaign confirms that the Shattering, not the Great Seals, created the Badlands' self-sustaining magical weather; that Storm has spent more than twelve centuries containing it rather than causing it; and that the historical "report incomplete" was a failed transmission from an injured Warden, not a failed Seal. It also physically rediscovers Alderwick, last seen alive and whole in The Crown Within, now a vast Shattering ruin — and ends with a permanent, bounded change to the Badlands themselves.

Where this sits relative to B17 and B18. B19 is not a sequel to B18. B18 was played after B17 but is a deliberately interleaved deep-history prequel set c. -938 ARV, more than twelve centuries before any of B19's events; it remains fully published and unchanged. B19 instead continues directly from B17's modern 342 ARV present, opening in mid-343 ARV — roughly a year later, not immediately after B18's in-world date. Both B17's completed Great Seal history and B18's directly-played pre-Shattering Alderwick geography are load-bearing here: B19 is where they meet.

Party & Key Figures

  • Benjamin "Ben" Strake PC
    Human Ranger, Level 2 → 3, Storm Walker. An experienced Farbound delver, not a novice hero — trained in route safety, rescue, and hazard reading, with Expertise in Survival. Raised communally by the guild after his parents died in an ordinary Badlands expedition when he was twelve. Ends the campaign permanently changed: silver hair, electric-blue eyes, and a lasting ability to sense storm pressure, gusts, and lightning before they arrive.
  • Galen Vardane
    Twenty-nine, a Greymount road-and-gate officer and Ben's primary romance. His presence on expeditions is justified by route closures and civic safety, not by guild membership — he remains a Greymount officer throughout, not a Farbound delver. Openly monogamous with Ben by campaign's end.
  • Maud Halcro
    Fifty-eight, Guildmaster of Farbound and one of Ben's parents' closest friends. A former delver with a bad knee and excellent risk judgement, impossible for Ben to fool.
  • Tessa Draith
    Forty-six, Farbound quartermaster and expedition medic — one of Ben's principal carers growing up, with a low tolerance for heroics and an absolute one for medical consequences.
  • Pella Ossel
    Nineteen, junior Farbound delver and Ben's little-sister figure. Moves from her seventh expedition through her first serious deep push over the course of the campaign; newly dating Rian Vesker by its end.
  • Rian Vesker
    Twenty-one, rope and climbing specialist who joined Farbound roughly six months before B19 and earns his recurring place on the team through field performance.
  • Jude Scarrow
    Thirty, Farbound field captain — calm, capable, and willing to let Ben lead when Ben has the better environmental read.
  • Veya
    Experienced Farbound rescue and extraction specialist, part of the core seven-person deep expedition.
  • Sparky
    Ben's name for a large wild storm-adapted creature encountered near the Storm Seal — dark-plated, with blue crystalline seams and a natural sense for safe paths through lightning. Earns Ben's trust through peaceful contact; remains wild afterward, not a companion or mount.
  • Grey House Antagonist
    A local Greymount-area criminal outfit exploiting storm-suppressing creatures and old storm-management infrastructure for salvage. Closed by a warehouse raid within the campaign's opening acts — a practical guild problem, not the campaign's true spine.

Act-by-Act

Act I — Farbound Lodge

B19 opens in Greymount rather than Highever, at the Farbound Lodge — professional exploration, route certification, rescue, and the communal family that raised Ben after his parents' deaths. He checks their old sketch before expeditions and tells it, "I'll be back." Galen Vardane, Greymount's road-and-gate officer, enters through route closures and overdue-expedition paperwork; the attraction develops alongside the actual plot rather than suspending it.

Act II–III — Marker Twelve and Grey House Closed

Ben, Jude, and Galen investigate deliberately sabotaged route markers around Marker Twelve and a large storm-suppressing creature bearing restraint marks and a healed implanted metal piece — clear evidence of human exploitation. A recovered note pointing to "GREY HOUSE" leads to a swift warehouse raid: seven arrests, stolen Farbound markers recovered, a second live storm-suppression creature secured. B19 deliberately refuses to escalate Grey House into another hidden hierarchy — Greymount watch takes the prisoners, and the campaign's real spine stays elsewhere.

Act IV–V — Twelve to Seventeen, and the Western Pressure Station

A proper Farbound survey of the Marker Twelve–Seventeen corridor finds a storm behaving unlike any normal front near Marker Sixteen — pulsing roughly every twenty-two seconds, blue ground-light converging on a fixed buried point. The full seven-person expedition returns to excavate it. Readable ancient Amoran plates identify the ruin as a Western Pressure Station — Field Measure and Discharge Control, with the site's own inscription drawing a hard line this campaign holds throughout: "The foci do not hold the storm. They give the storm a road to ground." This is ancient storm-management infrastructure — not the Storm Great Seal itself, and not a template for a chain of similar sites still waiting to be found.

Act VI–VII — Field Measure, and the Removed Focus

A side store preserves a functioning field measure, calibration rings, and maintenance rods. At the central junction, a dead Grey House survey hand's notes warn against reseating a removed focus after first discharge. Ben stabilises the already-removed, roughly eight-inch spherical focus using the builders' own four-rod cradle rather than carrying it back uncontrolled, and the remaining Grey House recovery team is captured nonlethally. Their own ledger had already wondered whether the focus was attracting, grounding, or redirecting the storm activity — proof that even the criminals understood it as infrastructure, not a source.

Act VIII — Galen

A Greymount rest period lets Ben and Galen become more than field flirtation — a first kiss at Ben's old inner-wall parapet, a proper date at the Lantern Room, and a private dominance/submission dynamic Ben explicitly wants monogamous. Galen uses the word "boyfriend" naturally, and it stays visible to the Lodge for the rest of the campaign. Pella, meanwhile, asks Rian out herself — Ben plays light-touch wingman at most, and the relationship stays hers.

Act IX — The Report His Parents Brought Home

In the Farbound archive, Ben finds an older report in his parents' handwriting — not their fatal expedition, but an earlier, ordinary one. They reached a comparable site, correctly judged they lacked the equipment to push further, logged it, and came home safely. Maud's reminder lands cleanly: not every page they left behind is a memorial. Ben is simply the next Farbound delver able to continue work they once responsibly recorded.

Act X — Primary Measure

A second push into Sixteen reaches the lower Primary Measure, a circular distribution chamber built to spread storm pressure across multiple roads and tolerate temporary failure. Ben restores two locally recoverable paths and rebalances the installation, substantially reducing Sixteen's local strain — a real expedition success, but explicitly not Ben's transformation and not a reinterpretation of the machinery as the Great Seal itself. The weather shift it triggers reveals a deeper eastern corridor, and the destination becomes Storm itself.

Act XI–XII — Beyond the Certified Map, and the Shelter of the Open Sky

Following Ben's parents' old route beyond Sixteen, the team crosses a vast field of moving black glass into terrain transformed since that earlier survey, reaching a newly exposed stair marked with a split spiral and the inscription Shelter of the Open Sky — Enter Beneath Storm. Leave Beneath Stars. The site is a self-contained magical sanctuary, not a dungeon: warm rain falling from an impossible night sky over a clean central pool. A knot of living storm magic rests in a weathered statue's hands. Ben reaches for it.

Act XIII — Hold On

The sanctuary's lightning strikes Ben directly. Every survival instinct says let go; he chooses to hold on. The discharge grounds through him and the pool without touching his companions. He wakes with his hair turned silver, his eyes vivid electric blue, and a new, lasting sense for pressure, gusts, and coming lightning. Rian recalls old western climbing stories using the term for people so adapted to magical storm country — storm walker. Ben reaches Ranger 3 here, earned by what he chose to endure rather than by routine advancement.

Act XIV–XV — Alderwick, and the Hall of the Open Sky

Beyond a moving field of lightning-charged glass columns, the team finds a ruined city large enough to change the scale of the whole campaign — a surviving marker still reading ALDERWICK. This is the same city played alive in Campaign B18, physically rediscovered roughly 1,281 years later; B19 does not assign every B18 street a modern ruin coordinate, and the first survey stays deliberately partial. A sealed Eastern Ward civic case directs citizens unable to reach western refuge during severe sky events to the Hall of the Open Sky — a real public civic refuge, not a hidden cult chamber, and a separate place entirely from the Shelter reached earlier. Reaching toward its central black pool, Ben perceives an immense storm current extending east and a colossal ring of magic within it.

Act XVI — The Storm Great Seal

East of Alderwick, within the clear centre of the great storm wall, the party finally reaches it: a vast vertical ring of pure magical weather — lightning, rain, wind, and pressure continuously forming and reforming the same stable shape. No stone frame, metal casing, or mechanism holds it together. The distinction the whole campaign has been building toward becomes physical: Sixteen is infrastructure, the focus is a regulator, and the Storm Great Seal is magic given measure. It does not ask Ben to repair it or appoint him its Warden. It asks him to witness.

Act XVII — Storm Answered, and the Shattering

Through direct contact, Ben receives preserved memory: living Alderwick; then the setting generation shaping Storm from raw weather into permanent measure, answering last among the eight but answering — confirming rather than rewriting B17's own record. Then, roughly eight years later, the Shattering strikes the fertile Alderwick Basin. Storm does not cause it. Storm opens wider and holds as much of the catastrophe's self-feeding magical weather as it can. The Badlands are the wound; Storm is the hand pressed against it — Ben's own understanding of what he's shown, not a line recovered from any ancient record. He also witnesses an injured post-Shattering Storm Warden's transmission repeatedly failing mid-report: the historical "report incomplete" was a broken signal from a survivor, not proof the Seal failed. The vision shows the burden growing markedly worse in relatively recent memory — its exact cause left unshown.

Act XVIII — The Release

Ben pushes back into contact with one direct question: can the pressure be eased here and now? Anchored by Galen, Rian, Jude, Pella, Veya, and Tessa, he identifies a moving high-atmospheric corridor capable of taking a safe release upward rather than out across inhabited land, on an exceptional Survival result. The Seal opens; a vast column of accumulated pressure vents skyward. Across the Badlands, self-feeding storm systems loosen — some breaking into ordinary rain, others simply losing their unnatural intensity. The permanent storm wall collapses. The Seal itself remains intact, active, and holding, but calms and withdraws after the release; Ben passes out for a little over three hours and wakes with the weather-sense still intact, confirming it as lasting change, not proximity to the Seal.

Act XIX — Home to Greymount

A broad, deliberately partial Alderwick survey — safe approaches, the Nine Arches, the reservoir-facing landscape — is recorded before the team turns west. All seven expedition members return to Greymount alive, where clear sky is already visible over territory that had been storm-locked for as long as anyone living can remember. Accurate dispatches go to Professor Halden and Silver Bastion, who provisionally record Storm as located, active, and holding; Nicholas Soren is copied as the researcher whose B17 work made the discovery possible, but does not arrive to become B19's protagonist.

Act XX — The Lodge, the Route, and Galen

The Farbound celebration centres the whole team, not just Ben — seven went deep and seven came home. Pella publicly reminds Ben of his old promise that she'd always have a place on his chosen team; he confirms it, and Rian is recognised as having earned his own place through the expedition rather than through Pella. The informal name that's already been fitting itself to Ben — Storm Walker — catches around the Lodge; he blushes at the attention but accepts that it fits. Maud's pride stays characteristically understated: she raises a cup rather than a speech.

Maud gives Ben a fresh Farbound copy of his parents' old Badlands route. Their original turn-back point is still marked — TURNED HERE. EASTERN GROUND UNSTABLE. ROUTE UNPROVEN. — but the map no longer stops there: new ink carries it through the black-glass field, the Shelter of the Open Sky, Alderwick, and the Storm expedition, annotated STRAKE EXPEDITION — ROUTE PROVISIONALLY OPEN, 343 ARV. Ben frames his parents' old sketch properly and hangs the new route map beside it — not proof he's completed their destiny, but because their expedition came home, and his continued from the work they responsibly recorded.

Galen is introduced to Maud explicitly as Ben's boyfriend; her only real question is whether he makes Ben happy, and Galen answers yes without hesitation. Ben later takes him back to the old inner-wall parapet where they first kissed and sketches him there, against the newly visible eastern horizon — a portrait meant to hang beside the family sketch and the route map, giving Galen a literal place inside Ben's home rather than leaving him as a romance that exists only on expeditions. The campaign closes with the two of them looking east: the land isn't fixed, the mysteries aren't exhausted, but the horizon is visible.

World State After Campaign B19

Benjamin Strake. Level 3 Ranger / Storm Walker, permanently marked by silver hair and electric-blue eyes, with a lasting sense for magical weather. Remains a Farbound delver — not a Warden, Seal owner, or secret bloodline heir. His parents' deaths remain an ordinary Badlands tragedy, not a conspiracy. Openly monogamous with Galen Vardane.

Storm Great Seal. Located east of ruined Alderwick and confirmed by direct contact: magic given stable form, successfully set before the Shattering — "Storm answered last, but Storm answered" is now witnessed evidence, not just recovered text. The Shattering created the Badlands' self-sustaining magical weather; Storm has spent more than twelve centuries containing it, not causing it. The historical "report incomplete" reflects a failed transmission from a surviving Warden, not a failed Seal. Remains intact, active, and holding after B19 — substantially relieved of its accumulated burden, not repaired or replaced. The cause of its more recent, comparatively sharper strain remains unidentified.

Sixteen and the Primary Measure. Confirmed as ancient storm-management infrastructure — a regulator, not the Great Seal itself, and not proof that the Badlands must be cleared by finding and repairing a chain of similar sites.

Alderwick. Physically rediscovered for the first time since its destruction in the Shattering. Its B18 living-city layer remains the historical baseline; B19's survey is real but partial, and most B18 locations do not yet have a confirmed modern ruin coordinate. It remains a ruin — glassed, broken, ecologically altered — not a restored city, and a major future archaeological priority rather than a site B19 exhausted.

The Badlands. The extreme, permanent, self-feeding magical storm regime is substantially and permanently calmed for the first time in more than twelve centuries. This is not restoration: the terrain remains Shattering-scarred, glassed, and dangerous, and improved weather does not mean improved safety. Large interior areas are newly visible and reachable, which the Farbound Guild treats as a new survey-and-certification problem rather than an invitation.

Farbound Guild. Becomes the practical gateway for responsible exploration of the newly accessible Badlands interior — a Greymount-local institution, not a continent-wide authority or owner of the region.

Open threads carried forward. The exact cause of Storm's more recent strain before B19; the long-term climatic pattern of the calmer Badlands; the full survey and reconstruction of ruined Alderwick and which specific B18 locations can be securely relocated within it; the builders and full history of the Shelter of the Open Sky; Storm's current Warden lineage, if any survives; and the modern condition of the remaining unresolved Great Seals.

Canon status: follows the source account closely. B19 directly resolves several questions B17 deliberately left open — see the Canon Ledger for the specific corrections and locks, the Magic Codex for the Storm Great Seal's technical distinction from Sixteen's infrastructure, and the Atlas for ruined Alderwick and the calmer Badlands.
