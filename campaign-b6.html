Core Premise

A tonal shift for Timeline B. What begins as a Luminara-aligned resistance investigation into Shah ritual influence in Greymount evolves into a deeply personal arc of autonomy, severance, and rooted independence — the first Timeline B campaign to resolve through civic stabilisation and character consolidation rather than conquest or metaphysical collapse.

Party & Key Figures

  • Dain Corvett PC Twilight Cleric leading a small Luminara resistance cell in Greymount. Survives an escalating conditioning arc and an attempted Ley-Heir overwrite; ends the campaign as founder of The Lantern in Evermere.
  • Joryn
    Co-leader of the resistance cell; stabilises Dain during the overwrite crisis and asserts her own emotional boundaries through the campaign, becoming his conscious partner rather than simply a co-leader under strain.
  • Cassian, Rafe & Edrin
    Remaining members of the Greymount resistance cell alongside Dain and Joryn.
  • Serio
    Cell member later revealed to have been casting rituals without full disclosure, contributing to resonance instability; expelled once exposed.
  • Lucan
    Shah-aligned former lover of Dain; carries out repeated conditioning rites that make him vulnerable to metaphysical manipulation, and functions as a partial vector in the attempted Ley-Heir overwrite. Survives, unpossessed, with no resolution arc concluded — a living thread left open.
  • Maera Holt
    Confirmed as permanent manager of The Lantern by campaign's end — civic stabiliser, information conduit, and protective matriarchal figure for the household forming around it.
  • Tomas Holt
    Maera's nephew, 18; performs an original piece at The Lantern showing natural bardic resonance and crowd command, marked as a potential future adventuring candidate.
  • Edrin (Ashvale)
    18; receives a quiet Luminara blessing of belonging during the campaign and begins forming real emotional stability — the first signs of genuine community integration.

Act-by-Act

Act I — Greymount: Ritual and Fracture

Dain's resistance cell investigates Shah-aligned ritual influence in Greymount. Lucan's repeated conditioning rites on Dain draw on dominance/submission symbolism as psychological and resonance-attunement rituals — intimate and voluntary in form, but functioning underneath as slow conditioning that steadily increases Dain's vulnerability to metaphysical manipulation. Serio, meanwhile, is exposed as having cast rituals without disclosing them to the cell, feeding resonance instability from inside their own ranks; once caught, he's expelled, and the cell's internal trust starts to fracture.

Act II — The Ley-Heir Threshold

Under escalating ritual exposure, dormant Ley-Heir architecture — a direct legacy of Campaign B5 — activates at a personal level between Dain, who reads as an echo of Lio/Vareth's resonance by proximity, and Lucan, a potential Caen-aligned vector. Conditions align for a partial overwrite attempt, and it fails: Dain's conscious resistance, Twilight's divine counter-field, Joryn's emotional grounding, and the simple absence of any active conduit infrastructure all work against it together. No body displacement occurs; no echo possession manifests. The event confirms something important for the timeline going forward — its Ley-Heir architecture remains viable, but it isn't autonomous yet.

Act III — The Murder and Withdrawal

A targeted killing tied to the cell's resistance activity sharply raises their exposure risk in Greymount. Combined with the metaphysical destabilisation and the emotional fracture from Serio's betrayal, the cell makes the call to withdraw entirely, choosing Evermere as their destination.

Act IV — Evermere: Rooting Instead of Running

In Evermere, Dain purchases a coastal tavern — The Lantern — marking a real philosophical shift from resistance cell to civic anchor. Beneath South Reef, the group discovers an ancient maritime governance vault; rather than claim control of it, Dain reinforces its seal using Twilight magic alone, leaving its infrastructure preserved and its civic function intact without any single faction capturing it.

Act V — House Corvett

Dain's estranged noble family issues a formal summons. Rather than confront them privately, Dain lets the truth of his exile circulate openly through the dock networks — that he was exiled for refusing an arranged political marriage, that his exile stemmed from his own autonomy, and that he wants no reinstatement. House Corvett formally severs him from the family lineage. Dain accepts it without protest. No retaliation follows, and the inland noble thread that's shadowed him ends cleanly.

Act VI — The Valmere Agreement

House Valmere proposes a maritime trade agreement. Dain revises the terms so the agreement binds to the Evermere Harbor Authority rather than to himself personally — no exclusivity, Evermere's sovereignty explicitly recognised, infrastructure shared rather than owned by any one party. Evermere is strengthened by it; no annexation of any kind occurs.

Act VII — Stability and Belonging

The campaign closes on quiet consolidation rather than crisis: Edrin receives a Luminara blessing and begins putting down real roots for the first time; Tomas Holt's performance at The Lantern marks him as a name worth watching in future campaigns; Maera Holt is confirmed as the tavern's permanent manager and its protective centre.

World State After Campaign B6

Geography. The Lantern is established as a coastal tavern in Evermere — the first dedicated civic anchor point for what becomes a recurring cast across Campaigns B6–B9. The South Reef Vault is preserved intact beneath the city, sealed but uncaptured by any faction.

Magic. No ley collapse, no conduit reactivation, no Dominion contamination anywhere in Timeline B. The Ley-Heir architecture seeded in B5 remains dormant — its overwrite potential confirmed real but still entirely untriggered. This campaign demonstrates that Timeline B's proto-Ley-Heir architecture can activate around any bonded pair under the right conditions, not just Caen and Vareth specifically — a worrying generalisation of the pattern the Order will need to account for going forward.

The Durrel Line. Still absent — this campaign continues the trend of Timeline B developing its coercion/consent and identity-conditioning storylines entirely independent of any Durrel-descended mechanism.

Institutions. Greymount ends the campaign stable, Shah influence covert but contained, no uprising. Evermere ends it with strengthened maritime autonomy, independent of inland noble control, and The Lantern established as a genuine anchor node. No war, no purge, no regime change anywhere on the continent — for once in Timeline B, nothing is collapsing.
