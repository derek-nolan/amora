The other half of Amora's history

Factions, Orders & Institutions

Amora is as much a history of institutions as characters — orders founded, corrupted, severed, and refounded across both timelines, often by the same handful of doctrines wearing new names. This page tracks each one: what it believed, when it stood, who led it, what it became, and how it ended (or didn't).

The Seal Wardens

Ancient custodial lineages, not a single order — related traditions, not one bloodline.

Doctrine
Each Great Seal regulates a distinct pressure in the world; each has its own Warden lineage trained to recognise and stabilise it. Their histories aren't uniform — some remained active, some lapsed, some fell.
Era
Predates Campaign I; the founding magic system of Vanmere itself.
Headquarters
None single — each lineage has its own seat; the Heartwardens federate several traditions at Silver Bastion.
Founders
Not directly attested as a single founding act — an ancient network older than any surviving record of its origin.
Successors
The Campaign I Heartwardens reunite several lapsed traditions into one federated order.
Fate
Varies by lineage — see below.

Eight Great Seals are currently recognised: Heart, Flame, Stone, Storm, Life, Shadow, Light, and Frost — each with its own Warden lineage. Only some lineage names, seats, and surviving orders are presently recovered. Known traditions: Heartwardens (Durrel-linked Heart lineage, below), Flamewardens (Arienna Lume's line, Flamebinder in older bloodline terminology), Stonewardens (confirmed lineage, representative unresolved), and Icewardens (see below — the only lineage with a confirmed fallen branch, and, as of Campaign B16, a confirmed unfallen Western branch too). Storm, Life, Shadow, and Light currently have no located Warden lineage or representative at all — recorded here as genuine gaps, not filled in. Older material describing Aether/Air, Veil, and Balance as Great Seals is now legacy terminology superseded by the current eight; see the Canon Ledger. See the Magic Codex for the full Seal-by-Seal registry and confidence levels.

The Icewardens

Not every Warden lineage stayed whole.

Doctrine
Original custodians of the Frost/Ice Seal — recognition, approach, and stabilisation of Menge's frost pressure.
Era
Ancient, predating Campaign I; the fall is contemporary with Campaign I itself, 0–27 ARV.
Headquarters
Menge, broadly — the exact original Seal sanctuary is unrecovered; Vyrn's Spring is the strongest surviving named marker.
Founders
Not directly attested.
Successors
The Frostborn (Timeline B) — descendants or cultural survivors of the unfallen branch, confirmed distinct from the fallen line below.
Fate
A branch fell and became the Icebinders (below); the parent lineage itself is not established as having fallen in full.

Central to one of Campaign I's core faction histories: a Warden lineage losing its custodial purpose without that implying the whole bloodline was ever inherently corrupt. See Campaign I and the Magic Codex's "Fall of the Icewardens" entry.

The Icebinders

The fallen branch — not the neutral name for the whole Frost lineage.

Doctrine
A cult tied to the Sleeper Beneath the Frost; spread destructive frost attacks through Menge rather than stabilising the Seal.
Era
Active through Campaign I, ended 0–27 ARV.
Headquarters
Menge — exact sanctuary unrecovered.
Founders
A fallen branch of the Icewardens; the break itself isn't dated precisely.
Successors
None — not to be confused with the Frostborn (unfallen northern descendants) or the Western Icewardens (an entirely separate branch that left before the fall, below).
Fate
Ended when Zethyr, Taren, and their allies settle the Frost Seal in Campaign I. Timeline B's Order of Anchors later concludes the Icebinders were "never a cult" after finding genuine unfallen survivors at Hollowrest — a conclusion this site treats as a documented overcorrection, not the final word; see the Magic Codex.

The clearest example on this site of a Warden lineage's fallen branch being distinct from — and not retroactively excusing, nor excused by — its unfallen kin.

The Western Icewardens

"No claim upon the Seal. No abandonment of Frost. Measure divided so memory cannot fall in one place."

Doctrine
Preservation over power — a deliberate post-Shattering redundancy system, not exile or apostasy. Three Frost households travelled west by agreement, carrying a duplicated "second measure," verification standards, and historical record while explicitly renouncing any claim to the northern Frost Seal.
Era
Founded c. -904 to -900 ARV, shortly after the Shattering (c. -905 ARV) damaged reporting and access across the newly-set Great Seal network — see the Ancient Chronology. Rediscovered and formally recognised by Silver Bastion in Campaign B17, c. 342 ARV.
Headquarters
Rimeholt, founded as their refuge and now an ordinary town that forgot its own origin until B16.
Founders
Three Frost households who separated from the northern Icewardens by mutual agreement, along with spouses, craftspeople, and others travelling under their protection — not a break, expulsion, or fall; northern and western households remain one older Frost kinship even after their offices divided.
Successors
None named beyond the current Custodian — recognition determines the office, not bloodline alone.
Fate
Formally recognised by Silver Bastion in Campaign B17 as a legitimate preservation branch with no northern Seal claim and no acquired northern authority. Nicholas Soren is the recognised Custodian, Western Branch, with the paired Way-hand office held by Jarek Flint; the non-private Western custodial corpus is now intentionally distributed across Rimeholt, Highever Collegium, Silver Bastion, and Durrel Ridge so memory can't fail in a single place again.

Carries no portion of the Frost Seal and makes no claim to Vyrn's Spring or the northern sanctuary — its entire function is preservation. See the Magic Codex for the Custodian/Way-hand pairing in full, Campaign B16 for its rediscovery, and Campaign B17 for its full institutional history and formal recognition.

The Heartwardens

"Through Heart, Balance."

Doctrine
Paladins and stewards of the Heart Seal — protection through balance, not conquest.
Era
Timeline A, Campaign I, 0–27 ARV (founding); revived generations after an original lapse.
Headquarters
Silver Bastion, its fortress-temple.
Founders
Originally founded by Tarenus Durrel, generations before Campaign I; revived (not refounded) by Zethyr Durrel and Taren Durrel.
Successors
The Veilwardens (Campaign II, after corruption); the New Heartwardens (Campaign III, after Severance); indirectly, the Order of Anchors in Timeline B.
Fate
Consumed from within — Taren renames them Veilwardens as the Dominion consolidates.

Banner of crimson, white, and gold; remembered oath sworn under Zethyr's creed. Founded as both paladins and stewards, their rise to continental trust is tied directly to the Frost Seal crisis they resolve in their first year. The order's own fall becomes the templates every later Amoran institution is built to guard against — a genuinely good order calcifying into empire from the inside.

See Campaign I for the founding.

The Veilwardens

"Through Shadow, Dominion" — the Heartwardens' own creed inverted.

Doctrine
Hierarchy and control in service of the Dominion; obedience recast as devotion.
Era
Timeline A, Campaign II, 28–55 ARV.
Headquarters
Silver Bastion, reforged as the Obsidian Bastion / Sanctum of the Veil.
Founders
Taren Durrel, renaming the order he once served after founding the Dominion.
Successors
The New Heartwardens (Campaign III), founded explicitly in reaction against them.
Fate
Ended by Zethyr's Severance — the first recorded use of the mechanism, though it has no name yet when he performs it.

Old Council figures who resist the transformation — Seraphine Vale among them — are executed as the new order replaces the old, institutional and spiritual resistance crushed in the same stroke. Everything the Heartwardens once protected, the Veilwardens enforce instead.

See Campaign II.

The Silver Dawn

Compassionate order, in Luminara's own image.

Doctrine
Justice, mercy, and light — the direct model for later Light-resistance traditions.
Era
Attested across Timeline B, notably Campaign B1 onward, and referenced in Timeline A's early paladin tradition.
Headquarters
Not fixed to a single seat; oversight and squire-training presence recorded at Durrel Ridge and beyond.
Founders
Not directly attested — a standing order rather than a single-campaign founding.
Successors
Its Timeline B graduates go on to found or join the Order of Anchors and, later, the Durrel Ridge Wardens.
Fate
Still active as of the latest recorded campaigns — Corvin Hale remains a Silver Dawn paladin through Campaigns B13–B14.

Lio Serrin begins Campaign B1 as a Silver Dawn squire before the founding rupture makes him Timeline B's first Anchor — the order provides oversight and mentorship in the Age of Founding's earliest days, one of several traveller-and-institution partnerships that stabilise the new timeline.

Order of Anchors

Partners, not specimens; service, not study.

Doctrine
Stabilising seals, containing ruptures, and safeguarding the six time-displaced travellers as people rather than research subjects.
Era
Timeline B, founded Campaign B1, 0 ARV.
Headquarters
Silver Bastion.
Founders
Seraphine Vale and Kellan Vance (later Caen), formally founded at Silver Bastion after the Mind's destruction.
Successors
None formal — its founding principle survives in the Durrel Ridge Wardens' own culture after Seraphine's death.
Fate
Loses its founding moral centre when Seraphine is killed in Campaign B5's Shah-aligned internal conflict; paladin authority is absorbed into Caen's own sovereign command structure with no senior figure left to check it.

Timeline B's entire founding institution — born the instant Taren and Zethyr are unmade, in the same chamber, on the same night. Seraphine's own principle, asserted directly against a Council leadership eager to treat the six travellers as research subjects, becomes the order's whole culture. See Campaign B1.

Collegium Arcanum

Magic catalogued, not merely wielded.

Doctrine
A mage college linking scholarship to the founding paladin order — study first, power second.
Era
Timeline A, Campaign I (founding ranks); rebuilt Campaign III.
Headquarters
Linked to Silver Bastion in its founding era; location shifts with rebuilding.
Founders
Arienna Lume (also recorded as Arienna Voss), Magister and Flamewarden, among the Heartwardens' own founding ranks.
Successors
None named directly — its cataloguing function is echoed later by the Lyricum.
Fate
Survives the Dominion's fall in diminished form; rebuilt after the fractured city-states era to catalogue Veil remnants.

Arienna's own research eventually brings her into conflict with Taren — the Collegium's scholarly instinct to study the Veil directly is exactly the kind of inquiry the Dominion can't tolerate once it consolidates.

Collegium Equilibria

"Through Memory, Rebirth" — magic follows choice and vow, not decree.

Doctrine
Relationship-bound magic — shared custody, genuine consent, distributed guardianship — as a deliberate answer to Taren and Zethyr's own history.
Era
Timeline A, founded Campaign VI, roughly 5500 ARV.
Headquarters
Evermere's Collegium.
Founders
Kael Duren, "The Restorer," after rediscovering a Heart Shard older than the Choir or the Dominion.
Successors
None named directly; its stabilising doctrine is cited across several later ages whenever institutions drift back toward command-based magic.
Fate
Endures as a body of doctrine long after Kael's own reign — a philosophy more than a standing power structure.

The first age to consciously build magical institutions to avoid repeating the Taren/Zethyr pattern rather than simply react to its aftermath. See Campaign VI.

The Wardens & the Executor Office

"Through Law, Salvation" — and, one generation later, "Through Discipline, Purity."

Doctrine
Theocratic law as the mechanism of civic salvation; discipline as a route to purity.
Era
Timeline A, Campaigns VII–VIII, roughly 5800–5900 ARV.
Headquarters
The Gate.
Founders
Elian Dorn founds the Executor office (Campaign VII); Sirian codifies the Gate Codex a generation later (Campaign VIII).
Successors
None named directly — the Theocracy collapses under its own accumulated law.
Fate
Falls in fire and open rebellion at the opening of Campaign X, "The Severing," when Serik Thorne dismantles the destiny engines built up under its rule.

The Gate Codex outlives the Theocracy itself by a wide margin — cited by courts seeking legitimacy and tyrants seeking cover in equal measure, long after the Wardens who wrote it are gone. See Campaign VII and Campaign VIII.

The Hidden Ledger

Balance over faction.

Doctrine
A secretive information and archival network descended from the ancient Editors — loyalty to balance itself, not any one side.
Era
Timeline A, active by Campaign XI, roughly 6550 ARV.
Headquarters
Cells operate covertly across the Republic; a known presence in Veylaris Rest's Old Choir Quarter.
Founders
Not directly attested — an old order predating the campaign, descended from the Editors.
Successors
None named directly.
Fate
Survives Campaign XI's upheavals intact; Ilyen Vael co-authors Clause XI and the later Breath-Bridge on the Ledger's behalf.

Operatives like Ilyen Vael and Verrin Mael move between factions rather than serving one outright — the Ledger's entire value lies in being trusted by everyone a little and owned by no one. See Campaign XI.

The Lyricum

Magic codified as jurisprudence.

Doctrine
Treats magical law and precedent the way a legal system treats statute — documented, cited, argued.
Era
Timeline A, emergent by Campaign XI, roughly 6550 ARV.
Headquarters
Freight tunnels beneath Veylaris Rest connect it to the wider Republic; an "Annex" also recorded at Evermere in later eras.
Founders
Not directly attested as a single founding act — described as emergent alongside the Republic Inquest.
Successors
None named directly.
Fate
Active and stable through Campaign XI; Leora Vance becomes its archivist of the Breath after Clause Zero's founding.

Leora Vance risks censure to preserve fragments of Choir law within it rather than let power quietly erase them — documenting truth methodically while others act on it. See Campaign XI.

The Republic Inquest

Harmonic inheritance, monitored and witnessed.

Doctrine
Chartered oversight of the Twelve Herald Houses' descendant lineages — Witnesses, Editors, Recorders, Judges — each tracing back to a different facet of the old law.
Era
Timeline A, chartered by the close of Campaign XI, roughly 6550 ARV.
Headquarters
Republic-wide; tied to the Twelve Herald Houses rather than one seat.
Founders
Chartered as an institutional outcome of Campaign XI's events, following Riven Mareth's discovery of his own Herald lineage.
Successors
None named directly.
Fate
Stands as a going institution at Campaign XI's close, restoring a suppressed truth — that modern Republic history had erased Zethyr's role in ending the Dominion, remembering only Taren as a lone Lawgiver.

Confirms the Twelve Herald Houses (Mareth, Vael, Vance, Serin, Avenar, Tarell, Rhos, Dorn, Selene, Korin, Kethar, Verris) as the founding lineages behind the Republic's own institutions. See Campaign XI.

The Concord

Law without a throne — proof over rumour, receipts over spectacle.

Doctrine
Civic law made visible: witness-hand consent, line-of-sight accountability, proof-first process. Codified as Card + Law + Aegis.
Era
Timeline A's Third Cyberpunk Age, Campaigns XV–XVI, roughly 8210–8245 ARV.
Headquarters
Highever, later extended corridor-wide to Tarenus Reclaimed and the Port Annex.
Founders
The Concord Charter passes under Valen and Dareth in Campaign XV, extended into full corridor practice by Kaeso Vyr and Coren in Campaign XVI.
Successors
None — its proof-first ethic doesn't survive the Age of Disconnection's collapse.
Fate
Collapses when the last neon towers of Highever dim at the opening of Campaign XVII — the whole "printer, ink, perimeter" architecture reverts to ink, whispers, and knives almost overnight.

The high-water mark of proof-first civic architecture anywhere in the record — badge unification, Sanctuary Architecture, and Doors Law all trace back to the same founding charter. See Campaign XV and Campaign XVI.

The Lantern

Chosen family over inherited cages.

Doctrine
A resistance hub disguised as an ordinary coastal tavern — mutual aid, safehousing, and quiet civic resistance to Shah-aligned pressure.
Era
Timeline B, founded Campaign B6, active through at least Campaign B15.
Headquarters
Evermere, a coastal tavern on the waterfront.
Founders
Dain Corvett, using the tavern to convert a resistance cell into a genuine home; Maera Holt becomes its permanent manager.
Successors
None named directly — remains The Lantern throughout its recorded history.
Fate
Standing and active as of the latest campaigns to reference it; forms a mutual-aid pact with Durrel Ridge in Campaign B7. Its latest confirmed operational use is the warded river safehouse and extraction network coordinated with Maera's office during Campaign B15.

Its Lantern Annex (a converted dockside warehouse) and the Underworks beneath it extend its reach into training, evidence storage, and old smuggler corridors. See Campaign B6 and Campaign B15.

The Durrel Ridge Wardens

Sanctuary, witness, consent — "Light with teeth."

Doctrine
A paladin institution built on witness-truth over authority, and household belonging over bloodline purity.
Era
Timeline B, active Campaigns B7–B15, roughly 85 ARV to late 162 ARV; current through Campaign B15.
Headquarters
Durrel Ridge.
Founders
Traces to the same Tarenus Durrel who founded the original Heartwardens; formally revived and modernised by Taren Durrel (Ridge) in Campaign B9.
Successors
None — remains the standing institution through Campaign B15; Jack Durrel is Taren's intended household successor, a negotiated choice rather than automatic inheritance.
Fate
Reclaims Silver Bastion outright in Campaign B14, then closes its own founding conflict in B15 — breaching and repairing the Ridge's Heart Ward, striking directly into Brevair to end the Keystone and kill Rhalen Valecour, and formally inducted Mike Rookvale as a scout along the way without requiring a religious oath.

The clearest through-line in Timeline B — from Seren's road-warden years, through Taren-Ridge's Warden induction and Renewed Oath, to Jack's Warden of Meaning, Caelen's Oath of Vengeance, and Taren's own return to close the arc against Brevair. See the Timeline B Durrel Line on Characters for the full succession, Campaign B9 for its formal revival, and Campaign B15 for how the arc closes.

House Valecour

A city does not fall to conquest if it can be taught to prefer its chains.

Doctrine
Political dependency dressed as noble restoration — control through quiet institutional capture rather than open force.
Era
Timeline B, Campaigns B10–B15, roughly 146–162 ARV.
Headquarters
Brevair, seat of House Valecour itself.
Founders
An established noble house; its engineered "fall and rebirth" is architected covertly by Rhalen Valecour.
Successors
None — Durrel Ridge deliberately refuses to annex Brevair or choose its successor government.
Fate
Rhalen becomes Brevair's effective ruler in all but title through Delryn — publicly his acknowledged bastard, privately revealed as blood kin, and the living Keystone conduit of his rule. Ends in Campaign B15: Rhalen is killed personally by Taren Durrel, the Keystone permanently destroyed, and Delryn placed in protected custody to answer for his own complicity rather than being declared either wholly innocent or automatically condemned.

Widow houses, redirected chapel and charity funds, and quiet merchant/ magistrate dependency all become channels of Rhalen's consolidation — control that never needs to look like conquest. See Campaign B10 and Campaign B15.

The Ashen Hand

They helped build the cage before they understood it was one.

Doctrine
Internal Brevair resistance — magistrates, soldiers, servants, priests, and officials who helped construct Rhalen's Keystone system before recognising what they'd made.
Era
Timeline B, active by Campaign B15, c. 162 ARV.
Headquarters
Covert, within Brevair itself — no fixed seat.
Founders
Not directly attested as a single founding act; grown organically among people already embedded in Rhalen's civic and military apparatus.
Successors
None named — survives past B15 as a possible internal witness network, its future structure unresolved.
Fate
Their Grey Witness, Delryn Valecour, is confirmed as the resistance's own hidden centre. Member Garran Vey makes contact with Durrel Ridge, enters as a captured intruder, and becomes guide and eventual Bastion detainee alongside Delryn — accepting judgment for his own role rather than claiming immunity as a resistance member.

Distinct from The Lantern — the Ashen Hand works from inside the very system it opposes, made up of people who helped build Rhalen's control architecture before turning against it, rather than an external safehouse network. See Campaign B15.

Thorn House

Destroyed — no surviving doctrine statement.

Doctrine
Identity conditioning and false-consent architecture dressed as devotion; see the Magic Codex for the mechanism.
Era
Timeline B, active until its destruction in Campaign B13, roughly 162 ARV.
Headquarters
Within the Brevair sphere / road network — exact location deliberately left unpinned; see the Canon Ledger.
Founders
Not directly attested; led at the time of its fall by Maelor Venn, with Sareth as its senior handler.
Successors
None — its transformation infrastructure and command architecture are destroyed in the same operation that ends it.
Fate
Destroyed outright by Caelen Durrel's Ridge-led strike in Campaign B13 — Maelor killed by Caelen personally, Sareth killed attempting to reach a victim one last time, its command-line severing rite freeing every active victim it held in a single motion.

Proves its own doctrine against itself: no one who conditions another is safe from being conditioned in turn by someone senior in the same structure — exactly what happens to Rul Hask once Thorn House turns its methods on him. See Campaign B13.

Shah-Aligned Bastion Hierarchy (The Veiled Citadel)

Devotion is doing most of the work already — the Veil barely needs to help.

Doctrine
Conquest through the ruler / ward-knight / ward-anchor structure — a single interlocking chain of command binding a corrupted fortress-soul mechanism to a living conduit to a ruling authority.
Era
Timeline B, Campaign B11–B14, roughly 148–162 ARV.
Headquarters
Silver Bastion, publicly renamed the Veiled Citadel upon its reconsecration.
Founders
Cameron, rising through Gavren's genuine devotion to become the Veiled Citadel's outright ruler.
Successors
None — the structure is designed to collapse as a whole if any single piece breaks.
Fate
Ended completely in Campaign B14: Mike Rookvale kills Gavren (the ward-knight) and Cameron (the ruler) in the same operation, while Jack Durrel restores the ward-anchor directly — the whole interlocking structure falls at once, and the fortress reverts to Silver Bastion.

See the Magic Codex's "Meaning, Identity, and Relational Coercion" section for the full mechanical breakdown of how the structure actually worked and why breaking any one piece was enough to end all three. Campaign pages: B11, B14.
