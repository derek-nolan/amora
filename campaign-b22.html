The Shadow Great Seal, Confirmed at Vesperhold

Core Premise

The Measure Holds returns Timeline B to the modern Age of Rediscovery, later in c. 343 ARV, after The Way Home — B21's deep-history prequel happened in between in production order, but doesn't touch this modern present. Twenty-four-year-old Corvane paladin Elias "Eli" Renn, devoted to Luminara and grounded by Dane House, family, and an ordinary protector's instinct, is not introduced as a chosen figure or a hidden Warden descendant. A routine Mercer Street intervention embeds a black-glass splinter in his hand, awakening a Shadow-linked response that draws the covert Radiant Office's attention. Freeing four collared captives — Lucan Veyr, Seris Vale, Joss Arlen, and Perrin Vos — from Office control forms Eli's field party, joined by Corvane investigator and budding boyfriend Tavian Rell. Investigating the Office's Quiet House and an ancient breach beneath the western freight district pulls Eli through an unresolved observation landscape, into an unwanted amber pressure, and finally back to Corvane just as uncontrolled Shadow and reactive Light pressure erupt into two colossal manifestations over the city. Destroying them saves Corvane from the immediate fight but catastrophically discharges pressure into the ground beneath it, permanently altering the city and the land beyond. The pressure trail leads into the newly named Greywake Range, where Provost Caer Veylan's sabotage of the ancient High Orrery of Caereth forces a last, unstable choice, exposes Tavian's concealed betrayal, and ultimately reaches Vesperhold — the intact sanctuary of the long-unlocated Shadow Great Seal. Eli holds its emergency closure alone long enough to save the wider Greywake region. The Seal holds. Eli does not survive.

Where this sits relative to B20 and B21. B22 is Campaign 47 overall and is played after B21 in production order, but chronologically it is the next modern event after B20, returning to later c. 343 ARV rather than advancing the deep-history clock. It does not require or reference Alec Verran, Finn Carrow, or the ancient Ways established in B21 — those remain a separate, much older layer of history. B22 deliberately ends without a modern epilogue: Corvane's reconstruction, the surviving scope of the Radiant Office, the location of the Light Great Seal, and the identity of any modern Shadow Warden are all left for a later return to the present.

Party & Key Figures

  • Elias "Eli" Renn PC
    Human, Level 1 → 3 Paladin, Oath of Devotion. Twenty-four, Corvane-born, socially confident, warm, physically protective, and impulsive in ways that stay consequential rather than being smoothed away. Begins with conventional Luminara-aligned magic only — no secret Shadow ancestry, hidden Light Warden lineage, or prophecy. Dies beneath Vesperhold after holding the Seal's emergency closure.
  • Tavian Rell
    Twenty-seven, Rogue turned Arcane Trickster, a Corvane civic investigator and Eli's lover. Confident, irreverent, and genuinely attracted to Eli — his sexual relationship with Eli is real and consensual — but secretly collaborating with Caer Veylan since before Mercer Street, using enchanted intimate gifts to covertly condition Eli's devotion. Killed by Eli after the betrayal is exposed.
  • Seris Vale
    Fighter, Battle Master. A former Radiant Office captive Eli frees from a collar at Greyhaven; becomes one of the party's core martial anchors through Corvane, Caereth, and Vesperhold. Survives B22.
  • Lucan Veyr
    Sorcerer, Clockwork Soul mechanical chassis (narrative magic remains force/protective — no clockwork cosmology implied). The first person Eli deliberately frees from a collar, and the clearest early witness to the difference between a person's own magic and imposed Office control. Survives B22.
  • Joss Arlen
    Ranger, Hunter. A former collared operative who knows the Quiet House's drainage culvert; the party's practical field planner for ropes, routes, and loot. Survives B22.
  • Perrin Vos
    Monk, Warrior of the Open Hand. A former collared operative — fast, independent, and often already moving before the party finishes discussing a plan. Survives B22.
  • Rowan Kess
    The young man detained on Mercer Street and the black-glass fragment's original intended carrier; the transfer reaches Eli instead. Recovered from the western freight depot with black-glass veins in one arm and left in Corvane for treatment rather than joining the Greywake expedition. His long-term condition remains unresolved.
  • Captain Vesra Orl
    Senior Corvane civic officer, sceptical of concentrated religious/policing authority but decisive once Quiet House evidence proves civic systems are compromised. Secures the site, limits access to trusted officers, and helps keep freed captives out of compromised registries.
  • Mara Dane
    Eli's closest friend, a practical field medic associated with Dane House. Grounds the early campaign, treats the wounded, and survives the Corvane Disaster.
  • Avar Sen
    A new figure associated with maintaining the High Orrery of Caereth — not established as a Light or Shadow Warden. Helps the party understand Veylan's sabotage and survives the Caereth crisis; later title and role remain open.
  • Naeva Corren
    Keeper of Vesperhold — not established as the Shadow Warden. Identifies the deep-intake emergency and survives the upper sanctuary; the stair rejects her and admits Eli alone.
  • Provost Caer Veylan Antagonist
    Senior architect of the Radiant Office's B22 operation, with public standing as a medical philanthropist. Deliberately corrupts the High Orrery's readings, believing inaccessible Seal sites can be forced to reveal themselves when equilibrium fails, and treats Corvane as acceptable experimental material. Killed by Eli at Caereth; his body is lost into the collapsing Way route.

Act-by-Act

Act I — Mercer Street

B22 opens on a rainy Corvane evening with Eli serving around Dane House and already late for family dinner — a deliberately mundane opening for a paladin with no hidden destiny. Two officers detain a young man carrying black glass; the street lamps fail and a human-shaped absence in the light attacks. Eli intervenes, invokes Luminara, and exposes a black-glass shard inside the entity. Trying to destroy the glass, a splinter embeds beneath the skin at the base of his right thumb — a fragment originally intended for the detained man, not for him. His swordlight briefly flickers black before returning to gold.

Act II — Lucan Veyr and the Collars

Following up at the Renn house, Eli confronts officers operating under a falsified "Night Authority" order and finds a pale-coated man attacking with colourless white radiance capable of bleaching masonry. A thin geometric collar controls him. Eli discovers his emerging Shadow response can distinguish a person from an imposed restraint — breaking the collar precisely without touching the wearer. The freed man is Lucan Veyr, who names the organisation behind the collars: the Radiant Office, a covert network compromised into civic, medical, and religious systems, hunting something it calls the "Black Measure." Three more collared operatives approach; Eli frees them the same way. Tavian catches a dangerous intact white-glass ampoule before it can break.

Act III — Greyhaven House and Deren Vale

Rather than trust a possibly compromised civic chain, Eli moves the rescued operatives and a captured Office leader, Deren Vale, to Greyhaven House — a discreet Dane House convalescent lodging that becomes the party's early base. The rescued captives are Lucan Veyr, Seris Vale, Joss Arlen, and Perrin Vos; Tavian Rell, an existing acquaintance and mutual attraction of Eli's, becomes the sixth active field member. Interrogating Deren reveals the Office is a network rather than an official department, that the black-glass fragment wasn't meant for Eli, and that it leads to a Corvane site called the Quiet House — publicly the respectable Veylan Recovery Residence — housing a "Pale Engine" used to suppress Shadow responses and enforce mass collar purges.

Act IV — The Quiet House Raid

The six-person party infiltrates Veylan Recovery Residence through a drainage culvert Joss knows, in Corvane's northern river quarter. Perrin silently takes down an uncollared guard while Eli frees a collared guard, Rellan Marr, who joins the rescue. Reaching the Pale Engine during an active purge, Eli attacks it directly with longsword and Divine Smite, destroying the central lattice and darkening every restraint in the building. The raid frees seventeen confirmed captives plus additional patients and staff. Captain Vesra Orl personally secures the site afterward, keeping freed captives out of compromised registries.

Act V — Director Korr and the Black Chamber

During the Engine-room fight, Quiet House commander Director Maeven Korr — who uses Pale Light without a standard collar — is badly wounded, opens a lower access, and is seized by a multi-shadow phenomenon from the dark stair below. Seris catches her; Eli deliberately tells Seris to let her go. Korr is dragged into the darkness and never recovered — presumed dead, not confirmed. Recovered records distinguish "Black Chamber — local site" from "Shadow Seal — location unresolved," and identify Provost Caer Veylan as the senior architect above Korr, with cover as a medical philanthropist. The party does not descend into the lower excavation.

Act VI — Rowan Kess and the Freight-Depot Breach

Vesra establishes that the original Mercer Street prisoner, Rowan Kess, was diverted by a false Night Authority order rather than reaching civic medical custody. Tracing a transport number leads the party to a Corvane Municipal Conveyance depot in the western freight district, where Rowan is found alive in a locked shed, black-glass veins spreading through one arm, as Office personnel prepare to move him through a deliberately opened breach beneath the depot. Eli jumps through the breach alone — the moment B22 pivots from covert civic conspiracy into large-scale Great Seal adventure.

Act VII — The Unknown Landscape and the Golden Shard

Beyond the breach, Eli reaches a vast ancient landscape of ruins, colossal beings, and observation/transit structures whose exact cosmological nature is never established — explicitly not confirmed as an eight-Seal nexus, another world, or the physical centre of the Great Seals. A colossal creature carrying ruins on its back approaches, controlled by a massive golden/amber shard driven through its body. Eli blasts it with radiance beyond his ordinary spells, climbs it, and tears the shard free, releasing the creature from its imposed path. He decides not to absorb a surviving fragment; it enters him anyway through his existing black-glass wound. A natural-20 Constitution save lets him contain the resulting amber pressure — distinct from Shadow, from Luminara, and never confirmed as Light — rather than be consumed by it.

Act VIII — The Ancient Guardian

Exploring the ancient observation complex, the party recovers practical Way-adjacent field equipment — a Waywarden Transit Key, a +1 Custodian's Shield, a Wayfinder Lens, a Bag of Holding, Relation Anchors, and a broken Field Calibration Blade — none of which makes the site a Great Seal hub. A hundred-foot ancient guardian recognises Eli's unusual black-and-amber compatibility. Eli establishes a Custodian Accord on a simple principle: protect the inhabited place, and never let people become collateral. The guardian accepts him as a compatible field custodian, not an owner or pilot, and retains its own judgement throughout.

Act IX — Return to Corvane

When a return opening stabilises, Eli chooses to come back through it with the guardian, reuniting in the city with Tavian and Perrin, who had followed a second carriage during the depot events. Corvane is already under supernatural assault: uncontrolled Shadow pressure has gathered into a colossal moving manifestation, and a separate colourless manifestation of genuine Light pressure has risen reactively over another part of the city — the two escalating against each other over inhabited streets, a pressure interaction rather than proof either force is morally good or evil.

Act X — The Corvane Disaster

Eli enters the guardian's inner sanctum for a stronger communion, directing it to stabilise streets and shield civilians under the same protection principle. Rather than let the two manifestations continue trading destructive blows over a populated city, Eli chooses to destroy both at once. The attempt succeeds at destroying the manifestations but catastrophically fails to direct the released pressure — a double natural 1, with Heroic Inspiration deliberately declined. The discharge crashes into Corvane and the surrounding land: the river gains a major new branch, the Corvane Rupture Basin floods former farmland, a long fracture and escarpment corridor tears into the countryside, and more than a hundred deaths are confirmed during the campaign, with the final toll left open. The guardian throws Eli and Tavian clear before destroying itself protecting the city, its hundred-foot body becoming a permanent Corvane landmark.

Act XI — Oath of Devotion

The Corvane Disaster and the protector principle Eli established through the guardian earn him his Level 3 milestone: Paladin 3, Oath of Devotion, genuinely centred on Luminara rather than on Shadow, amber pressure, or any ancient custodial system. Seris, Lucan, Joss, and Perrin advance alongside him to their own Level 3 chassis. Eli and Tavian's relationship deepens into a genuine, consensual sexual and romantic partnership during this interval — a bond the campaign is explicit will later coexist, tragically, with Tavian's concealed betrayal.

Act XII — The Greywake Range

The pressure trail leads the party north-west into the newly named Greywake Range. Brannoch, a mountain town where three old roads meet, becomes their last substantial settlement before the ascent — its people already evacuating as word of a Caereth anomaly spreads. Caereth Peak has visibly split: one face locked in impossible daylight, the other in star-filled night, with snow falling upward along the boundary and an impossible staircase descending from the summit. The party crosses an unstable ravine bridge using the Waywarden Transit Key while colossal Light and Shadow regulator-creatures clash overhead.

Act XIII — The High Orrery of Caereth

Inside the mountain, the party meets Avar Sen, custodian of the High Orrery — an ancient Light/Shadow pressure-management and observation complex, not a Great Seal itself. All three regulator systems, Lumen, Umbra, and the central Meridian, are functioning but being fed false readings. Hearing movement behind the sealed Meridian door, Eli impulsively forces it open rather than first stabilising either side — succeeding, but worsening the mountain's instability. Inside stands Provost Caer Veylan with Office operatives and sabotage equipment, who admits plainly: stable Seals are inaccessible Seals, and he intends to force hidden routes, Wardens, and sites into visibility regardless of the cost to inhabited regions.

Act XIV — The Deep Conduits

While Eli protects Avar with Shield of Faith, Veylan removes both regulator rods, uncoupling Lumen and Umbra and threatening to collapse the mountain outright. Avar offers three emergency options: sacrifice more of Caereth, vent the pressure into the upper atmosphere, or send it into ancient deep conduits of unknown destination. Eli chooses the conduits to protect Brannoch and the valleys below — a consequential, protective decision rather than a malicious one. It saves Caereth from the worst immediate collapse, but the pressure branches away through routes Avar can't fully track; one branch's eventual destination becomes the delayed cause of Vesperhold's finale.

Act XV — Veylan Dies

Veylan flees through a spatially unstable side passage. Eli's successful Insight check catches Tavian deliberately helping him escape — turning aside a clear strike and directing Veylan toward the east stair. Tavian physically blocks Eli's pursuit; Eli drives through him and reaches Veylan at an unstable Way exit, striking with Sacred Weapon and a critical Divine Smite. Caer Veylan dies, his body lost into the collapsing spatial network as the route he was maintaining tears apart. Eli declines Heroic Inspiration on the emergency anchoring attempt; Seris and Perrin are separated from Eli and Tavian in the failure.

Act XVI — The Hidden Relay

Eli and Tavian are thrown together into an ancient underground relay containing fresh Radiant Office equipment and a prepared fallback site. Tavian recognises it immediately, confirming deeper prior involvement, and admits he had been collaborating with Veylan since before Mercer Street — helping create the conditions meant to provoke a measurable Light/Shadow response, though he insists he never intended Corvane's destruction.

Act XVII — Tavian Rell

Tavian attempts to use a hidden enchantment in the black wrist cuff Eli wears — an intimate gift — to calm and seduce him into forgetting what he's just learned. Eli knowingly allows the approach and a kiss, then uses the opening to drive his longsword through Tavian's heart with Divine Smite. Tavian dies; there is no escape, resurrection, or secret survival. Only after removing the cuff and a matching chastity device does Eli see concealed markings and recognise the equipment had been enchanted without his informed consent — the sexual relationship itself was genuine and consensual throughout; the conditioning layered secretly onto it was not.

Act XVIII — Change the Anchor

Searching the relay, the party finds Tavian's private notes. His central line: IF DEVOTION IS THE ANCHOR, CHANGE THE ANCHOR. He believed Eli's capacity to hold Luminara devotion while carrying Shadow and amber pressure meant that devotion itself might function as a stabilising anchor — and intended to make himself part of the hierarchy of that devotion rather than demand Eli abandon Luminara outright. The notes confirm his genuine desire for Eli to survive alongside his willingness to experiment on him; both remain true at once.

Act XIX — Vesperhold

The reunited surviving party reaches Vesperhold by ancient transit — a vast fortress cut into a remote Greywake cliff, farther north-northwest beyond Caereth Peak. Deep within, they find the colossal, stable Shadow structure itself: ancient text reading SHADOW IS SET. Vesperhold's Keeper, Naeva Corren, confirms the Shadow Great Seal is intact — the first Volume II campaign to directly confirm a previously unlocated Great Seal's modern location.

Act XX — The Cause of the Crisis

The danger at Vesperhold isn't the Seal awakening or attacking — it's the delayed arrival of the mixed Light/Shadow pressure Eli diverted into the deep conduits at Caereth, now reaching Vesperhold's surrounding infrastructure. The Shadow Great Seal performs its function and holds incoming Shadow within measure; what fails is the ancient conduit and sanctuary systems around it, with Light-heavy pressure from the same discharge also colliding inside the chamber. Naeva identifies an emergency closure beneath the Seal meant to sever the old deep relation — built for a Shadow Warden, but the descending stair rejects Naeva and admits only Eli.

Act XXI — The Measure Holds

Eli finds one mechanism: SEVER DEEP RELATION. Pulling it normally would cut the conduits but trap the already-arrived pressure inside, risking an explosive discharge into the wider Greywake region; remaining personally connected while it closes can draw the conflicting pressure inward instead. Eli chooses to stay. Luminara remains present and distinct throughout, anchoring him rather than being replaced by either pressure — his devotion stays chosen, never owned. His survival check fails. The closure succeeds anyway: every required deep relation severs, Vesperhold remains damaged but standing, Brannoch and the inhabited Greywake valleys are spared the worst collapse, and the Shadow Great Seal continues holding its permanent measure. Elias Renn dies beneath it. His black and amber marks burn out in the closure. The final surviving record reads SHADOW IS SET / MEASURE HOLDS / DEEP RELATION CLOSED. Seris reaches the sealed access too late. B22 cuts to black.

World State After Campaign B22

Elias Renn. Dead beneath Vesperhold, his death confirmed and permanent unless later canon explicitly reopens it. He was never a Shadow Warden and was never identified as a Light Warden; his Oath of Devotion remained centred on Luminara until the end. His Custodian's Shield and attuned Waywarden Transit Key are presumed sealed with his body.

The surviving field party. Seris Vale (Fighter 3, Battle Master), Lucan Veyr (Sorcerer 3, Clockwork Soul chassis), Joss Arlen (Ranger 3, Hunter), and Perrin Vos (Monk 3, Warrior of the Open Hand) all survive B22. Their grief, leadership, and next choices are deliberately left unplayed.

Tavian Rell. Dead, killed by Eli after his concealed collaboration with Veylan and non-consensual magical conditioning were exposed. His genuine attraction to and care for Eli, and his abuse of Eli's trust, are both held as true — B22 does not flatten him into either a redeemed victim or a loveless predator.

The Radiant Office. The Quiet House is exposed and its Pale Engine destroyed; Veylan and Tavian are dead, and Director Maeven Korr is presumed dead with her body unrecovered. The exact scale of surviving Office remnants, safehouses, and compromised personnel remains deliberately open — B22 does not add a hidden superior mastermind above Veylan.

The Shadow Great Seal. Confirmed intact at Vesperhold in the remote north-western Greywake highlands — the third Great Seal with a directly confirmed modern sanctuary, alongside Heart at Silver Bastion and Storm east of Alderwick. The modern Shadow Warden lineage, if any survives, remains unresolved; Eli's emergency access was compatibility, not Warden recognition.

The Light Great Seal. Location, sanctuary, and modern Warden all remain unrecovered. The Corvane Light manifestation and the Light pressure encountered at Caereth are both genuine Light pressure, but neither is the Seal itself, and Luminara remains distinct from Light pressure throughout.

Corvane and the Greywake Range. Corvane survives but is permanently altered — a new river branch, the Corvane Rupture Basin, a long fracture/escarpment corridor, and the fallen ancient guardian's body all become lasting features. The Greywake Range, Brannoch, Caereth Peak, the High Orrery, and Vesperhold are all newly established modern geography; Caereth survives damaged, and Vesperhold survives damaged and sealed.

Open threads carried forward. The modern Shadow Warden's identity, if one exists; the Light Great Seal's location; the other deep-conduit branches exported from Caereth and their consequences elsewhere in Brevair; the true origin of Eli's amber/Conduit pressure and the unresolved observation landscape; Rowan Kess's black-glass graft; the surviving scale of the Radiant Office; and Corvane's long-term reconstruction. B22 deliberately ends without a modern epilogue.

Canon status: follows the source account closely. B22 is a modern Age of Rediscovery campaign and does not alter B18 or B21's deep-history state — see the Canon Ledger for the hard separation between an intact Great Seal and the mortal infrastructure around it, and between Light/Shadow pressure and moral alignment, the Magic Codex for the Shadow Great Seal's confirmation at Vesperhold and the amber Conduit phenomenon's unresolved identity, and the Atlas for Corvane's post-disaster geography and the Greywake Range.
